Lesson 22 · Topic 4: Fourier series

Half the function, all the series: even and odd extensions

The one win: given $f$ only on $(0,L)$, extend it evenly or oddly, sketch three periods, and compute the half-range coefficients, including a trig piece that needs product-to-sum and one special $n$.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: the most common Fourier opener on past papers is "$f$ is given on $(0,a)$ with $f(-x)=\pm f(x)$ and $f(x+2a)=f(x)$: even, odd or neither? Sketch three periods. Compute only the non-zero coefficients." That is 5 to 9 marks. The formula sheet has the full-range $a_n,b_n$ only. The half-range versions below are not on it, so memorise them (or re-derive in one line).

0 · Warm-up from Lesson 21

W1. $f$ is odd on $(-L,L)$. Then $f(x)\cos\!\big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{L}\big)$ is…

odd × even = odd, and $\int_{-L}^{L}(\text{odd})=0$. So $a_n=0$ for all $n$, including $a_0$.

W2. For $m=1,2,3,\dots$, $\;\sin\!\big(\tfrac{(2m-1)\pi}{2}\big)=$…

$m=1$: $\sin\frac\pi2=1$; $m=2$: $\sin\frac{3\pi}2=-1$; alternates starting at $+1$.

1 · Two ways to extend $f$ from $(0,L)$

A Fourier series needs $f$ on a full period $(-L,L]$. Given only $(0,L)$, you choose the left half (Lecture 18, slide 6):

on $(-L,0)$seriessketch move
even$F(x)=f(-x)$cosine only, $b_n=0$mirror in the $y$-axis
odd$F(x)=-f(-x)$sine only, $a_n=0$rotate $180^\circ$ about the origin

Then $F(x+2L)=F(x)$: copy the $(-L,L]$ block left and right. Toggle below for $f(x)=2-x$ on $(0,2)$.

Sketching for marks (2 marks, 2 minutes).

  1. Draw $f$ on $(0,L)$. Mark its end values $f(0^+)$, $f(L^-)$.
  2. Mirror (even) or rotate (odd) onto $(-L,0)$.
  3. Copy that $2L$ block to fill three periods, e.g. $-3L<x<3L$. Label $\pm L,\pm2L,\pm3L$ and the heights.
  4. Show jumps as clear vertical gaps. Check $x=0$ and $x=\pm L$: that is where jumps appear.

Continuity check (no sketch needed). For $f$ continuous on $[0,L]$:
even extension: always continuous.
odd extension: continuous only if $f(0)=0$ and $f(L)=0$. Otherwise it jumps at $x=0$ (by $2f(0)$) and/or at $x=\pm L$ (by $2f(L)$).

Q1. $f(x)=2-x$ on $(0,2)$, even extension $F$ with period $4$. Then $F(3)=$…

Shift into $(-2,2]$: $F(3)=F(-1)$. Even: $F(-1)=f(1)=1$. Plugging $3$ into $2-x$ gives the trap answer $-1$: the formula only holds on $(0,2)$.

Q2. $f(x)=x$ on $(0,L)$. Which periodic extension is continuous on all of $\mathbb R$?

Even: a triangle wave, continuous. Odd: $F=x$ on $(-L,L)$, a sawtooth jumping from $L$ to $-L$ at $x=\pm L$, since $f(L)=L\ne0$.

2 · Half-range formulas (memorise)

Even integrand on $(-L,L)$: $\int_{-L}^{L}=2\int_0^L$. Apply to the sheet's formulas:

$$\textbf{even ext. (cosine series):}\quad a_n=\frac2L\int_0^L f(x)\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx,\qquad b_n=0.$$ $$\textbf{odd ext. (sine series):}\quad b_n=\frac2L\int_0^L f(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx,\qquad a_n=0.$$

Series: $\dfrac{a_0}2+\sum a_n\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}L$ or $\sum b_n\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}L$. "Half-range cosine/sine series" means exactly these.

Q3. $f$ is given on $(0,L)$ and you want its odd periodic extension. The coefficient to compute is…

Odd extension → sine series. The $\frac1L\int_{-L}^L$ of the sheet becomes $\frac2L\int_0^L$ because $F\sin$ is even.

Worked: both series for $f(x)=2-x$ on $(0,2)$ ($L=2$, one integration by parts each).

Odd extension: $b_n$
$$b_n=\int_0^2(2-x)\sin\tfrac{n\pi x}2\,dx=\Big[-(2-x)\tfrac{2}{n\pi}\cos\tfrac{n\pi x}2\Big]_0^2-\tfrac2{n\pi}\int_0^2\cos\tfrac{n\pi x}2\,dx=\frac{4}{n\pi}-0.$$ $$F_{\text{odd}}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac4{n\pi}\sin\frac{n\pi x}2.$$
Even extension: $a_0$, $a_n$
$$a_0=\int_0^2(2-x)\,dx=2,\qquad a_n=\Big[(2-x)\tfrac2{n\pi}\sin\tfrac{n\pi x}2\Big]_0^2+\tfrac2{n\pi}\int_0^2\sin\tfrac{n\pi x}2\,dx=\frac{4\,(1-(-1)^n)}{n^2\pi^2}.$$

Zero for even $n$, $\frac8{n^2\pi^2}$ for odd $n$. Write odd $n=2m-1$:

$$F_{\text{even}}(x)=1+\frac8{\pi^2}\sum_{m=1}^\infty\frac{1}{(2m-1)^2}\cos\frac{(2m-1)\pi x}2.$$

Drag the slider in the plot above: the continuous even extension is matched fast ($1/n^2$); the odd one, with jumps at $0,\pm4,\dots$, slowly ($1/n$). Why that happens is Lesson 23.

3 · Reading the exam's wording

  • "$f(-x)=f(x)$, $f(x+2a)=f(x)$" or "extend to an even function, then periodically" → even extension, $L=a$. Say: $f$ even, so $f\sin$ is odd and $b_n=0$.
  • "$f(-x)=-f(x)$" → odd, $L=a$. Say: $f$ odd, so $f\cos$ is odd and $a_n=0$ (incl. $a_0$).
  • "Compute only the non-zero coefficients" → that one-line parity reason earns the mark for the zero family. Then compute the other family, simplify with $\cos n\pi=(-1)^n$, $\sin\frac{n\pi}2$, and say which further $n$ give $0$.
  • Write the final series over the surviving $n$ only (e.g. $n=2m-1$).

Q4. You find $b_n=\dfrac{4\sin(n\pi/2)}{n^2\pi^2}$. Which $n$ give $b_n=0$?

$\sin(n\pi/2)=0$ when $n\pi/2$ is a multiple of $\pi$, i.e. $n$ even. Survivors: $n=2m-1$ with $\sin=(-1)^{m+1}$ (W2), so $b_{2m-1}=\frac{4(-1)^{m+1}}{(2m-1)^2\pi^2}$.

4 · A trig piece: product-to-sum and the special $n$

Example. $f(x)=\sin x$ for $0<x<\frac\pi2$, $f(x)=0$ for $\frac\pi2<x<\pi$. Find the cosine series of its even extension ($L=\pi$).

$a_n=\dfrac2\pi\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x\cos nx\,dx$ (the zero piece drops out). The sheet gives $\sin x\cos nx=\tfrac12\big[\sin((1-n)x)+\sin((1+n)x)\big]$.

The special $n$
Integrating $\sin((1-n)x)$ gives $-\frac{\cos((1-n)x)}{1-n}$: division by zero at $n=1$. The special case is whichever $n$ makes the two frequencies equal ($n\pi/L$ equal to the trig piece's frequency). Do it separately from the original integral. Also check $n=0$ separately if your formula breaks there.
$n=1$ (do first)
$$a_1=\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x\cos x\,dx=\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}\tfrac12\sin2x\,dx=\frac2\pi\cdot\frac12=\frac1\pi.$$
$n\ne1$
$$\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x\cos nx\,dx=\frac12\left[\frac{1-\cos\frac{(n-1)\pi}2}{1-n}+\frac{1-\cos\frac{(n+1)\pi}2}{1+n}\right].$$

Use $\cos\frac{(n-1)\pi}2=\sin\frac{n\pi}2$ and $\cos\frac{(n+1)\pi}2=-\sin\frac{n\pi}2$. With $s=\sin\frac{n\pi}2$:

$$\frac12\left[\frac{1-s}{1-n}+\frac{1+s}{1+n}\right]=\frac{(1-s)(1+n)+(1+s)(1-n)}{2(1-n^2)}=\frac{1-ns}{1-n^2}.$$ $$a_n=\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{1-n\sin\frac{n\pi}2}{1-n^2}\quad(n\ne1),\qquad a_0=\frac2\pi.$$
Final series
$$F(x)=\frac1\pi+\frac1\pi\cos x+\frac2\pi\sum_{n=2}^\infty\frac{1-n\sin\frac{n\pi}2}{1-n^2}\cos nx.$$

Check: $n=0$ in the general formula gives $\frac2\pi=a_0$ ✓, so no separate work there. Plugging $n=1$ gives $\frac00$: that is why it was done separately.

Q5. $L=3$ and $b_n=\tfrac23\int_0^1\sin(\pi x)\sin\!\big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{3}\big)dx$. Which $n$ needs separate treatment?

Frequencies $\pi$ and $\frac{n\pi}3$ coincide at $n=3$, where $\cos\big((1-\frac n3)\pi x\big)$ becomes $1$ and the antiderivative formula divides by $0$. It is $n=1$ only when $L=\pi$ and the piece is $\sin x$.

5 · Practice (exam level)

Do each on paper, then open the solution.

P1. $f=1$ on $(0,\frac\pi2)$, $f=0$ on $(\frac\pi2,\pi)$. Find the half-range sine series and say which $b_n$ vanish.

P2. Find the Fourier series of the odd periodic extension of $f(x)=\cos x$ on $(0,\pi)$. Is the extension continuous?

P3. Sketch the even periodic extension of $f(x)=\sin x$ on $(0,\pi)$ over $-3\pi<x<3\pi$, and find its Fourier series.

P1 solution
$$b_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin nx\,dx=\frac{2}{n\pi}\Big(1-\cos\frac{n\pi}2\Big).$$

$\cos\frac{n\pi}2$ cycles $0,-1,0,1$ for $n=1,2,3,4$: $b_n=\frac2{n\pi},\frac4{n\pi},\frac2{n\pi},0$. So $b_n=0$ exactly for multiples of $4$.

P2 solution

$b_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi\cos x\sin nx\,dx$, with $\sin nx\cos x=\frac12[\sin(n-1)x+\sin(n+1)x]$.

$n=1$: $\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi\frac12\sin2x\,dx=0$.

$n\ne1$: $\int_0^\pi\sin kx\,dx=\frac{1-(-1)^k}{k}$ and $(-1)^{n\pm1}=-(-1)^n$, so $$b_n=\frac2\pi\cdot\frac{1+(-1)^n}2\Big(\frac1{n-1}+\frac1{n+1}\Big)=\frac{2n\,(1+(-1)^n)}{\pi(n^2-1)}.$$

Odd $n$: $0$. Even $n=2m$: $b_{2m}=\frac{8m}{\pi(4m^2-1)}$. So $F(x)=\frac8\pi\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{m}{4m^2-1}\sin2mx$.

Not continuous: $f(0)=1\ne0$ and $f(\pi)=-1\ne0$, so it jumps at every multiple of $\pi$.

P3 solution

Sketch: $F(x)=|\sin x|$, arches of height $1$ touching $0$ at every multiple of $\pi$; continuous.

$a_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi\sin x\cos nx\,dx$. $n=1$: $\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi\frac12\sin2x\,dx=0$. $n\ne1$: same identity as §4, giving $$a_n=\frac2\pi\cdot\frac{1+(-1)^n}{1-n^2},\qquad a_0=\frac4\pi.$$

Odd $n$: $0$. Even $n=2m$: $a_{2m}=-\frac{4}{\pi(4m^2-1)}$. So $|\sin x|=\frac2\pi-\frac4\pi\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\cos2mx}{4m^2-1}$.

6 · Exam-level challenge (12 marks, about 24 minutes)

Let $f(x)=\sin(\pi x)$ for $0<x<1$ and $f(x)=0$ for $1\le x<2$, extended to $\mathbb R$ by $f(-x)=-f(x)$ and $f(x+4)=f(x)$.

(a) Is the extended $f$ even, odd or neither? Hence state which Fourier coefficients are zero, with a reason. [2]
(b) Sketch $f$ for $-6<x<6$. [2]
(c) Compute all non-zero Fourier coefficients. [5]
(d) Write the Fourier series of $f$ using only the non-zero terms. [1]
(e) If instead $f(-x)=f(x)$, write down (do not evaluate) the integral for the non-zero coefficients, and state which $n$ would need separate treatment. [2]

Full marking-style solution: worked/0022-challenge-full-solution.html. Open it only after attempting every part.

Answer key (quick check)

(a) Odd; $a_n=0$ for all $n\ge0$. (b) Continuous; positive arch on $(0,1)$, negative on $(-1,0)$, flat zero on $1<|x|<2$, period 4.
(c) $b_2=\frac12$; $b_n=\dfrac{4\sin(n\pi/2)}{\pi(4-n^2)}$ for $n\ne2$, so $0$ for even $n\ne2$ and $b_{2m-1}=\dfrac{4(-1)^{m+1}}{\pi(4-(2m-1)^2)}$.
(d) $f(x)=\frac12\sin\pi x+\frac4\pi\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{4-(2m-1)^2}\sin\frac{(2m-1)\pi x}{2}$.
(e) $a_n=\int_0^1\sin(\pi x)\cos\frac{n\pi x}2\,dx$; $n=2$.
