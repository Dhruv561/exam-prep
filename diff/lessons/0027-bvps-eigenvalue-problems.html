Lesson 27 · Topic 5: BVPs & PDEs · Eigenvalue problems

Three cases, every boundary condition

The one win: given $X''-\lambda X=0$ and any pair of homogeneous BCs, write the $\lambda>0$, $\lambda=0$, $\lambda<0$ analysis the way markers award it, and land on the right eigenvalues and eigenfunctions in under 5 minutes.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: Topic 5 is predicted to be the biggest question on the paper (about 18 marks; the masked 2026 paper's Q6 is 18 marks, parts (a)–(c), with a long wordy intro). Every separation-of-variables question has a "three cases for the separation constant" part worth 3–6 marks, and every later lesson (heat, Laplace, wave) reuses the eigen-table below. The 2026 formula sheet has no separation-of-variables recipe and no BVP results: the three cases and the eigen-table must be memorised.

0 · Warm-up

W1. The general solution of $X''-9X=0$ that is most convenient when $X(0)=0$ is…

Roots $\pm3$. Exponentials are correct too, but $\cosh$/$\sinh$ give $X(0)=A$ and $X'(0)=3B$ in one line ($\cosh0=1$, $\sinh0=0$). Option (b) is wrong: $X(0)=A+B=0$ forces $B=-A$, not $B=A$.

W2. For $k>0$, which statement is true?

This single fact kills every $\lambda>0$ case below. The zeros at $n\pi$ and $(n-\frac12)\pi$ belong to $\sin$ and $\cos$.

1 · Boundary value problems (Lecture 20)

A 2nd-order linear BVP: $f_2y''+f_1y'+f_0y=g(x)$ with conditions at two different points, $a_1y'(x_1)+b_1y(x_1)=c_1$, $a_2y'(x_2)+b_2y(x_2)=c_2$.

0, 1 or ∞ solutions

Unlike IVPs, a BVP need not have a unique solution. Method: general solution first, then apply both BCs and see what the constants do. Example: $y''+4y=8x$ on $(0,\pi)$. Here $y=2x+A\cos2x+B\sin2x$.

BCsalgebraverdict
$y(0)=0,\ y(\pi/4)=0$$A=0$; $\frac\pi2+B=0$one: $y=2x-\frac\pi2\sin2x$
$y(0)=0,\ y(\pi)=0$$A=0$; $2\pi+A=0$none (contradiction)
$y(0)=0,\ y(\pi)=2\pi$$A=0$; $2\pi+A=2\pi$ ✓, $B$ never appearsinfinitely many: $y=2x+B\sin2x$

Structure (Lecture 20, slide 7). Solutions = one particular solution + all solutions of the homogeneous BVP. The homogeneous BVP's solutions form a vector space, possibly just $\{0\}$. If it has a non-zero solution (here $\sin2x$ vanishes at $0$ and $\pi$), the full BVP has either none or infinitely many.

Q1. $y''+y=0$, $y'(0)=0$, $y'(\pi)=c$. How many solutions?

$y=A\cos x+B\sin x$, $y'=-A\sin x+B\cos x$. $y'(0)=B=0$. $y'(\pi)=-B=0$, so we need $c=0$, and then $A$ is free: $y=A\cos x$.

2 · Where the eigenvalue problem comes from

Lecture 22: $u_t=\alpha u_{xx}$, try $u=X(x)T(t)$:

$$XT'=\alpha X''T\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{T'}{\alpha T}=\frac{X''}{X}=\lambda\ \Rightarrow\ X''-\lambda X=0,\quad T'-\alpha\lambda T=0.$$

Write "LHS depends only on $t$, RHS only on $x$, so both equal a constant $\lambda$" (that sentence is a mark). A BC like $u(0,t)=X(0)T(t)=0$ for all $t$ gives $X(0)=0$, because $T\equiv0$ would give only $u\equiv0$. Homogeneous BCs on $u$ become BCs on $X$. Then you have a BVP that always has $X=0$; the job is to find the $\lambda$ for which it has non-zero solutions.

Sign convention
Lectures and past solutions: $X''-\lambda X=0$, so $\lambda<0$ is the oscillating case and $\lambda_n=-k_n^2$. The booklet, tutes and Paul's notes use $X''+\lambda X=0$ (or $-X''=\lambda X$), where $\lambda>0$ oscillates. If the exam gives you the ODE, use its sign. If you choose, state it. Either way: the case where $X$ is $\cos$/$\sin$ is the one that survives.

3 · The three cases, written for marks

Dirichlet–Dirichlet on $0<x<2$: $\;X''-\lambda X=0,\ X(0)=0,\ X(2)=0.$ Everything inside the red rule goes on your paper.

Case $\lambda=k^2>0$ ($k>0$): $X=A\cosh kx+B\sinh kx$.
$X(0)=A=0$. Then $X(2)=B\sinh2k=0$. Since $k>0$, $\sinh 2k\neq0$, so $B=0$. Only the trivial solution.

Case $\lambda=0$: $X=Ax+B$.
$X(0)=B=0$, $X(2)=2A=0$. Only the trivial solution.

Case $\lambda=-k^2<0$ ($k>0$): $X=A\cos kx+B\sin kx$.
$X(0)=A=0$. Then $X(2)=B\sin2k=0$. For a non-trivial solution $B\neq0$, so $\sin2k=0$, i.e. $2k=n\pi$: $$k_n=\frac{n\pi}{2},\qquad \lambda_n=-\frac{n^2\pi^2}{4},\qquad X_n(x)=\sin\frac{n\pi x}{2},\qquad n=1,2,3,\dots$$

What the marker ticks.

  1. All three cases named, with $k>0$ stated.
  2. Correct general solution in each case.
  3. Both BCs applied, with the reason a factor is non-zero ("$\sinh2k\ne0$ as $k>0$").
  4. "For non-trivial solutions $B\ne0$, so $\sin2k=0$."
  5. Eigenvalues and eigenfunctions, with the range of $n$. ($n\le0$ adds nothing new: $n=0$ gives $X=0$, negative $n$ repeat up to sign.)

The other four BC types. Same skeleton; only the BC algebra changes. Try each on paper before opening.

Neumann–Neumann: $X'(0)=0,\ X'(2)=0$

$\lambda=k^2>0$: $X'=k(A\sinh kx+B\cosh kx)$. $X'(0)=kB=0\Rightarrow B=0$. $X'(2)=kA\sinh2k=0\Rightarrow A=0$ ($k>0$). Trivial.

$\lambda=0$: $X=Ax+B$, $X'=A$. Both BCs give $A=0$; $B$ is free. Eigenvalue $\lambda_0=0$, $X_0=1$.

$\lambda=-k^2<0$: $X'=k(-A\sin kx+B\cos kx)$. $X'(0)=kB=0\Rightarrow B=0$. $X'(2)=-kA\sin2k=0$; non-trivial needs $A\ne0$, so $\sin2k=0$: $$\lambda_n=-\frac{n^2\pi^2}4,\quad X_n=\cos\frac{n\pi x}2,\quad n=1,2,\dots\quad(\text{plus }\lambda_0=0,\ X_0=1).$$

The $\lambda=0$ mode is the one students drop. It becomes the $\frac{a_0}2$ term (the steady state of an insulated rod, Lesson 28).

Dirichlet–Neumann: $X(0)=0,\ X'(2)=0$

$\lambda=k^2>0$: $X(0)=A=0$. $X'(2)=kB\cosh2k=0$; $\cosh2k\ge1$, so $B=0$. Trivial.

$\lambda=0$: $X(0)=B=0$, $X'(2)=A=0$. Trivial.

$\lambda=-k^2<0$: $X(0)=A=0$. $X'(2)=kB\cos2k=0$; non-trivial needs $\cos2k=0$, so $2k=(n-\frac12)\pi$: $$k_n=\frac{(2n-1)\pi}{4},\quad \lambda_n=-\frac{(2n-1)^2\pi^2}{16},\quad X_n=\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{4},\quad n=1,2,\dots$$

Check $n=1$: $\sin\frac{\pi x}4$ is $0$ at $x=0$ and peaks (flat) at $x=2$. A quarter-wave.

Neumann–Dirichlet: $X'(0)=0,\ X(2)=0$

$\lambda=k^2>0$: $X'(0)=kB=0\Rightarrow B=0$. $X(2)=A\cosh2k=0\Rightarrow A=0$. Trivial.

$\lambda=0$: $X'(0)=A=0$, $X(2)=B=0$. Trivial.

$\lambda=-k^2<0$: $X'(0)=kB=0\Rightarrow B=0$. $X(2)=A\cos2k=0\Rightarrow\cos2k=0$: $$\lambda_n=-\frac{(2n-1)^2\pi^2}{16},\quad X_n=\cos\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{4},\quad n=1,2,\dots$$

Periodic: $X(-\pi)=X(\pi),\ X'(-\pi)=X'(\pi)$ (a disc in $\theta$, a ring)

$\lambda=k^2>0$: $X=A\cosh kx+B\sinh kx$ ($\cosh$ even, $\sinh$ odd).
$X(-\pi)=X(\pi)$: $A\cosh k\pi-B\sinh k\pi=A\cosh k\pi+B\sinh k\pi\Rightarrow 2B\sinh k\pi=0\Rightarrow B=0$.
$X'=k(A\sinh kx+B\cosh kx)$; $X'(-\pi)=X'(\pi)$: $-kA\sinh k\pi=kA\sinh k\pi\Rightarrow A=0$. Trivial.

$\lambda=0$: $X=Ax+B$: $-A\pi+B=A\pi+B\Rightarrow A=0$; derivative BC holds. $\lambda_0=0$, $X_0=1$.

$\lambda=-k^2<0$: $X=A\cos kx+B\sin kx$. First BC: $2B\sin k\pi=0$. Second BC: $2kA\sin k\pi=0$. Non-trivial ($A$ or $B\ne0$) needs $\sin k\pi=0$, so $k=n$, and then both $A,B$ are free: $$\lambda_n=-n^2,\qquad X_n=\cos nx\ \text{ and }\ \sin nx,\qquad n=1,2,\dots\quad(\text{plus }X_0=1).$$

Two eigenfunctions per eigenvalue: that's why a disc gives a full Fourier series in $\theta$ (Lesson 30). Lecture 24 does this with $\Theta''+\mu\Theta=0$ on $(0,2\pi)$, so its signs are flipped; the result is identical.

Q2. $X''-\lambda X=0$, $X(0)=0$, $X'(3)=0$, case $\lambda=-k^2<0$. The BC algebra gives…

$A=0$ from $X(0)=0$; $X'(3)=kB\cos3k=0$. $3k=(n-\frac12)\pi$ gives $k=\frac{(2n-1)\pi}{6}$. Option (c) forgets the $\frac12$.

Q3. For which BC pair is $\lambda=0$ an eigenvalue of $X''-\lambda X=0$?

$X=Ax+B$. Any Dirichlet condition pins $X$ to $0$ somewhere; with the other BC that forces $X\equiv0$. Only "no Dirichlet anywhere" (Neumann–Neumann, or periodic) leaves the constant $X_0=1$.

Q4. In the $\lambda=k^2>0$ case with $X(0)=0$, $X(2)=0$ you reach $B\sinh2k=0$. The line the marker wants next is…

$\sinh$ has only one real zero, at $0$, and $k>0$. Option (b) is the $\sin$ reasoning from the $\lambda<0$ case pasted into the wrong case.

4 · The eigen-table (memorise)

$X''-\lambda X=0$ on $0<x<L$ (periodic: $-L<x<L$). In all five, $\lambda>0$ gives only the trivial solution.

Two hooks. (1) What happens at $x=0$ picks the function: Dirichlet at $0$ → $\sin$, Neumann at $0$ → $\cos$. (2) Same type at both ends → whole half-waves $\frac{n\pi}L$; mixed → odd quarter-waves $\frac{(2n-1)\pi}{2L}$.

Coefficients later (orthogonality on $(0,L)$): sine or cosine rows use $\frac2L\int_0^L f\,X_n\,dx$ (Neumann: $\frac{a_0}2$ with $a_0=\frac2L\int_0^Lf$). Mixed rows use the same $\frac2L\int_0^L f X_n\,dx$ (Booklet Q1). Periodic: full Fourier, $\frac1L\int_{-L}^L$. When $f$ is a finite sum of the $X_n$, just match coefficients.

Q5. A rod $0<x<\frac\pi2$ is insulated at $x=0$ and kept at $0^\circ$ at $x=\frac\pi2$. The spatial eigenfunctions are…

Neumann at $0$ → $\cos$. Mixed → $k_n=\frac{(2n-1)\pi}{2L}=\frac{(2n-1)\pi}{\pi}=2n-1$. Check: $\cos\big((2n-1)\frac\pi2\big)=0$ ✓.

Q6. After separating $u_{tt}=4u_{xx}$ as $X''-\lambda X=0$, $T''-4\lambda T=0$, which case gives oscillation in time?

$\lambda=-k^2$: $T=a\cos2kt+b\sin2kt$. With the other sign convention ($X''+\lambda X=0$) the answer flips to "$\lambda>0$". Always read the ODE you were given.

5 · PDE classification drill (Lecture 21)

Order: highest derivative. Linear: $u$ and its derivatives appear only to the first power, not multiplied together, with coefficients depending only on the independent variables. Homogeneous: every term contains $u$; a term with only $x,y,t$ makes it inhomogeneous.

Type for $Au_{xx}+Bu_{xy}+Cu_{yy}+\text{lower}=0$ (here $B$ is the whole $u_{xy}$ coefficient): $B^2-4AC<0$ elliptic, $=0$ parabolic, $>0$ hyperbolic. Heat is parabolic, wave hyperbolic, Laplace elliptic. Treat $t$ as one of the variables.

Q7. $u_t=u_{xx}+u^2$ is…

$u^2$ makes it nonlinear, but it still contains $u$, so every term has $u$: homogeneous (compare Lecture 21's $\nabla^2u=-e^{\lambda u}$).

Q8. $x\,u_{xx}+u_y=\sin(xy)$ is…

A coefficient $x$ is fine (independent variable). $\sin(xy)$ has no $u$: inhomogeneous.

Q9. $u_{xx}+4u_{xy}+3u_{yy}-u_x=0$ is…

$A=1$, $B=4$, $C=3$: $16-12=4>0$. Option (a) uses $B=2$ (halving the $u_{xy}$ coefficient), which belongs to the $B^2-AC$ convention, not this course's.

Q10. $u_{tt}=9u_{xx}$ (variables $x,t$) is…

$9u_{xx}-u_{tt}=0$: $A=9$, $B=0$, $C=-1$, so $0-4(9)(-1)=36$. The minus sign between the second derivatives is what makes it hyperbolic.

Q11. $u_{xx}+x\,u_{yy}=0$ is…

$B^2-4AC=-4x$. Negative when $x>0$, positive when $x<0$, zero (parabolic) on $x=0$.

6 · Practice (exam level)

P1 [5] $X''-\lambda X=0$, $X'(0)=0$, $X(\frac\pi2)=0$. Consider all three cases and find all eigenvalues and eigenfunctions.

P2 [4] $y''+9y=9$ on $(0,\frac\pi3)$. (a) With $y(0)=0$, $y(\frac\pi3)=\beta$: for which $\beta$ is there a solution, and how many? (b) With $y(0)=0$, $y(\frac\pi6)=\beta$ instead?

P3 [7] A string $0<x<\pi$ is fixed at $x=0$; the end $x=\pi$ slides freely on a vertical rod. Its displacement satisfies $u_{tt}=4u_{xx}$. (a) Write the BCs. (b) Separate variables with constant $\lambda$ and give the BCs on $X$. (c) Do the three cases. (d) Solve for $T_n$ and say which case gives oscillation.

P4 [3] Show $X_n=e^{-x}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{2}$ satisfies $X(0)=X(2)=0$ and $-X''-2X'=\mu X$, and find $\mu_n$.

P1 solution

$\lambda=k^2>0$: $X'(0)=kB=0\Rightarrow B=0$; $X(\frac\pi2)=A\cosh\frac{k\pi}2=0\Rightarrow A=0$ ($\cosh\ge1$). Trivial.

$\lambda=0$: $X'=A=0$; $X(\frac\pi2)=B=0$. Trivial.

$\lambda=-k^2<0$: $X'(0)=kB=0\Rightarrow B=0$; $X(\frac\pi2)=A\cos\frac{k\pi}2=0$. Non-trivial: $\frac{k\pi}2=(n-\frac12)\pi\Rightarrow k=2n-1$. $$\lambda_n=-(2n-1)^2,\qquad X_n=\cos\big((2n-1)x\big),\qquad n=1,2,\dots$$

P2 solution

$y_p=1$, so $y=1+A\cos3x+B\sin3x$. $y(0)=1+A=0\Rightarrow A=-1$.

(a) $y(\frac\pi3)=1+A\cos\pi+B\sin\pi=1-A=2$. So $\beta=2$: infinitely many, $y=1-\cos3x+B\sin3x$. $\beta\ne2$: none.

(b) $y(\frac\pi6)=1+A\cdot0+B=\beta\Rightarrow B=\beta-1$: exactly one for every $\beta$, $y=1-\cos3x+(\beta-1)\sin3x$.

Why: $\sin3x$ vanishes at $0$ and $\frac\pi3$ (a non-zero homogeneous solution) but not at $\frac\pi6$.

P3 solution

(a) $u(0,t)=0$, $u_x(\pi,t)=0$.

(b) $u=XT$: $XT''=4X''T\Rightarrow\frac{T''}{4T}=\frac{X''}{X}=\lambda$ (LHS only $t$, RHS only $x$). So $X''-\lambda X=0$, $T''-4\lambda T=0$, with $X(0)=0$, $X'(\pi)=0$ (else $T\equiv0$, $u\equiv0$).

(c) $\lambda=k^2>0$: $X(0)=A=0$; $X'(\pi)=kB\cosh k\pi=0\Rightarrow B=0$. Trivial.
$\lambda=0$: $X(0)=B=0$, $X'(\pi)=A=0$. Trivial.
$\lambda=-k^2<0$: $A=0$; $X'(\pi)=kB\cos k\pi=0\Rightarrow k\pi=(n-\frac12)\pi$: $$k_n=n-\tfrac12,\quad \lambda_n=-(n-\tfrac12)^2,\quad X_n=\sin\big((n-\tfrac12)x\big),\quad n=1,2,\dots$$

(d) $T_n''+4k_n^2T_n=0\Rightarrow T_n=a_n\cos\big((2n-1)t\big)+b_n\sin\big((2n-1)t\big)$. The $\lambda<0$ case gives oscillation (and it's the only case with non-trivial $X$).

P4 solution

BCs: $e^{0}\sin0=0$, $e^{-2}\sin n\pi=0$ ✓. With $s=\sin kx$, $k=\frac{n\pi}2$: $X'=e^{-x}(s'-s)$, $X''=e^{-x}(s''-2s'+s)$, so $$X''+2X'=e^{-x}(s''-s)=e^{-x}(-k^2-1)s\ \Rightarrow\ -X''-2X'=(1+k^2)X,\qquad \mu_n=1+\frac{n^2\pi^2}{4}.$$

7 · Exam-level challenge (15 marks, about 30 minutes)

A thin wire is bent into a closed loop of length $1$. Its temperature $u(x,t)$, with $x$ the arc length from a marked point, satisfies $u_t=2u_{xx}$ for $0<x<1$, $t>0$.

(a) Classify the PDE: order, linear or not, homogeneous or not, and elliptic/parabolic/hyperbolic with reason. [2]
(b) Explain why $u(0,t)=u(1,t)$ and $u_x(0,t)=u_x(1,t)$. Separate variables with constant $\lambda$; give both ODEs and the conditions on $X$. [3]
(c) Consider the three cases for $\lambda$ and find all eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. [6]
(d) Write the general series solution and the limit of $u$ as $t\to\infty$ in terms of $u(x,0)=f(x)$. [2]
(e) Solve the problem when $u(x,0)=3+\sin^2(\pi x)$. [2]

Full marking-style solution: worked/0027-challenge-full-solution.html. Open it only after attempting every part.

Answer key (quick check)

(a) 2nd order, linear, homogeneous, parabolic ($B^2-4AC=0$). (b) $X''-\lambda X=0$, $T'-2\lambda T=0$, $X(0)=X(1)$, $X'(0)=X'(1)$.
(c) $\lambda>0$: determinant $2-2\cosh k\ne0$, trivial. $\lambda=0$: $X_0=1$. $\lambda<0$: $2-2\cos k=0$, $k=2n\pi$, $X_n=\cos2n\pi x,\ \sin2n\pi x$, $\lambda_n=-4n^2\pi^2$.
(d) $u=\frac{a_0}2+\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-8n^2\pi^2t}(a_n\cos2n\pi x+b_n\sin2n\pi x)\to\frac{a_0}{2}=\int_0^1f\,dx$.
(e) $u=\frac72-\frac12e^{-8\pi^2t}\cos2\pi x$.
