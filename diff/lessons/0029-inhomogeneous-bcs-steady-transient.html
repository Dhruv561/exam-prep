Lesson 29 · Topic 5: BVPs & PDEs · Steady state + transient

Inhomogeneous BCs: split off the steady state, then it's Lesson 28 again

The one win: given a heat problem with non-zero boundary values or a source term, write $u=u_S+u_T$, find $u_S$ from an ODE, hand $u_T$ homogeneous BCs and the IC $f-u_S$, solve by separation of variables, then answer the limit, point-value and sketch parts.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: this is the single most examined Topic 5 pattern. 2021 Q6 (22 marks: linear source, mixed BCs, polynomial IC) and 2024 Q5 (30 marks: mixed inhomogeneous BCs, IC that is "steady state + one eigenfunction"). The masked 2026 paper's Q6 is 18 marks in parts (a)–(c) with a very long intro: expect a worded heat problem. The formula sheet has no separation-of-variables recipe and no eigenfunction table: everything below is memorised.

W1. $X''-\lambda X=0$, $X(0)=0$, $X'(L)=0$. The eigenfunctions are…

$X(0)=0$ kills $\cos$, leaving $B\sin kx$. $X'(L)=kB\cos kL=0$ needs $kL=(n-\frac12)\pi$. Dirichlet at the left end → sine; Neumann at the right end → half-integer.

Eigen-table (from Lesson 27; memorise). $X''-\lambda X=0$ with $\lambda=-k^2$; $T_n=e^{-\alpha k_n^2 t}$.

1 · The recipe (Lecture 23, Problem 3)

Separation of variables needs homogeneous BCs: $X(0)T(t)=5$ for all $t$ is impossible. So peel off a time-independent piece that carries the non-zero data.

General problem: $u_t=\alpha u_{xx}+q(x)$ on $0<x<L$, non-zero BCs, $u(x,0)=f(x)$. Write $u(x,t)=u_S(x)+u_T(x,t)$.

  1. Find $u_S$. It doesn't depend on $t$, so $u_t=0$. Then solve an ODE and apply the original BCs: $$\begin{aligned} &0=\alpha u_S''+q(x) &&\text{set } u_t=0\\ &u_S''=-\frac{q(x)}{\alpha} &&\text{rearrange}\\ &u_S'=-\frac1\alpha\int q\,dx+A &&\text{integrate once}\\ &u_S=-\frac1\alpha\iint q\,dx\,dx+Ax+B &&\text{integrate again}\\ &\text{BC at }x=0\;\Rightarrow\;\text{equation 1 in }A,B &&u_S(0)=\text{value or }u_S'(0)=\text{slope}\\ &\text{BC at }x=L\;\Rightarrow\;\text{equation 2 in }A,B &&\text{same at }x=L\\ &\text{solve for }A,B\;\Rightarrow\;u_S(x) && \end{aligned}$$ No source ($q=0$): $u_S''=0$, so $u_S=Ax+B$.
  2. PDE for $u_T$. Subtract the $u_S$ equation from the PDE: $$\begin{aligned} &u_T=u-u_S &&\text{definition}\\ &(u_T)_t=u_t &&u_S\text{ has no }t\\ &\phantom{(u_T)_t}=\alpha u_{xx}+q &&\text{original PDE}\\ &\phantom{(u_T)_t}=\alpha (u_T)_{xx}+\big(\alpha u_S''+q\big) &&u_{xx}=(u_T)_{xx}+u_S''\\ &\phantom{(u_T)_t}=\alpha (u_T)_{xx} &&\alpha u_S''+q=0\text{ from step 1} \end{aligned}$$ The source drops out only if it depends on $x$ alone.
  3. BCs for $u_T$. At each end, subtract the $u_S$ value from the $u$ value. The BC type stays the same: $$\begin{aligned} &u_T(0,t)=u(0,t)-u_S(0)=T_0-T_0=0 &&\text{Dirichlet end}\\ &\partial_xu_T(L,t)=u_x(L,t)-u_S'(L)=c-c=0 &&\text{Neumann end} \end{aligned}$$
  4. IC for $u_T$. $$\begin{aligned} &u_T(x,0)=u(x,0)-u_S(x) &&\text{set }t=0\\ &\phantom{u_T(x,0)}=f(x)-u_S(x)=:g(x) &&\text{simplify} \end{aligned}$$
  5. Solve for $u_T$ by SoV (Lesson 28): $$\begin{aligned} &X_n,\;k_n &&\text{eigen-table, from }u_T\text{'s BCs}\\ &T_n=e^{-\alpha k_n^2t} &&\text{time ODE}\\ &u_T=\sum_n b_n\,e^{-\alpha k_n^2t}X_n(x) &&\text{superpose}\\ &\sum_n b_nX_n(x)=g(x) &&\text{set }t=0\\ &b_n &&\text{match terms, or half-range integral} \end{aligned}$$
  6. Answer. $$\begin{aligned} &u(x,t)=u_S(x)+u_T(x,t) &&\text{add}\\ &\lim_{t\to\infty}u=u_S(x) &&\text{every }e^{-\alpha k_n^2t}\to0 \end{aligned}$$

Example A (inhomogeneous Dirichlet, IC by matching). $u_t=2u_{xx}$, $0<x<\pi$; $u(0,t)=1$, $u(\pi,t)=3$; $u(x,0)=1+\frac{2x}{\pi}+4\sin2x-\sin5x$.

Step 1: $u_S$
$$\begin{aligned} &2u_S''=0\;\Rightarrow\;u_S''=0 &&\text{set }u_t=0\\ &u_S'=A &&\text{integrate}\\ &u_S=Ax+B &&\text{integrate}\\ &u_S(0)=B=1 &&\text{BC at }x=0\\ &u_S(\pi)=A\pi+1=3\;\Rightarrow\;A=\tfrac2\pi &&\text{BC at }x=\pi\\ &u_S=1+\frac{2x}{\pi} &&\text{substitute} \end{aligned}$$
Steps 2–4: PDE, BCs, IC for $u_T$
$$\begin{aligned} &(u_T)_t=2(u_T)_{xx} &&\text{no source, so same PDE}\\ &u_T(0,t)=1-u_S(0)=1-1=0 &&\text{left BC}\\ &u_T(\pi,t)=3-u_S(\pi)=3-3=0 &&\text{right BC}\\ &u_T(x,0)=\Big(1+\tfrac{2x}{\pi}+4\sin2x-\sin5x\Big)-\Big(1+\tfrac{2x}{\pi}\Big) &&f-u_S\\ &\phantom{u_T(x,0)}=4\sin2x-\sin5x &&\text{simplify} \end{aligned}$$
Steps 5–6: solve for $u_T$, then $u$
$$\begin{aligned} &X_n=\sin nx,\;k_n=n &&\text{Dirichlet–Dirichlet},\;L=\pi\\ &T_n=e^{-2n^2t} &&\alpha=2\\ &u_T=\sum_{n\ge1}b_ne^{-2n^2t}\sin nx &&\text{superpose}\\ &\sum_{n\ge1}b_n\sin nx=4\sin2x-\sin5x &&t=0\\ &b_2=4,\;b_5=-1,\;\text{all other }b_n=0 &&\text{match terms}\\ &u_T=4e^{-8t}\sin2x-e^{-50t}\sin5x &&\text{substitute}\\ &u=1+\frac{2x}{\pi}+4e^{-8t}\sin2x-e^{-50t}\sin5x &&u_S+u_T\\ &u(\tfrac\pi2,t)=2+4e^{-8t}\sin\pi-e^{-50t}\sin\tfrac{5\pi}2=2-e^{-50t} &&\text{midpoint}\\ &u(\tfrac\pi2,t)\to2 &&t\to\infty \end{aligned}$$

Sketch at $t=0,t_1,t_2,\infty$ (2–3 marks).

  1. $t=0$: draw $f$. Label the end values.
  2. $t\to\infty$: draw $u_S$ (a line, or a parabola/cubic if there is a source). Label its ends.
  3. $t_1$, $t_2$: in between, closer to $u_S$ as $t$ grows. High-$n$ wiggles die first ($e^{-50t}$ vs $e^{-8t}$), so $t_1$ looks like "$u_S$ + slowest mode".
  4. For $t>0$ every curve obeys the BCs: Dirichlet ends pinned at the boundary value; an insulated end meets the wall flat ($u_x=0$); a Neumann end with $u_x=c$ has that slope.

Q1. $u(0,t)=5$, $u(2,t)=1$. The transient BCs are…

$u_S(0)=5$ and $u_S(2)=1$ absorb the data: $u_T(0,t)=5-5=0$, $u_T(2,t)=1-1=0$. The BC type never changes, only its right-hand side becomes $0$.

Q2. $u_t=u_{xx}$, $u(0,t)=0$, $u_x(1,t)=2$. Then $u_S$ and the transient BCs are…

$u_S=Ax+B$, $B=0$, $u_S'=A=2$. Then $\partial_xu_T(1,t)=2-u_S'(1)=0$. Eigenfunctions: $\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2}$.

Q3. Same problem as Q2 with $u(x,0)=2x+3\sin\frac{3\pi x}{2}$. The transient IC is…

$g=f-u_S$. It is the eigenfunction $n=2$, so $u_T=3e^{-9\pi^2t/4}\sin\frac{3\pi x}{2}$ with no integral at all. This is the 2024 shape.

2 · Words → BCs and ICs

  • "rod insulated except at its ends" / "laterally insulated" → heat flows only along $x$: the 1D heat equation. Says nothing about the ends.
  • "end $x=a$ is insulated" → $u_x(a,t)=0$.
  • "end held at temperature $T_0$" / "kept in a bath at $T_0$" / "in ice" → $u(a,t)=T_0$ (ice: $0$).
  • "constant temperature gradient $c$ at $x=a$" → $u_x(a,t)=c$.
  • "initially at temperature $f(x)$" / "initially at uniform temperature $T_1$" → $u(x,0)=f(x)$ / $T_1$.
  • "heat generated uniformly inside" → constant source, $u_t=\alpha u_{xx}+q$.

Q4. "A rod $0\le x\le 3$, insulated except at its ends. The left end is kept in ice, the right end is insulated." The BCs are…

"Insulated except at its ends" is about the sides (1D model). Ice → $u=0$ at $x=0$. Insulated right end → $u_x(3,t)=0$.

3 · Source term: polynomial steady state

$u_t=\alpha u_{xx}+q(x)$ with polynomial $q$: $u_S''=-q/\alpha$, integrate twice. A constant source gives a parabola, a linear source a cubic. (The 2021 paper phrased it as "trial $\sum_{n=0}^3 a_nx^n$": same thing.)

Example B. $u_t=u_{xx}+2$, $u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0$.

$$\begin{aligned} &0=u_S''+2 &&\text{set }u_t=0\\ &u_S''=-2 &&\text{rearrange}\\ &u_S'=-2x+A &&\text{integrate}\\ &u_S=-x^2+Ax+B &&\text{integrate}\\ &u_S(0)=B=0 &&\text{BC at }x=0\\ &u_S(1)=-1+A=0\;\Rightarrow\;A=1 &&\text{BC at }x=1\\ &u_S=x-x^2=x(1-x) &&\text{substitute} \end{aligned}$$

The generated heat leaves through both cold ends, so the rod is hottest in the middle.

Divide by $\alpha$
$u_t=3u_{xx}+6$ gives $3u_S''=-6$, i.e. $u_S''=-2$, not $-6$. And the sign: $u_S''=-q/\alpha$ (a positive source makes $u_S$ concave down).

Q5. $u_t=3u_{xx}+6$, $u(0,t)=0$, $u_x(1,t)=0$. Then $u_S=$…

$u_S''=-2\Rightarrow u_S=-x^2+Ax+B$. $u_S(0)=0\Rightarrow B=0$; $u_S'(1)=-2+A=0\Rightarrow A=2$. Option (d) is the Dirichlet–Dirichlet answer.

4 · Neumann–Neumann: the constant is free, the mean fixes it

Tute 10 Q1: $u_t=\alpha u_{xx}$, $u_x(0,t)=u_x(L,t)=b$. Then $u_S''=0$, $u_S'=b$, so $u_S=bx+d$ with $d$ arbitrary: the BCs only see slopes.

It still works: the transient has Neumann–Neumann BCs, so its series includes the $n=0$ mode $\frac{a_0}{2}$, and $$\tfrac{a_0}2=\tfrac1L\int_0^L (f-bx-d)\,dx\quad\Rightarrow\quad d+\tfrac{a_0}{2}=\tfrac1L\int_0^L(f-bx)\,dx.$$ $d$ cancels. As $t\to\infty$, $u\to bx+C$ with $C$ chosen so that $bx+C$ has the same mean as $f$ (heat in at one end = heat out at the other, so the average temperature never changes).

Example C. $u_t=u_{xx}$, $0<x<\pi$, $u_x(0,t)=u_x(\pi,t)=1$, $u(x,0)=1+x+4\cos2x$.

Solution

$u_S$:

$$\begin{aligned} &u_S''=0 &&\text{set }u_t=0\\ &u_S=Ax+d &&\text{integrate twice}\\ &u_S'(0)=A=1 &&\text{BC at }x=0\\ &u_S'(\pi)=A=1\;\checkmark &&\text{BC at }x=\pi\text{: no new info}\\ &u_S=x+d,\;d\text{ arbitrary} &&\text{substitute} \end{aligned}$$

$u_T$:

$$\begin{aligned} &\partial_xu_T(0,t)=1-1=0,\quad\partial_xu_T(\pi,t)=1-1=0 &&\text{BCs}\\ &u_T(x,0)=(1+x+4\cos2x)-(x+d)=(1-d)+4\cos2x &&f-u_S\\ &u_T=\tfrac{a_0}2+\sum_{n\ge1}a_ne^{-n^2t}\cos nx &&\text{Neumann–Neumann},\;L=\pi\\ &\tfrac{a_0}2=1-d,\;a_2=4,\;\text{other }a_n=0 &&\text{match at }t=0\\ &u_T=(1-d)+4e^{-4t}\cos2x &&\text{substitute} \end{aligned}$$

$u$:

$$\begin{aligned} &u=(x+d)+(1-d)+4e^{-4t}\cos2x &&u_S+u_T\\ &\phantom{u}=1+x+4e^{-4t}\cos2x &&d\text{ cancels}\\ &u\to1+x &&t\to\infty \end{aligned}$$
Unequal fluxes: no steady state
$u_x(0,t)=1$, $u_x(\pi,t)=2$ with no source: $u_S'=A$ can't equal both $1$ and $2$. No steady state exists (net heat flows in, the rod keeps warming). In the exam: say "$u_S''=0$ can't satisfy both BCs, so there is no steady state". Solving such problems is beyond the lectures. With a source, the test is $\alpha\big(u_S'(L)-u_S'(0)\big)=-\int_0^L q\,dx$; just try to solve for $u_S$ and see if the BCs clash.

Q6. $u_t=u_{xx}$ on $(0,2)$, $u_x(0,t)=u_x(2,t)=-1$, $u(x,0)=3-x$. As $t\to\infty$, $u\to$…

$f=3-x$ is of the form $-x+d$ and satisfies the BCs, so $g=f-u_S$ can be taken as $0$: it is already steady. Check by mean: mean of $3-x$ on $(0,2)$ is $2$; mean of $-x+C$ is $C-1$, so $C=3$.

Q7. $u_t=u_{xx}$ on $(0,1)$ with $u_x(0,t)=0$, $u_x(1,t)=2$. Which is true?

$u_S''=0$ forces a constant slope; $0\ne2$. ($x^2$ does meet both slopes, but $x^2$ fails $u_S''=0$: it is not time-independent under $u_t=u_{xx}$.)

5 · Practice (exam level)

Full exam-style write-ups. Give each part a go on paper before opening. Do P1 and P2 in one sitting, P3 and P4 in another.

P1 (inhomogeneous Dirichlet, one integration by parts). [12 marks] A rod $0\le x\le1$, insulated except at its ends, is at uniform temperature $2$. From $t=0$ the end $x=0$ is kept at $2$ and the end $x=1$ is put in ice. Take $u_t=u_{xx}$.

(a) Write the BCs and IC. [1]
(b) Find the steady state $u_S$. [2]
(c) State the IBVP for $u_T=u-u_S$. [2]
(d) Solve for $u_T$ (you may quote the Dirichlet eigenfunctions) and write $u(x,t)$. [4]
(e) Find $u(\frac12,t)$ as a series. What is its limit, and the leading correction for large $t$? [1]
(f) Sketch $u$ at $t=0$, two later times and $t\to\infty$. [2]

P1 solution

(a) $u(0,t)=2$, $u(1,t)=0$, $u(x,0)=2$.

(b)

$$\begin{aligned} &u_S''=0 &&\text{set }u_t=0\\ &u_S=Ax+B &&\text{integrate twice}\\ &u_S(0)=B=2 &&\text{BC at }x=0\\ &u_S(1)=A+2=0\;\Rightarrow\;A=-2 &&\text{BC at }x=1\\ &u_S=2-2x &&\text{substitute} \end{aligned}$$

(c)

$$\begin{aligned} &(u_T)_t=(u_T)_{xx} &&\text{PDE}\\ &u_T(0,t)=2-u_S(0)=2-2=0 &&\text{left BC}\\ &u_T(1,t)=0-u_S(1)=0-0=0 &&\text{right BC}\\ &u_T(x,0)=2-(2-2x)=2x &&f-u_S \end{aligned}$$

(d)

$$\begin{aligned} &u_T=\sum_{n\ge1}b_ne^{-n^2\pi^2t}\sin n\pi x &&\text{Dirichlet–Dirichlet},\;L=1\\ &b_n=2\int_0^1 2x\sin n\pi x\,dx &&\text{half-range sine}\\ &\phantom{b_n}=4\Big[-\frac{x\cos n\pi x}{n\pi}\Big]_0^1+\frac{4}{n\pi}\int_0^1\cos n\pi x\,dx &&\text{IBP}\\ &\phantom{b_n}=-\frac{4(-1)^n}{n\pi}+\frac{4}{n\pi}\Big[\frac{\sin n\pi x}{n\pi}\Big]_0^1 &&\cos n\pi=(-1)^n\\ &\phantom{b_n}=\frac{4(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}+0 &&\sin n\pi=0\\ &u(x,t)=2-2x+\frac4\pi\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}e^{-n^2\pi^2t}\sin n\pi x &&u_S+u_T \end{aligned}$$

(e) $\sin\frac{n\pi}2=0$ for even $n$; for $n=2m-1$ it is $(-1)^{m+1}$ and $(-1)^{n+1}=1$: $$u(\tfrac12,t)=1+\frac4\pi\sum_{m=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{2m-1}e^{-(2m-1)^2\pi^2t}\to1,\qquad u(\tfrac12,t)\approx1+\tfrac4\pi e^{-\pi^2t}.$$ (Check $t=0$: $1+\frac4\pi\cdot\frac\pi4=2$ ✓.)

(f) See plot: $t=0$ flat at $2$ (jumps to $0$ at $x=1$); for $t>0$ the right end is pinned at $0$ and a cold dip spreads leftwards; curves approach the line $2-2x$.

P2 (mixed Neumann/Dirichlet, IC by matching). [16 marks] $u_t=3u_{xx}$ for $0<x<\pi$, $t>0$, with $$u_x(0,t)=-1,\qquad u(\pi,t)=0,\qquad u(x,0)=\pi-x+3\cos\tfrac x2-\cos\tfrac{5x}2.$$ (a) Find $u_S$. [2]
(b) Write the BCs and IC for $u_T$. [2]
(c) Separate $u_T=X(x)T(t)$ and derive the ODEs. Considering all three signs of the separation constant, find the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. [6]
(d) Find $u(x,t)$. [3]
(e) Find the temperature at $x=0$ for all $t$, and its limit. [1]
(f) Sketch $u$ at $t=0$, $t_1$, $t_2$, $\infty$. [2]

P2 solution

(a)

$$\begin{aligned} &u_S''=0 &&\text{set }u_t=0\\ &u_S=Ax+B &&\text{integrate twice}\\ &u_S'(0)=A=-1 &&\text{BC at }x=0\\ &u_S(\pi)=-\pi+B=0\;\Rightarrow\;B=\pi &&\text{BC at }x=\pi\\ &u_S=\pi-x &&\text{substitute} \end{aligned}$$

(b)

$$\begin{aligned} &\partial_xu_T(0,t)=u_x(0,t)-u_S'(0)=-1-(-1)=0 &&\text{left BC}\\ &u_T(\pi,t)=u(\pi,t)-u_S(\pi)=0-0=0 &&\text{right BC}\\ &u_T(x,0)=\Big(\pi-x+3\cos\tfrac x2-\cos\tfrac{5x}2\Big)-(\pi-x) &&f-u_S\\ &\phantom{u_T(x,0)}=3\cos\tfrac x2-\cos\tfrac{5x}2 &&\text{simplify} \end{aligned}$$

(c) $X\dot T=3X''T\Rightarrow\frac{\dot T}{3T}=\frac{X''}{X}=\lambda$: $\dot T-3\lambda T=0$, $X''-\lambda X=0$, with $X'(0)=0$, $X(\pi)=0$ (else $T\equiv0$).

  • $\lambda=k^2>0$: $X=A\cosh kx+B\sinh kx$. $X'(0)=kB=0\Rightarrow B=0$; $X(\pi)=A\cosh k\pi=0\Rightarrow A=0$. Trivial.
  • $\lambda=0$: $X=Ax+B$. $X'(0)=A=0$, $X(\pi)=B=0$. Trivial.
  • $\lambda=-k^2<0$: $X=A\cos kx+B\sin kx$. $X'(0)=kB=0\Rightarrow B=0$; $X(\pi)=A\cos k\pi=0\Rightarrow k\pi=(n-\frac12)\pi$.

$k_n=\frac{2n-1}{2}$, $\lambda_n=-k_n^2$, $X_n=\cos\frac{(2n-1)x}{2}$, $n=1,2,\dots$

(d) $T_n=e^{-3k_n^2t}$, $u_T=\sum c_ne^{-3k_n^2t}\cos k_nx$. At $t=0$ match: $c_1=3$ ($k_1=\frac12$), $c_3=-1$ ($k_3=\frac52$), rest $0$. $$u=\pi-x+3e^{-3t/4}\cos\tfrac x2-e^{-75t/4}\cos\tfrac{5x}2.$$

(e) $u(0,t)=\pi+3e^{-3t/4}-e^{-75t/4}\to\pi$. (At $t=0$: $\pi+2$ ✓ $=f(0)$.)

(f) $t=0$: $f$, from $\pi+2$ at $x=0$ down to $0$ at $\pi$, slope $-1$ at $x=0$. $t_1$: the $\cos\frac{5x}2$ wiggle already gone, $\approx\pi-x+3e^{-3t_1/4}\cos\frac x2$. $t_2$: closer to the line. $\infty$: $\pi-x$. Every curve has slope $-1$ at $x=0$ and value $0$ at $x=\pi$.

P3 (linear source, cubic steady state). [14 marks] $u_t=u_{xx}+6x$, $0<x<1$, with $u(0,t)=1$, $u(1,t)=0$, $u(x,0)=1+x-x^3$.

(a) Trial $u_S=a_0+a_1x+a_2x^2+a_3x^3$. Find the $a_i$ from the ODE and BCs. [3]
(b) Show $u_T=u-u_S$ satisfies the homogeneous heat equation with homogeneous BCs and $u_T(x,0)=x$. [3]
(c) Solve for $u_T$ (quote eigenfunctions) and write $u$. [5]
(d) Does the problem have a steady state? If so, give it and the limiting temperature at $x=\frac12$. [2]
(e) Where is the steady temperature highest? [1]

P3 solution

(a)

$$\begin{aligned} &0=u_S''+6x\;\Rightarrow\;u_S''=-6x &&\text{set }u_t=0\\ &u_S''=2a_2+6a_3x &&\text{differentiate trial twice}\\ &2a_2+6a_3x=-6x &&\text{equate}\\ &2a_2=0\;\Rightarrow\;a_2=0 &&\text{constant terms}\\ &6a_3=-6\;\Rightarrow\;a_3=-1 &&x\text{ terms}\\ &u_S(0)=a_0=1 &&\text{BC at }x=0\\ &u_S(1)=1+a_1+0-1=0\;\Rightarrow\;a_1=0 &&\text{BC at }x=1\\ &u_S=1-x^3 &&\text{substitute} \end{aligned}$$

(b)

$$\begin{aligned} &(u_T)_t=u_t &&u_S\text{ has no }t\\ &\phantom{(u_T)_t}=u_{xx}+6x &&\text{PDE}\\ &\phantom{(u_T)_t}=(u_T)_{xx}+u_S''+6x &&u=u_S+u_T\\ &\phantom{(u_T)_t}=(u_T)_{xx}-6x+6x=(u_T)_{xx} &&u_S''=-6x\\ &u_T(0,t)=1-u_S(0)=1-1=0 &&\text{left BC}\\ &u_T(1,t)=0-u_S(1)=0-0=0 &&\text{right BC}\\ &u_T(x,0)=(1+x-x^3)-(1-x^3)=x &&f-u_S \end{aligned}$$

(c) $u_T=\sum b_ne^{-n^2\pi^2t}\sin n\pi x$, $b_n=2\int_0^1x\sin n\pi x\,dx=\frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}$ (one IBP, as in P1). $$u=1-x^3+\frac2\pi\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}e^{-n^2\pi^2t}\sin n\pi x.$$

(d) Yes: every term has $e^{-n^2\pi^2t}\to0$, so $u\to u_S=1-x^3$. $u(\frac12,t)\to\frac78$.

(e) $u_S'=-3x^2\le0$: decreasing, highest at $x=0$ (value $1$).

P4 (inhomogeneous Neumann–Neumann). [10 marks] $u_t=2u_{xx}$, $0<x<2$, with $u_x(0,t)=u_x(2,t)=3$ and $u(x,0)=3x-1+6\cos^2\frac{\pi x}{2}$.

(a) Show $u_S=3x+d$ satisfies the BCs for every $d$. [1]
(b) Write the IBVP for $u_T$ and its general series solution. [3]
(c) Find $u(x,t)$ and show $d$ drops out. [3]
(d) Find $\lim_{t\to\infty}u(1,t)$ and check the answer by the mean of the IC. [2]
(e) If instead $u_x(2,t)=4$, does a steady state exist? [1]

P4 solution

(a)

$$\begin{aligned} &u_S''=0\;\checkmark &&\text{steady ODE}\\ &u_S'(0)=3,\;u_S'(2)=3\;\checkmark &&\text{both BCs, any }d \end{aligned}$$

(b)

$$\begin{aligned} &(u_T)_t=2(u_T)_{xx} &&\text{PDE}\\ &\partial_xu_T(0,t)=3-3=0,\quad\partial_xu_T(2,t)=3-3=0 &&\text{BCs}\\ &u_T(x,0)=f(x)-3x-d &&f-u_S\\ &k_n=\tfrac{n\pi}2,\;X_n=\cos\tfrac{n\pi x}2,\;n\ge0 &&\text{Neumann–Neumann},\;L=2\\ &T_n=e^{-2(n\pi/2)^2t}=e^{-n^2\pi^2t/2} &&\alpha=2\\ &u_T=\tfrac{a_0}2+\sum_{n\ge1}a_ne^{-n^2\pi^2t/2}\cos\tfrac{n\pi x}2 &&\text{superpose} \end{aligned}$$

(c)

$$\begin{aligned} &6\cos^2\tfrac{\pi x}2=3+3\cos\pi x &&\text{double angle}\\ &f=3x+2+3\cos\pi x &&\text{rewrite IC}\\ &u_T(x,0)=(3x+2+3\cos\pi x)-(3x+d)=(2-d)+3\cos\pi x &&f-u_S\\ &\cos\pi x=\cos\tfrac{2\pi x}2\;\Rightarrow\;n=2 &&\text{identify mode}\\ &\tfrac{a_0}2=2-d,\;a_2=3,\;\text{other }a_n=0 &&\text{match at }t=0\\ &u=(3x+d)+(2-d)+3e^{-2\pi^2t}\cos\pi x &&u_S+u_T\\ &\phantom{u}=3x+2+3e^{-2\pi^2t}\cos\pi x &&d\text{ cancels} \end{aligned}$$

(d) $u(1,t)=5-3e^{-2\pi^2t}\to5$. Mean of $f$ on $(0,2)$: $\frac12\int_0^2(3x+2+3\cos\pi x)\,dx=\frac12(6+4+0)=5$; mean of $3x+C$ is $3+C$, so $C=2$ ✓ and $u_S(1)=5$.

(e) No: $u_S'$ would need to be $3$ at $x=0$ and $4$ at $x=2$, impossible for a line. Net heat flows in; no steady state.
