Lesson 34 · Topic 6: Fourier transforms · Definition, first principles

The Fourier transform from the definition

The one win: compute the FT of any step function written with $H_a$'s, any (one- or two-sided) exponential and any even/odd extension straight from the definition, in a few clean lines, and say what $\mathcal F^{-1}\mathcal F f$ gives at a jump.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: Topic 6 is the last question on every paper (7–15 marks; masked 2026 Q7 = 14 marks, parts (a)–(c)). Its part (a) is usually "use the definition to compute…": $\mathcal F\{\delta_a\}$, a combination of shifted Heavisides, or a one-sided exponential. These are the cheapest marks on the paper. The formula sheet gives the definition but no table, so every pair in this lesson must be derived in under a minute.

0 · Warm-up from Lessons 15–16 and 24

W1. For $a>0$, $\mathcal L\{H_a(t)\}=\displaystyle\int_0^\infty H(t-a)e^{-st}dt=$…

$\int_a^\infty e^{-st}dt=\big[-\frac{e^{-st}}s\big]_a^\infty=\frac{e^{-as}}s$; the upper limit vanishes only because $s>0$. Today the same integral appears with $s$ replaced by $ik$, and then the upper limit does not vanish.

W2. $\mathcal L\{\delta(t-a)\}$ for $a>0$ is…

$\int\delta(t-a)g(t)\,dt=g(a)$ with $g=e^{-st}$. Same move today with $g=e^{-ikx}$.

W3. The complex Fourier series of $f$ on $(-L,L]$ is $\sum c_ne^{ik_nx}$, $k_n=\frac{n\pi}L$, with $c_n=$…

Lesson 24. Note the minus sign in the coefficient integral and the plus sign in the series: the FT copies exactly this pattern.

1 · Definition, and how it differs from Laplace

$$\boxed{\hat f(k)=\mathcal F\{f\}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\,e^{-ikx}\,dx}\qquad\boxed{f(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat f(k)\,e^{ikx}\,dk}$$

Both are on the formula sheet. $\mathcal F$ is linear. $k$ is real.

Where it comes from (L28 s13–14): write the complex FS as $f=\frac1{2\pi}\sum\Delta k\,\hat f_n e^{ik_nx}$ with $\hat f_n=2Lc_n=\int_{-L}^Lfe^{-ik_nx}dx$ and $\Delta k=\frac\pi L$. Let $L\to\infty$: the sum becomes the integral above.

Convention warning (L28 s15)
Books differ: $\frac1{2\pi}$ or $\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}$ in front of $\hat f$, $e^{+ikx}$ instead of $e^{-ikx}$, $e^{-2\pi ikx}$… Herman's LibreTexts book, for instance, uses $e^{+ikx}$, so its answers are ours with $k\to-k$. The booklet writes $\omega$ for $k$. On the exam use only the formula-sheet version above.

2 · The three integrals you will ever need

  1. Delta (sifting). $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(x-a)e^{-ikx}dx=e^{-ika}.$ So $\boxed{\mathcal F\{\delta_a\}=e^{-iak}}$; $\mathcal F\{\delta\}=1$.
  2. Constant on a finite interval ($k\ne0$): $$\int_a^b e^{-ikx}dx=\Big[\frac{e^{-ikx}}{-ik}\Big]_a^b=\frac{e^{-ika}-e^{-ikb}}{ik}.$$ Box $f=H_{-a}-H_a$ (1 on $|x|<a$): $\hat f=\dfrac{e^{ika}-e^{-ika}}{ik}=\dfrac{2\sin ak}{k}=2a\,\mathrm{sinc}(ak)$ (L28 Ex 6.1 with $a=1$).
  3. Exponential on a half-line ($a>0$): $$\int_0^\infty e^{-ax}e^{-ikx}dx=\Big[\frac{e^{-(a+ik)x}}{-(a+ik)}\Big]_0^\infty=\frac1{a+ik},$$ because $|e^{-(a+ik)x}|=e^{-ax}\to0$. Write that reason: it is the mark for "justify the limit".

Two-sided: split at $0$. $\mathcal F\{e^{-a|x|}\}=\int_{-\infty}^0e^{(a-ik)x}dx+\int_0^\infty e^{-(a+ik)x}dx=\frac1{a-ik}+\frac1{a+ik}=\boxed{\frac{2a}{a^2+k^2}}$ (L28 Ex 6.2).

Always check $k=0$
$\hat f(0)=\int_{-\infty}^\infty f\,dx$ (set $k=0$ in the definition). A formula like $\frac{2\sin ak}{k}$ is "undefined" at $0$, but its limit is $2a$ = area of the box ✓. Use this to catch sign/coefficient slips in 10 seconds.

Q1. $\mathcal F\{\,2\delta_{-3}(x)\,\}=$…

$\delta_{-3}(x)=\delta(x+3)$ sifts out $x=-3$: $2e^{-ik(-3)}=2e^{3ik}$.

Q2. $f(x)=3e^{-2x}$ for $x>0$, $f(x)=0$ for $x<0$. Then $\hat f(k)=$…

Integral 3 with $a=2$, times 3. Check: $\hat f(0)=\frac32=\int_0^\infty3e^{-2x}dx$ ✓. (c) is the two-sided $3e^{-2|x|}$ halved; (d) is two-sided.

See the width trade-off. Pick a family and slide $a$. A narrow $f$ has a wide $\hat f$ and vice versa; the height $\hat f(0)$ is always the area under $f$. For the one-sided exponential $\hat f$ is complex (Re solid, Im dashed). Dots on the left mark the value the inverse FT returns at a jump (section 5).

3 · Step functions written with $H_a$

Exam style: "Compute the FT of $f=c_1H_{a_1}+c_2H_{a_2}+c_3H_{a_3}$." A single $H_a$ has no FT (it is $1$ out to $+\infty$), but the coefficients will sum to $0$, so $f$ vanishes for large $x$ and is a finite staircase.

  1. Tabulate the value on each interval: left of every step, $f=0$; after each $a_j$, add $c_j$. Sketch it.
  2. Split the integral over the intervals where $f\ne0$ and use integral 2 on each.
  3. Collect the exponentials over the common $ik$.
  4. Check $\hat f(0)=\int f$ (expand the numerator to first order in $k$).

Worked. $f(x)=3H_{-1}(x)-H_2(x)-2H_4(x)$.

Solution

Values: $x<-1$: $0$. $-1<x<2$: $3$. $2<x<4$: $3-1=2$. $x>4$: $2-2=0$ ✓.

$$\hat f(k)=3\int_{-1}^{2}e^{-ikx}dx+2\int_{2}^{4}e^{-ikx}dx=\frac{3(e^{ik}-e^{-2ik})+2(e^{-2ik}-e^{-4ik})}{ik}$$ $$=\frac{3e^{ik}-e^{-2ik}-2e^{-4ik}}{ik}.$$

Check: numerator $\approx(3-1-2)+ik(3+2+8)=13ik$, so $\hat f(0)=13=3\cdot3+2\cdot2$ ✓.

Shortcut (check only). When $\sum c_j=0$: $\;\mathcal F\Big\{\sum_j c_jH_{a_j}\Big\}=\dfrac1{ik}\sum_j c_je^{-ika_j}$. Each step $c_jH_{a_j}$ contributes $\frac{c_j e^{-ika_j}}{ik}$; compare the worked answer. On the exam the question says "from the definition", so write the interval integrals, then use this to check.

Q3. $\mathcal F\{H_1(x)-H_4(x)\}=$…

$f=1$ on $(1,4)$: integral 2 with $a=1,b=4$. Check: numerator $\approx(-ik)-(-4ik)=3ik$, so $\hat f(0)=3$ = length ✓. (b) has the sign flipped ($\hat f(0)=-3$).

4 · Even and odd extensions

Booklet style: "$f$ is given for $x>0$; find the FT of its even (odd) extension." Let $F(x)=f(x)$ for $x>0$ and $0$ for $x<0$. Then $f_e(x)=F(x)+F(-x)$ and $f_o(x)=F(x)-F(-x)$ (except at $0$, which doesn't affect integrals).

Flip rule: $\mathcal F\{F(-x)\}(k)=\int F(-x)e^{-ikx}dx\overset{u=-x}{=}\int F(u)e^{iku}du=\hat F(-k)$. Hence

$$\hat f_e(k)=\hat F(k)+\hat F(-k)=2\int_0^\infty f(x)\cos kx\,dx,$$ $$\hat f_o(k)=\hat F(k)-\hat F(-k)=-2i\int_0^\infty f(x)\sin kx\,dx.$$

Real even $f$ ⇒ $\hat f$ real and even. Real odd $f$ ⇒ $\hat f$ purely imaginary and odd. (Same as Lesson 24: even $f$ ⇒ real $c_n$.)

Example. $F=e^{-ax}H(x)$, $\hat F=\frac1{a+ik}$. Even extension $=e^{-a|x|}$: $\frac1{a+ik}+\frac1{a-ik}=\frac{2a}{a^2+k^2}$ ✓ (matches section 2). Odd extension $=\mathrm{sgn}(x)e^{-a|x|}$: $\frac1{a+ik}-\frac1{a-ik}=\frac{-2ik}{a^2+k^2}$.

Q4. $f$ is real and odd, with $\int|f|<\infty$. Without computing, $\hat f(k)$ is…

$e^{-ikx}=\cos kx-i\sin kx$; the $\cos$ part integrates an odd function to $0$, leaving $-i\int f\sin kx\,dx$: imaginary, and odd because $\sin(-kx)=-\sin kx$. Use this to check every extension answer.

5 · Inverse, existence, and the value at a jump

Theorem (L28 s12). If $f$ is piecewise continuous (finitely many jumps/critical points on each closed interval) and absolutely integrable, $\int_{-\infty}^\infty|f|\,dx<\infty$, then $\hat f$ exists and

$$\mathcal F^{-1}\mathcal F\{f\}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),&f\text{ continuous at }x,\\[2pt] \tfrac12\big(f(x^-)+f(x^+)\big),&f\text{ jumps at }x.\end{cases}$$

Same midpoint rule as Fourier series (Lesson 23). The value $f$ is assigned at the jump is irrelevant. This is why Booklet 6 Q1 sets $f(0)=\frac\pi2$: it is the midpoint of $0$ and $\pi$, so $\mathcal F^{-1}\mathcal F f=f$ everywhere.

No FT (integral diverges): $1$, $H(x)$, $x^n$, $e^{\pm x}$, $\sin x$, $\cos x$. Lesson 35 gives $1$ and $e^{iax}$ generalised transforms via $\delta$; Lesson 37 uses "no FT" to discard $e^{\pm x}$ solutions of ODEs.

Q5. $f=4$ on $-1<x<2$, $f=1$ on $2<x<5$, $f=0$ elsewhere. Then $\mathcal F^{-1}\mathcal F f$ at $x=2$ and $x=5$ equals…

Midpoints: $\frac{4+1}2$ at $x=2$; $\frac{1+0}2$ at $x=5$.

Q6. Which function has a Fourier transform in the sense of the theorem?

$\int|x|e^{-|x|}dx=2<\infty$. $e^{-x}$ blows up as $x\to-\infty$ (it has a Laplace transform, not an FT); $\cos2x$ and $H$ don't decay.

6 · Practice (exam level)

P1. $f(x)=H_{-1}(x)+2H_1(x)-3H_3(x)$. (a) Sketch $f$. (b) Use the definition to find $\hat f(k)$. (c) What is $\mathcal F^{-1}\mathcal F f$ at $x=-1,\,1,\,3$?

P2. $f(x)=e^{2x}$ for $x<0$ and $f(x)=2e^{-x}$ for $x>0$. (a) Find $\hat f(k)$, justifying the limits. (b) State $\mathcal F^{-1}\mathcal F f(0)$.

P3. $f(x)=2$ for $0<x<1$, $f(x)=1$ for $1<x<3$, $f(x)=0$ for $x>3$. Find the FT of (a) the even extension, (b) the odd extension. Simplify to real trig functions.

P1 solution

(a) $0$ for $x<-1$; $1$ on $(-1,1)$; $3$ on $(1,3)$; $0$ for $x>3$.

(b) $\displaystyle\hat f=\int_{-1}^1e^{-ikx}dx+3\int_1^3e^{-ikx}dx=\frac{(e^{ik}-e^{-ik})+3(e^{-ik}-e^{-3ik})}{ik}=\frac{e^{ik}+2e^{-ik}-3e^{-3ik}}{ik}.$
Check: numerator $\approx ik(1-2+9)=8ik$ ⇒ $\hat f(0)=8=2+6$ ✓.

(c) $\frac{0+1}2=\frac12$, $\;\frac{1+3}2=2$, $\;\frac{3+0}2=\frac32$.

P2 solution
$$\hat f=\int_{-\infty}^0e^{(2-ik)x}dx+2\int_0^\infty e^{-(1+ik)x}dx=\frac1{2-ik}+\frac2{1+ik},$$

since $|e^{(2-ik)x}|=e^{2x}\to0$ as $x\to-\infty$ and $|e^{-(1+ik)x}|=e^{-x}\to0$ as $x\to\infty$. Check $\hat f(0)=\frac12+2=\int f$ ✓.
(b) Jump from $1$ to $2$ at $0$: $\frac32$.

P3 solution

One-sided $F$: $\hat F=\frac{2(1-e^{-ik})+(e^{-ik}-e^{-3ik})}{ik}=\frac{2-e^{-ik}-e^{-3ik}}{ik}$.

(a) $\hat F(k)+\hat F(-k)=\dfrac{(2-e^{-ik}-e^{-3ik})-(2-e^{ik}-e^{3ik})}{ik}=\dfrac{2i\sin k+2i\sin3k}{ik}=\boxed{\dfrac{2(\sin k+\sin3k)}k}$. Real, even, value $8$ at $k=0$ = area ✓.

(b) $\hat F(k)-\hat F(-k)=\dfrac{4-2\cos k-2\cos3k}{ik}=\boxed{-\dfrac{2i(2-\cos k-\cos3k)}{k}}$. Imaginary, odd, $0$ at $k=0$ ✓.

Or directly: $2\int_0^\infty f\cos kx\,dx$ and $-2i\int_0^\infty f\sin kx\,dx$.

7 · Exam-level challenge (13 marks, about 20 minutes)

(a) Use the definition of the Fourier transform to evaluate $\mathcal F\{\delta_a(x)\}$, $a\in\mathbb R$. [1]
(b) Let $f(x)=4e^{-x}$ for $x>0$ and $f(x)=0$ for $x<0$. Use the definition to find $\hat f(k)$, justifying any limits. What value does $\mathcal F^{-1}\mathcal F f$ take at $x=0$? [3]
(c) Using (b) and a substitution, find the Fourier transform of the odd extension $f_o$ of $4e^{-x}$, $x>0$. Comment on its parity. [2]
(d) Let $g(x)=2H_{-2}(x)-3H_0(x)+H_3(x)$. Sketch $g$ and use the definition to compute $\hat g(k)$. Check your answer at $k=0$. [5]
(e) Give $\mathcal F^{-1}\mathcal F g$ at $x=-2,\,0,\,3,\,4$. [2]

Full marking-style solution: worked/0034-challenge-full-solution.html. Attempt every part first.

Answer key (quick check)

(a) $e^{-iak}$. (b) $\frac4{1+ik}$; value $2$. (c) $\frac4{1+ik}-\frac4{1-ik}=-\frac{8ik}{1+k^2}$, imaginary and odd.
(d) $\hat g=\dfrac{2e^{2ik}-3+e^{-3ik}}{ik}$, $\hat g(0)=1$. (e) $1,\ \frac12,\ -\frac12,\ 0$.
