Lesson 14 · Topic 3: Laplace transforms

Solving IVPs with the Laplace transform

One win: turn any constant-coefficient IVP into algebra in four steps, with the initial conditions built in. No $y_h + y_p$, no undetermined coefficients, no solving for constants at the end.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: Laplace is about 12 marks (roughly one question), and almost every part of it is an IVP solved this way. Later lessons add Heaviside and delta forcing, but the skeleton is this lesson: transform, solve for $Y$, decompose, invert. Marks are lost on the decomposition and inversion, not on the transform.

0 · Warm-up from Lesson 13

W1. $\mathcal L^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+4}\right\}$ is…

Table: $\frac{s}{s^2+4}\leftrightarrow\cos 2t$. Replace $s$ by $s+1$ (s-shift with $a=-1$) and multiply by $e^{-t}$.

W2. The right partial-fraction form for $\dfrac{5}{s(s+1)^2}$ is…

A repeated linear factor $(s+1)^2$ needs one term for each power up to 2, each with a constant numerator.

W3. The sheet has $t^{n-1}/(n-1)!\leftrightarrow 1/s^n$. So $\mathcal L\{t e^{-3t}\}=$

$n=2$: $t\leftrightarrow 1/s^2$. The factor $e^{-3t}$ replaces $s$ by $s+3$. This pair is not on the sheet, so derive it this way each time.

1 · The derivative rule is the whole trick

Integrate by parts once (Lecture 12, slide 3):

$$\mathcal L\{y'\}=sY-y(0),\qquad \mathcal L\{y''\}=s^2Y-s\,y(0)-y'(0),\qquad \mathcal L\{y'''\}=s^3Y-s^2y(0)-s\,y'(0)-y''(0).$$

The sheet gives the general $n$ formula, but know $n=1,2$ cold. Pattern: powers of $s$ fall as the derivative order falls, and the signs are all $-$. A derivative becomes multiplication by $s$, and the initial values appear automatically.

The four steps for $a\,y''+b\,y'+c\,y=g(t)$, $y(0)=y_0,\ y'(0)=y_1$:

  1. Transform every term. Do not divide by $a$ first: just carry it.
  2. Solve for $Y$. $\;(as^2+bs+c)\,Y=a(sy_0+y_1)+b\,y_0+G(s)$. Left: the characteristic polynomial. Right: ICs plus forcing.
  3. Decompose $Y$ into pieces that are on the sheet after an s-shift: partial fractions, complete the square.
  4. Invert term by term. Then check $y(0)$ and $y'(0)$ against the given ICs.

What is in $Y$'s denominator: the characteristic polynomial, times the denominator of $G(s)$. That tells you the decomposition before you start.

1. With $y(0)=2,\ y'(0)=-1$, $\;\mathcal L\{y''\}=$

$s^2Y-s\cdot2-(-1)=s^2Y-2s+1$. The minus sign multiplies the whole value, including its own negative. Option (d) swaps which IC goes with $s$.

2. For $2y''-3y'+y=0,\ y(0)=1,\ y'(0)=3$, step 2 gives $Y=$

$2(s^2Y-s-3)-3(sY-1)+Y=0$, so $(2s^2-3s+1)Y=2s+6-3=2s+3$. The $2$ multiplies both IC terms from $y''$, and $-3\,\mathcal L\{y'\}$ contributes $+3\,y(0)$ on the right-hand side with the sign flipped. Option (b) forgets that term, (d) gets its sign wrong.

2 · Worked example: forcing that repeats a root

Solve $\;y''+4y'+3y=6e^{-3t},\quad y(0)=1,\ y'(0)=0.$ Try each step on paper first.

Step 1–2: transform and solve for $Y$
$$(s^2Y-s)+4(sY-1)+3Y=\frac{6}{s+3}\;\Rightarrow\;(s+1)(s+3)\,Y=s+4+\frac{6}{s+3}.$$ $$Y=\frac{s+4}{(s+1)(s+3)}+\frac{6}{(s+1)(s+3)^2}=\frac{s^2+7s+18}{(s+1)(s+3)^2}.$$

The forcing's pole $s=-3$ coincides with a root of the characteristic polynomial, so $(s+3)^2$ appears.

Step 3: partial fractions (repeated factor)
$$\frac{s^2+7s+18}{(s+1)(s+3)^2}=\frac{A}{s+1}+\frac{B}{s+3}+\frac{C}{(s+3)^2}.$$

Cover-up: $A=\dfrac{1-7+18}{(2)^2}=3$ at $s=-1$, and $C=\dfrac{9-21+18}{-2}=-3$ at $s=-3$. For $B$, match $s^2$: $A+B=1\Rightarrow B=-2$.

Step 4: invert and check
$$y=3e^{-t}-2e^{-3t}-3t\,e^{-3t}.$$

Check: $y(0)=3-2=1$ ✓. $y'=-3e^{-t}+6e^{-3t}-3e^{-3t}+9te^{-3t}$, so $y'(0)=-3+6-3=0$ ✓.

The $t e^{-3t}$ term is the resonance-type response to forcing at a natural rate.

3 · Resonance: $(s^2+\omega^2)^2$

Forcing at the natural frequency, e.g. $y''+4y=8\cos 2t,\ y(0)=0,\ y'(0)=1$:

$$(s^2+4)Y=1+\frac{8s}{s^2+4}\;\Rightarrow\;Y=\frac{1}{s^2+4}+\frac{8s}{(s^2+4)^2}.$$

The sheet has no $(s^2+4)^2$ pair. Derive it from the $t$-rule you proved in Lesson 13, $\mathcal L\{tf\}=-F'(s)$. Take $f=\sin\omega t/\omega$, $F=1/(s^2+\omega^2)$, so $-F'=2s/(s^2+\omega^2)^2$:

$$\mathcal L^{-1}\left\{\frac{s}{(s^2+\omega^2)^2}\right\}=\frac{t\sin\omega t}{2\omega},\qquad \mathcal L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{(s^2+\omega^2)^2}\right\}=\frac{\sin\omega t-\omega t\cos\omega t}{2\omega^3}.$$

The second one is hard to derive quickly (convolution, Lesson 17). In an exam it would be given or you'd get it from convolution. The first you can always derive in two lines.

Here, $\omega=2$: $\;y=\tfrac12\sin2t+8\cdot\dfrac{t\sin2t}{4}=\big(\tfrac12+2t\big)\sin 2t.$ The $t\sin 2t$ is the growing amplitude: resonance. Check $y(0)=0$ ✓, $y'(0)=\tfrac12\cdot2=1$ ✓.

3. For $y''+16y=5\cos4t$, the denominator of $Y$ is…

Characteristic polynomial $s^2+16$, forcing $\frac{5s}{s^2+16}$. Same quadratic twice means resonance, so the answer contains $t\sin4t$.

4 · Non-unit leading coefficient, complex roots

Complex roots show up as an irreducible quadratic. Complete the square, then split the numerator into a multiple of $(s+\alpha)$ plus a multiple of $\beta$:

$$\frac{ps+q}{(s+\alpha)^2+\beta^2}=p\,\frac{s+\alpha}{(s+\alpha)^2+\beta^2}+\frac{q-p\alpha}{\beta}\cdot\frac{\beta}{(s+\alpha)^2+\beta^2}\;\longrightarrow\;e^{-\alpha t}\Big(p\cos\beta t+\tfrac{q-p\alpha}{\beta}\sin\beta t\Big).$$

Do not memorise this; do the split each time. With a leading coefficient $a\ne1$, the characteristic polynomial is $as^2+bs+c$: factor out $a$ before completing the square.

4. $\mathcal L^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{(s-2)^3}\right\}=$

Sheet: $t^{n-1}/(n-1)!\leftrightarrow1/s^n$ with $n=3$ gives $t^2/2$. The shift $s\to s-2$ multiplies by $e^{+2t}$.

5. $\mathcal L^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{3s+5}{s^2+2s+5}\right\}=$

$s^2+2s+5=(s+1)^2+4$ and $3s+5=3(s+1)+2$. So $3\frac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+4}+\frac{2}{(s+1)^2+4}$, and the table has $\sin2t\leftrightarrow\frac{2}{s^2+4}$ with the $2$ already there. Coefficient of $\sin$ is $1$.

5 · Sanity checks (30 seconds, they catch most errors)

  • ICs: evaluate your $y(0)$ and $y'(0)$. Wrong sign on an IC term in step 1 shows up here.
  • $Y\to0$ as $s\to\infty$ (Lesson 13): the numerator degree must be below the denominator's. If it isn't, you've mis-transformed.
  • Behaviour: roots with negative real part mean decay. Doesn't match your answer? Recheck.
  • Forced part: for $g=Ke^{at}$ with $a$ not a characteristic root, the $e^{at}$ term of $y$ is $\dfrac{K}{p(a)}e^{at}$, where $p(s)=as^2+bs+c$. A fast cross-check of the decomposition.

6. $y''+2y'+2y=0,\ y(0)=1,\ y'(0)=0$. Which candidate is the solution?

All four have $y(0)=1$, so the IC $y'(0)=0$ decides. $y'(0)=-1+b$ for $e^{-t}(\cos t+b\sin t)$, so $b=1$. (a): $y'(0)=-2$. (b): $-1$. (d): $+1$. Check by the Laplace route: $Y=\frac{s+2}{(s+1)^2+1}=\frac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+1}+\frac{1}{(s+1)^2+1}$.
Traps
(1) Dividing the LHS by $a$ but not the forcing (or the ICs). (2) Dropping a sign when an IC is negative. (3) Numerator $ps+q$ over a completed square: the $\cos$ coefficient is $p$, not $q$. (4) Forgetting that a constant forcing transforms to $K/s$, not $K$.

6 · Practice (a notch above the notes)

Paper first, then open the solution.

P1. $\;9y''-6y'+y=18,\ y(0)=0,\ y'(0)=0$. (Non-unit leading coefficient, repeated root, and the forcing adds another pole at $0$.)

P2. $\;y''+2y'+10y=10t,\ y(0)=1,\ y'(0)=0$. (Complete the square, with a $1/s^2$ forcing.)

P3. $\;y'''-3y''+3y'-y=0,\ y(0)=1,\ y'(0)=0,\ y''(0)=2$. (Triple root: write the numerator in powers of $(s-1)$.)

P1 solution
$$(9s^2-6s+1)Y=\frac{18}{s}\;\Rightarrow\;Y=\frac{18}{s(3s-1)^2}=\frac{2}{s\,(s-\tfrac13)^2}.$$ $$\frac{2}{s(s-\frac13)^2}=\frac As+\frac{B}{s-\frac13}+\frac{C}{(s-\frac13)^2}:\quad A=\frac{2}{1/9}=18,\ \ C=\frac{2}{1/3}=6,\ \ A+B=0\Rightarrow B=-18.$$ $$y=18-18e^{t/3}+6t\,e^{t/3}.$$

Check: $y(0)=0$ ✓; $y'=-6e^{t/3}+6e^{t/3}+2te^{t/3}$, $y'(0)=0$ ✓. Also constant forcing gives equilibrium $y=18/1=18$, matching $A$.

P2 solution
$$(s^2+2s+10)Y=s+2+\frac{10}{s^2}\;\Rightarrow\;Y=\frac{s^3+2s^2+10}{s^2(s^2+2s+10)}.$$ $$\frac{s^3+2s^2+10}{s^2(s^2+2s+10)}=\frac As+\frac B{s^2}+\frac{Cs+D}{s^2+2s+10}.$$

Match coefficients in $s^3+2s^2+10=As(s^2+2s+10)+B(s^2+2s+10)+(Cs+D)s^2$. $s^0$: $B=1$. $s^1$: $10A+2B=0\Rightarrow A=-\tfrac15$. $s^3$: $A+C=1\Rightarrow C=\tfrac65$. $s^2$: $2A+B+D=2\Rightarrow D=\tfrac75$.

Then $\dfrac{\frac65s+\frac75}{(s+1)^2+9}=\tfrac65\cdot\dfrac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+9}+\tfrac1{15}\cdot\dfrac{3}{(s+1)^2+9}$, so

$$y=-\tfrac15+t+e^{-t}\Big(\tfrac65\cos3t+\tfrac1{15}\sin3t\Big).$$

Check: $y(0)=-\tfrac15+\tfrac65=1$ ✓. $y'(0)=1+\big(-\tfrac65+3\cdot\tfrac1{15}\big)=1-1=0$ ✓. The non-decaying part $t-\tfrac15$ is the steady response: it solves $y''+2y'+10y=10t$ directly ($0+2+10t-2=10t$ ✓).

P3 solution
$$(s^3-3s^2+3s-1)Y=s^2-3s+5\;\Rightarrow\;Y=\frac{s^2-3s+5}{(s-1)^3}.$$

(Terms: $s^2y_0+sy_1+y_2-3(sy_0+y_1)+3y_0=s^2+2-3s+3$.) Write the numerator in powers of $u=s-1$: $s^2-3s+5=(u+1)^2-3(u+1)+5=u^2-u+3$.

$$Y=\frac1{u}-\frac1{u^2}+\frac3{u^3}\;\Rightarrow\;y=e^{t}\Big(1-t+\tfrac32t^2\Big).$$

Check: $y(0)=1$ ✓; $y'(0)=1-1=0$ ✓; $y''=e^t(1-t+\tfrac32t^2)+2e^t(-1+3t)+3e^t$, $y''(0)=1-2+3=2$ ✓.

7 · Exam-level challenge (12 marks)

About 18 minutes, on paper, then open one part at a time.

A damped oscillator satisfies $\;2y''+4y'+10y=20e^{-t}\;$ and is released from displacement $1$ with zero velocity, so $y(0)=1,\ y'(0)=0$.

(a) Take Laplace transforms and show that $\;Y(s)=\dfrac{s+2}{s^2+2s+5}+\dfrac{10}{(s+1)(s^2+2s+5)}.$ [3]
(b) Express $\dfrac{10}{(s+1)(s^2+2s+5)}$ as partial fractions with real constants. [4]
(c) Hence find $y(t)$. [3]
(d) Check your answer against both initial conditions, then describe in words how the motion behaves for large $t$ and why. [2]

(a) Transform
$$2(s^2Y-s)+4(sY-1)+10Y=\frac{20}{s+1}\;\Rightarrow\;(2s^2+4s+10)Y=2s+4+\frac{20}{s+1}.$$

Divide by $2$: $\;(s^2+2s+5)Y=(s+2)+\dfrac{10}{s+1}$ ✓.

(b) Partial fractions

$\dfrac{10}{(s+1)(s^2+2s+5)}=\dfrac A{s+1}+\dfrac{Bs+C}{s^2+2s+5}$. Cover up at $s=-1$: $A=\dfrac{10}{1-2+5}=\dfrac52$. Then $10=\tfrac52(s^2+2s+5)+(Bs+C)(s+1)$: $s^2$: $B=-\tfrac52$. $s^0$: $\tfrac{25}2+C=10\Rightarrow C=-\tfrac52$. So

$$\frac{10}{(s+1)(s^2+2s+5)}=\frac{5/2}{s+1}-\frac52\cdot\frac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+4}.$$
(c) Invert

$\dfrac{s+2}{(s+1)^2+4}=\dfrac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+4}+\tfrac12\dfrac{2}{(s+1)^2+4}$. Adding (b):

$$Y=\frac{5/2}{s+1}-\frac32\cdot\frac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+4}+\frac12\cdot\frac2{(s+1)^2+4}$$ $$\boxed{y=\tfrac12e^{-t}\big(5-3\cos2t+\sin2t\big).}$$
(d) Checks and interpretation

$y(0)=\tfrac12(5-3)=1$ ✓. With $g=5-3\cos2t+\sin2t$: $g'=6\sin2t+2\cos2t$, $y'=\tfrac12e^{-t}(g'-g)$, and $g'(0)=2=g(0)$, so $y'(0)=0$ ✓.

Large $t$: everything has a factor $e^{-t}$, so $y\to0$. The $\tfrac52e^{-t}$ is the forced response (it also solves the ODE: $8k=20$ for $ke^{-t}$ gives $k=\tfrac52$), the cos/sin part is the damped oscillation (frequency 2, decay rate 1). Both decay because the characteristic roots $-1\pm2i$ and the forcing exponent $-1$ all have negative real part.
