No new teaching. Five new questions, 52 marks, built on the 2021–2024 PDE questions: one long inhomogeneous heat question, a polar Laplace problem, a string, a short conceptual block and a Bessel plate. Each has a hidden marking scheme.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: Topic 5 is predicted to be the biggest question on the paper. Q6 of the masked 2026 paper is worth 18 marks, has parts (a)–(c) and a very long intro, which almost certainly means a modelling setup like Q1 below. 1 · Warm-up (5 min, spaced retrieval)

W1. $X''=\lambda X$ with $X(0)=0$ and $X'(L)=0$ has eigenfunctions…

$X(0)=0$ means sine. $X'(L) = k\cos kL = 0$ means $kL = (n-\frac12)\pi$. Option (d) is the mirror case, $X'(0)=0$ and $X(L)=0$ (Lesson 27).

W2. $u_t = u_{xx}$ on $0\lt x\lt 2$, both ends insulated, $u(x,0)=x$. As $t\to\infty$, $u\to$…

Neumann–Neumann: every mode decays except the $\lambda=0$ constant, which is the mean of the IC, $\frac12\int_0^2x\,dx = 1$ (Lesson 28).

W3. $u_{xx} + 6u_{xy} + 9u_{yy} = 0$ is…

$B^2-4AC = 36-36 = 0$ (Lesson 27).

W4. Laplace's equation on the annulus $1\lt r\lt 3$. The $\theta$-independent ($\mu=0$) part of the solution is…

$rR''+R'=0$ means $(rR')'=0$, so $R = C + D\ln r$. $\ln r$ is dropped only when $r=0$ is in the domain (Lesson 30).

W5. A heat-equation series solution is shown to be genuine on $t\ge T>0$ rather than on $t\ge0$ because…

Differentiating twice in $x$ brings down $k_n^2$. At $t=0$ nothing controls that, and $\sum k_n^2|c_n|$ usually diverges (Lesson 32).

2 · The set (≈ 105 min, 52 marks)

Exam pace is 2 min per mark. Sitting 1: Q1 alone (19 marks, ≈ 38 min). Sitting 2: Q2–Q5 (33 marks, ≈ 66 min). Use paper and the 2026 formula sheet only. Open each marking scheme only after you have written the whole question.

Question 1 [19 marks]

A metal rod of length 1 has its curved surface insulated, so heat flows only along the rod. It has been sitting in iced water, so initially its temperature is $0$ everywhere. From $t=0$ the end $x=1$ is kept in the iced water (temperature $0$). At the end $x=0$ a heater pumps heat in at a constant rate, which holds the temperature gradient there at $-1$. The temperature $u(x,t)$ satisfies $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = 2\,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2},\qquad 0\lt x\lt 1,\ t>0.$$

(a) Write down the boundary conditions and the initial condition for $u$. [2]

(b) Find the steady-state temperature $u_S(x)$. [2]

(c) Let $u = u_S(x) + v(x,t)$. Write down the IBVP (PDE, BCs, IC) satisfied by $v$. [2]

(d) Use separation of variables, $v = X(x)T(t)$, to find the ODEs for $X$ and $T$. Considering all three cases for the separation constant, find the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. [5]

(e) Hence find $u(x,t)$. [4]

(f) Sketch $u(x,t)$ against $x$ at $t=0$, at two intermediate times $0\lt t_1\lt t_2$, and as $t\to\infty$. [2]

(g) What does the temperature at the heated end tend to as $t\to\infty$? Using only the leading term of the series, estimate the time after which $u(0,t)$ is within $0.01$ of this limit. [2]

Q1 marking scheme

(a) [1] BCs: $u_x(0,t) = -1$ and $u(1,t) = 0$ for $t>0$. [1] IC: $u(x,0) = 0$ for $0\lt x\lt 1$.

(b) [1] $u_S''=0$, so $u_S = Ax+B$. [1] $u_S'(0)=A=-1$ and $u_S(1) = -1+B = 0$, so $u_S = 1-x$.

(c) [1] $v_t = 2v_{xx}$, with $v_x(0,t) = -1-(-1) = 0$ and $v(1,t)=0$ (homogeneous). [1] $v(x,0) = 0-u_S = x-1$.

(d) [1] $X\dot T = 2X''T$, so $\frac{\dot T}{2T} = \frac{X''}{X} = \lambda$, which gives $\dot T = 2\lambda T$ and $X''=\lambda X$ with $X'(0)=0$, $X(1)=0$. [1] $\lambda=k^2>0$: $X = A\cosh kx + B\sinh kx$. $X'(0)=kB=0$, then $X(1)=A\cosh k=0$ forces $A=0$, so only the trivial solution. [1] $\lambda=0$: $X=A+Bx$. $X'(0)=B=0$, $X(1)=A=0$: trivial. [1] $\lambda=-k^2$: $X = A\cos kx + B\sin kx$. $X'(0)=kB=0$, $X(1)=A\cos k=0$, so $k_n = \frac{(2n-1)\pi}{2}$. [1] Eigenvalues $\lambda_n = -\frac{(2n-1)^2\pi^2}{4}$, eigenfunctions $X_n = \cos\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2}$, $n=1,2,\dots$

(e) [1] $T_n = e^{-2k_n^2t}$, so $v = \sum_{n\ge1} c_n e^{-2k_n^2t}\cos k_nx$. [1] $c_n = 2\int_0^1(x-1)\cos k_nx\,dx$. [1] By parts: $\big[(x-1)\frac{\sin k_nx}{k_n}\big]_0^1 - \int_0^1\frac{\sin k_nx}{k_n}dx = \frac{\cos k_n - 1}{k_n^2} = -\frac1{k_n^2}$, since $\cos k_n = 0$. So $c_n = -\frac{2}{k_n^2} = -\frac{8}{(2n-1)^2\pi^2}$. [1] $$u(x,t) = 1-x-\frac{8}{\pi^2}\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{(2n-1)^2}\,e^{-(2n-1)^2\pi^2t/2}\cos\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2}.$$

(f) [1] $t=0$: the line $u=0$. As $t\to\infty$: the line $1-x$. [1] Intermediate curves lie between the two, rise with $t$, all pass through $(1,0)$, and all leave $x=0$ with slope $-1$. The biggest change is near $x=0$.

(g) [1] $u(0,t)\to u_S(0) = 1$. [1] $u(0,t)\approx 1-\frac8{\pi^2}e^{-\pi^2t/2}$. Setting $\frac8{\pi^2}e^{-\pi^2t/2}=0.01$ gives $t = \frac2{\pi^2}\ln\frac{800}{\pi^2}\approx 0.89$.

Question 2 [10 marks]

A thin plate occupies the quarter-annulus $1\lt r\lt 2$, $0\lt\theta\lt\frac\pi2$. Its two straight edges ($\theta=0$ and $\theta=\frac\pi2$) are insulated, the inner arc $r=1$ is held at temperature $0$, and the outer arc is held at $u(2,\theta) = 3 + \cos4\theta$. The steady-state temperature $u(r,\theta)$ satisfies $$\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial r^2}+\frac1r\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}+\frac1{r^2}\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial\theta^2}=0.$$

(a) Write $u = R(r)\Theta(\theta)$. Show that $\Theta''+\mu\Theta=0$ and $r^2R''+rR'-\mu R=0$, and state the boundary conditions on $\Theta$. [2]

(b) Considering all three cases for $\mu$, find the eigenvalues $\mu_n$ and eigenfunctions $\Theta_n$. [3]

(c) Solve the $R$ equation for each eigenvalue and write down the general series solution. [2]

(d) Apply the arc conditions to find $u(r,\theta)$, and hence find $u(\sqrt2,0)$. [3]

Q2 marking scheme

(a) [1] $R''\Theta + \frac1rR'\Theta + \frac1{r^2}R\Theta''=0$. Multiply by $\frac{r^2}{R\Theta}$: $\frac{r^2R''+rR'}{R} = -\frac{\Theta''}{\Theta} = \mu$, which gives both ODEs. [1] Insulated edges: $u_\theta=0$ there, so $\Theta'(0)=\Theta'(\frac\pi2)=0$.

(b) [1] $\mu=-k^2$: $\Theta = A\cosh k\theta + B\sinh k\theta$. $\Theta'(0)=kB=0$ and $\Theta'(\frac\pi2) = kA\sinh\frac{k\pi}2=0$, so $A=0$: trivial. [1] $\mu=0$: $\Theta = A+B\theta$, $B=0$, so $\Theta_0 = 1$ (non-trivial). [1] $\mu=k^2$: $\Theta = A\cos k\theta+B\sin k\theta$, $B=0$, and $-kA\sin\frac{k\pi}2=0$ gives $k=2n$. So $\mu_n = 4n^2$, $\Theta_n = \cos2n\theta$, $n = 0,1,2,\dots$

(c) [1] $n\ge1$: the Euler equation with trial $r^m$ gives $m^2=4n^2$, so $R = C_nr^{2n}+D_nr^{-2n}$. $n=0$: $R = C_0 + D_0\ln r$. [1] $$u = C_0 + D_0\ln r + \sum_{n\ge1}\big(C_nr^{2n}+D_nr^{-2n}\big)\cos2n\theta.$$ Both powers are kept because $r=0$ is not in the domain.

(d) [1] $r=1$: $C_0 = 0$ and $C_n + D_n = 0$. [1] $r=2$, matching coefficients: $D_0\ln2 = 3$; for $n=2$, $C_2(16-\frac1{16}) = 1$ so $C_2 = \frac{16}{255}$; all other $C_n=0$. $$u = \frac{3\ln r}{\ln 2} + \frac{16}{255}\big(r^4-r^{-4}\big)\cos4\theta.$$ [1] $u(\sqrt2,0) = \frac32 + \frac{16}{255}\cdot\frac{15}{4} = \frac32+\frac4{17} = \frac{59}{34}\approx1.74$.

Question 3 [8 marks]

A string of length 1 is fixed at both ends, and its transverse displacement $u(x,t)$ satisfies $u_{tt} = 4u_{xx}$. At $t=0$ the string has the shape $\sin3\pi x$ and is given an initial velocity $4\pi\sin\pi x$.

(a) Write down the boundary and initial conditions. [1]

(b) Separate variables with $u = X(x)T(t)$. State which sign of the separation constant gives non-trivial $X$, and show that $T$ then oscillates. Write down the general series solution. (You may quote the eigenvalues of the $X$-problem.) [3]

(c) Find $u(x,t)$. [2]

(d) Which normal frequencies are present? Is the motion periodic in $t$? If so, give the period. [1]

(e) Suppose instead the string started from rest with a corner in it (plucked). In one sentence each, what happens to the corner under the wave equation, and what would happen to a corner in the initial temperature under the heat equation? [1]

Q3 marking scheme

(a) [1] $u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0$; $u(x,0) = \sin3\pi x$, $u_t(x,0) = 4\pi\sin\pi x$.

(b) [1] $\frac{\ddot T}{4T} = \frac{X''}{X}=\lambda$ with $X(0)=X(1)=0$. Only $\lambda = -k^2\lt 0$ gives non-trivial $X$: $k_n = n\pi$, $X_n = \sin n\pi x$. [1] $\ddot T_n + 4n^2\pi^2T_n = 0$, so $T_n = a_n\cos2n\pi t + b_n\sin2n\pi t$, which oscillates. [1] $u = \sum_{n\ge1}(a_n\cos2n\pi t + b_n\sin2n\pi t)\sin n\pi x$.

(c) [1] $u(x,0) = \sum a_n\sin n\pi x = \sin3\pi x$, so $a_3 = 1$ and all other $a_n = 0$. $u_t(x,0) = \sum 2n\pi b_n\sin n\pi x = 4\pi\sin\pi x$, so $2\pi b_1 = 4\pi$, $b_1 = 2$, others $0$. [1] $$u = 2\sin(2\pi t)\sin\pi x + \cos(6\pi t)\sin3\pi x.$$

(d) [1] $\omega_1 = 2\pi$ and $\omega_3 = 6\pi$. Yes, periodic: $\omega_3 = 3\omega_1$, so the period is $\frac{2\pi}{2\pi} = 1$.

(e) [1] Wave: the corner does not smooth out. It splits and travels along the string at speed $c=2$, reflecting off the ends forever. Heat: the corner is smoothed instantly, and $u$ is smooth for every $t>0$.

Question 4 [7 marks]

(a) State the order of each PDE, whether it is linear, whether it is homogeneous, and its type (elliptic, parabolic or hyperbolic). [2]

(i) $2u_{xx} - 4u_{xy} + 2u_{yy} + u_y = e^x$    (ii) $u_{xx} - 2u_{xy} + x\,u_{yy} = 0$

(b) Consider $y''+9y=0$, $y(0)=0$, $y(L)=\beta$, with $L>0$. For which $L$ and $\beta$ does this BVP have exactly one solution, no solution, and infinitely many solutions? [2]

(c) Show that the series for $u(x,t)$ found in Q1(e) is a genuine solution of $u_t = 2u_{xx}$ for $0\le x\le1$, $t\ge T$, for any fixed $T>0$. Why can't $T=0$ be used? [3]

Q4 marking scheme

(a) [1] (i) 2nd order, linear, inhomogeneous ($e^x$). $B^2-4AC = 16-16=0$: parabolic. [1] (ii) 2nd order, linear, homogeneous. $B^2-4AC = 4-4x$: hyperbolic for $x\lt 1$, parabolic on $x=1$, elliptic for $x>1$.

(b) [1] $y = A\cos3x+B\sin3x$. $y(0)=0$ gives $A=0$, then $B\sin3L = \beta$. If $\sin 3L\ne0$ ($L\ne\frac{m\pi}3$): exactly one solution, $B = \beta/\sin3L$. [1] If $L = \frac{m\pi}3$: no solution when $\beta\ne0$; infinitely many ($B\sin3x$, any $B$) when $\beta=0$.

(c) [1] $u_S = 1-x$ is smooth, so only the series $\sum u_n$, $u_n = c_ne^{-2k_n^2t}\cos k_nx$, needs checking. On $D = [0,1]\times[T,\infty)$: $|u_n|\le|c_n|e^{-2k_n^2T}$, $|\partial_xu_n|\le |c_n|k_ne^{-2k_n^2T}$, $|\partial_x^2u_n|\le|c_n|k_n^2e^{-2k_n^2T} = 2e^{-2k_n^2T}$, and $\partial_tu_n = 2\partial_x^2u_n$. Since $|c_n|\le1$ and $|c_n|k_n\le\frac4\pi$, all four are $\le M_n = 4e^{-2k_n^2T}$. [1] $\sum M_n$ converges (ratio test: $M_{n+1}/M_n = e^{-4\pi^2nT}\to0$). By the Weierstrass M-test, the series for $u$, $u_x$, $u_{xx}$ and $u_t$ all converge uniformly on $D$. So term-by-term differentiation is justified, each term solves the PDE, and $u$ is genuine for $t\ge T$. [1] At $t=0$ the bound becomes $\sum|c_n|k_n^2 = \sum 2$, which diverges, so the M-test gives nothing. (Also, the IC $u=0$ has $u_x=0\ne-1$ at $x=0$, so it is incompatible with the BC.)

Question 5 [8 marks]

A thin circular plate of radius 2 has insulated faces. Its edge is kept at temperature $0$, and its initial temperature $f(r)$ depends only on the distance $r$ from the centre. The temperature $u(r,t)$ satisfies $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = 3\Big(\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial r^2}+\frac1r\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\Big),\qquad 0\lt r\lt 2,\ t>0.$$

(a) State the boundary conditions, including the one at $r=0$. [1]

(b) Separate variables, $u = R(r)T(t)$, and find the two ODEs. [2]

(c) Take the separation constant to be $-k^2$. Use the substitution $s=kr$ to show that the $R$ equation becomes Bessel's equation of order 0. Hence find the eigenfunctions and the condition on $k$. [3]

(d) Write down the general series solution, with a formula for its coefficients (do not evaluate it). For large $t$, which term dominates, and at what rate does it decay? The first zero of $J_0$ is $2.405$. [2]

Q5 marking scheme

(a) [1] $u(2,t) = 0$ for $t>0$, and $u(r,t)$ finite (bounded) as $r\to0$.

(b) [1] $R\dot T = 3(R''+\frac1rR')T$, so $\frac{\dot T}{3T} = \frac{R''+R'/r}{R}=\lambda$. [1] $\dot T - 3\lambda T = 0$ and $R''+\frac1rR'-\lambda R = 0$, with $R(2)=0$ and $R$ bounded.

(c) [1] $\lambda=-k^2$: $R''+\frac1rR'+k^2R=0$. With $S(s)=R(r)$, $R' = kS'$ and $R''=k^2S''$, so $k^2S''+\frac{k^2}{s}S'+k^2S=0$, i.e. $S''+\frac1sS'+S=0$: Bessel order 0. [1] $R = AJ_0(kr)+BY_0(kr)$. $Y_0$ is unbounded (log) at $0$, so $B=0$. [1] $R(2)=0$ gives $J_0(2k)=0$, so $k_n = j_n/2$, where $j_n$ is the $n$-th positive zero of $J_0$. Eigenfunctions $J_0(k_nr)$.

(d) [1] $T_n = e^{-3k_n^2t}$ and $u = \sum_{n\ge1}C_ne^{-3k_n^2t}J_0(k_nr)$, with $C_n = \frac{2}{4J_1^2(2k_n)}\int_0^2f(r)J_0(k_nr)\,r\,dr$ (from $J_0$ orthogonality with weight $r$). [1] The $n=1$ term: $u\approx C_1e^{-3k_1^2t}J_0(1.2024r)$, with decay rate $3(1.2024)^2\approx4.34$.

Where marks leak in this set
(1) Q1(a): writing $u_x(0,t)=1$. The text says the gradient is $-1$, which is heat flowing into the rod. (2) Q1(c): forgetting to subtract $u_S$ from the IC, or leaving the BC for $v$ inhomogeneous. (3) Q1(d), Q2(b): writing only the case that works. Each "trivial" case is a mark. (4) Q1(e): using $\frac2L\int_0^L$ with sine eigenfunctions out of habit. Here they are $\cos k_nx$. (5) Q2(c): dropping $\ln r$ or $r^{-2n}$ as if $r=0$ were in the domain. (6) Q3(c): $b_n = $ coefficient, forgetting to divide by $\omega_n$. (7) Q4(c): bounding only $u$ and not $u_{xx}$ and $u_t$.

4 · Before you close this tab

Retrieval: cover §0 and write the four eigen-pairs for $X''=\lambda X$ on $[0,L]$

Dirichlet–Dirichlet: $\sin\frac{n\pi x}L$, $n\ge1$. Neumann–Neumann: $\cos\frac{n\pi x}L$, $n\ge0$ (includes the constant). $X(0)=0$, $X'(L)=0$: $\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}$. $X'(0)=0$, $X(L)=0$: $\cos\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}$. In every case $\lambda_n = -k_n^2$, where $k_n$ is the number multiplying $x$. None of this is on the formula sheet.
