Laplace's equation: rectangles, discs, wedges and annuli

The one win: given $\nabla^2u=0$ on a rectangle or a polar region, decide in one glance which variable gets the eigenvalue problem, pick the right partner functions ($\sinh$/$\cosh$ or $r^{\pm k}$), and finish by matching coefficients.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: Topic 5 is predicted to be the biggest question (masked 2026 paper Q6 = 18 marks, parts (a)–(c), long intro). Laplace appears as "the steady state of the 2D heat equation". 2022 had a 28-mark semi-annulus question: show the separated ODEs $r^2R''+rR'-\lambda R=0$, $S''+\lambda S=0$, then solve with boundary data that is a finite sine sum. The formula sheet has no separation recipe and no eigenfunction table, so memorise §1.

0 · Warm-up (Lessons 3 and 27)

W1. $X''+k^2X=0$, $X'(0)=0$, $X'(L)=0$. The eigenfunctions are…

Neumann–Neumann: cosines, and $k=0$ (constant) survives. Forgetting $n=0$ loses the mean of the data (Lesson 27).

W2. General solution of $r^2R''+rR'-9R=0$, $r>0$:

Euler equation (Lesson 3): try $r^m$: $m(m-1)+m-9=m^2-9=0$, $m=\pm3$. Option (a) is the constant-coefficient slip.

1 · The decision rule and the table to memorise

Rule. After $u=X(x)Y(y)$ (or $R(r)\Theta(\theta)$), the eigenvalue problem goes to the variable whose two boundaries are both homogeneous ($u=0$ or $\partial u/\partial n=0$, or periodic). The other variable gets the "partner" ODE with no BCs used yet, except any homogeneous one it has. The nonzero side is used last, to find coefficients.

Q1. $0<x<2$, $0<y<3$: $u(0,y)=g(y)$, $u(2,y)=0$, $u_y(x,0)=0$, $u_y(x,3)=0$. The eigenfunctions are…

Only $y$ has two homogeneous ends, both Neumann, so cosines in $y$ including $n=0$. $x$ has a nonzero end ($g$), so $x$ gets the partner ($\sinh$, or $A+Bx$ for $n=0$).

2 · Rectangle, one nonzero side

Example. $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$ on $0<x<\pi$, $0<y<1$; $u(0,y)=u(\pi,y)=0$, $u(x,0)=0$, $u(x,1)=3\sin2x-\sin5x$.

Steps 1–2: separate, eigenvalue problem in $x$

$u=XY$: $X''Y+XY''=0\Rightarrow \dfrac{X''}{X}=-\dfrac{Y''}{Y}=\lambda$. So $X''-\lambda X=0$, $Y''+\lambda Y=0$, with $X(0)=X(\pi)=0$.

$\lambda=k^2>0$: $X=Ae^{kx}+Be^{-kx}$, BCs give $A=B=0$. $\lambda=0$: $X=A+Bx$, $A=B=0$. $\lambda=-k^2<0$: $X=A\cos kx+B\sin kx$, $A=0$, $\sin k\pi=0\Rightarrow k=n$. So $X_n=\sin nx$, $\lambda_n=-n^2$.

Steps 3–5: partner, superpose, match

$Y_n''-n^2Y_n=0$, $Y_n(0)=0$ ⇒ $Y_n=\sinh ny$. Superpose: $$u=\sum_{n\ge1}B_n\sinh(ny)\sin nx.$$ At $y=1$: $\sum B_n\sinh n\,\sin nx=3\sin2x-\sin5x$. Match: $B_2\sinh2=3$, $B_5\sinh5=-1$, all others $0$. $$u=\frac{3\sinh2y}{\sinh2}\sin2x-\frac{\sinh5y}{\sinh5}\sin5x.$$

Pick the partner that kills a side directly
Zero at $y=0$: use $\sinh(ky)$. Zero at $y=b$: use $\sinh\big(k(b-y)\big)$, not $A\cosh ky+B\sinh ky$ with messy $A/B$. Neumann zero at $y=0$: $\cosh ky$; at $y=b$: $\cosh(k(b-y))$. Then normalise: $\dfrac{\sinh(k(b-y))}{\sinh(kb)}$ equals $1$ on the data side, so the coefficient is just the Fourier coefficient of the data.

Q2. Same strip but now $u(x,1)=0$ and $u(x,0)=\sin3x$ (sides still zero). Then $u=$…

Zero at $y=1$ ⇒ $\sinh(3(1-y))$; dividing by $\sinh 3$ makes it $1$ at $y=0$. (c) misses the normalisation; (a) is zero at the wrong side.

Q3. $u_x=0$ on $x=0$ and $x=a$. The $n=0$ eigenfunction $X_0=1$ pairs with $Y_0=$…

$k=0$: $Y_0''=0$ ⇒ linear. It carries the mean of the data; e.g. data $c$ at $y=0$ and $0$ at $y=b$ gives $c(1-y/b)$.

3 · Two nonzero sides

  • Opposite sides nonzero ($y=0$ and $y=b$), other pair homogeneous: no split needed. Use $Y_n=A_n\sinh(k_n(b-y))+B_n\sinh(k_ny)$, match each side separately.
  • Adjacent sides nonzero (e.g. $x=a$ and $y=b$): no variable has two homogeneous ends. Split $u=v+w$: $v$ keeps the $y=b$ data with zero on the other three sides; $w$ keeps the $x=a$ data with zero on the other three. Solve each as §2, add.

Example. Unit square, $u=0$ on $x=0$ and $y=0$, $u(x,1)=\sin\pi x$, $u(1,y)=\sin2\pi y$.

Solution

$v$: zero on $x=0,1$ ⇒ $\sin n\pi x$, zero at $y=0$ ⇒ $\sinh n\pi y$. Match at $y=1$: only $n=1$. $v=\dfrac{\sinh\pi y}{\sinh\pi}\sin\pi x$.

$w$: zero on $y=0,1$ ⇒ $\sin n\pi y$, zero at $x=0$ ⇒ $\sinh n\pi x$. Match at $x=1$: only $n=2$. $w=\dfrac{\sinh2\pi x}{\sinh2\pi}\sin2\pi y$.

$$u=\frac{\sinh\pi y}{\sinh\pi}\sin\pi x+\frac{\sinh2\pi x}{\sinh2\pi}\sin2\pi y.$$

Check: on $y=1$, $w=\dots\sin2\pi=0$ ✓; on $x=1$, $v=\dots\sin\pi=0$ ✓.

Q4. On $0<x<1$, $0<y<1$: $u(x,0)=f(x)$, $u(x,1)=g(x)$, $u(0,y)=0$, $u(1,y)=0$. You should…

$x$ has two zero ends, so one eigenfunction family works for both data sides: $Y_n=A_n\sinh(n\pi(1-y))+B_n\sinh(n\pi y)$. Splitting is allowed but unnecessary.

4 · Polar coordinates

$$\nabla^2u=u_{rr}+\frac1ru_r+\frac1{r^2}u_{\theta\theta}=0.$$ $u=R(r)\Theta(\theta)$, multiply by $\frac{r^2}{R\Theta}$: $$\frac{r^2R''+rR'}{R}=-\frac{\Theta''}{\Theta}=\lambda\ \Rightarrow\ r^2R''+rR'-\lambda R=0,\quad \Theta''+\lambda\Theta=0.$$ Note the sign: here positive $\lambda=k^2$ gives oscillation in $\theta$ (2022 exam and Lecture 24 use this form; Lecture 24 calls it $\mu$). The $R$-equation is an Euler equation: $r^m$ gives $m^2=\lambda$.

Disc example. $r<2$, $u(2,\theta)=1+4\cos\theta-8\sin3\theta$.

Solution
$$u=\tfrac12a_0+\sum_{n\ge1}r^n(a_n\cos n\theta+b_n\sin n\theta).$$

At $r=2$ match: $\frac12a_0=1$, $2a_1=4$, $8b_3=-8$. So $u=1+2r\cos\theta-r^3\sin3\theta$. Centre value $u(0)=1$ = mean of the boundary data.

Wedge example. $0\le r<1$, $0\le\theta\le\frac\pi2$, $u=0$ on both straight edges, $u(1,\theta)=\sin2\theta+3\sin6\theta$.

Solution

Dirichlet both ends, $\beta=\frac\pi2$: $\Theta_n=\sin\frac{n\pi\theta}{\pi/2}=\sin2n\theta$, $k=2n$. Bounded: $R_n=r^{2n}$. $u=\sum B_nr^{2n}\sin2n\theta$. Match at $r=1$: $B_1=1$, $B_3=3$. $$u=r^2\sin2\theta+3r^6\sin6\theta.$$

Annulus example. $1<r<2$, full $\theta$, $u(1,\theta)=0$, $u(2,\theta)=3\sin2\theta$.

Solution

Periodic: $n\ge0$. Keep both: $u=A_0+B_0\ln r+\sum (A_nr^n+B_nr^{-n})\cos n\theta+(C_nr^n+D_nr^{-n})\sin n\theta$.

Only $\sin2\theta$ appears in either boundary, so all other terms are $0$ (match at $r=1$ and $r=2$ gives a $2\times2$ system with zero RHS). For $\sin2\theta$: $C_2+D_2=0$, $4C_2+\frac14D_2=3$ ⇒ $C_2=\frac45$, $D_2=-\frac45$. $$u=\tfrac45\big(r^2-r^{-2}\big)\sin2\theta.$$

Shortcut, like $\sinh(k(b-y))$: $\big(\frac ra\big)^k-\big(\frac ar\big)^k$ vanishes at $r=a$.

Q5. Wedge $0\le\theta\le\frac\pi3$ with $u_\theta=0$ on $\theta=0$ and $u=0$ on $\theta=\frac\pi3$. The $\Theta$ eigenfunctions are…

Neumann at $0$ ⇒ cosine; Dirichlet at $\beta=\frac\pi3$ ⇒ $\cos(k\pi/3)=0$, $k=3(n-\frac12)$.

Q6. Why is $r^{-k}$ discarded in a wedge $0\le r<a$ but kept in $1<r<2$?

Boundedness (regularity) at the origin is the "missing BC" in a disc or wedge. An annulus excludes $r=0$, so both are allowed and the two arcs fix both constants.
Exam habits
(1) Show the three cases for the variable with the homogeneous BCs unless told to skip them (2022: "assume $\lambda\le0$ trivial"). (2) State boundedness explicitly when you drop $r^{-k}$ or $\ln r$. (3) Finite trig sum on the boundary ⇒ match, don't integrate. Otherwise half-range formula: e.g. $B_n\,b^{k_n}=\frac2\beta\int_0^\beta g(\theta)\sin\frac{n\pi\theta}{\beta}d\theta$.

P1 [9] $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$, $0<x<2$, $0<y<1$. The plate's vertical edges are insulated, the top edge is held at $0$, and $u(x,0)=2+5\cos\pi x$.
(a) Write the four BCs. [1] (b) Separate variables and find the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions, all cases. [3] (c) Find $u$. [4] (d) Find $u(1,\frac12)$ in terms of $\cosh$. [1]

P2 [6] On $0<x<\pi$, $0<y<\pi$: $u(0,y)=0$, $u(x,0)=0$, $u(\pi,y)=2\sin3y$, $u(x,\pi)=\sin2x$. Solve.

P3 [6] Wedge $0\le r<2$, $0\le\theta\le\frac\pi3$, $u_\theta(r,0)=u_\theta(r,\frac\pi3)=0$, $u(2,\theta)=1+8\cos3\theta$. (a) Find the eigenfunctions in $\theta$ including $\lambda=0$. (b) Solve, justifying each discarded term. (c) $u$ at the origin?

P1 solution

(a) $u_x(0,y)=u_x(2,y)=0$, $u(x,1)=0$, $u(x,0)=2+5\cos\pi x$.

(b) $X''-\lambda X=0$, $Y''+\lambda Y=0$, $X'(0)=X'(2)=0$. $\lambda=k^2>0$: $X'=k(Ae^{kx}-Be^{-kx})$, BCs ⇒ $A=B=0$. $\lambda=0$: $X=A+Bx$, $B=0$: $X_0=1$. $\lambda=-k^2$: $X=A\cos kx+B\sin kx$, $X'(0)=0\Rightarrow B=0$, $\sin2k=0\Rightarrow k=\frac{n\pi}2$. Eigenfunctions $\cos\frac{n\pi x}2$, $n\ge0$.

(c) $Y_0''=0$, $Y_0(1)=0$ ⇒ $Y_0=1-y$. $Y_n''-k_n^2Y_n=0$, $Y_n(1)=0$ ⇒ $\sinh(k_n(1-y))$. $$u=A_0(1-y)+\sum_{n\ge1}A_n\frac{\sinh(\frac{n\pi}2(1-y))}{\sinh\frac{n\pi}2}\cos\frac{n\pi x}2.$$ At $y=0$ match: $A_0=2$, $n=2$ term $\cos\pi x$: $A_2=5$. $$u=2(1-y)+5\frac{\sinh(\pi(1-y))}{\sinh\pi}\cos\pi x.$$

(d) $u(1,\frac12)=1+5\cdot\frac{\sinh(\pi/2)}{2\sinh(\pi/2)\cosh(\pi/2)}\cdot(-1)=1-\dfrac{5}{2\cosh(\pi/2)}\approx0.0037$.

P2 solution

Adjacent nonzero sides ⇒ split. $v$ (top data): $\sin nx$, $\sinh ny$, match $n=2$: $v=\dfrac{\sinh2y}{\sinh2\pi}\sin2x$. $w$ (right data): $\sin ny$, $\sinh nx$, match $n=3$: $w=\dfrac{2\sinh3x}{\sinh3\pi}\sin3y$. $$u=\frac{\sinh2y}{\sinh2\pi}\sin2x+\frac{2\sinh3x}{\sinh3\pi}\sin3y.$$

P3 solution

(a) $\Theta''+\lambda\Theta=0$, $\Theta'(0)=\Theta'(\frac\pi3)=0$. $\lambda<0$: trivial. $\lambda=0$: $\Theta_0=1$. $\lambda=k^2$: $\cos k\theta$ with $\sin\frac{k\pi}3=0$, $k=3n$: $\Theta_n=\cos3n\theta$, $n\ge0$.

(b) $R_0=A+B\ln r$, $R_n=Ar^{3n}+Br^{-3n}$. $\ln r$ and $r^{-3n}$ unbounded as $r\to0$ ⇒ $B=0$. $u=a_0+\sum a_nr^{3n}\cos3n\theta$. Match at $r=2$: $a_0=1$, $8a_1=8$. $$u=1+r^3\cos3\theta.$$

(c) $u(0)=1$.

6 · Exam-level challenge (20 marks, about 30 minutes)

A thin plate occupies the quarter-annulus $1<r<2$, $0\le\theta\le\frac\pi2$. Its steady temperature satisfies $\nabla^2u=u_{rr}+\frac1ru_r+\frac1{r^2}u_{\theta\theta}=0$. The edge $\theta=0$ is held at $0$, the edge $\theta=\frac\pi2$ is insulated, the outer arc is held at $0$, and the inner arc has temperature $\sin\theta-2\sin5\theta$.

(a) Write the four boundary conditions. [2]
(b) With $u=R(r)\Theta(\theta)$, show that $r^2R''+rR'-\lambda R=0$ and $\Theta''+\lambda\Theta=0$. [3]
(c) Find all eigenvalues and eigenfunctions $\Theta_n$, working through all three cases for $\lambda$. [5]
(d) Solve for $R_n$, using the outer-arc condition. [4]
(e) Find $u(r,\theta)$. [4]
(f) Find the temperature at $r=\sqrt2$ on the insulated edge. [2]

Full marking-style solution: worked/0030-challenge-full-solution.html. Open it only after attempting every part.

Answer key (quick check)

(a) $u(r,0)=0$, $u_\theta(r,\frac\pi2)=0$, $u(2,\theta)=0$, $u(1,\theta)=\sin\theta-2\sin5\theta$. (c) $\lambda_n=(2n-1)^2$, $\Theta_n=\sin(2n-1)\theta$. (d) $R_n=\big(\frac2r\big)^{2n-1}-\big(\frac r2\big)^{2n-1}$.
(e) $u=\dfrac{4-r^2}{3r}\sin\theta-\dfrac{2(1024-r^{10})}{1023\,r^5}\sin5\theta$. (f) $\dfrac{\sqrt2}{11}$.
