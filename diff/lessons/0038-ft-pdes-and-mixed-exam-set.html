Lesson 38 · Topic 6: Fourier transforms · PDEs on ℝ + timed mixed set

PDEs on the whole line, then the Topic 6 exam set

The one win: turn a PDE on $x\in\mathbb R$ into an ODE for $\hat u(k,\cdot)$, solve it with "constants" $A(k)$, $B(k)$, and get back to $u$ by table + shift theorem, or leave it as an integral. Part 2 is a 43-mark timed set covering all of Topic 6.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: the FT question is last on the paper (masked 2026: Q7, 14 marks, parts (a)–(c)). A PDE on ℝ has appeared in most years: advection with a δ IC, "find the IVP for $\hat u$ and describe the remaining steps" for a wave, Laplace on an infinite strip with "show the imaginary part vanishes". The formula sheet gives $\mathcal F\{\partial_x^n u\}=(ik)^n\hat u$ but not the Gaussian pair, the shift theorem or the heat kernel.

Part 1 · PDEs on ℝ

1 · The 4-step recipe

  1. Transform in $x$. $\hat u(k,t)=\int u(x,t)e^{-ikx}dx$. Then $u_x\to ik\hat u$, $u_{xx}\to-k^2\hat u$, and $u_t\to\partial_t\hat u$ ($t$ is just a parameter). Transform the ICs too: $\hat u(k,0)=\hat f(k)$.
  2. Write the ODE IVP for $\hat u$. An ODE in $t$ (or $y$) with $k$ as a parameter.
  3. Solve it. The constants of integration are functions $A(k)$, $B(k)$. Fix them with the transformed ICs/BCs.
  4. Invert. By table + shift theorem + convolution if possible. Otherwise $u=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty\hat u(k,t)e^{ikx}dk$, left as an integral.

Why FT is allowed: "$u\to0$ as $x\to\pm\infty$" (or "$\hat u$ exists") kills the boundary terms in $\mathcal F\{u_x\}=ik\hat u$. Say this in one line on the exam.

Same idea: $e^{ikx}$ is the "eigenfunction" of $\partial_x^2$. A bounded interval only allows discrete $k_n$; the line allows all $k$, so the sum becomes an integral.

Two facts you must carry in (not on the sheet).

$$\mathcal F\{e^{-ax^2}\}=\sqrt{\tfrac{\pi}{a}}\,e^{-k^2/4a},\qquad \mathcal F\{f(x-s)\}=e^{-iks}\hat f(k).$$

Proof of the shift, one line: $\int f(x-s)e^{-ikx}dx\overset{z=x-s}{=}e^{-iks}\int f(z)e^{-ikz}dz$.

2 · Heat equation $u_t=\alpha u_{xx}$ on ℝ

Steps 1–3 (L30 s9–10): $\partial_t\hat u=-\alpha k^2\hat u$, so $\hat u=A(k)e^{-\alpha k^2t}$, and $\hat u(k,0)=\hat f(k)$ gives $$\boxed{\hat u(k,t)=\hat f(k)\,e^{-\alpha k^2t}}$$ Three ways the inversion goes:

(i) δ initial condition → heat kernel (L30 s11)

$\hat f=1$, so $\hat u=e^{-\alpha k^2t}$. In the Gaussian pair, $\frac1{4a}=\alpha t\Rightarrow a=\frac1{4\alpha t}$: $\mathcal F\{e^{-x^2/4\alpha t}\}=\sqrt{4\pi\alpha t}\,e^{-\alpha k^2t}$. Divide: $$G(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\alpha t}}\,e^{-x^2/4\alpha t}.$$ Area $=\hat G(0,t)=1$ for all $t$ (heat is conserved); width $\sim\sqrt{\alpha t}$.

(ii) Gaussian IC → exact answer

Worked. $u(x,0)=e^{-x^2}$. $\hat f=\sqrt\pi\,e^{-k^2/4}$, so $\hat u=\sqrt\pi\,e^{-k^2(1+4\alpha t)/4}$. Match to the pair with $\frac1{4b}=\frac{1+4\alpha t}{4}$, i.e. $b=\frac1{1+4\alpha t}$: $\hat u=\sqrt b\cdot\sqrt{\pi/b}\,e^{-k^2/4b}$, so $$u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+4\alpha t}}\;e^{-x^2/(1+4\alpha t)}.$$ Check: $t=0$ gives $e^{-x^2}$ ✓; area $\sqrt\pi$ for all $t$ ✓.

(iii) General IC → convolution with the kernel

$\hat u=\hat f\cdot\hat G$, so by the convolution theorem (on the sheet) $$u(x,t)=(G*f)(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\alpha t}}\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-(x-\xi)^2/4\alpha t}f(\xi)\,d\xi.$$ That is a complete answer when $f$ is, e.g., $\frac1{1+x^2}$ (booklet 6 Q9(e)).

C1. $u_t=u_{xx}$ on ℝ, $u(x,0)=e^{-x^2/2}$. Then $u=$…

$a=\frac12$: $\hat f=\sqrt{2\pi}e^{-k^2/2}$, $\hat u=\sqrt{2\pi}e^{-k^2(2+4t)/4}$, so $b=\frac1{2+4t}$ and $u=\sqrt{2b}\,e^{-bx^2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2t}}e^{-x^2/(2+4t)}$. Shortcut: $e^{-x^2/(4s)}$ evolves to $\sqrt{\frac{s}{s+t}}\,e^{-x^2/4(s+t)}$ (here $s=\frac12$).

3 · Wave equation $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$ on ℝ

IVP for $\hat u$ (this alone is often 2–3 marks): $$\frac{\partial^2\hat u}{\partial t^2}+c^2k^2\hat u=0,\qquad \hat u(k,0)=\hat f(k),\quad \partial_t\hat u(k,0)=\hat g(k).$$ Solve: $\hat u=A(k)\cos ckt+B(k)\sin ckt$, with $A=\hat f$, $ckB=\hat g$: $$\hat u=\hat f(k)\cos ckt+\hat g(k)\,\frac{\sin ckt}{ck}.$$

Recovering d'Alembert ($g=0$). $\hat f\cos ckt=\frac12\hat f\,e^{-ik(ct)}+\frac12\hat f\,e^{-ik(-ct)}$. By the shift theorem, each term is a translate: $$u(x,t)=\tfrac12\big[f(x-ct)+f(x+ct)\big].$$ The bump splits into two half-height copies moving apart at speed $c$. Corners are kept (contrast heat).

Worked: velocity δ. $u(x,0)=0$, $u_t(x,0)=\delta(x)$. Then $\hat u=\frac{\sin ckt}{ck}$. The box pair $H_{-a}-H_a\leftrightarrow\frac{2\sin ak}{k}$ with $a=ct$ gives $$u=\frac1{2c}\big[H(x+ct)-H(x-ct)\big]:\ \text{height }\tfrac1{2c}\text{ on }|x|\lt ct,\ 0\text{ outside}.$$ (Same as d'Alembert's $\frac1{2c}\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}g$.)

"Describe in words the remaining steps" (model answer, 2 marks):

  1. Solve the ODE in $t$: $\hat u=A(k)\cos ckt+B(k)\sin ckt$.
  2. Use the two transformed ICs to fix $A(k)$ and $B(k)$.
  3. Write $\cos ckt$, $\sin ckt$ as complex exponentials, and invert term by term with the shift theorem (or the table). If no pair fits, leave $u=\frac1{2\pi}\int\hat u\,e^{ikx}dk$.

C2. $u_{tt}=9u_{xx}$, $u(x,0)=0$, $u_t(x,0)=e^{-|x|}$. The IVP for $\hat u$ is…

$u_{xx}\to(ik)^2\hat u=-k^2\hat u$, so $\hat u_{tt}=-9k^2\hat u$. And $e^{-a|x|}\leftrightarrow\frac{2a}{a^2+k^2}$ with $a=1$.

4 · Advection (transport): first order in $t$

$u_t+cu_x=0$: $\partial_t\hat u=-ick\hat u\Rightarrow\hat u=\hat f(k)e^{-ikct}\Rightarrow u=f(x-ct)$ by the shift theorem (moves right).
$u_t=cu_x$: $\hat u=\hat f\,e^{+ikct}\Rightarrow u=f(x+ct)$ (moves left). With $f=\delta$: $u=\delta(x+ct)$.

Add a diffusion term and the two effects multiply in $k$-space: drift ($e^{-ikct}$) times spreading ($e^{-\alpha k^2t}$). That combination is Q4 of the set below.

C3. $u_t+3u_x=0$ on ℝ, $u(x,0)=e^{-x^2}$. Then $u=$…

$\hat u=\hat f e^{-3ikt}=\hat f e^{-ik(3t)}\leftrightarrow f(x-3t)$. Pure transport: no spreading, no prefactor. Check: $u_t=6(x-3t)u$, $u_x=-2(x-3t)u$, so $u_t+3u_x=0$ ✓.

Below: the box $f=H_{-1}-H_1$ under heat ($\alpha=1$), wave ($c=1$) and advection ($c=1$). Heat rounds the corners instantly and spreads; wave splits and keeps corners; advection just slides.

5 · Laplace's equation on an infinite strip

Worked. $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$, $x\in\mathbb R$, $0\lt y\lt 2$, $u(x,0)=e^{-|x|}$, $u(x,2)=0$, $u\to0$ as $|x|\to\infty$.

  1. Transform in $x$ ($y$ is the parameter): $-k^2\hat u+\hat u_{yy}=0$, i.e. $\hat u_{yy}=k^2\hat u$.
  2. General solution, two unknown functions: $\hat u=A(k)\cosh ky+B(k)\sinh ky$. Smarter basis for a zero at $y=2$: $\hat u=C(k)\sinh k(2-y)+D(k)\sinh ky$.
  3. BCs: $\hat u(k,2)=0\Rightarrow D\sinh2k=0\Rightarrow D=0$. $\hat u(k,0)=\frac{2}{1+k^2}\Rightarrow C\sinh2k=\frac2{1+k^2}$: $$\hat u(k,y)=\frac{2}{1+k^2}\,\frac{\sinh k(2-y)}{\sinh 2k}\qquad(\text{at }k=0\text{: limit }1-\tfrac y2).$$
  4. Invert as an integral: $u=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{2\sinh k(2-y)}{(1+k^2)\sinh2k}\,e^{ikx}dk.$

"Show $u$ is real": $\hat u(k,y)$ is real and even in $k$ (ratio of two odd sinh's is even). Write $e^{ikx}=\cos kx+i\sin kx$: $\hat u\sin kx$ is odd in $k$, so its integral over ℝ is $0$. Hence $$u(x,y)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{2\sinh k(2-y)}{(1+k^2)\sinh 2k}\cos kx\,dk\in\mathbb R.$$

General strip $0\lt y\lt b$ with $u(x,0)=f$, $u(x,b)=g$: $\hat u=\dfrac{\hat f\sinh k(b-y)+\hat g\sinh ky}{\sinh kb}$. On the half-plane $y>0$ (bounded $u$) only $e^{-|k|y}$ survives instead.

C4. Laplace on the half-plane $y>0$, $u(x,0)=f(x)$, $u$ bounded as $y\to\infty$. After the FT in $x$, $\hat u(k,y)=$…

$\hat u=Ae^{ky}+Be^{-ky}$. For $k>0$ drop $e^{ky}$; for $k\lt0$ drop $e^{-ky}$. Both cases: $e^{-|k|y}$. (c) blows up for $k\lt 0$; (d) is heat.

6 · Exam-style practice

P1 [6]. $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$ on $x\in\mathbb R$, $0\lt y\lt1$, with $u_y(x,0)=0$ (insulated edge), $u(x,1)=e^{-2|x|}$, $u\to0$ as $|x|\to\infty$. (a) Find the ODE and general solution for $\hat u(k,y)$, with two unknown functions [2]. (b) Fix them [2]. (c) Write $u$ as a real integral, justifying that it is real [2].

P1 solution

(a) $\hat u_{yy}=k^2\hat u$, $\hat u=A(k)\cosh ky+B(k)\sinh ky$.

(b) $\hat u_y(k,0)=kB=0\Rightarrow B=0$ (for $k\ne0$; $k=0$ is measure zero). $\hat u(k,1)=A\cosh k=\mathcal F\{e^{-2|x|}\}=\frac{4}{4+k^2}$. So $\hat u=\frac{4\cosh ky}{(4+k^2)\cosh k}$.

(c) $u=\frac1{2\pi}\int\hat u\,e^{ikx}dk$. $\hat u$ is real and even in $k$, so the $i\sin kx$ part is odd and integrates to $0$: $$u(x,y)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{4\cosh ky}{(4+k^2)\cosh k}\cos kx\,dk.$$ (Numerically checked: $u$ is harmonic, $u_y(x,0)=0$, and $u(x,1^-)\to e^{-2|x|}$.)

P2 [5]. $u_{tt}=4u_{xx}$ on ℝ, $u(x,0)=e^{-x^2}$, $u_t(x,0)=0$, $u\to0$ as $|x|\to\infty$. (a) Write down the IVP for $\hat u(k,t)$ [2]. (b) Describe in words the remaining steps [1]. (c) Carry them out [2].

P2 solution

(a) $\hat u_{tt}+4k^2\hat u=0$, $\hat u(k,0)=\sqrt\pi e^{-k^2/4}$, $\hat u_t(k,0)=0$.

(b) Solve the constant-coefficient ODE in $t$ ($A\cos2kt+B\sin2kt$), fix $A$, $B$ from the two ICs, write cos as exponentials and invert with the shift theorem.

(c) $\hat u=\sqrt\pi e^{-k^2/4}\cos2kt=\frac12\hat f\,(e^{-ik(2t)}+e^{ik(2t)})$, so $u=\frac12\big[e^{-(x-2t)^2}+e^{-(x+2t)^2}\big]$.

Traps on PDE-on-ℝ questions
(1) Writing $\hat u=Ae^{-\alpha k^2t}$ with a constant $A$: it is $A(k)$. (2) $\mathcal F\{u_{xx}\}=+k^2\hat u$: it is $(ik)^2=-k^2$. (3) Shift direction: $e^{-iks}\hat f\leftrightarrow f(x-s)$; $u_t=cu_x$ moves left. (4) Forgetting to transform the ICs (or forgetting the second one for the wave equation). (5) "Show it's real": state which part is odd in $k$ and why.

Question 1 [10 marks]

Let $f(x)=3H_{-1}(x)-2H_{2}(x)-H_{4}(x)$, where $H_a(x)=H(x-a)$.

(a) Sketch $f$, and find its Fourier transform $\hat f(k)$ from the definition. Give $\hat f(0)$. [4]

(b) $\mathcal F^{-1}\{\hat f\}$ returns a function equal to $f$ except possibly at finitely many points. Give its value at $x=-1$, $x=2$ and $x=4$. [2]

(c) Find the inverse Fourier transform of $h(k)=4\sin(3k)\cos(k)$. [3]

(d) Write $f'(x)$ in terms of delta functions, and check that $\mathcal F\{f'\}=ik\hat f$ holds for your answer to (a). [1]

Q1 marking scheme

(a) [1] $f=3$ on $(-1,2)$, $1$ on $(2,4)$, $0$ elsewhere (sketch: two steps down). [1] $\hat f=\int_{-1}^{2}3e^{-ikx}dx+\int_2^4e^{-ikx}dx$. [1] $=\frac{3}{ik}(e^{ik}-e^{-2ik})+\frac1{ik}(e^{-2ik}-e^{-4ik})$, [1] $$\hat f(k)=\frac{3e^{ik}-2e^{-2ik}-e^{-4ik}}{ik},\ k\ne0;\qquad \hat f(0)=\int f=3\cdot3+1\cdot2=11.$$

(b) [2] Midpoints of the jumps: $x=-1$: $\frac{0+3}2=\frac32$; $x=2$: $\frac{3+1}{2}=2$; $x=4$: $\frac{1+0}{2}=\frac12$. (1 mark for the midpoint rule, 1 for all three values.)

(c) [1] $4\sin3k\cos k=2\sin4k+2\sin2k$. [1] $\sin ak=\frac{e^{iak}-e^{-iak}}{2i}$, and $\mathcal F\{\delta_a\}=e^{-iak}$, so $\mathcal F^{-1}\{\sin ak\}=\frac{\delta_{-a}-\delta_a}{2i}=\frac i2(\delta_a-\delta_{-a})$. [1] $$\mathcal F^{-1}\{h\}=i\big[\delta(x-4)-\delta(x+4)+\delta(x-2)-\delta(x+2)\big].$$

(d) [1] $f'=3\delta_{-1}-2\delta_2-\delta_4$, whose FT is $3e^{ik}-2e^{-2ik}-e^{-4ik}=ik\hat f$ ✓.

Question 2 [11 marks]

(a) Using the definition of convolution on the formula sheet, prove that $\mathcal F\{f*g\}=\hat f\,\hat g$. You may assume $f$, $g$ are absolutely integrable, so the order of integration can be swapped. [4]

(b) Let $f(x)=e^{-x}H(x)$. Find $\hat f(k)$ from the definition. [2]

(c) Compute $(f*f)(x)$ directly for all $x\in\mathbb R$. [3]

(d) Hence find $\mathcal F\{x\,e^{-x}H(x)\}$, and confirm it using the formula-sheet rule for $\mathcal F\{x f(x)\}$. [2]

Q2 marking scheme

(a) [1] $\mathcal F\{f*g\}=\int_{-\infty}^\infty\Big(\int_{-\infty}^\infty f(x-\xi)g(\xi)\,d\xi\Big)e^{-ikx}dx$. [1] Swap: $=\int g(\xi)\Big(\int f(x-\xi)e^{-ikx}dx\Big)d\xi$. [1] Inner: $s=x-\xi$, $e^{-ikx}=e^{-iks}e^{-ik\xi}$, giving $e^{-ik\xi}\hat f(k)$. [1] So $=\hat f(k)\int g(\xi)e^{-ik\xi}d\xi=\hat f(k)\hat g(k)$. ∎

(b) [1] $\hat f=\int_0^\infty e^{-(1+ik)x}dx=\Big[\frac{-e^{-(1+ik)x}}{1+ik}\Big]_0^\infty$. [1] $|e^{-(1+ik)x}|=e^{-x}\to0$, so $\hat f=\frac1{1+ik}$.

(c) [1] $(f*f)(x)=\int e^{-(x-\xi)}H(x-\xi)\,e^{-\xi}H(\xi)\,d\xi$; integrand is non-zero only for $0\lt\xi\lt x$. [1] If $x\le0$ that interval is empty: $0$. [1] If $x>0$: $e^{-x}\int_0^xd\xi=xe^{-x}$. So $f*f=xe^{-x}H(x)$.

(d) [1] Convolution theorem: $\mathcal F\{xe^{-x}H(x)\}=\hat f^2=\frac1{(1+ik)^2}$. [1] Check: $i\frac{d}{dk}(1+ik)^{-1}=i\cdot(-i)(1+ik)^{-2}=\frac{1}{(1+ik)^2}$ ✓.

Question 3 [8 marks]

Consider $\;y''-2y'-xy=0,\quad x\in\mathbb R.$

(a) Show that the Fourier transform $\hat y(k)$ satisfies $\hat y'(k)=(ik^2-2k)\,\hat y(k)$. [2]

(b) Solve for $\hat y$ using an integrating factor. [3]

(c) Hence find the most general solution of the ODE that has a Fourier transform, as a definite integral. Show that, for real $y$, this can be written as an integral over $0\lt k\lt\infty$ of a real function. [2]

(d) The ODE is second order but your answer has one arbitrary constant. Explain. [1]

Q3 marking scheme

(a) [1] $\mathcal F\{y''\}=-k^2\hat y$, $\mathcal F\{y'\}=ik\hat y$, $\mathcal F\{xy\}=i\hat y'$. [1] $-k^2\hat y-2ik\hat y-i\hat y'=0\Rightarrow\hat y'=\frac{-k^2-2ik}{i}\hat y=(ik^2-2k)\hat y$.

(b) [1] $\hat y'-(ik^2-2k)\hat y=0$; IF $=e^{-\int(ik^2-2k)dk}=e^{-ik^3/3+k^2}$. [1] $\frac{d}{dk}\big(\hat y\,e^{-ik^3/3+k^2}\big)=0$. [1] $\hat y=A\,e^{ik^3/3-k^2}$.

(c) [1] $y(x)=\frac{A}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-k^2}e^{i(k^3/3+kx)}dk$. [1] $e^{-k^2}\sin(k^3/3+kx)$ is odd in $k$, so it integrates to $0$; $e^{-k^2}\cos(\cdot)$ is even. With $A$ real: $$y(x)=\frac{A}{\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-k^2}\cos\!\Big(\frac{k^3}3+kx\Big)dk.$$

(d) [1] The second independent solution grows as $x\to\infty$ (not absolutely integrable), so it has no FT; the method only finds solutions that have one. (Aside, not required: $y\propto e^{x}\,\mathrm{Ai}(x+1)$; the lost one is $e^x\mathrm{Bi}(x+1)$.)

Question 4 [14 marks]

A dye is released at $x=0$ into a long channel of moving water. Its concentration $u(x,t)$ satisfies $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+2\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2},\qquad x\in\mathbb R,\ t>0,$$ with $u(x,0)=\delta(x)$ and $u,\ u_x\to0$ as $x\to\pm\infty$. You may use $\mathcal F\{e^{-ax^2}\}=\sqrt{\pi/a}\,e^{-k^2/4a}$ for $a>0$.

(a) Starting from the definition, show that $\mathcal F\{\partial u/\partial x\}=ik\,\hat u(k,t)$, stating where the conditions at $\pm\infty$ are used. [3]

(b) Write down the IVP satisfied by $\hat u(k,t)$, and solve it. [3]

(c) State and prove the shift theorem $\mathcal F\{g(x-s)\}=e^{-iks}\hat g(k)$, and hence find $u(x,t)$. [4]

(d) Show that $\int_{-\infty}^\infty u\,dx=1$ for all $t>0$, and describe in words how the dye moves. [2]

(e) If instead $u(x,0)=f(x)$, write $u(x,t)$ as a single integral involving $f$. [2]

Q4 marking scheme

(a) [1] $\mathcal F\{u_x\}=\int u_xe^{-ikx}dx=\big[u\,e^{-ikx}\big]_{-\infty}^{\infty}+ik\int ue^{-ikx}dx$ (by parts). [1] $|ue^{-ikx}|=|u|\to0$ at $\pm\infty$, so the boundary term vanishes. [1] Hence $ik\hat u$.

(b) [1] $\partial_t\hat u+2ik\hat u=-k^2\hat u$, $\hat u(k,0)=\mathcal F\{\delta\}=1$. [1] $\hat u=A(k)e^{-(k^2+2ik)t}$. [1] $A=1$: $\hat u=e^{-k^2t}e^{-2ikt}$.

(c) [1] $\int g(x-s)e^{-ikx}dx\overset{z=x-s}=\int g(z)e^{-ik(z+s)}dz=e^{-iks}\hat g(k)$. [1] Given pair with $a=\frac1{4t}$: $\mathcal F\{e^{-x^2/4t}\}=\sqrt{4\pi t}\,e^{-k^2t}$, so $e^{-k^2t}=\mathcal F\{G\}$, $G(x,t)=\frac{e^{-x^2/4t}}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}$. [1] $\hat u=e^{-ik(2t)}\hat G$, a shift by $s=2t$. [1] $$u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}\,e^{-(x-2t)^2/4t}.$$

(d) [1] $\int u\,dx=\hat u(0,t)=1$ (or substitute in the Gaussian integral). [1] A Gaussian pulse whose centre moves right at speed $2$ ($x=2t$) while spreading (width $\propto\sqrt t$, peak $\frac1{\sqrt{4\pi t}}$ falling); total dye conserved.

(e) [1] $\hat u=\hat f(k)\cdot e^{-k^2t-2ikt}=\hat f\cdot\hat h$ with $h(x,t)=G(x-2t,t)$. [1] Convolution theorem: $$u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-(x-\xi-2t)^2/4t}f(\xi)\,d\xi.$$

Where marks leak in this set
(1) Q1(a): $\int_a^be^{-ikx}dx=\frac{e^{-ika}-e^{-ikb}}{ik}$; flipping the order flips the sign. (2) Q1(b): giving $f$'s value instead of the midpoint. (3) Q1(c): $\sin ak\leftrightarrow$ needs the $\frac1{2i}$; $\frac1{2i}=-\frac i2$. (4) Q2(c): not treating $x\le0$ separately. (5) Q3(a): $\mathcal F\{xy\}=i\hat y'$, not $-i\hat y'$. (6) Q4(b): writing a constant $A$, or forgetting $\hat u(k,0)=1$. (7) Q4(c): shift sign; $e^{-2ikt}$ means $x-2t$ (moves right, with the flow).

Retrieval: write the three $\hat u$'s (heat, wave, advection) and their inverses

Heat: $\hat u=\hat fe^{-\alpha k^2t}$, $u=G*f$, $G=\frac{e^{-x^2/4\alpha t}}{\sqrt{4\pi\alpha t}}$. Wave: $\hat u=\hat f\cos ckt+\hat g\frac{\sin ckt}{ck}$; $g=0$ gives $\frac12[f(x-ct)+f(x+ct)]$. Advection $u_t+cu_x=0$: $\hat u=\hat fe^{-ikct}$, $u=f(x-ct)$.
