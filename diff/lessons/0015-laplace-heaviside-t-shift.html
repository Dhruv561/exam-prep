Lesson 15 · Topic 3: Laplace transforms

Switches and windows: Heaviside functions and the t-shift

The one win: given any forcing that switches on or off (piecewise, ramp, window), write it with Heavisides, transform it without falling into the shift trap, solve the IVP, and hand back a clean piecewise answer.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: Laplace is about 12 marks, and a switched or windowed forcing shows up in most papers (a damped spring forced on a window, $\delta$ plus Heaviside mixed). The formula sheet gives the t-shift $\mathcal L\{f(t-a)H(t-a)\}=e^{-as}F(s)$ but not $\mathcal L\{H_a\}$, and not what to do when the function isn't already written in terms of $t-a$. That gap is where marks are lost.

0 · Warm-up from Lessons 13 and 14

W1. $\mathcal L\{e^{-3t}\sin 2t\}$ (s-shift of the sheet's $\sin\omega t$) is…

$\mathcal L\{e^{at}f\}=F(s-a)$ with $a=-3$ gives $s\to s+3$ in $\dfrac{2}{s^2+4}$.

W2. $\mathcal L^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{s+1}{s^2+2s+5}\right\}$ is…

Complete the square: $s^2+2s+5=(s+1)^2+4$. The numerator is exactly $s+1$, so it is $\dfrac{s-a}{(s-a)^2+\omega^2}$ with $a=-1$, $\omega=2$.

1 · Heaviside functions: switches and windows

$H_a(t)=H(t-a)$ is $0$ for $t<a$ and $1$ for $t>a$. Think "a switch flipped at $t=a$". A window is two switches: $H_a-H_b$ is $1$ on $[a,b)$ and $0$ elsewhere (for $a<b$).

Piecewise to Heaviside: add the change at each switch.
If $f=f_1$ on $[0,a)$, $f_2$ on $[a,b)$, $f_3$ on $[b,\infty)$, then $$f=f_1+(f_2-f_1)\,H_a+(f_3-f_2)\,H_b.$$ Every function is multiplied by its own $H$, not shifted yet.

Q1. $f=1$ for $0\le t<2$, $f=4$ for $2\le t<5$, $f=0$ for $t\ge5$. Then $f=$…

At $t=2$ the value changes by $4-1=+3$. At $t=5$ it changes by $0-4=-4$. Check $t\ge5$: $1+3-4=0$ ✓. Option (d) fails at $t<2$ (gives 4).

Worked. $g=2$ on $[0,1)$, $g=t$ on $[1,3)$, $g=0$ on $[3,\infty)$. Changes: at $1$: $t-2$; at $3$: $0-t=-t$. So $g=2+(t-2)H_1-t\,H_3$.

2 · Transforming: the t-shift and the trap

From the sheet: $\mathcal L\{f(t-a)H(t-a)\}=e^{-as}F(s)$. With $f=1$:

$$\mathcal L\{H_a\}=\frac{e^{-as}}{s}\quad(\text{derive it; it is not on the sheet}).$$
The trap
The theorem needs the function of $(t-a)$, not of $t$. So $\mathcal L\{t\,H_3\}\ne e^{-3s}/s^2$.
Fix: write $g(t)H_a(t)$ as $h(t-a)H_a(t)$ where $h(u)=g(u+a)$ (substitute $t=u+a$). Then $\mathcal L=e^{-as}\mathcal L\{h\}$.

$\mathcal L\{t^2H_2\}$: $h(u)=(u+2)^2=u^2+4u+4$, so $\;e^{-2s}\!\left(\dfrac2{s^3}+\dfrac4{s^2}+\dfrac4s\right)$.
$\mathcal L\{\cos t\;H_\pi\}$: $h(u)=\cos(u+\pi)=-\cos u$, so $\;-e^{-\pi s}\dfrac{s}{s^2+1}$.
$\mathcal L\{e^{t}H_1\}$: $h(u)=e^{u+1}=e\cdot e^{u}$, so $\;e\,e^{-s}\dfrac1{s-1}=\dfrac{e^{1-s}}{s-1}$.

Q2. $\mathcal L\{t\,H_3(t)\}=$…

$t=u+3$, so $h(u)=u+3$ and $\mathcal L\{h\}=\frac1{s^2}+\frac3s$.

Worked: transform of $g$ from §1. $g=2+(t-2)H_1-tH_3$. For $(t-2)H_1$: $h(u)=u+1-2=u-1$. For $tH_3$: $h(u)=u+3$. $$G(s)=\frac2s+e^{-s}\!\left(\frac1{s^2}-\frac1s\right)-e^{-3s}\!\left(\frac1{s^2}+\frac3s\right).$$

3 · Inverting: strip, invert, shift, attach

  1. Split into terms $e^{-as}F(s)$ (a term with no exponential is just $a=0$).
  2. Invert each $F$ with partial fractions: get $f(t)$.
  3. Replace every $t$ in $f$ by $t-a$, and multiply by $H_a(t)$.

Q3. $\mathcal L^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{e^{-2s}}{s^2+9}\right\}=$…

$f(t)=\frac13\sin3t$. Replace $t\to t-2$: $\frac13\sin3(t-2)=\frac13\sin(3t-6)$, attached to $H_2$. The shift must hit every $t$ inside, and the 3 multiplies the 2.

Worked. $F=\dfrac{e^{-s}-e^{-4s}}{s(s+2)}$. Here $\dfrac1{s(s+2)}=\dfrac12\!\left(\dfrac1s-\dfrac1{s+2}\right)$, so $f(t)=\dfrac{1-e^{-2t}}2$ and $$\mathcal L^{-1}\{F\}=f(t-1)H_1-f(t-4)H_4.$$

As a piecewise answer: $0$ for $t<1$; $\tfrac12\big(1-e^{-2(t-1)}\big)$ for $1\le t<4$; and for $t\ge4$ the two combine to $\tfrac12\big(e^{-2(t-4)}-e^{-2(t-1)}\big)$.

4 · Solving an IVP with a window, and reading the answer

Example. $y''+3y'+2y=2\,(H_1-H_3)$, $\;y(0)=1,\;y'(0)=0$. In words: a push of size 2 from $t=1$ to $t=3$, then released, starting displaced by 1 at rest.

Transform and solve for $Y$
$$(s^2+3s+2)Y-s-3=\frac{2(e^{-s}-e^{-3s})}{s}\;\Rightarrow\; Y=\frac{s+3}{(s+1)(s+2)}+\frac{2\,(e^{-s}-e^{-3s})}{s(s+1)(s+2)}.$$
Partial fractions
$$\frac{s+3}{(s+1)(s+2)}=\frac2{s+1}-\frac1{s+2},\qquad \frac{2}{s(s+1)(s+2)}=\frac1s-\frac2{s+1}+\frac1{s+2}.$$ So the second inverts to $G(t)=1-2e^{-t}+e^{-2t}$.
Invert, then write piecewise
$$y=2e^{-t}-e^{-2t}+G(t-1)H_1(t)-G(t-3)H_3(t).$$

$0\le t<1$: $y=2e^{-t}-e^{-2t}$.
$1\le t<3$: add $G(t-1)$, so $y=1-2e^{-(t-1)}+e^{-2(t-1)}+2e^{-t}-e^{-2t}$.
$t\ge3$: subtract $G(t-3)$; the constants cancel, leaving $y=2e^{-t}-e^{-2t}-2e^{-(t-1)}+e^{-2(t-1)}+2e^{-(t-3)}-e^{-2(t-3)}$.

Sanity checks (always, they take 20 seconds).

  • $y(0),y'(0)$ from the first piece match the data.
  • For an $n$th-order ODE with a switching forcing, $y,\dots,y^{(n-1)}$ are continuous at each switch. Only $y^{(n)}$ jumps. (Lecture 13, slide 5.) Here the new piece starts as $G(t-1)$, which has value $0$ and slope $0$ at its start.
  • Long-run: with forcing $2$ on, $2y\approx2$, so $y\to1$ in the window. After release $y\to0$ ✓.

Q4. $y''+4y'+3y=H_3(t)$, $y(0)=y'(0)=0$. At $t=3$…

$y''=H_3-4y'-3y$. The forcing jumps by $1$, and $y,y'$ are continuous, so $y''$ jumps by exactly $1$ and nothing else does.

Small remark: at a jump of the original function, $\mathcal L^{-1}$ returns the midpoint (Lecture 11). That is why the value of $H$ at the switch never matters for the transform.

5 · Practice (a notch above the notes)

Do each fully on paper, then open the solution.

P1. Find $\mathcal L\{f\}$ for $\;f(t)=e^{2t}$ on $[0,1)$ and $f(t)=3$ for $t\ge1$.

P2. Find $\mathcal L^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{(s+3)\,e^{-2s}}{s^2+2s+5}\right\}$ and write it with no Heavisides.

P3. Solve $\;y''+9y=9\,(1-H_\pi(t))$, $\;y(0)=0,\;y'(0)=3$, as a piecewise function, and check $y,y'$ are continuous at $t=\pi$.

P1 solution

$f=e^{2t}+(3-e^{2t})H_1$. For $e^{2t}H_1$: $h(u)=e^{2(u+1)}=e^2e^{2u}$.

$$F=\frac1{s-2}+e^{-s}\!\left(\frac3s-\frac{e^2}{s-2}\right).$$
P2 solution

$\dfrac{s+3}{(s+1)^2+4}=\dfrac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+4}+\dfrac{2}{(s+1)^2+4}\to f(t)=e^{-t}(\cos2t+\sin2t)$. Then

$$\mathcal L^{-1}=e^{-(t-2)}\big[\cos2(t-2)+\sin2(t-2)\big]H_2(t)=\begin{cases}0,&t<2\\ e^{-(t-2)}\big[\cos(2t-4)+\sin(2t-4)\big],&t\ge2.\end{cases}$$
P3 solution

$(s^2+9)Y-3=\dfrac9s(1-e^{-\pi s})$, so $Y=\dfrac3{s^2+9}+\Big(\dfrac1s-\dfrac{s}{s^2+9}\Big)(1-e^{-\pi s})$, since $\dfrac9{s(s^2+9)}=\dfrac1s-\dfrac s{s^2+9}$. With $G=1-\cos3t$,

$$y=\sin3t+G(t)-G(t-\pi)H_\pi(t).$$

Trap: $\cos3(t-\pi)=\cos(3t-3\pi)=-\cos3t$. So

$$y=\begin{cases}\sin3t+1-\cos3t,&0\le t<\pi\\ \sin3t-2\cos3t,&t\ge\pi.\end{cases}$$

At $\pi$: $y=2$ from both sides; $y'=3\cos3t+3\sin3t\to-3$ and $3\cos3t+6\sin3t\to-3$ ✓.

6 · Exam-level challenge (12 marks, about 18 minutes)

A mass-spring-damper obeys $\;y''+3y'+2y=f(t),\;\; y(0)=0,\; y'(0)=1,$ where the load ramps up at unit rate until $t=2$ and is then held constant: $f(t)=t$ for $0\le t<2$, $\;f(t)=2$ for $t\ge2$.

(a) Write $f$ using one Heaviside function and find $F(s)$. [2]
(b) Find $Y(s)$, and decompose every part into partial fractions. [4]
(c) Find $y(t)$ with a Heaviside, then as an explicit piecewise function for $0\le t<2$ and $t\ge2$. [3]
(d) Show $y,y',y''$ are all continuous at $t=2$, and say which derivative of $y$ first jumps there, and by how much. Find $\lim_{t\to\infty}y$ and interpret it. [3]

Full line-by-line marking-style solution: worked/0015-challenge-full-solution.html. Open it only after attempting all four parts.

Answer key (for a quick check)

(a) $f=t-(t-2)H_2$, $F=\dfrac{1-e^{-2s}}{s^2}$.
(b) $Y=\dfrac1{(s+1)(s+2)}+\dfrac{1-e^{-2s}}{s^2(s+1)(s+2)}$, with $\dfrac1{s^2(s+1)(s+2)}=-\dfrac3{4s}+\dfrac1{2s^2}+\dfrac1{s+1}-\dfrac1{4(s+2)}$.
(c) $y=e^{-t}-e^{-2t}+h(t)-h(t-2)H_2$, $h(t)=-\tfrac34+\tfrac t2+e^{-t}-\tfrac14e^{-2t}$. Piecewise: $-\tfrac34+\tfrac t2+2e^{-t}-\tfrac54e^{-2t}$ on $[0,2)$; $1+(2-e^2)e^{-t}+\tfrac{e^4-5}4e^{-2t}$ for $t\ge2$.
(d) $y'''$ jumps by $-1$; $y\to1$.
