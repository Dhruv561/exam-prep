Lesson 19 · Topic 3: Laplace · Timed mixed set

Six mixed questions, no labels

No new teaching. The exam doesn't say "this is a delta question", so this set shuffles lessons 13–18 and makes you pick the tool. Six new questions, 50 marks, each with a hidden marking scheme.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: Laplace is about 12 of the 90 marks, usually one question built from 3 to 4 of these tools at once. Past papers pair a delta or window forcing with a repeated-root denominator, add a two-point condition, or hide a variable-coefficient ODE behind a theory part. Practise recognising which one you're facing.

1 · Warm-up (5 min, spaced retrieval)

W1. $\mathcal L\{t\,e^{2t}\}$ is… (not on the formula sheet)

Table: $\mathcal L\{t\} = 1/s^2$. The s-shift replaces $s$ by $s-2$ (Lesson 13).

W2. $\mathcal L\{t\,H(t-2)\}$ is… (the key trap)

The formula needs $f(t-2)$. Write $t = (t-2)+2$, so the function is $(t-2)+2$ shifted: $e^{-2s}(1/s^2 + 2/s)$ (Lesson 15).

W3. $y'' + 4y = 5\,\delta(t-1)$ at rest. At $t=1$…

Integrate the ODE across $t=1$: $[y']$ jumps by the strength 5, and $y$ is continuous because $y'$ stays finite (Lesson 16).

W4. If $\mathcal L\{g\} = G(s)$, then $\mathcal L\!\left\{\displaystyle\int_0^t g(u)\,du\right\}$ is…

The integral is $1 * g$, so the convolution theorem gives $\frac1s\,G(s)$ (Lesson 17).

W5. Which cannot be a Laplace transform of any function in this course?

We need $F(s)\to0$ as $s\to\infty$. Option (c) tends to $1$ (Lesson 18). The others tend to $0$.

2 · The set (≈ 100 min, 50 marks)

Exam pace is 2 min per mark. Do Q1–Q3 in one sitting (24 marks, ≈ 48 min), the rest in a second sitting. Paper only, formula sheet only: the 2026 sheet. It has the table (1, $t^{n-1}/(n-1)!$, $e^{at}$, $\sin\omega t$, $\cos\omega t$, $\sinh$, $\cosh$, $\delta$), the two shifts, the derivative rule and convolution. It doesn't have $\mathcal L\{tf\}$, $\mathcal L\{\int f\}$ or $H$'s transform. Open each marking scheme only after you've written the whole question.

Question 1 [7 marks]

(a) Find $\mathcal L\{e^{t}\sin t\cos t\}$. [2]

(b) Find $\mathcal L^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{3}{s^2(s-1)}\right\}$. [2]

(c) Find $\mathcal L^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{s\,e^{-s}}{s^2+4s+13}\right\}$, and write the answer without Heaviside functions. [3]

Q1 marking scheme

(a) [1] $\sin t\cos t = \tfrac12\sin 2t$. [1] table gives $\tfrac12\cdot\frac{2}{s^2+4}$, then $s\to s-1$: $$\frac{1}{(s-1)^2+4}.$$

(b) [1] $\frac{3}{s^2(s-1)} = \frac3{s-1} - \frac3s - \frac3{s^2}$. [1] $\;3e^t - 3 - 3t$.

(c) [1] Complete the square: $\frac{s}{(s+2)^2+9} = \frac{s+2}{(s+2)^2+9} - \frac23\cdot\frac{3}{(s+2)^2+9}$. [1] Inverse of the bracket: $h(t) = e^{-2t}\big(\cos3t - \tfrac23\sin3t\big)$. [1] $e^{-s}$ means shift by 1: $$f(t) = \begin{cases}0 & 0\le t<1,\\ e^{-2(t-1)}\big(\cos3(t-1) - \tfrac23\sin3(t-1)\big) & t\ge1.\end{cases}$$ Common loss: forgetting the $-\tfrac23\sin$ term (numerator $s$, not $s+2$).

Question 2 [8 marks]

Solve $\;y'' + 4y' + 4y = 4\big[H(t-1) - H(t-2)\big],\quad y(0)=0,\; y'(0)=1.$

(a) Find $Y(s)$. [2]

(b) Find $y(t)$ and write it piecewise on $[0,1)$, $[1,2)$ and $[2,\infty)$. [5]

(c) In words, describe the forcing and the initial conditions as a physical situation. What happens as $t\to\infty$? [1]

Q2 marking scheme

(a) [1] transform with ICs: $(s^2Y - 1) + 4sY + 4Y = \frac{4(e^{-s}-e^{-2s})}{s}$. [1] $Y = \dfrac{1}{(s+2)^2} + \dfrac{4(e^{-s}-e^{-2s})}{s(s+2)^2}$.

(b) [1] $\frac{4}{s(s+2)^2} = \frac1s - \frac1{s+2} - \frac2{(s+2)^2}$ (repeated root: both powers needed). [1] $G(t) = 1 - e^{-2t} - 2te^{-2t}$. [1] $y = te^{-2t} + G(t-1)H(t-1) - G(t-2)H(t-2)$. [2] piecewise (1 each for the correct middle and last lines): $$y = \begin{cases} te^{-2t} & 0\le t<1,\\ 1 + e^{-2t}\big[t + e^{2}(1-2t)\big] & 1\le t<2,\\ e^{-2t}\big[t - e^{2}(2t-1) + e^{4}(2t-3)\big] & t\ge2.\end{cases}$$ Check: $y$ and $y'$ are continuous at $t=1$ and $t=2$ (forcing has jumps, so only $y''$ does).

(c) [1] Critically damped oscillator starting at equilibrium with unit velocity, pushed with constant force 4 only for $1\le t<2$. All terms decay like $e^{-2t}\cdot$polynomial in the last piece, so $y\to0$.

Question 3 [9 marks]

A unit mass on an undamped spring with $k=4$ starts at the equilibrium position $y(0)=0$ with an unknown initial velocity. At $t=\pi/2$ it is struck with an impulse of strength 4. Its position at $t=3\pi/4$ is measured as $y(3\pi/4)=3$.

(a) Write the IVP, with the unknown velocity $y'(0)=b$. [1]
(b) Use Laplace transforms to find $y(t)$ in terms of $b$. [3]
(c) Find $b$ and give $y(t)$ piecewise. [2]
(d) Check $y$ is continuous at $t=\pi/2$ and find the jump in $y'$. Sketch $y$ on $[0,\pi]$, marking the intercepts and extreme values. [3]

Q3 marking scheme

(a) [1] $y'' + 4y = 4\delta(t-\pi/2)$, $y(0)=0$, $y'(0)=b$.

(b) [1] $Y = \dfrac{b}{s^2+4} + \dfrac{4e^{-\pi s/2}}{s^2+4}$. [1] inverse: $\frac b2\sin2t + 2\sin\!\big(2(t-\tfrac\pi2)\big)H(t-\tfrac\pi2)$. [1] $\sin(2t-\pi) = -\sin2t$, so $$y = \tfrac b2\sin2t - 2\sin2t\;H(t-\tfrac\pi2).$$

(c) [1] $3\pi/4 > \pi/2$, so the Heaviside is on: $y(3\pi/4) = (\tfrac b2 - 2)\sin\tfrac{3\pi}2 = 2 - \tfrac b2 = 3$, so $b=-2$. (Using only the first term gives the wrong $b$.) [1] $y = -\sin2t$ for $0\le t<\pi/2$, and $y = -3\sin2t$ for $t\ge\pi/2$.

(d) [1] Both pieces are $0$ at $\pi/2$, so $y$ is continuous. $y'$: left limit $-2\cos\pi = 2$, right limit $-6\cos\pi = 6$, jump $=4$ = the strength. [2] Sketch: zero at $0$, $\pi/2$, $\pi$ (intercepts); dips to $-1$ at $t=\pi/4$; rises to $+3$ at $t=3\pi/4$; corner (not smooth) at $\pi/2$, with a steeper upward slope than the downward slope arriving.

Question 4 [9 marks]

(a) Let $F(s) = \mathcal L\{f\}(s)$. Prove that $\mathcal L\{t\,f(t)\} = -F'(s)$. [2]

(b) Apply $\mathcal L$ to $\;y'' + t\,y' - y = 0,\;\; y(0)=0,\; y'(0)=1$, and show that $Y = \mathcal L\{y\}$ satisfies $$Y' - \Big(s - \frac2s\Big)Y = -\frac1s.$$ [3]

(c) Solve the ODE in (b). Use $\lim_{s\to\infty}Y(s)=0$ to fix the constant. [3]

(d) Hence find $y(t)$, and check it in the original ODE. [1]

Q4 marking scheme

(a) [1] $F'(s) = \frac{d}{ds}\int_0^\infty e^{-st}f(t)\,dt = \int_0^\infty (-t)e^{-st}f(t)\,dt$ (differentiate under the integral). [1] $= -\mathcal L\{tf\}$.

(b) [1] $\mathcal L\{y''\} = s^2Y - 1$. [1] $\mathcal L\{ty'\} = -\frac{d}{ds}\big(sY - y(0)\big) = -Y - sY'$. [1] Sum: $s^2Y - 1 - Y - sY' - Y = 0$, so $sY' = (s^2-2)Y - 1$, i.e. $Y' - (s - \tfrac2s)Y = -\tfrac1s$.

(c) [1] Integrating factor $\mu = e^{-s^2/2 + 2\ln s} = s^2e^{-s^2/2}$. [1] $(\mu Y)' = -s\,e^{-s^2/2}$, so $\mu Y = e^{-s^2/2} + C$ and $Y = \dfrac1{s^2} + C\,\dfrac{e^{s^2/2}}{s^2}$. [1] $e^{s^2/2}/s^2\to\infty$, but $Y\to0$, so $C=0$ and $Y = 1/s^2$.

(d) [1] $y = t$. Check: $0 + t\cdot1 - t = 0$ ✓, $y(0)=0$, $y'(0)=1$ ✓.

Question 5 [9 marks]

(a) Show that the solution of $\;y'' + y = g(t),\; y(0)=y'(0)=0\;$ is $\;y(t)=\displaystyle\int_0^t \sin(t-\tau)\,g(\tau)\,d\tau$. [2]

(b) Evaluate it for $g(t) = e^{-t}$. [3]

(c) Solve for $f(t)$ for $t \ge 0$: $\displaystyle f(t) = t + \int_0^t \sin(t-\tau)\,f(\tau)\,d\tau.$ [4]

Q5 marking scheme

(a) [1] $(s^2+1)Y = G$, so $Y = G\cdot\frac1{s^2+1}$. [1] Convolution theorem with $\mathcal L^{-1}\{1/(s^2+1)\} = \sin t$.

(b) [1] $\int_0^t\sin(t-\tau)e^{-\tau}d\tau$: either integrate by parts twice, or use $\sin(t-\tau)=\sin t\cos\tau-\cos t\sin\tau$. [1] $\int_0^t e^{-\tau}\cos\tau\,d\tau = \tfrac12\big(e^{-t}(\sin t - \cos t) + 1\big)$ and $\int_0^t e^{-\tau}\sin\tau\,d\tau = \tfrac12\big(1 - e^{-t}(\sin t+\cos t)\big)$. [1] Combine: $$y = \tfrac12\big(e^{-t} + \sin t - \cos t\big).$$ Cross-check by partial fractions: $\frac1{(s+1)(s^2+1)}$ gives the same, and $y(0)=0$, $y'(0)=\tfrac12(-1+1)=0$ ✓.

(c) [1] The integral is $\sin * f$, so $F = \frac1{s^2} + \frac{1}{s^2+1}F$. [1] $F\cdot\frac{s^2}{s^2+1} = \frac1{s^2}$, so $F = \frac{s^2+1}{s^4}$. [1] $F = \frac1{s^2}+\frac1{s^4}$. [1] $f(t) = t + \frac{t^3}{6}$.

Question 6 [8 marks]

Let $x(t), y(t)$ be the amounts of salt in two connected tanks, modelled by $$x' = -3x + y,\qquad y' = x - 3y,\qquad x(0)=2,\; y(0)=0.$$ (a) Take Laplace transforms and solve for $X(s)$ and $Y(s)$. [4]
(b) Find $x(t)$ and $y(t)$. [2]
(c) Find the maximum amount of salt ever in the second tank, and the time it occurs. [2]

Q6 marking scheme

(a) [1] $(s+3)X - Y = 2$ and $-X + (s+3)Y = 0$. [1] Second gives $Y = \frac{X}{s+3}$. [1] $X\big[(s+3) - \frac1{s+3}\big]=2$, so $X = \frac{2(s+3)}{(s+3)^2-1} = \frac{2(s+3)}{(s+2)(s+4)}$. [1] $Y = \frac{2}{(s+2)(s+4)}$.

(b) [1] $X = \frac1{s+2}+\frac1{s+4}$, so $x = e^{-2t}+e^{-4t}$. [1] $Y = \frac1{s+2}-\frac1{s+4}$, so $y = e^{-2t}-e^{-4t}$. Check: $x' = -2e^{-2t}-4e^{-4t}$ and $-3x+y = -2e^{-2t}-4e^{-4t}$ ✓.

(c) [1] $y' = -2e^{-2t}+4e^{-4t}=0 \Rightarrow e^{-2t}=\tfrac12$, so $t=\tfrac12\ln2$. [1] $y = \tfrac12-\tfrac14 = \tfrac14$ (the sign of $y'$ goes from + to −, so it's a maximum).

Where marks leak in this set
(1) Q1(c): numerator $s$ vs $s+2$. (2) Q2: shifting only the $\frac1s$ part, not the whole $\frac{4}{s(s+2)^2}$. (3) Q3(c): using the pre-impulse formula at $t=3\pi/4$. (4) Q4(b): losing the sign in $\mathcal L\{ty'\}=-(sY)'$. (5) Q5(c): forgetting that the integral is a convolution of $f$ with $\sin$. (6) Everywhere: not checking $y(0)$ or a continuity condition at the end.

4 · Before you close this tab

Retrieval: cover §0 and list the six triggers and their first move

Exponential or trig product: s-shift or identity. Irreducible quadratic: complete the square. $H$: shift the whole function. $\delta$: $e^{-as}$, $y'$ jumps. $\int_0^t$ or general $g$: convolution. $t$ in a coefficient: $-F'$ and a first-order ODE in $Y$. Systems: algebra on $X, Y$.
