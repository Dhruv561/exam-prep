Strings and drums: the wave equation and its normal modes

The one win: solve $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$ on a string with fixed ends from two initial conditions (shape and velocity), say which harmonics are missing, list a drum's frequencies, and explain in one sentence why a wave keeps its kinks while heat smooths them.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: Topic 5 is predicted to be the biggest question (masked 2026 paper Q6 = 18 marks, parts (a)–(c), long intro). Wave pieces seen before: a drum question (19 marks, 2023), a 2-mark "heat vs wave, how does the discontinuity propagate?" part, and booklet/tute strings. The formula sheet has no separation-of-variables recipe: the three cases, the eigenfunctions and the $\frac2L\int_0^L$ coefficient formula must be memorised.

0 · Warm-up from Lessons 27–28

W1. $X''-\lambda X=0$, $X(0)=X(L)=0$. The non-trivial solutions are…

Only $\lambda=-k^2<0$ survives Dirichlet–Dirichlet: $X=B\sin kx$ with $\sin kL=0$, $k_n=\frac{n\pi}L$. $\lambda\ge0$ gives $X\equiv0$. Cosines are the Neumann family.

W2. For the heat equation $u_t=\alpha u_{xx}$ the time factor is $T_n=e^{-\alpha k_n^2t}$. For $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$, the same separation gives…

$\ddot T-c^2\lambda T=0$ with $\lambda=-k_n^2$: simple harmonic motion with angular frequency $\omega_n=ck_n$. Two constants, so two ICs are needed.

1 · Words → IBVP

Lecture 26's guitar string: $u(x,t)$ = transverse displacement, $c$ = wave speed. The 2026 paper wants you to translate text yourself.

The question says…You write
"pinned / fixed / clamped at both ends"$u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0$
"released from rest" / "initially not moving"$u_t(x,0)=0$
"from its equilibrium (straight) position"$u(x,0)=0$
"struck / hit with velocity $g(x)$"$u_t(x,0)=g(x)$
"plucked at $x=\ell$ to height $h$" (and let go)$u(x,0)=\begin{cases}hx/\ell,&0\le x\le\ell\\ h(L-x)/(L-\ell),&\ell\le x\le L\end{cases}$, $u_t(x,0)=0$
"ends free to slide" (ring on a frictionless rod)$u_x(0,t)=u_x(L,t)=0$ (Neumann)

A wave problem always needs two ICs, a heat problem one. If a text gives only a shape, the missing one is usually "released from rest".

Q1. "A string of length 3 with fixed ends lies in its equilibrium position. At $t=0$ the segment $1<x<2$ is hit by a hammer and given speed $v_0$." The ICs are…

"Equilibrium position" = zero displacement. Speed is a velocity, so it goes in $u_t(x,0)$, and only on the struck segment.

2 · Separation of variables: same 5 steps as heat

  1. Separate. $u=X(x)T(t)$: $\;X\ddot T=c^2X''T\Rightarrow\dfrac{\ddot T}{c^2T}=\dfrac{X''}{X}=\lambda$, so $X''-\lambda X=0$, $\;\ddot T-c^2\lambda T=0$.
  2. BCs → eigenproblem. $u(0,t)=X(0)T(t)=0$ for all $t$ ⇒ $X(0)=0$ (else $u\equiv0$); same at $L$. Three cases (Lesson 27): only $\lambda_n=-k_n^2$, $k_n=\frac{n\pi}L$, $X_n=\sin k_nx$, $n=1,2,\dots$
  3. Time ODE. $\ddot T_n+c^2k_n^2T_n=0\Rightarrow T_n=a_n\cos\omega_nt+b_n\sin\omega_nt$, $\;\omega_n=ck_n=\dfrac{cn\pi}{L}$.
  4. Superpose. $u(x,t)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\big(a_n\cos\omega_nt+b_n\sin\omega_nt\big)\sin k_nx$.
  5. ICs. Differentiate in $t$, then set $t=0$: $$u(x,0)=\sum a_n\sin k_nx=f(x),\qquad u_t(x,0)=\sum \omega_n b_n\sin k_nx=g(x).$$
$$\boxed{a_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf(x)\sin k_nx\,dx,\qquad b_n=\frac{2}{L\,\omega_n}\int_0^Lg(x)\sin k_nx\,dx}$$
The $\omega_n$ trap
The velocity series has coefficients $\omega_nb_n$, not $b_n$. Forgetting to divide by $\omega_n=\frac{cn\pi}{L}$ is the most common lost mark. Check: your $b_n$ should carry an extra $\frac1n$ compared with an $a_n$-type integral.

Worked (matching, no integrals). $u_{tt}=4u_{xx}$, $0<x<\pi$, ends fixed, $u(x,0)=3\sin2x-\sin5x$, $u_t(x,0)=6\sin x$.

Solution

$c=2$, $L=\pi$: $k_n=n$, $\omega_n=2n$. So $u=\sum(a_n\cos2nt+b_n\sin2nt)\sin nx$.

$t=0$: $\sum a_n\sin nx=3\sin2x-\sin5x$ ⇒ $a_2=3$, $a_5=-1$, others $0$.

$u_t(x,0)=\sum 2n\,b_n\sin nx=6\sin x$ ⇒ $2b_1=6$, $b_1=3$, others $0$.

$$u(x,t)=3\sin2t\,\sin x+3\cos4t\,\sin2x-\cos10t\,\sin5x.$$

Check: $t=0$ gives $3\sin2x-\sin5x$ ✓; $u_t(x,0)=6\sin x$ ✓; each term has $T''/T=-4n^2=4X''/X$ ✓.

Q2. $u_{tt}=9u_{xx}$ on $0<x<1$, fixed ends, $u(x,0)=0$, $u_t(x,0)=\sin(2\pi x)$. Then $u=$…

$n=2$: $\omega_2=3\cdot2\pi=6\pi$. $u(x,0)=0$ kills all $a_n$. $\omega_2b_2=1\Rightarrow b_2=\frac1{6\pi}$. (b) divided by $k_2$ instead of $\omega_2=ck_2$.

3 · Plucked strings and missing harmonics

Pluck at $x=\ell$ to height $h$ on $(0,1)$, released from rest. Then $b_n=0$ and the two-piece integral (one integration by parts per piece; derived in Tute 11 Q1(b), and for $\ell=\frac12$ in Lecture 26) gives $$a_n=\frac{2h\,\sin(n\pi\ell)}{n^2\pi^2\,\ell(1-\ell)},\qquad u=\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n\cos(n\pi ct)\sin(n\pi x).$$

Missing harmonics. Mode $n$ is absent exactly when $a_n=0$ (and $b_n=0$), here when $\sin(n\pi\ell)=0$, i.e. $n\ell\in\mathbb Z$. Physically: mode $n$ has a node (a zero of $\sin n\pi x$) at the pluck point, so plucking there cannot excite it.
$\ell=\frac12$: all even $n$ missing. $\ell=\frac13$: $n=3,6,9,\dots$ missing. $\ell=\frac14$: $n=4,8,\dots$ missing.

When both ICs are present, a mode is missing only if both $a_n$ and $b_n$ vanish.

Below: plucked at $\ell=\frac13$ ($h=1$, $c=1$, 80 terms). Wave (solid) vs heat $u_t=0.1u_{xx}$ from the same shape (dashed). Press Play.

Watch the wave: the corner splits into two corners that travel at speed $c$, reflect (and flip) at the fixed ends, and the shape returns exactly at $t=2L/c=2$. The heat profile rounds off the corner instantly and decays to $0$.

Q3. A string on $(0,1)$ is plucked at $\ell=\frac25$ and released from rest. Which harmonics are absent?

$\sin\frac{2n\pi}5=0\iff\frac{2n}5\in\mathbb Z\iff5\mid n$.

4 · Normal modes and frequencies

Normal mode (Lecture 27): $u_n(x,t)=\big(a_n\cos\omega_nt+b_n\sin\omega_nt\big)\sin k_nx$, one term of the series. Every point oscillates at the same frequency; nodes at the zeros of $\sin k_nx$ ($n-1$ interior nodes).
Normal (resonant) frequencies: $\omega_n=ck_n=\dfrac{cn\pi}{L}$. Fundamental $\omega_1=\frac{c\pi}L$; the rest are integer multiples (harmonics). In Hz: $\omega_n/2\pi=\frac{nc}{2L}$.
Period of the whole motion: $\frac{2\pi}{\omega_1}=\frac{2L}c$. Any finite sum of normal modes is a genuine solution.

Q4. A string's length is halved and its wave speed doubled. Its fundamental frequency is multiplied by…

$\omega_1=\frac{c\pi}L$: doubling $c$ gives $\times2$, halving $L$ gives another $\times2$.

5 · Free ends (Neumann): the $n=0$ mode

Not in Lectures 26–27 (only Dirichlet strings); appears in Booklet Q14. Low priority, but know the one twist.

$u_x(0,t)=u_x(L,t)=0$ ⇒ $X_n=\cos k_nx$, $n=0,1,2,\dots$ For $n=0$ ($\lambda=0$): $\ddot T_0=0\Rightarrow T_0=A_0+B_0t$, not a cosine. $$u=A_0+B_0t+\sum_{n\ge1}\big(a_n\cos\omega_nt+b_n\sin\omega_nt\big)\cos k_nx,$$ with $A_0=$ mean of $f$, $B_0=$ mean of $g$ over $(0,L)$. If the initial velocity has non-zero mean, the whole string drifts off at speed $B_0$.

Q5. $u_{tt}=u_{xx}$, $0<x<1$, $u_x=0$ at both ends, $u(x,0)=0$, $u_t(x,0)=2+\cos\pi x$. Then $u=$…

$f=0$ ⇒ $A_0=a_n=0$. $B_0=2$ (the constant part). $n=1$: $\omega_1=\pi$, $\omega_1b_1=1\Rightarrow b_1=\frac1\pi$.

6 · Rectangular drum: two indices, a dispersion relation

$u_{tt}=c^2(u_{xx}+u_{yy})$ on $0<x<L_x$, $0<y<L_y$, $u=0$ on all four edges (Lecture 27).

$u=X(x)Y(y)T(t)$: divide by $c^2XYT$, so $\frac{\ddot T}{c^2T}=\frac{X''}X+\frac{Y''}Y$. Each ratio is constant: $$X''-\mu X=0,\qquad Y''-\nu Y=0,\qquad \ddot T-c^2(\mu+\nu)T=0.$$ Dirichlet in both: $X_m=\sin\frac{m\pi x}{L_x}$, $Y_n=\sin\frac{n\pi y}{L_y}$, $m,n\ge1$. Then $T_{m,n}=a_{m,n}\cos\omega_{m,n}t+b_{m,n}\sin\omega_{m,n}t$ with the dispersion relation $$\omega_{m,n}^2=c^2\big(k_{x,m}^2+k_{y,n}^2\big)=c^2\pi^2\Big(\frac{m^2}{L_x^2}+\frac{n^2}{L_y^2}\Big).$$ Normal mode: $u_{m,n}=T_{m,n}(t)\sin\frac{m\pi x}{L_x}\sin\frac{n\pi y}{L_y}$, with $m-1$ nodal lines parallel to the $y$-axis and $n-1$ parallel to the $x$-axis.

Degenerate frequencies: different $(m,n)$ with the same $\omega$. Square, side $\pi$, $c=1$: $\omega=\sqrt{m^2+n^2}$.

modes(1,1)(1,2),(2,1)(2,2)(1,3),(3,1)(2,3),(3,2)…(1,7),(7,1),(5,5)

Swapping $m\leftrightarrow n$ always works on a square. Rectangles with rational $L_x/L_y$ can also be degenerate: $L_x=2$, $L_y=1$ gives $\frac{m^2}4+n^2$, equal $5$ for $(2,2)$ and $(4,1)$.
Unlike the string, $\omega_{m,n}/\omega_{1,1}$ is not an integer in general ($\sqrt{5/2}$, $2$, $\sqrt5$, …): a drum's overtones are not harmonics.

Q6. Drum $0<x<3$, $0<y<1$, fixed edges, $c=1$. Which mode has the second-lowest frequency?

$\omega^2/\pi^2=\frac{m^2}9+n^2$: $(1,1)\to\frac{10}9$, $(2,1)\to\frac{13}9$, $(3,1)\to2$, $(1,2)\to\frac{37}9$. Wiggling along the long side is cheap.

Q7. On a square drum (side $\pi$, $c=1$), how many normal modes share the frequency $\omega=5$?

$m^2+n^2=25$ with $m,n\ge1$: $(3,4)$ and $(4,3)$. $(5,0)$ and $(0,5)$ are not allowed ($\sin0=0$).

7 · Heat vs wave (the 1–2 sentence answer)

heat $u_t=\alpha u_{xx}$wave $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$
time factor$e^{-\alpha k_n^2t}$: high $n$ die fastest$\cos\omega_nt,\sin\omega_nt$: no decay
kink / jump in ICsmoothed instantly for $t>0$kept; travels at speed $c$, reflects at ends
$t\to\infty$steady state $u_S(x)$no steady state; periodic, period $2L/c$
genuine?yes for $t>0$ (Lecture 25)only weak if IC has a kink or jump

Model answers (write one of these).
Heat: "The exponential factors $e^{-\alpha k_n^2t}$ damp the high-frequency modes immediately, so the discontinuity is smoothed out for every $t>0$ and $u$ tends to the steady state."
Wave: "The modes oscillate without decay, so the discontinuity is not smoothed; it propagates along the string at speed $c$, reflecting at the ends, and recurs periodically in time, with no steady state."

Q8. A string released from rest has a corner in its initial shape. For $t>0$ the series solution…

Each term has period $\frac{2\pi}{\omega_n}=\frac{2L}{nc}$, which divides $\frac{2L}c$. Amplitudes don't decay, so the corners never round off (so it is only a weak solution).

8 · Practice (exam level)

P1. $u_{tt}=9u_{xx}$, $0<x<2$, fixed ends, $u(x,0)=\sin\pi x+2\sin\frac{3\pi x}2$, $u_t(x,0)=3\pi\sin\frac{\pi x}2$. (a) Solve. (b) Give the fundamental frequency and the period of the motion.

P2. A string of length 1 with $c=1$ and fixed ends is at rest in equilibrium; at $t=0$ the segment $0<x<\frac13$ is struck, given velocity $1$. (a) Write the IBVP. (b) Find $u$. (c) Which harmonics are missing?

P3. Drum $0<x<\pi$, $0<y<\pi$, fixed edges, $c=1$. (a) List the four lowest distinct frequencies and their modes. (b) Find a frequency shared by three modes.

P4. $u_t=u_{xx}$ and $v_{tt}=v_{xx}$ on $0<x<1$, zero at both ends, with $u(x,0)=v(x,0)=1$ for $\frac13<x<\frac23$ and $0$ otherwise ($v_t(x,0)=0$). In 1–2 sentences each: what happens to the jumps, and to each solution as $t\to\infty$?

P1 solution

$k_n=\frac{n\pi}2$, $\omega_n=\frac{3n\pi}2$. Matching: $\sin\pi x=\sin k_2x$ ⇒ $a_2=1$; $2\sin\frac{3\pi x}2$ ⇒ $a_3=2$. Velocity: $\omega_1b_1=3\pi$ ⇒ $b_1=\frac{3\pi}{3\pi/2}=2$. $$u=2\sin\tfrac{3\pi t}2\sin\tfrac{\pi x}2+\cos(3\pi t)\sin\pi x+2\cos\tfrac{9\pi t}2\sin\tfrac{3\pi x}2.$$ (b) $\omega_1=\frac{3\pi}2$; period $\frac{2\pi}{\omega_1}=\frac43=\frac{2L}c$ ✓.

P2 solution

(a) $u_{tt}=u_{xx}$, $u(0,t)=u(1,t)=0$, $u(x,0)=0$, $u_t(x,0)=1$ on $(0,\frac13)$, $0$ on $(\frac13,1)$.

(b) $a_n=0$. $\omega_n=n\pi$, $\omega_nb_n=2\int_0^{1/3}\sin n\pi x\,dx=\frac{2}{n\pi}\big(1-\cos\frac{n\pi}3\big)$, so $$b_n=\frac{2\big(1-\cos\frac{n\pi}3\big)}{n^2\pi^2},\qquad u=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{2\big(1-\cos\frac{n\pi}3\big)}{n^2\pi^2}\sin(n\pi t)\sin(n\pi x).$$

(c) $b_n=0\iff\cos\frac{n\pi}3=1\iff n=6,12,18,\dots$

P3 solution

(a) $\omega=\sqrt{m^2+n^2}$: $\sqrt2$ $(1,1)$; $\sqrt5$ $(1,2),(2,1)$; $\sqrt8$ $(2,2)$; $\sqrt{10}$ $(1,3),(3,1)$.
(b) $\sqrt{50}$: $(1,7),(7,1),(5,5)$. (Also $\sqrt{65}$: four modes $(1,8),(8,1),(4,7),(7,4)$.)

P4 solution

Heat: the factors $e^{-n^2\pi^2t}$ smooth the jumps instantly, so $u$ is smooth for $t>0$ and decays to the steady state $u\equiv0$.
Wave: the modes $\cos(n\pi t)\sin(n\pi x)$ don't decay, so each jump splits into two half-height jumps travelling at speed $1$, reflecting (with sign flip) at the ends; the motion has period $2$ and no steady state.

9 · Exam-level challenge (18 marks, about 30 minutes)

A taut string of length $1$ with wave speed $c=3$ is fixed at both ends. At $t=0$ it has displacement $\frac1{20}\sin(2\pi x)$, and at the same instant a hammer strikes the middle third $\frac13<x<\frac23$, giving it velocity $v_0>0$ (the rest of the string has zero initial velocity).

(a) Write down the PDE, BCs and ICs for the displacement $u(x,t)$. [2]
(b) Using $u=X(x)T(t)$, show that $X''-\lambda X=0$ and $\ddot T-9\lambda T=0$. [2]
(c) Consider $\lambda>0$, $\lambda=0$, $\lambda<0$ and find the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. [4]
(d) Write the general series solution and identify the normal frequencies. [2]
(e) Apply the ICs to find $u(x,t)$. [5]
(f) Which harmonics are absent? What is the period of the motion? [2]
(g) Does $u$ approach a steady state? What happens to the kinks created at $x=\frac13,\frac23$? Answer in 1–2 sentences. [1]

Full marking-style solution: worked/0031-challenge-full-solution.html. Attempt every part first.

Answer key (quick check)

(c) $\lambda_n=-n^2\pi^2$, $X_n=\sin n\pi x$, $n\ge1$. (d) $\omega_n=3n\pi$.
(e) $u=\frac1{20}\cos(6\pi t)\sin(2\pi x)+\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{2v_0\big(\cos\frac{n\pi}3-\cos\frac{2n\pi}3\big)}{3n^2\pi^2}\sin(3n\pi t)\sin(n\pi x)$.
(f) Even $n\ge4$ absent ($n=2$ survives through the displacement); period $\frac23$. (g) No steady state; kinks travel at speed $3$ and reflect, never smoothing.
