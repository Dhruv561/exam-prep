Lesson 37 · Topic 6: Fourier transforms · ODEs on ℝ

Solving ODEs on the whole line with the Fourier transform

The one win: one 5-step recipe that handles both exam types. Constant coefficients with $\delta$/box/$e^{-|x|}$ forcing (answer by table and shift), and $y^{(n)}\pm xy=0$-type equations (a first-order ODE in $k$, answer left as an integral).

Why this matters for 5 Nov: the "most general solution that has a Fourier transform, leave it as a definite integral" question has been a whole 7-mark question, marked line by line: move to Fourier space · simplify · integrating factor · solve for $\hat y$ · invert · final answer. Booklet Q5/Q6 are the constant-coefficient version. The formula sheet gives $\mathcal F\{f^{(n)}\}=(ik)^n\hat f$ and $\mathcal F\{x^nf\}=i^n\frac{d^n\hat f}{dk^n}$, but not the table or the shift theorem.

0 · Warm-up from Lessons 34–36 (and Lesson 1)

W1. $\mathcal F^{-1}\Big\{\dfrac{e^{-2ik}}{k^2+9}\Big\}=$…

$e^{-3|x|}\leftrightarrow\frac{6}{9+k^2}$, so $\frac1{k^2+9}\leftrightarrow\frac16e^{-3|x|}$. Multiplying by $e^{-iyk}$ shifts $x\to x-y$, here $y=2$.

W2. Using the formula sheet rules, $\mathcal F\{y'''\}$ and $\mathcal F\{xy\}$ are…

$(ik)^3=i^3k^3=-ik^3$. $\mathcal F\{xf\}=i\frac{d\hat f}{dk}$ (one power of $i$ per power of $x$). Keep the powers: $(ik)^1=ik$, $(ik)^2=-k^2$, $(ik)^3=-ik^3$, $(ik)^4=k^4$, $(ik)^5=ik^5$.

W3. $\mathcal F\{f\}=\frac{2}{1+k^2}$ and $\mathcal F\{g\}=\frac{2\sin k}{k}$. Then $\mathcal F^{-1}\Big\{\dfrac{4\sin k}{k(1+k^2)}\Big\}=$…

$\hat f\hat g=\frac{4\sin k}{k(1+k^2)}$, and the convolution theorem says $\mathcal F\{f*g\}=\hat f\hat g$ (no $2\pi$ in this direction). Here $f=e^{-|x|}$, $g$ = box on $|x|<1$.

W4 (Lesson 1). $\hat y'+2k\,\hat y=0$. Integrating factor and solution:

$\hat y'+p\hat y=0$: $\mu=e^{\int p\,dk}=e^{k^2}$; $(\mu\hat y)'=0\Rightarrow\hat y=A/\mu=Ae^{-k^2}$. Same as Lesson 1 with $t\to k$.

1 · The recipe

  1. Move to Fourier space. Transform every term: $y^{(n)}\to(ik)^n\hat y$, $\;xy\to i\hat y'$, $\;\delta_a\to e^{-iak}$, forcing $f\to\hat f$.
  2. Simplify. Constant coefficients: algebra, $\hat y=\hat f/P(ik)$. With an $x$: a first-order linear ODE $\hat y'+p(k)\hat y=0$.
  3. Integrating factor (variable coefficients only): $\mu=e^{\int p\,dk}$.
  4. Solve for $\hat y$. Partial fractions in $k^2$ if needed; or $\hat y=A/\mu$.
  5. Invert. Table + shift (+ convolution) if it fits; otherwise write $y=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty\hat y\,e^{ikx}\,dk$ and simplify with parity.

2 · Constant coefficients: the decaying solution

Worked A1. Solve $y''-9y=\delta(x-2)$ subject to the condition that the FT of $y$ exists.

Solution

FT: $(ik)^2\hat y-9\hat y=e^{-2ik}\Rightarrow\hat y=-\dfrac{e^{-2ik}}{k^2+9}$.

$\frac1{k^2+9}\leftrightarrow\frac16e^{-3|x|}$ (from $e^{-a|x|}\leftrightarrow\frac{2a}{a^2+k^2}$, $a=3$), then shift by $2$: $$y(x)=-\tfrac16e^{-3|x-2|}.$$ Check: $y''-9y=0$ away from $x=2$ ✓; $y'$ jumps from $-\frac12$ to $+\frac12$ at $x=2$, a jump of $1$, which is exactly what $\delta(x-2)$ demands ✓.

Where did $C_1e^{3x}+C_2e^{-3x}$ go? The general solution is $y=-\frac16e^{-3|x-2|}+C_1e^{3x}+C_2e^{-3x}$. But $e^{3x}$ blows up at $+\infty$ and $e^{-3x}$ at $-\infty$, so neither has a FT. Transforming the ODE assumes $\hat y$ exists, so the method only returns solutions that decay: $C_1=C_2=0$. That is what "subject to the condition that the FT exists" means, and it makes the answer unique (L30 s3).

Worked A2 (partial fractions in $k^2$). Solve $y''-y=e^{-2|x|}$ subject to the FT of $y$ existing.

Solution

FT: $-(k^2+1)\hat y=\dfrac{4}{k^2+4}\Rightarrow\hat y=-\dfrac{4}{(k^2+1)(k^2+4)}$.

Partial fractions with $u=k^2$: $\frac{1}{(u+1)(u+4)}=\frac13\Big(\frac1{u+1}-\frac1{u+4}\Big)$, so $$\hat y=-\frac43\cdot\frac1{k^2+1}+\frac43\cdot\frac1{k^2+4}.$$ Table: $\frac1{k^2+1}\leftrightarrow\frac12e^{-|x|}$, $\;\frac1{k^2+4}\leftrightarrow\frac14e^{-2|x|}$: $$y=-\tfrac23e^{-|x|}+\tfrac13e^{-2|x|}.$$ Check ($x>0$): $y''-y=\big(-\frac23e^{-x}+\frac43e^{-2x}\big)-\big(-\frac23e^{-x}+\frac13e^{-2x}\big)=e^{-2x}$ ✓, and $y'(0^+)=\frac23-\frac23=0$, so $y'$ is continuous (no $\delta$ on the right) ✓.

When no pair fits, stop at the integral (booklet Q5/Q6 style). E.g. $y''-y=$ box ($1$ on $|x|<1$): $\hat y=-\dfrac{2\sin k}{k(1+k^2)}$, so $$y=-\frac1{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin k}{k(1+k^2)}e^{ikx}\,dk=-\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin k\,\cos kx}{k(1+k^2)}\,dk.$$ Parity: $\hat y$ is even, so $e^{ikx}=\cos kx+i\sin kx$ keeps only the even part, doubled over $(0,\infty)$. (Or get a closed form by convolution: Practice P2.)

Q1. $y''-16y=\delta(x+3)$, FT of $y$ exists. $y=$…

$\hat y=-\frac{e^{3ik}}{k^2+16}$; $\frac1{k^2+16}\leftrightarrow\frac18e^{-4|x|}$; $e^{3ik}=e^{-i(-3)k}$ shifts to $x+3$. Sign: $(ik)^2-16=-(k^2+16)$.

Q2. The beam equation $u''''+4u=\delta(x)$, FT of $u$ exists. Which is $u$?

$(ik)^4=k^4$, so $\hat u=\frac1{k^4+4}$, even in $k$. $\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty$ with $\cos kx$. (It equals $\frac18e^{-|x|}(\cos x+\sin|x|)$, but you'd never be asked to evaluate it.)

3 · Variable coefficients: a first-order ODE in $k$

An $x$ multiplying $y$ turns into a $k$-derivative, so the ODE in $x$ becomes an ODE in $k$. With only $xy$ (no $xy'$) it is first order, whatever the order in $x$.

termFThow
$xy$$i\,\hat y'$sheet, $n=1$
$xy'$$i\frac{d}{dk}(ik\hat y)=-\hat y-k\hat y'$apply $x$-rule to $f=y'$
$x^2y$$-\hat y''$sheet, $n=2$ (second order: avoid)

Sanity example. $y'+xy=0$: $ik\hat y+i\hat y'=0\Rightarrow\hat y'=-k\hat y\Rightarrow\hat y=Ae^{-k^2/2}$, a Gaussian, so $y=Ce^{-x^2/2}$. Separation in $x$ gives the same ✓.

Worked B1. $y'''-xy=0$ on $x\in\mathbb R$. Find the most general solution that has a FT, as a definite integral.

Solution (one line per mark)

Fourier space: $(ik)^3\hat y-i\hat y'=0$, i.e. $-ik^3\hat y-i\hat y'=0$.

Simplify (divide by $-i$): $\hat y'+k^3\hat y=0$.

IF: $\mu=e^{\int k^3dk}=e^{k^4/4}$.

Solve: $(e^{k^4/4}\hat y)'=0\Rightarrow\hat y=Ae^{-k^4/4}$.

Invert: $$y(x)=\frac{A}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-k^4/4}e^{ikx}\,dk=\frac{A}{\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-k^4/4}\cos(kx)\,dk.$$ (Last step: $e^{-k^4/4}$ even, $\sin kx$ odd, so the imaginary part integrates to zero.) Checked numerically: $y'''-xy=0$ to $10^{-12}$ at several $x$.

"Most general" = one constant. $y'''-xy=0$ has a 3-dimensional solution space, but the FT returns a 1-parameter family: the other solutions blow up and have no FT. Same story as Airy (L30 s6–7): $y''-xy=0\to\hat y=Ae^{ik^3/3}$ gives $\mathrm{Ai}$, while $\mathrm{Bi}$ grows and is lost.

Can $\hat y$ be inverted? Look at the real part of the exponent:

  • $\to-\infty$ both ways (e.g. $e^{-k^4/4}$): fine, absolutely convergent.
  • purely imaginary (e.g. $e^{ik^3/3}$): fine, oscillatory, as in the lecture's Airy example.
  • $\to+\infty$ (e.g. $e^{+k^4/4}$): no inverse FT exists, so only $y\equiv0$ has a FT. Say so.
Sign traps
(1) $(ik)^3=-ik^3$, not $ik^3$. (2) $\mathcal F\{xy\}=+i\hat y'$; with $-xy$ in the ODE the term is $-i\hat y'$. (3) Divide by $i$ carefully: $\frac{1}{i}=-i$. One sign slip turns $e^{-k^4/4}$ into $e^{+k^4/4}$ and "no solution". Check the final $\hat y$ by substituting back.

Q3. $\mathcal F\{x\,y'\}=$…

$\mathcal F\{xf\}=i\hat f'$ with $\hat f=ik\hat y$: $i(i\hat y+ik\hat y')=-\hat y-k\hat y'$. (a) forgot the product rule. An $xy'$ term puts $k\hat y'$ in the ODE (a singular point at $k=0$), so exam questions use $xy$ only.

Q4. $y''+4xy=0$. The FT method gives $\hat y=$…

$-k^2\hat y+4i\hat y'=0\Rightarrow\hat y'=\frac{k^2}{4i}\hat y=-\frac{ik^2}{4}\hat y\Rightarrow\hat y=Ae^{-ik^3/12}$. Purely imaginary exponent: Airy-type, invertible.

Q5. $y''+2y'-xy=0$. What does the FT method give?

$(-k^2+2ik)\hat y-i\hat y'=0\Rightarrow\hat y'=\frac{-k^2+2ik}{i}\hat y=(ik^2+2k)\hat y\Rightarrow\hat y=Ae^{ik^3/3+k^2}$. $|\hat y|=|A|e^{k^2}\to\infty$: no inverse FT unless $A=0$.

4 · Practice (exam level)

P1. Solve $y''-4y=\delta(x-1)+\delta(x+1)$, subject to the FT of $y$ existing.

P2. $y''-y=g(x)$, $g=1$ on $|x|<1$, $0$ otherwise. (a) Find $\hat y$ and write $y$ as a real integral. (b) Using W3 and Worked A1's idea ($\mathcal F^{-1}\{\frac{-1}{1+k^2}\}=-\frac12e^{-|x|}$), find $y$ in closed form for $|x|<1$.

P3. $y''+2y'+xy=0$, $x\in\mathbb R$. Find the most general solution that has a FT; write it as a real definite integral.

P4. Solve $y'+3xy=0$ by FT (Gaussian pair $e^{-ax^2}\leftrightarrow\sqrt{\pi/a}\,e^{-k^2/4a}$ given), then check by separation.

P1 solution

$-(k^2+4)\hat y=e^{-ik}+e^{ik}\Rightarrow\hat y=-\frac{e^{-ik}+e^{ik}}{k^2+4}$. $\frac1{k^2+4}\leftrightarrow\frac14e^{-2|x|}$, shift each: $$y=-\tfrac14\big(e^{-2|x-1|}+e^{-2|x+1|}\big).$$

P2 solution

(a) $\hat g=\frac{2\sin k}{k}$, $\hat y=-\frac{2\sin k}{k(1+k^2)}$, $\;y=-\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin k\cos kx}{k(1+k^2)}dk$.

(b) $\hat y=\hat g\cdot\big(-\frac1{1+k^2}\big)$, so $y=g*\big(-\frac12e^{-|x|}\big)=-\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-|x-s|}ds$. For $|x|<1$ split at $s=x$: $$\begin{aligned}-\tfrac12\Big[\int_{-1}^xe^{-(x-s)}ds+\int_x^1e^{-(s-x)}ds\Big]&=-\tfrac12\big[(1-e^{-(x+1)})+(1-e^{-(1-x)})\big]\\&=-1+e^{-1}\cosh x.\end{aligned}$$ (For $|x|>1$: $-\sinh(1)\,e^{-|x|}$.) Check: $y''-y=1$ inside ✓; matches the integral numerically ✓.

P3 solution

$(-k^2+2ik)\hat y+i\hat y'=0\Rightarrow\hat y'=\frac{k^2-2ik}{i}\hat y=(-ik^2-2k)\hat y$. IF $\mu=e^{ik^3/3+k^2}$, $\hat y=Ae^{-ik^3/3-k^2}$ (decays ✓). $$y=\frac{A}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-k^2}e^{i(kx-k^3/3)}dk=\frac{A}{\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-k^2}\cos\!\big(kx-\tfrac{k^3}3\big)dk,$$ since $kx-\frac{k^3}{3}$ is odd (its sine is odd, its cosine even). Verified numerically.

P4 solution

$ik\hat y+3i\hat y'=0\Rightarrow\hat y'=-\frac k3\hat y\Rightarrow\hat y=Ae^{-k^2/6}$. Match $\frac1{4a}=\frac16$: $a=\frac32$, so $y=Ce^{-3x^2/2}$. Separation: $\frac{y'}y=-3x\Rightarrow y=Ce^{-3x^2/2}$ ✓.

5 · Exam-level challenge (14 marks, about 25 minutes)

You may use $\mathcal F\{e^{-a|x|}\}=\dfrac{2a}{a^2+k^2}$ for $a>0$.

(a) Find the solution of $y''-4y=6e^{-|x-1|}$, $x\in\mathbb R$, subject to the condition that the Fourier transform of $y$ exists. [5]
(b) Explain briefly why no multiple of $e^{2x}$ or $e^{-2x}$ appears in your answer. [1]
(c) Use the method of Fourier transforms to find the most general solution of $y''-y'-xy=0$, $x\in\mathbb R$, that has a Fourier transform. You may leave it as a definite integral. [6]
(d) Show that your answer to (c) with a real constant is real, and write it as an integral over $(0,\infty)$ of a real function. [2]

Full marking-style solution: worked/0037-challenge-full-solution.html. Attempt every part first.

Answer key (quick check)

(a) $y=-2e^{-|x-1|}+e^{-2|x-1|}$. (b) They have no FT (grow at $\pm\infty$).
(c) $\hat y=Ae^{ik^3/3-k^2/2}$, $\;y=\frac{A}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-k^2/2}e^{i(k^3/3+kx)}dk$. (d) $y=\frac A\pi\int_0^\infty e^{-k^2/2}\cos\big(\frac{k^3}3+kx\big)dk$.
