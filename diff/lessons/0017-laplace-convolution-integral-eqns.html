Lesson 17 · Topic 3: Laplace

Convolution, general forcing and integral equations

The one win: solve $a y'' + b y' + c y = g(t)$ with unspecified $g$, as "homogeneous part + (impulse response) $*\,g$". The same tool transforms any integral of the form $\int_0^t f(t-\tau)g(\tau)\,d\tau$, so integro-differential equations become algebra.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: past papers pair a first-order IVP with general $g(t)$ with a specific $g$ afterwards, and RLC-style integro-differential equations appear in the booklet (Q16, 19–21) and tute 6. The convolution theorem is on your formula sheet, but $\mathcal L\{\int_0^t f\}=F/s$ is not. You derive it in one line (§2).

0 · Warm-up from Lessons 13–16

W1. $\mathcal L^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{s+1}{s^2+2s+5}\right\}=$

Complete the square: $s^2+2s+5=(s+1)^2+4$. Numerator is exactly $s+1$, so cos with $\omega=2$, shifted by $s\to s+1$ (Lesson 13).

W2. $\mathcal L^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{e^{-2s}}{s+1}\right\}=$

$\mathcal L\{f(t-a)H(t-a)\}=e^{-as}F(s)$ with $F=\frac1{s+1}$, $f=e^{-t}$: replace every $t$ by $t-2$ (Lesson 15).

1 · Convolution

$$(f*g)(t)=\int_0^t f(t-\tau)\,g(\tau)\,d\tau.$$

  • Commutative: $f*g=g*f$ (sub $u=t-\tau$). Pick the easier order.
  • Not $1*f=f$: $\;1*f=\int_0^t f(\tau)d\tau$, an antiderivative.
  • With the delta: $\delta(t-\cdot)$ sifts, so $\displaystyle\int_0^t\delta(t-\tau)g(\tau)d\tau=g(t)$ ($\delta*g=g$). Booklet 19(c) is this.
  • Theorem (on your sheet): $\mathcal L\{f*g\}=F(s)\,G(s)$.

Worked: transform of $\displaystyle\int_0^t(t-\tau)\,e^{2\tau}d\tau$. The argument $t-\tau$ sits on the first factor, so it is $t*e^{2t}$. Transform: $\dfrac1{s^2}\cdot\dfrac1{s-2}$. No integrating needed.

Trap. Only $f(t-\tau)g(\tau)$ is a convolution. $\int_0^t \sin\tau\,e^{2\tau}d\tau$ is $1*(\sin t\,e^{2t})$, transform $\frac1s\cdot\frac1{(s-2)^2+1}$, not $\frac{1}{s^2+1}\cdot\frac1{s-2}$. Always ask "which factor carries $t-\tau$?"

Q1. Which has Laplace transform $\dfrac{1}{s^2(s^2+1)}$?

Product of $\frac1{s^2}=\mathcal L\{t\}$ and $\frac1{s^2+1}=\mathcal L\{\sin t\}$. (Others: $1*\sin t$ and $t*\cos t$ both give $\frac1{s(s^2+1)}$; $t*\sinh t$ gives $\frac1{s^2(s^2-1)}$.)

Q2. $\displaystyle\int_0^t\delta(t-\tau)\cos 2\tau\,d\tau=$

$\delta*g=g$. Sifting sets $\tau=t$. Check by transform: $1\cdot\frac{s}{s^2+4}$.

2 · Two uses of the theorem

(i) Antiderivative rule, derived not memorised

$$\mathcal L\Big\{\int_0^t f(\tau)d\tau\Big\}=\mathcal L\{1*f\}=\frac1s F(s).$$

This is not on the sheet. Derive it from the theorem and $\mathcal L\{1\}=1/s$.

(ii) Inverse transform of a product

Partial fractions fails or is awkward when the factor is an irreducible quadratic squared. Example: $\mathcal L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{(s^2+9)^2}\right\}$.

$\frac1{s^2+9}=\mathcal L\{\tfrac13\sin3t\}$, so the answer is $\tfrac19\sin3t*\sin3t=\tfrac19\int_0^t\sin3(t-\tau)\sin3\tau\,d\tau$. Use $\sin A\sin B=\tfrac12[\cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)]$:

$$=\frac1{18}\int_0^t\big[\cos(3t-6\tau)-\cos3t\big]d\tau=\frac1{18}\Big[\frac{\sin3t}{3}-t\cos3t\Big]=\frac{\sin3t-3t\cos3t}{54}.$$

Here $\int_0^t\cos(3t-6\tau)d\tau=\big[-\tfrac16\sin(3t-6\tau)\big]_0^t=\tfrac13\sin3t$.

3 · General forcing $g(t)$

Take $\;y''+2y'+5y=g(t),\;y(0)=1,\;y'(0)=0$. Transform, with $P(s)=s^2+2s+5$:

$$P(s)Y=(s+2)+G(s)\;\Rightarrow\;Y=\underbrace{\frac{s+2}{(s+1)^2+4}}_{\text{from ICs}}+\underbrace{\frac1{(s+1)^2+4}}_{H(s)}G(s).$$

Invert: $\;y=e^{-t}\big(\cos2t+\tfrac12\sin2t\big)+\big(h*g\big)(t)$, where $h=\tfrac12e^{-t}\sin2t$.

The recipe: $y=y_{\text{hom}}$ (the solution with $g=0$ and the given ICs) $+\,h*g$, where $h=\mathcal L^{-1}\{1/P(s)\}$ is the response to $g=\delta$ with zero ICs ("impulse response"). Leading coefficient $a\neq1$: $h=\mathcal L^{-1}\{1/(as^2+bs+c)\}$, keep the $a$.

First order, then specific $g$ (a recurring pair). $y'+3y=g,\;y(0)=2$: $\;(s+3)Y=2+G$, so

$$y=2e^{-3t}+\int_0^te^{-3(t-\tau)}g(\tau)\,d\tau.$$

Specific $g=e^{-3t}$: integrand $e^{-3t}$ is constant in $\tau$, so $y=2e^{-3t}+te^{-3t}=(2+t)e^{-3t}$. (Resonance shows up as the extra factor $t$.) Specific $g=\sin t$: either integrate by parts twice, or go back to $Y=\frac{2}{s+3}+\frac{1}{(s+3)(s^2+1)}$ and use partial fractions. Giving the integral form is acceptable when $g$ is unspecified; for specific $g$ finish the integral.

Q3. The solution of $y'+3y=g(t)$, $y(0)=2$ is…

$Y=\frac{2}{s+3}+\frac{1}{s+3}G$. The IC rides with the homogeneous part only; $g$ gets coefficient $1$.

4 · Integral and integro-differential equations

  1. Rewrite each integral as a convolution (spot $t-\tau$). Antiderivative $\int_0^t y\to Y/s$.
  2. Transform everything; derivative terms bring the ICs.
  3. Solve for $Y$, partial fractions, invert.
  4. Sanity-check $y(0)$ (and the equation at $t=0$).

Type A, integro-differential (RLC-like). $\;y'+2y+\int_0^ty(\tau)d\tau=4,\;y(0)=1$:

$$sY-1+2Y+\frac Ys=\frac4s\;\Rightarrow\;(s^2+2s+1)Y=s+4\;\Rightarrow\;Y=\frac{s+4}{(s+1)^2}=\frac1{s+1}+\frac3{(s+1)^2}.$$

$y=(1+3t)e^{-t}$. Check: $y(0)=1$; at $t=0$ the equation says $y'(0)+2=4$, and $y'=(3-1-3t)e^{-t}\to2$ ✓.

Type B, Volterra (unknown only inside). $\;y(t)=t+\int_0^t\sin(t-\tau)\,y(\tau)d\tau$. The integral is $\sin*y$:

$$Y=\frac1{s^2}+\frac{Y}{s^2+1}\;\Rightarrow\;Y\frac{s^2}{s^2+1}=\frac1{s^2}\;\Rightarrow\;Y=\frac{s^2+1}{s^4}=\frac1{s^2}+\frac1{s^4}\;\Rightarrow\;y=t+\frac{t^3}6.$$

Q4. Solve $\;y(t)=1+\displaystyle\int_0^t(t-\tau)\,y(\tau)\,d\tau$.

$Y=\frac1s+\frac{Y}{s^2}\Rightarrow Y=\frac{s}{s^2-1}$, the $\cosh t$ entry on your sheet. Check $t=0$: $y(0)=1$ ✓.

5 · Exam-level challenge (12 marks)

About 18 minutes, on paper, then open one part at a time.

Let $y$ solve $\;y''+6y'+13y=g(t),\;y(0)=0,\;y'(0)=2$, where $g$ is a given function with Laplace transform $G$.

(a) Show that $Y(s)=\dfrac{2+G(s)}{(s+3)^2+4}$. [2]
(b) Express $y(t)$ as the sum of a function of $t$ and an integral involving $g$. [3]
(c) Now let $g(t)=e^{-3t}$. Evaluate your integral to find $y(t)$, and verify $y'(0)=2$. [3]
(d) Find the continuous $\varphi$ with $\;\varphi(t)=\sin t+2\displaystyle\int_0^t\cos(t-\tau)\,\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau$. [4]

(a)

$\mathcal L\{y''\}=s^2Y-sy(0)-y'(0)=s^2Y-2$, $\mathcal L\{y'\}=sY$. So $(s^2+6s+13)Y-2=G$, and $s^2+6s+13=(s+3)^2+4$.

(b)

$\frac{2}{(s+3)^2+4}\to e^{-3t}\sin2t$. $\;H(s)=\frac1{(s+3)^2+4}\to h(t)=\tfrac12e^{-3t}\sin2t$.

$$y(t)=e^{-3t}\sin2t+\frac12\int_0^te^{-3(t-\tau)}\sin\big(2(t-\tau)\big)\,g(\tau)\,d\tau.$$

(Or with $g(t-\tau)$ and $\tau$-argument on $h$; equivalent.)

(c)

Use the form $\tfrac12\int_0^te^{-3\tau}\sin2\tau\;g(t-\tau)d\tau$ with $g(t-\tau)=e^{-3(t-\tau)}$: the exponentials combine to $e^{-3t}$, constant in $\tau$.

$$\tfrac12e^{-3t}\int_0^t\sin2\tau\,d\tau=\tfrac14e^{-3t}(1-\cos2t).$$ $$y=e^{-3t}\Big(\sin2t+\tfrac14-\tfrac14\cos2t\Big).$$

$y(0)=0$ ✓. $\;y'=-3y+e^{-3t}(2\cos2t+\tfrac12\sin2t)$, so $y'(0)=0+2=2$ ✓.

(d)

$\cos*\varphi$ transforms to $\frac{s}{s^2+1}\Phi$:

$$\Phi=\frac1{s^2+1}+\frac{2s}{s^2+1}\Phi\;\Rightarrow\;\frac{(s-1)^2}{s^2+1}\Phi=\frac1{s^2+1}\;\Rightarrow\;\Phi=\frac1{(s-1)^2}\;\Rightarrow\;\varphi=te^{t}.$$

Check at $t=0$: $\varphi(0)=0=\sin0+0$ ✓.

6 · Before you close this tab

Retrieval: from memory, write the general-$g$ recipe, the antiderivative rule, and how to spot a convolution

General $g$: $y=y_{\text{hom}}+h*g$, $h=\mathcal L^{-1}\{1/(as^2+bs+c)\}$. ICs only enter $y_{\text{hom}}$.

Antiderivative: $\mathcal L\{\int_0^tf\}=F/s$ (from $1*f$). Not on the sheet.

Spot it: $t-\tau$ inside one factor, $\tau$ inside the other. $\int_0^t f(\tau)g(\tau)$ is not a convolution. $\delta*g=g$.

Integral equation: transform, solve for $Y$ algebraically, check $y(0)$.
