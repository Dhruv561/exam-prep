Lesson 16 · Topic 3: Laplace transforms

The Dirac delta: impulses and jumps in $y'$

The goal: solve $a\,y'' + b\,y' + c\,y = K\,\delta(t-t_0)$ with the Laplace transform, write the answer as a piecewise function, and know without solving that $y$ stays continuous while $y'$ jumps by $K/a$. Then use that to hit two-point conditions where $y'(0)$ is unknown.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: a Laplace question (about 12 marks) in 2017, 2021 and 2024 had a delta forcing. Typical asks: solve, give the answer piecewise, check continuity and differentiability at the impulse, sketch with intercepts, or find $y'(0)$ from a later condition. The delta needs only one new idea, and $\delta(t-a)$ is on your formula sheet.

0 · Warm-up from Lessons 13 and 15

W1. $\mathcal L^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{e^{-3s}}{s-2}\right\}$ is…

$\mathcal L^{-1}\{1/(s-2)\} = e^{2t}$, then t-shift: replace $t$ by $t-3$ everywhere and multiply by $H(t-3)$ (Lesson 15).

W2. $\mathcal L\{t\,H(t-1)\}$ is…

Rewrite first: $t\,H(t-1) = (t-1)H(t-1) + H(t-1)$. Shift each piece. Not on the sheet, derive it (Lesson 15).

1 · The delta in four lines

A unit-area pulse of width $\epsilon$ is $\delta_\epsilon(t) = 1/\epsilon$ on $(0,\epsilon]$ and $0$ elsewhere. As $\epsilon\to0$ it gets taller and thinner but keeps area $1$, and $\mathcal L\{\delta_\epsilon\} = \dfrac{1-e^{-\epsilon s}}{\epsilon s}\to 1$ (L'Hôpital). So we define $\delta(t)$ by:

  1. $\delta(t) = 0$ for $t\neq0$.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_0^a \delta(t)\,dt = 1$ for every $a>0$.
  3. $\mathcal L\{\delta(t)\} = 1$.

Sifting: for $c\ge0$, $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty f(t)\,\delta(t-c)\,dt = f(c)$. Taking $f=e^{-st}$ gives $$\mathcal L\{\delta(t-c)\} = e^{-cs}\qquad(\text{on the formula sheet}).$$
Booklet notation: $\delta_a(t) = \delta(t-a)$ and $H_a(t) = H(t-a)$.

Two look-alikes
$\mathcal L\{\delta(t-a)\} = e^{-as}$ but $\mathcal L\{H(t-a)\} = e^{-as}/s$. The delta has no $1/s$. And $\mathcal L^{-1}\{e^{-as}\} = \delta(t-a)$, not $H(t-a)$.

1. $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty (t^2+1)\,\delta(t-3)\,dt =$

Sifting: evaluate $t^2+1$ at $t=3$, giving $10$. Option (d) is $f(0)$, which is what you'd get from $\delta(t)$.

2. $\mathcal L^{-1}\{3e^{-4s}\}$ is…

No $1/s$ factor means no Heaviside. Read straight off the sheet entry $\delta(t-a)\leftrightarrow e^{-as}$ with $a=4$.

2 · What an impulse does: $y$ continuous, $y'$ jumps

Take $a\,y'' + b\,y' + c\,y = K\,\delta(t-t_0)$. Integrate over $[t_0-\eta,\,t_0+\eta]$ and let $\eta\to0$. The terms $b\,y'$ and $c\,y$ integrate to $0$ (they stay bounded), leaving $a\,[y']^{t_0^+}_{t_0^-} = K$.

Order of ODEAt $t=t_0$
$a\,y'' + \dots = K\delta(t-t_0)$$y$ continuous. $y'$ jumps by $\;K/a\;$ (so $y'(t_0^+) = y'(t_0^-) + K/a$).
$a\,y' + c\,y = K\delta(t-t_0)$$y$ itself jumps by $\;K/a$.

So an impulse is the same as restarting the solution at $t_0$ with the same $y$ and a kicked $y'$. Compare a Heaviside forcing $H(t-t_0)$, which jumps only $y''$: both $y$ and $y'$ stay continuous (Lecture 13, slide 5).

Use this as a check on every answer: compute $y(t_0^\pm)$ and $y'(t_0^\pm)$ from your piecewise formula and confirm the jump is $K/a$. It also answers the exam's "is $y$ differentiable at $t_0$?": no, $y'$ jumps, so $y$ has a corner.

3. For $\,2y'' + y' + 5y = 6\,\delta(t-1)$, $y(0)=y'(0)=0$, the jump in $y'$ at $t=1$ is…

$K/a = 6/2 = 3$. The $y'$ and $y$ terms contribute nothing to the jump.

4. A second-order ODE has forcing $K\delta(t-t_0)$. At $t_0$…

Integrating $y''$ once gives $y'$, which absorbs the jump; integrating again smooths it into a corner in $y$.

3 · Method and Example 1

  1. Transform with the derivative rules. Delta terms become $K e^{-t_0 s}$.
  2. Solve for $Y$: a part with no exponential (from ICs) plus a part $\times\,e^{-t_0 s}$.
  3. Partial fractions / complete the square on each part. Invert, t-shifting the $e^{-t_0s}$ part.
  4. Write piecewise (drop the $H$) and check the jump.

Example 1. Solve $\;9y'' + 6y' + y = 18\,\delta(t-3),\;\; y(0)=1,\; y'(0)=0.$ Give $y$ piecewise.

Transform: $\;9(s^2Y - s) + 6(sY - 1) + Y = 18e^{-3s}$, so $(3s+1)^2\,Y = 9s + 6 + 18e^{-3s}$ and, since $(3s+1)^2 = 9(s+\tfrac13)^2$, $$Y = \frac{s+\tfrac23}{(s+\tfrac13)^2} + \frac{2e^{-3s}}{(s+\tfrac13)^2}= \frac{1}{s+\tfrac13} + \frac{\tfrac13}{(s+\tfrac13)^2} + \frac{2e^{-3s}}{(s+\tfrac13)^2}.$$ Invert (s-shift of $1$ and $t$, then t-shift): $$y = e^{-t/3}\Big(1+\frac t3\Big) + 2(t-3)\,e^{-(t-3)/3}H(t-3).$$ $$\boxed{\,y=\begin{cases} e^{-t/3}\big(1+\tfrac t3\big), & 0\le t<3,\\[2pt] e^{-t/3}\big(1+\tfrac t3\big) + 2(t-3)\,e^{-(t-3)/3}, & t\ge3.\end{cases}}$$

Check. The extra term is $0$ at $t=3$, so $y$ is continuous. Its derivative at $t=3$ is $2$ (the $(t-3)$ factor differentiates to $1$, the rest is multiplied by $0$). Predicted jump: $K/a = 18/9 = 2$ ✓.

Non-unit leading coefficient
The jump is $K/a$, not $K$. In the transform, divide through by $a$ before reading off $\omega$: $9s^2+6s+1$ must become $(s+\tfrac13)^2$ before you invert, or you'll get the wrong exponent and the wrong factor.

4 · Example 2: sketching with intercepts

Example 2. Solve $\;y'' + 4y = 4\,\delta(t-\tfrac\pi2),\;\; y(0)=0,\; y'(0)=2,$ and sketch for $0\le t\le 2\pi$.

$(s^2+4)Y = 2 + 4e^{-\pi s/2}$, so $Y = \dfrac{2}{s^2+4} + 4e^{-\pi s/2}\dfrac{1}{s^2+4}$ and $$y = \sin 2t + 2\sin\!\big(2t-\pi\big)H(t-\tfrac\pi2) = \sin 2t - 2\sin 2t\,H(t-\tfrac\pi2).$$

$$y=\begin{cases}\sin 2t, & 0\le t<\pi/2,\\ -\sin 2t, & t\ge\pi/2.\end{cases}$$

Features to read off: zeros at $t=0,\tfrac\pi2,\pi,\tfrac{3\pi}2,2\pi$; $y(\tfrac\pi2)=0$ from both sides (continuous); $y'(\tfrac\pi2^-) = 2\cos\pi=-2$ and $y'(\tfrac\pi2^+) = -2\cos\pi = 2$, a jump of $4 = K/1$ ✓ (a V-shaped corner). The impulse flipped the sign of the oscillation.

5 · Example 3: two-point conditions (unknown $y'(0)$)

Pattern from 2024: one condition at $t=0$, the other at a later time. Keep $y'(0)=A$ symbolic, solve, then impose the later condition on the correct branch.

Example 3. $\;y'' + 16y = 8\,\delta(t-\tfrac\pi4),\;\; y(0)=0,\; y\big(\tfrac{3\pi}8\big)=3.$ Find $y$.

Let $y'(0)=A$. $(s^2+16)Y = A + 8e^{-\pi s/4}$, so $$y = \tfrac A4\sin4t + 2\sin(4t-\pi)H(t-\tfrac\pi4) = \Big(\tfrac A4 - 2H(t-\tfrac\pi4)\Big)\sin 4t.$$ Since $\tfrac{3\pi}8 > \tfrac\pi4$, use the second branch $(\tfrac A4-2)\sin4t$: $\;y(\tfrac{3\pi}8) = (\tfrac A4-2)\sin\tfrac{3\pi}2 = 2-\tfrac A4 = 3$, so $A=-4$. $$y=\begin{cases}-\sin 4t, & 0\le t<\pi/4,\\ -3\sin 4t, & t\ge\pi/4.\end{cases}$$ Check: $y'$ at $\pi/4$ goes from $-4\cos\pi=4$ to $-12\cos\pi=12$, a jump of $8=K/1$ ✓.

Wrong branch
Using the pre-impulse formula $\tfrac A4\sin4t$ at $t=\tfrac{3\pi}8$ gives $-\tfrac A4=3$, so $A=-12$. Always ask: is the condition time before or after $t_0$?

6 · Text to ODE, and impulse trains

WordsMaths
"released from rest at displacement $d$"$y(0)=d,\; y'(0)=0$
"given initial velocity $v$"$y'(0)=v$ (an IC, not a delta)
"struck at time $t_0$ with impulse $p$" (N·s, i.e. momentum)add $p\,\delta(t-t_0)$ to the right of $m y'' + c y' + k y = \dots$
"in the negative direction"$-p$
"starts from equilibrium at rest"$y(0)=y'(0)=0$

A velocity equation $m\,v' = p\sum_{k=0}^{n}\delta(t-k)$ transforms to $V = \dfrac pm\sum e^{-ks}/s$, so $v=\dfrac pm\sum_k H(t-k)$: every impulse adds a jump of $p/m$ to $v$. (Booklet 18.)

5. A 2 kg mass on a spring of stiffness 8 N/m is released from rest at displacement 1, then struck at $t=3$ with a 5 N·s impulse (positive direction). The model is…

(b) swaps the ICs. (c) is a switched-on force, not an impulse. (d) wrongly puts the later impulse into $y'(0)$.

6. For $\,y''+\omega^2y = K\delta(t-a)$, $y(0)=y'(0)=0$, the solution for $t>a$ is…

$\mathcal L^{-1}\{K/(s^2+\omega^2)\} = (K/\omega)\sin\omega t$, then t-shift. Check by the jump rule: $y'(a^+) = (K/\omega)\,\omega = K$ ✓.

7 · Practice (a notch above the notes)

Paper first, then open the solution.

P1. $\;y' + 2y = 6\,\delta(t-1) - 4\,\delta(t-2),\;\; y(0)=1.$ Give $y$ piecewise on three intervals, and state the jump in $y$ at $t=1$ and $t=2$.

P2. $\;y'' + 4y = 2H(t-\pi) + 4\,\delta(t-2\pi),\;\; y(0)=y'(0)=0.$ Give $y$ piecewise. Which of $y,y'$ is continuous at $\pi$, and at $2\pi$?

P1 solution

$sY - 1 + 2Y = 6e^{-s} - 4e^{-2s}$, so $Y = \dfrac{1 + 6e^{-s} - 4e^{-2s}}{s+2}$ and $y = e^{-2t} + 6e^{-2(t-1)}H(t-1) - 4e^{-2(t-2)}H(t-2).$

$$y=\begin{cases} e^{-2t}, & 0\le t<1,\\ e^{-2t}+6e^{-2(t-1)}, & 1\le t<2,\\ e^{-2t}+6e^{-2(t-1)}-4e^{-2(t-2)}, & t\ge2.\end{cases}$$

$y$ jumps by $+6$ at $t=1$ and $-4$ at $t=2$ ($K/a$ with $a=1$) ✓. First-order, so $y$ itself jumps.

P2 solution

$Y = \dfrac{1}{s^2+4}\Big(\dfrac{2e^{-\pi s}}{s} + 4e^{-2\pi s}\Big)$. Use $\dfrac{2}{s(s^2+4)} = \dfrac12\Big(\dfrac1s - \dfrac{s}{s^2+4}\Big)$. Then $y = \tfrac12\big(1-\cos2(t-\pi)\big)H(t-\pi) + 2\sin2(t-2\pi)\,H(t-2\pi)$, and $2\pi$-periodicity of $\sin2t$, $\cos2t$ removes the shifts inside:

$$y=\begin{cases}0, & 0\le t<\pi,\\ \tfrac12(1-\cos2t), & \pi\le t<2\pi,\\ \tfrac12(1-\cos2t)+2\sin2t, & t\ge2\pi.\end{cases}$$

At $t=\pi$ (Heaviside): $y$ and $y'$ both continuous ($y'=\sin2t\to0$ both sides). At $t=2\pi$ (delta): $y$ continuous but $y'$ goes from $0$ to $4\cos 4\pi = 4$, a jump of $4=K/a$ ✓.

8 · Exam-level challenge (12 marks)

About 18 minutes on paper, then open one part at a time.

A mass of 4 kg moves on a spring of stiffness 1 N/m with no damping; $y(t)$ is its displacement. At $t=0$ it is at $y=1$ moving with unknown velocity $v$. At $t=2\pi$ it is struck with an impulse of 8 N·s in the positive direction. It is observed that $y(3\pi)=6$.

(a) Write down the initial value problem for $y(t)$, with $y'(0)=v$. [2]
(b) Use Laplace transforms to solve it, in terms of $v$. Give $y$ piecewise. [4]
(c) Find $v$. [2]
(d) Show $y$ is continuous at $t=2\pi$, find the jump in $y'$ there, and confirm it equals $K/a$. [2]
(e) Give the amplitude of the oscillation before and after the impulse. [2]

(a) The IVP

$4y'' + y = 8\,\delta(t-2\pi),\quad y(0)=1,\; y'(0)=v.$ (Impulse $p$ goes on the right as $p\,\delta(t-t_0)$.)

(b) Solve

$4(s^2Y - s - v) + Y = 8e^{-2\pi s}$, so $Y = \dfrac{s+v}{s^2+\frac14} + \dfrac{2e^{-2\pi s}}{s^2+\frac14}$.

$y = \cos\tfrac t2 + 2v\sin\tfrac t2 + 4\sin\!\big(\tfrac t2-\pi\big)H(t-2\pi)$, and $\sin(\tfrac t2-\pi) = -\sin\tfrac t2$:

$$y=\begin{cases}\cos\tfrac t2 + 2v\sin\tfrac t2, & 0\le t<2\pi,\\ \cos\tfrac t2 + (2v-4)\sin\tfrac t2, & t\ge2\pi.\end{cases}$$
(c) Find $v$

$3\pi>2\pi$, so use the second branch: $y(3\pi) = \cos\tfrac{3\pi}2 + (2v-4)\sin\tfrac{3\pi}2 = -(2v-4) = 4-2v = 6$, so $v=-1$.

(d) Continuity and the jump

$y(2\pi^-) = \cos\pi + 2v\sin\pi = -1 = y(2\pi^+)$: continuous. $y' = -\tfrac12\sin\tfrac t2 + v\cos\tfrac t2$ before and $-\tfrac12\sin\tfrac t2 + (v-2)\cos\tfrac t2$ after. At $2\pi$: $y'^- = -v = 1$, $y'^+ = 2-v = 3$. Jump $=2 = K/a = 8/4$ ✓. So $y$ has a corner there (not differentiable).

(e) Amplitudes

$A\cos\tfrac t2 + B\sin\tfrac t2$ has amplitude $\sqrt{A^2+B^2}$. Before: $\sqrt{1+(2v)^2}=\sqrt5$. After: $\sqrt{1+(2v-4)^2}=\sqrt{1+36}=\sqrt{37}$. The impulse increased the amplitude.
