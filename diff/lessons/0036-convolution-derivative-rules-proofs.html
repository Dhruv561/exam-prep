Convolution, and the three proofs worth memorising

The one win: write the convolution-theorem proof in exam form (four lines), and use the theorem to get a transform you can't look up: $\mathcal F\{1/(a^2+x^2)^2\}$, a triangle, or the convolution of two Lorentzians.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: Topic 6 is the last question (7–15 marks). "Prove the convolution theorem", "show $\mathcal F\{f'\}=ik\hat f$" and "use convolution with a given pair to find a new transform" have each appeared. The formula sheet gives you the statements (convolution theorem, $\mathcal F\{f^{(n)}\}=(ik)^n\hat f$, $\mathcal F\{x^nf\}=i^n\hat f^{(n)}$) but not the proofs, and not the $\frac1{2\pi}$ "product in $x$" version.

0 · Warm-up from Lessons 34–35 and 17

W1. From the definition, $\mathcal F\{e^{-3|x|}\}=$…

$\int_{-\infty}^0e^{(3-ik)x}dx+\int_0^\infty e^{-(3+ik)x}dx=\frac1{3-ik}+\frac1{3+ik}=\frac{6}{9+k^2}$. Pattern $\frac{2a}{a^2+k^2}$.

W2. Using the $x$-shift theorem, $\mathcal F\{e^{-2|x-1|}\}=$…

$\mathcal F\{f(x-y)\}=e^{-iky}\hat f(k)$ with $y=1$. (c) is the $k$-shift, which comes from multiplying by $e^{ix}$.

W3. $\mathcal F^{-1}\{\cos 3k\}=$…

$\cos3k=\frac12(e^{3ik}+e^{-3ik})$ and $\mathcal F\{\delta_a\}=e^{-iak}$, so $e^{3ik}=\mathcal F\{\delta_{-3}\}$, $e^{-3ik}=\mathcal F\{\delta_3\}$. No $2\pi$: the pair is used, not the inverse integral.

W4 (Lesson 17, Laplace). With $(f*g)(t)=\int_0^tf(t-\tau)g(\tau)\,d\tau$, the convolution $1*e^{2t}$ is…

$\int_0^te^{2\tau}d\tau=\frac12(e^{2t}-1)$. Check: $\mathcal L=\frac1s\cdot\frac1{s-2}$ ✓. The Fourier version below is the same idea with limits $-\infty$ to $\infty$.

1 · Convolution on ℝ and the theorem

Definition (on the sheet, L29 s5): $\displaystyle (f*g)(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x-\xi)\,g(\xi)\,d\xi.$ Commutative ($f*g=g*f$, sub $u=x-\xi$).

Theorem (on the sheet): $\mathcal F\{f*g\}=\hat f(k)\,\hat g(k)$.

Laplace link (Booklet Q8): if $f,g$ vanish for $x<0$, the integrand is zero unless $0<\xi<x$, so the integral collapses to $\int_0^xf(x-\xi)g(\xi)d\xi$, the Lesson 17 convolution.

The proof, in exam form. Lecture 29 states the theorem without proof; past papers ask for it. Write exactly these lines:

  1. $\displaystyle\mathcal F\{f*g\}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x-\xi)g(\xi)\,d\xi\right]e^{-ikx}\,dx$ (definitions)
  2. $\displaystyle\phantom{\mathcal F\{f*g\}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\xi)\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x-\xi)\,e^{-ikx}\,dx\right]d\xi$ (swap order of integration)
  3. Inner integral, $\xi$ fixed: put $u=x-\xi$, $du=dx$, $x=u+\xi$, limits unchanged: $$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(u)\,e^{-ik(u+\xi)}\,du=e^{-ik\xi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(u)e^{-iku}\,du=e^{-ik\xi}\hat f(k).$$
  4. $\displaystyle\mathcal F\{f*g\}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\xi)\,e^{-ik\xi}\hat f(k)\,d\xi=\hat f(k)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\xi)e^{-ik\xi}d\xi=\hat f(k)\,\hat g(k).\qquad\blacksquare$

Marks sit on: the double integral, the swap, the substitution with $e^{-ik(u+\xi)}$ split, pulling $\hat f$ out. The swap is allowed because $f,g$ are absolutely integrable (one phrase is enough).

Drill: click the lines in proof order.

  1. Write $\mathcal F\{f*g\}$ as $\iint f(x-\xi)g(\xi)e^{-ikx}\,d\xi\,dx$
  2. Swap the order: $\xi$ outside, $x$ inside
  3. In the inner integral substitute $u=x-\xi$ ($\xi$ fixed)
  4. Split $e^{-ik(u+\xi)}=e^{-iku}e^{-ik\xi}$, recognise $\hat f(k)$
  5. Pull $\hat f(k)$ out; the remaining $\int g(\xi)e^{-ik\xi}d\xi$ is $\hat g(k)$

Q1. Spot the faulty line. A student writes:
(1) $\mathcal F\{f*g\}=\int\!\big[\int f(x-\xi)g(\xi)d\xi\big]e^{-ikx}dx$
(2) $=\int g(\xi)\big[\int f(x-\xi)e^{-ikx}dx\big]d\xi$
(3) inner, $u=x-\xi$: $\int f(u)e^{-ik(u-\xi)}du=e^{ik\xi}\hat f(k)$
(4) so $\mathcal F\{f*g\}=\hat f(k)\int g(\xi)e^{ik\xi}d\xi=\hat f(k)\hat g(-k)$.
The first wrong line is…

$u=x-\xi$ gives $x=u+\xi$, so $e^{-ikx}=e^{-ik(u+\xi)}$. The sign slip propagates into (4). Sanity check: the answer must be symmetric in $f,g$, and $\hat f(k)\hat g(-k)$ isn't.

2 · The other direction: product in $x$ ⇒ convolution in $k$

$$\boxed{\mathcal F\{f(x)g(x)\}=\frac1{2\pi}\,(\hat f*\hat g)(k)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat f(q)\,\hat g(k-q)\,dq}$$

Not on the sheet; 2-line proof. Replace $f$ by its inverse transform, $f(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\int\hat f(q)e^{iqx}dq$, and swap: $$\begin{aligned}\mathcal F\{fg\}&=\int g(x)\Big[\frac1{2\pi}\int\hat f(q)e^{iqx}dq\Big]e^{-ikx}dx\\&=\frac1{2\pi}\int\hat f(q)\Big[\int g(x)e^{-i(k-q)x}dx\Big]dq=\frac1{2\pi}\int\hat f(q)\,\hat g(k-q)\,dq.\end{aligned}$$

The $2\pi$ trap
Convolution in $x$ ⇒ plain product $\hat f\hat g$. Product in $x$ ⇒ convolution in $k$ with $\frac1{2\pi}$ (it comes from the inverse transform). Check any answer at $k=0$: $\hat h(0)=\int h\,dx$.

Q2. $\hat f(k)=\pi e^{-|k|}$ is $\mathcal F\{\frac1{1+x^2}\}$. Then $\mathcal F\{\frac1{(1+x^2)^2}\}(k)$ equals…

$\frac1{2\pi}\cdot\pi\cdot\pi=\frac\pi2$. (c) squares the transform, which is the rule for $\mathcal F\{f*f\}$, not $\mathcal F\{f^2\}$. (The integral is $(1+|k|)e^{-|k|}$, see §4.)

3 · Derivative and $x$-multiplication rules (L29 s9–11)

$\mathcal F\{f'\}=ik\hat f$ (Ex 6.7). By parts with $u=e^{-ikx}$, $dv=f'dx$: $$\begin{aligned}\mathcal F\{f'\}&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f'(x)e^{-ikx}dx=\Big[f(x)e^{-ikx}\Big]_{-\infty}^{\infty}-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)(-ik)e^{-ikx}dx\\&=0+ik\hat f(k).\end{aligned}$$ The boundary term vanishes because $|f(x)e^{-ikx}|=|f(x)|\to0$ as $x\to\pm\infty$ (say this: it's the mark). Repeat: $\mathcal F\{f''\}=(ik)^2\hat f=-k^2\hat f$ (needs $f'\to0$ too).

$\mathcal F\{xf\}=i\,\dfrac{d\hat f}{dk}$ (Ex 6.8). Since $\frac{d}{dk}e^{-ikx}=-ix\,e^{-ikx}$, $\;x\,e^{-ikx}=i\frac{d}{dk}e^{-ikx}$: $$\begin{aligned}\mathcal F\{xf\}&=\int f(x)\,x\,e^{-ikx}dx=\int f(x)\,i\frac{d}{dk}e^{-ikx}dx\\&=i\frac{d}{dk}\int f(x)e^{-ikx}dx=i\,\hat f'(k).\end{aligned}$$ (Differentiating under the integral needs $xf$ absolutely integrable.)

Worked. $\mathcal F\{x e^{-2|x|}\}=i\dfrac{d}{dk}\dfrac{4}{4+k^2}=i\cdot\dfrac{-8k}{(4+k^2)^2}=-\dfrac{8ik}{(4+k^2)^2}$. Odd function ⇒ purely imaginary, odd transform ✓.

Consistency check with a jump (Booklet Q7's last question). Box $b=H_{-a}-H_a$: $b'=\delta_{-a}-\delta_a$, so $\mathcal F\{b'\}=e^{iak}-e^{-iak}=2i\sin ak$, and $ik\cdot\hat b=ik\cdot\frac{2\sin ak}{k}=2i\sin ak$ ✓. Jumps in $f$ become deltas in $f'$.

Q3. Given $\mathcal F\{e^{-x^2}\}=\sqrt\pi\,e^{-k^2/4}$, $\;\mathcal F\{x e^{-x^2}\}=$…

$i\frac{d}{dk}\big(\sqrt\pi e^{-k^2/4}\big)=i\sqrt\pi\cdot(-\frac k2)e^{-k^2/4}$. Cross-check: $xe^{-x^2}=-\frac12(e^{-x^2})'$, so $\mathcal F=-\frac12\,ik\sqrt\pi e^{-k^2/4}$ ✓.
  1. $\mathcal F\{f'\}=\int_{-\infty}^\infty f'(x)e^{-ikx}dx$
  2. Integrate by parts: $\big[f e^{-ikx}\big]_{-\infty}^{\infty}+ik\int f e^{-ikx}dx$
  3. $f\to0$ as $x\to\pm\infty$ and $|e^{-ikx}|=1$, so the boundary term is $0$
  4. The remaining integral is $\hat f(k)$, so $\mathcal F\{f'\}=ik\hat f(k)$

4 · Using convolution to get new transforms

Recipe. Spot that the new function is $f*g$ (multiply the transforms) or $f\cdot g$ (convolve the transforms, $\frac1{2\pi}$). For $e^{-a|\cdot|}$-type integrands, split the line at every kink: at $0$ and at $k$.

(a) Triangle = box $*$ box

$b(x)=H_{-1}(x)-H_1(x)$ (height 1 on $|x|<1$). $(b*b)(x)=$ length of the overlap of $(-1,1)$ and $(x-1,x+1)$ $=2-|x|$ for $|x|\le2$, else $0$: a triangle. So $$\mathcal F\{(2-|x|)_+\}=\big(2\,\mathrm{sinc}\,k\big)^2=\frac{4\sin^2k}{k^2}.$$ Slide the flipped box across: the overlap length grows linearly, then shrinks.

Q4. $T(x)=6-|x|$ for $|x|\le6$, $0$ otherwise. $\hat T(k)=$…

$T=b*b$ with $b=H_{-3}-H_3$ (overlap length $6-|x|$), $\hat b=6\,\mathrm{sinc}\,3k$, so $\hat T=36\,\mathrm{sinc}^2 3k$. Check $k=0$: area of triangle $=\frac12\cdot12\cdot6=36$ ✓.

(b) $\mathcal F\{1/(4+x^2)^2\}$ by splitting the line

$f=\frac1{4+x^2}$, $\hat f=\frac\pi2e^{-2|k|}$. By §2, $$\mathcal F\{f^2\}=\frac1{2\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{4}\,I(k),\qquad I(k)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2|k-q|}e^{-2|q|}\,dq.$$ $I$ is even in $k$ (sub $q\to-q$), so take $k\ge0$; kinks at $q=0$ and $q=k$:

piece$|q|$, $|k-q|$integral
$q<0$$-q$, $k-q$$e^{-2k}\int_{-\infty}^0e^{4q}dq=\frac14e^{-2k}$
$0<q<k$$q$, $k-q$$\int_0^ke^{-2k}dq=k\,e^{-2k}$
$q>k$$q$, $q-k$$e^{2k}\int_k^\infty e^{-4q}dq=\frac14e^{-2k}$

$I(k)=\big(|k|+\frac12\big)e^{-2|k|}$, so $$\boxed{\mathcal F\Big\{\frac{1}{(4+x^2)^2}\Big\}=\frac{\pi}{8}\Big(|k|+\frac12\Big)e^{-2|k|}=\frac{\pi}{16}(1+2|k|)e^{-2|k|}}$$ Check $k=0$: $\int\frac{dx}{(4+x^2)^2}=\frac{\pi}{16}$ ✓ (general: $\frac{\pi}{2a^3}(1+a|k|)e^{-a|k|}$). Middle piece: the exponents cancel, so you get a bare length $k$; this is where the $|k|e^{-2|k|}$ term comes from.

Splitting traps
(1) Writing $e^{-2|k-q|}e^{-2|q|}=e^{-2|k|}$: false, $|k-q|+|q|\ne|k|$ outside $0<q<k$. (2) Doing only $k>0$ and forgetting to write $|k|$ in the final answer. (3) Dropping the $\frac1{2\pi}$.

(c) Computing a convolution via the transform

Lorentzians: $\frac1{1+x^2}*\frac1{4+x^2}$. Transform: $\pi e^{-|k|}\cdot\frac\pi2e^{-2|k|}=\frac{\pi^2}{2}e^{-3|k|}$. Since $e^{-3|k|}=\frac3\pi\mathcal F\{\frac1{9+x^2}\}$: $$\frac{1}{1+x^2}*\frac{1}{4+x^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{2}\cdot\frac{3}{\pi}\cdot\frac{1}{9+x^2}=\frac{3\pi}{2(9+x^2)}.$$ Rule of thumb: Lorentzian widths add ($1+2=3$); Gaussian $\frac1a$'s add.

Q5. Using $\mathcal F\{e^{-ax^2}\}=\sqrt{\pi/a}\,e^{-k^2/4a}$, $\;e^{-2x^2}*e^{-2x^2}=$…

Product: $\frac\pi2e^{-k^2/4}$. Match $e^{-k^2/4a}$: $a=1$, $\mathcal F\{e^{-x^2}\}=\sqrt\pi e^{-k^2/4}$. So $f*g=\frac{\pi/2}{\sqrt\pi}e^{-x^2}=\frac{\sqrt\pi}2e^{-x^2}$. Convolution spreads: width grows.

Q6. $\dfrac{1}{x^2+1}*\dfrac{1}{x^2+9}=$…

$\pi e^{-|k|}\cdot\frac\pi3e^{-3|k|}=\frac{\pi^2}3e^{-4|k|}=\frac{\pi^2}{3}\cdot\frac4\pi\mathcal F\{\frac1{x^2+16}\}$. Widths add: $1+3=4$, so $16$ not $10$.

5 · Practice (exam style)

P1 [3]. State a condition on $f$ and prove $\mathcal F\{f''\}=-k^2\hat f(k)$ directly from the definition.

P2 [4]. (a) Show that $T(x)=(4-|x|)_+$ is $b*b$ for a box $b$, and find $\hat T$. (b) Hence, using the inverse transform at $x=0$, evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin^2 2k}{k^2}dk$.

P3 [4]. Use the convolution theorem and partial fractions in $k$ to compute $e^{-|x|}*e^{-2|x|}$. Check your answer at $x=0$ directly.

P4 [2]. Find $\mathcal F\{x^2e^{-|x|}\}$ using the sheet's $\mathcal F\{x^nf\}$ rule.

P1 solution

Need $f,f'\to0$ as $x\to\pm\infty$ (and $f,f',f''$ absolutely integrable). By parts twice: $$\begin{aligned}\int f''e^{-ikx}dx&=\big[f'e^{-ikx}\big]_{-\infty}^{\infty}+ik\int f'e^{-ikx}dx\\&=ik\Big(\big[fe^{-ikx}\big]_{-\infty}^{\infty}+ik\int fe^{-ikx}dx\Big)=(ik)^2\hat f=-k^2\hat f,\end{aligned}$$ both boundary terms $0$ by decay.

P2 solution

(a) $b=H_{-2}-H_2$: overlap of $(-2,2)$ and $(x-2,x+2)$ has length $4-|x|$ for $|x|\le4$. $\hat b=4\,\mathrm{sinc}\,2k=\frac{2\sin2k}{k}$, so $\hat T=\frac{4\sin^22k}{k^2}$.
(b) $T(0)=4=\frac1{2\pi}\int\frac{4\sin^22k}{k^2}dk$ ⇒ $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{\sin^22k}{k^2}dk=2\pi$.

P3 solution

$\frac2{1+k^2}\cdot\frac4{4+k^2}=\frac{8}{(1+k^2)(4+k^2)}=\frac83\Big(\frac1{1+k^2}-\frac1{4+k^2}\Big)=\frac43\cdot\frac2{1+k^2}-\frac23\cdot\frac4{4+k^2}$. $$e^{-|x|}*e^{-2|x|}=\tfrac43e^{-|x|}-\tfrac23e^{-2|x|}.$$ $x=0$: $\int e^{-3|\xi|}d\xi=\frac23$, and $\frac43-\frac23=\frac23$ ✓.

P4 solution

$\mathcal F\{x^2f\}=i^2\hat f''=-\frac{d^2}{dk^2}\frac{2}{1+k^2}$. $\frac{d}{dk}=-\frac{4k}{(1+k^2)^2}$, $\frac{d^2}{dk^2}=\frac{-4(1+k^2)+16k^2}{(1+k^2)^3}=\frac{12k^2-4}{(1+k^2)^3}$. So $\mathcal F\{x^2e^{-|x|}\}=\dfrac{4-12k^2}{(1+k^2)^3}$. Check $k=0$: $\int x^2e^{-|x|}dx=4$ ✓.

6 · Exam-level challenge (14 marks, about 22 minutes)

(a) Define $(f*g)(x)$ and prove that $\mathcal F\{f*g\}=\hat f(k)\hat g(k)$. [4]
(b) Using the inverse Fourier transform, show that $\mathcal F\{f(x)g(x)\}=\dfrac1{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat f(q)\,\hat g(k-q)\,dq$. [2]
(c) You may use $\mathcal F\Big\{\dfrac1{a^2+x^2}\Big\}=\dfrac\pi a e^{-a|k|}$ ($a>0$). Use part (b) to show that $$\mathcal F\Big\{\frac1{(1+x^2)(9+x^2)}\Big\}=\frac{\pi}{24}\big(3e^{-|k|}-e^{-3|k|}\big).$$ [5]
(d) Verify (c) using partial fractions. [1]
(e) Hence evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos 2x}{(1+x^2)(9+x^2)}\,dx$. [2]

Full marking-style solution: worked/0036-challenge-full-solution.html. Attempt every part first.

Answer key (quick check)

(c) For $k\ge0$: $\int e^{-3|k-q|}e^{-|q|}dq=\frac14e^{-3k}+\frac12(e^{-k}-e^{-3k})+\frac14e^{-k}=\frac34e^{-k}-\frac14e^{-3k}$; times $\frac1{2\pi}\cdot\frac\pi3\cdot\pi=\frac\pi6$.
(d) $\frac1{(1+x^2)(9+x^2)}=\frac18\big(\frac1{1+x^2}-\frac1{9+x^2}\big)$. (e) $\frac{\pi}{24}\big(3e^{-2}-e^{-6}\big)\approx0.0528$.
