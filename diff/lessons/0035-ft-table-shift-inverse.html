Lesson 35 · Topic 6: Fourier transforms · Table, shifts, inverses

Transforms without integrating: the table, two shifts, and recognition

The one win: find a forward or inverse FT by matching it to a table pair plus at most one shift, with no integration, and evaluate a real integral like $\int\cos(bx)e^{-ax^2}dx$ straight from a given pair.

Why this matters for 5 Nov: Topic 6 is the last question on the paper (7–15 marks; masked 2026 Q7 = 14). Past papers have had an inverse of $\sin(ak)$ or $\cos(ak)$ (answer: deltas), and "use the given transform $\mathcal F\{e^{-ax^2}\}$ to evaluate $\int\cos(\cdot)e^{-(\cdot)x^2}dx$". The formula sheet has the definitions, the derivative/$x^n$ rules and convolution, but no table and no shift theorems. So you need to memorise them or be able to rederive them in a few lines.

0 · Warm-up from Lessons 34 and 15

W1. From the definition, $\mathcal F\{\delta(x+2)\}=$…

$\int\delta(x+2)e^{-ikx}dx=e^{-ik(-2)}=e^{2ik}$ (sifting at $x=-2$). The $2\pi$ belongs to the inverse transform.

W2. $\mathcal F\{H_{-3}(x)-H_3(x)\}=$…

$\int_{-3}^3e^{-ikx}dx=\frac{e^{3ik}-e^{-3ik}}{ik}=\frac{2\sin3k}{k}=6\,\mathrm{sinc}(3k)$. Use the half-width $3$, not the width $6$.

W3. $\mathcal F\{e^{-4|x|}\}=$…

$\frac1{4+ik}+\frac1{4-ik}=\frac{8}{16+k^2}$, i.e. $\frac{2a}{a^2+k^2}$ with $a=4$.

W4. $f=H_0-H_2$ (so $f=1$ on $(0,2)$, else $0$). The inverse FT of $\hat f$, evaluated at $x=2$, equals…

At a jump, $\mathcal F^{-1}\mathcal F f$ gives the midpoint $\frac12\big(f(2^-)+f(2^+)\big)=\frac12(1+0)$, whatever value $f(2)$ was given (L28).

W5 (spaced, Lesson 15). $\mathcal L^{-1}\Big\{\dfrac{e^{-3s}}{s^2+4}\Big\}=$…

Strip $e^{-3s}$, invert $\frac{1}{s^2+4}=\frac12\cdot\frac{2}{s^2+4}\to\frac12\sin2t$, then shift $t\to t-3$ and attach $H(t-3)$. The FT version below is the same idea, but the FT needs no $H$ because $x$ runs over all of $\mathbb R$.

1 · The table: what to memorise, how to rederive

Convention (on the sheet): $\hat f(k)=\int_{-\infty}^\infty f(x)e^{-ikx}dx$, $\;f(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty\hat f(k)e^{ikx}dk$. The table is L29 s3. None of it is on the formula sheet.

Duality (not named in the slides, but it is how the right-hand column follows from the left). If $\mathcal F\{f\}=g$ then $$\mathcal F\{g(x)\}(k)=2\pi f(-k).$$ Proof. $f(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\int g(q)e^{iqx}dq$. Put $x=-k$: $2\pi f(-k)=\int g(q)e^{-ikq}dq=\mathcal F\{g\}(k)$. ∎
Use. $\mathcal F\{\frac{2a}{a^2+x^2}\}=2\pi e^{-a|k|}$ ⇒ $\mathcal F\{\frac1{a^2+x^2}\}=\frac\pi ae^{-a|k|}$. Box: $\mathcal F\{2a\,\mathrm{sinc}(ax)\}=2\pi\cdot\mathbb 1_{|k|<a}$ ⇒ the sinc row.

Gaussian in 3 lines, using only sheet rules. $f=e^{-ax^2}$ satisfies $f'=-2ax\,f$. Transform with $\mathcal F\{f'\}=ik\hat f$ and $\mathcal F\{xf\}=i\hat f'$: $$ik\hat f=-2a\,i\hat f'\;\Rightarrow\;\hat f'=-\frac{k}{2a}\hat f\;\Rightarrow\;\hat f=\hat f(0)\,e^{-k^2/4a},$$ $$\hat f(0)=\int e^{-ax^2}dx=\sqrt{\pi/a}.$$

Q1. $\mathcal F\Big\{\dfrac{\sin 2x}{x}\Big\}=$…

$\frac{\sin2x}{x}=2\,\mathrm{sinc}(2x)\leftrightarrow2\cdot\frac\pi2(H_{-2}-H_2)$. The box in $k$ has half-width $a=2$, the frequency of the sine.

2 · Two shift theorems, with 2-line proofs

$x$-shift. $\;\mathcal F\{f(x-y)\}=e^{-iky}\hat f(k)$, $\;y\in\mathbb R$.
Proof. $\int f(x-y)e^{-ikx}dx\overset{u=x-y}{=}\int f(u)e^{-ik(u+y)}du=e^{-iky}\hat f(k)$. ∎

$k$-shift (modulation). $\;\mathcal F\{e^{iqx}f(x)\}=\hat f(k-q)$, $\;q\in\mathbb R$.
Proof. $\int f(x)e^{iqx}e^{-ikx}dx=\int f(x)e^{-i(k-q)x}dx=\hat f(k-q)$. ∎

Consequences (write $\cos,\sin$ as exponentials, then use the $k$-shift): $$\mathcal F\{\cos(qx)f\}=\tfrac12\big[\hat f(k-q)+\hat f(k+q)\big],$$ $$\mathcal F\{\sin(qx)f\}=\tfrac1{2i}\big[\hat f(k-q)-\hat f(k+q)\big].$$ In $k$-space, multiplying by $\cos(ky)$ means averaging two $x$-shifts: $\cos(ky)\hat f\leftrightarrow\frac12[f(x+y)+f(x-y)]$.

Sign check with $\delta$
Unsure of the sign? $\delta(x-y)$ is $\delta$ shifted by $y$, and $\mathcal F\{\delta(x-y)\}=e^{-iky}$. So a shift to the right gives a minus in the exponent. Also: $\mathcal F\{f(x-y)\}$ is not $\hat f(k-y)$. A shift in one variable becomes a phase factor in the other. Unlike Laplace there is no $H$ to attach.

Worked (L29 s8 style). (a) $\mathcal F\{e^{-2|x+1|}\}$. (b) $\mathcal F\{\frac{\cos3x}{1+x^2}\}$. (c) $\mathcal F\{\frac{1}{x^2-4x+5}\}$.

Solutions

(a) $e^{-2|x|}\leftrightarrow\frac4{4+k^2}$; shift $y=-1$: $\;\dfrac{4e^{ik}}{4+k^2}$.

(b) $\frac1{1+x^2}\leftrightarrow\pi e^{-|k|}$; cosine rule with $q=3$: $\;\dfrac\pi2\big(e^{-|k-3|}+e^{-|k+3|}\big)$.

(c) Complete the square: $x^2-4x+5=(x-2)^2+1$. So $f=g(x-2)$ with $g=\frac1{1+x^2}$: $\;\pi e^{-2ik}e^{-|k|}$.

(All three checked numerically against the definition.)

Q2. $\mathcal F\Big\{\dfrac{1}{x^2+2x+10}\Big\}=$…

$(x+1)^2+9$: this is $\frac1{x^2+9}$ shifted by $y=-1$, so the factor is $e^{-ik(-1)}=e^{ik}$. Here $a=3$, not $9$. (c) uses a $k$-shift, but the shift is in $x$.

Q3. $\mathcal F\{\sin(2x)\,e^{-|x|}\}=$…

$\hat f=\frac2{1+k^2}$. Sine rule: $\frac1{2i}\big[\frac2{1+(k-2)^2}-\frac2{1+(k+2)^2}\big]$, and $\frac{2}{2i}=-i$. Check: $f$ is real and odd, so $\hat f$ must be purely imaginary and odd ✓.

3 · Inverse transforms by recognition

  1. Rewrite. Trig in $k$ → complex exponentials. Quadratics in $k$ → complete the square.
  2. Peel off factors $e^{-iyk}$: each one is an $x$-shift by $y$ in the answer.
  3. Argument $k-q$ instead of $k$: a $k$-shift, so multiply the answer by $e^{iqx}$.
  4. Match the core to a table pair and fix the constant (e.g. $\frac1{k^2+a^2}=\frac1{2a}\cdot\frac{2a}{k^2+a^2}$).
  5. Check one property: real/even $f$ ⇔ real/even $\hat f$; real/odd $f$ ⇔ imaginary/odd $\hat f$.

Trig in $k$ → deltas (L29 s4, a past-exam favourite). From $\delta_{\mp a}\leftrightarrow e^{\pm iak}$: $$\cos(ak)=\tfrac12\big(e^{iak}+e^{-iak}\big)\;\Rightarrow\;\mathcal F^{-1}\{\cos ak\}=\tfrac12\big[\delta(x+a)+\delta(x-a)\big],$$ $$\sin(ak)=\tfrac1{2i}\big(e^{iak}-e^{-iak}\big)\;\Rightarrow\;\mathcal F^{-1}\{\sin ak\}=\tfrac{i}2\big[\delta(x-a)-\delta(x+a)\big].$$ In an exam, write the line "$=\frac1{2i}\mathcal F\{\delta(x+a)-\delta(x-a)\}$" and cite linearity. That line earns the method mark.

Worked. Find $\mathcal F^{-1}$ of (a) $\dfrac{e^{-3ik}}{k^2+4}$, (b) $\dfrac1{k^2+2k+5}$, (c) $e^{-(k-2)^2}$, (d) $\dfrac{\sin 2k}{k}$, (e) $\cos(k)\,e^{-|k|}$.

Solutions

(a) $\frac1{k^2+4}=\frac14\cdot\frac{4}{4+k^2}=\mathcal F\{\frac14e^{-2|x|}\}$; the factor $e^{-3ik}$ shifts by $3$: $\;\frac14e^{-2|x-3|}$.

(b) $(k+1)^2+4$ is $\hat g(k-q)$ with $q=-1$, $\hat g=\frac1{k^2+4}$: $\;\frac14e^{-ix}e^{-2|x|}$.

(c) Gaussian with $\frac1{4a}=1$, so $a=\frac14$: $\mathcal F\{e^{-x^2/4}\}=\sqrt{4\pi}\,e^{-k^2}$, so $\mathcal F^{-1}\{e^{-k^2}\}=\frac{e^{-x^2/4}}{2\sqrt\pi}$. Then the $k$-shift with $q=2$: $\;\dfrac{e^{2ix}e^{-x^2/4}}{2\sqrt\pi}$.

(d) $\frac{2\sin2k}{k}\leftrightarrow$ box on $|x|<2$, so $\frac{\sin2k}{k}\leftrightarrow\frac12\big(H_{-2}(x)-H_2(x)\big)$.

(e) $\mathcal F^{-1}\{e^{-|k|}\}=\frac1{\pi(1+x^2)}$ (duality). $\cos k$ averages the shifts by $\pm1$: $\;\dfrac1{2\pi}\Big[\dfrac1{1+(x+1)^2}+\dfrac1{1+(x-1)^2}\Big]$.

Q4. $\mathcal F^{-1}\{\cos(2k)\,e^{-k^2}\}=$…

$e^{-k^2}\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{2\sqrt\pi}e^{-x^2/4}$, and $\cos2k$ gives $\frac12[\,\cdot\,(x+2)+\cdot\,(x-2)]$. (b) forgot the $\frac12$, and (d) used $e^{-x^2}$ instead of $e^{-x^2/4}$.

Recognition drill

Random parameters, so every set is different. Each item is one table pair plus at most one shift. Aim for 7/8 at about 20 s per item.

4 · Real integrals from a known pair

Set $k$ to a number in the definition. For real $g$: $$\int_{-\infty}^\infty g\,dx=\hat g(0),\qquad \int g\cos(bx)\,dx=\tfrac12\big[\hat g(b)+\hat g(-b)\big],$$ $$\int g\sin(bx)\,dx=\tfrac{i}{2}\big[\hat g(b)-\hat g(-b)\big].$$ For even $g$ the cosine integral is just $\hat g(b)$. Going the other way, $f(0)=\frac1{2\pi}\int\hat f(k)\,dk$.
Why: $\hat g(\pm b)=\int g\cos bx\,dx\mp i\int g\sin bx\,dx$. Add or subtract.

Worked A. Given $\mathcal F\{e^{-ax^2}\}=\sqrt{\pi/a}\,e^{-k^2/4a}$, evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\cos(3x)e^{-2x^2}dx$.

Solution

$g=e^{-2x^2}$ is even and real, so $\int g\cos3x\,dx=\int g\,(\cos3x-i\sin3x)\,dx=\hat g(3)$, because the $\sin$ part integrates an odd function to $0$. With $a=2$: $\hat g(3)=\sqrt{\pi/2}\,e^{-9/8}$.

Worked B. Show $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{\sin k}{k}dk=\pi$.

Solution

$f=H_{-1}-H_1$ has $\hat f=\frac{2\sin k}{k}$. $f$ is continuous at $0$ with $f(0)=1$, so $1=f(0)=\frac1{2\pi}\int\frac{2\sin k}{k}dk$, which gives $\int\frac{\sin k}k\,dk=\pi$. (At $x=1$ the same integral returns the midpoint $\frac12$, as in Lesson 34.)

Matching the given pair
For $e^{-x^2/3}$ the parameter is $a=\frac13$, so $\sqrt{\pi/a}=\sqrt{3\pi}$ and $e^{-k^2/4a}=e^{-3k^2/4}$. Write "$a=\dots$" explicitly before substituting. Most lost marks here are slips in reading off $a$.

Q5. $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\cos 2x}{x^2+4}\,dx=$…

$\frac1{x^2+4}\leftrightarrow\frac\pi2e^{-2|k|}$, even, so the integral is $\hat g(2)=\frac\pi2e^{-4}$.

5 · Practice (exam level)

P1. Find $\mathcal F\{e^{-|x-1|}+e^{-|x+1|}\}$ and simplify it to a real expression.

P2. Find $\mathcal F^{-1}$ of (a) $\hat f(k)=4\sin k\cos k$, (b) $\hat g(k)=\dfrac{2i\sin k}{1+k^2}$.

P3. Using $\mathcal F\{e^{-ax^2}\}=\sqrt{\pi/a}\,e^{-k^2/4a}$, evaluate $\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-x^2/3}dx$ and $\int_{-\infty}^\infty\cos(2x)e^{-x^2/3}dx$.

P4. Using $\mathcal F\{\frac1{1+x^2}\}=\pi e^{-|k|}$, evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{\cos 3x}{x^2+2x+2}\,dx$.

P1 solution

$e^{-|x|}\leftrightarrow\frac2{1+k^2}$; shifts by $\pm1$ give $e^{\mp ik}$: $\;\frac{2}{1+k^2}(e^{-ik}+e^{ik})=\dfrac{4\cos k}{1+k^2}$. Real and even, as it must be: $f$ is real and even.

P2 solution

(a) $4\sin k\cos k=2\sin2k$, so $f=2\cdot\frac i2[\delta(x-2)-\delta(x+2)]=i\big[\delta(x-2)-\delta(x+2)\big]$.

(b) $2i\sin k=e^{ik}-e^{-ik}$, so $\hat g=\frac{e^{ik}}{1+k^2}-\frac{e^{-ik}}{1+k^2}$. Since $\frac1{1+k^2}\leftrightarrow\frac12e^{-|x|}$, the shifts give $\;g=\frac12\big(e^{-|x+1|}-e^{-|x-1|}\big)$. Check: $g$ is real and odd, and $\hat g$ is imaginary and odd ✓.

P3 solution

$a=\frac13$: $\hat g(k)=\sqrt{3\pi}\,e^{-3k^2/4}$. $\int g=\hat g(0)=\sqrt{3\pi}$. $g$ is even, so $\int g\cos2x=\hat g(2)=\sqrt{3\pi}\,e^{-3}$.

P4 solution

$g=\frac1{(x+1)^2+1}$ is $\frac1{1+x^2}$ shifted by $-1$: $\hat g=\pi e^{ik}e^{-|k|}$. $g$ is not even, so use the general rule: $$\int g\cos3x\,dx=\tfrac12[\hat g(3)+\hat g(-3)]=\tfrac\pi2e^{-3}(e^{3i}+e^{-3i})=\pi e^{-3}\cos3.$$ (Check by substituting $u=x+1$: $\cos(3u-3)=\cos3u\cos3+\sin3u\sin3$, and the $\sin$ term integrates to $0$. Numerically $\approx-0.1548$ ✓.)

6 · Exam-level challenge (15 marks, about 22 minutes)

You may use $\mathcal F\{e^{-ax^2}\}=\sqrt{\pi/a}\;e^{-k^2/4a}$ for $a>0$. Any other transform you use must be derived.

(a) Prove that for real $q$, $\mathcal F\{e^{iqx}f(x)\}=\hat f(k-q)$, and deduce a formula for $\mathcal F\{\cos(qx)f(x)\}$. [3]
(b) Find the inverse Fourier transform of $\hat g(k)=e^{-k^2+2k}$. [3]
(c) Find the inverse Fourier transform of $\hat h(k)=\dfrac{\cos 2k}{k^2+1}$. [4]
(d) Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos(4x)\,e^{-2x^2}\,dx$. [2]
(e) Using $\mathcal F\{xf\}=i\,\dfrac{d\hat f}{dk}$, evaluate $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\sin(2x)\,e^{-2x^2}\,dx$. [3]

Full marking-style solution: worked/0035-challenge-full-solution.html. Attempt every part first.

Answer key (quick check)

(a) $\mathcal F\{\cos(qx)f\}=\frac12[\hat f(k-q)+\hat f(k+q)]$. (b) $g(x)=\dfrac{e^{1+ix-x^2/4}}{2\sqrt\pi}$. (c) $h(x)=\frac14\big(e^{-|x+2|}+e^{-|x-2|}\big)$.
(d) $\sqrt{\pi/2}\,e^{-2}$. (e) $\frac12\sqrt{\pi/2}\,e^{-1/2}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4}e^{-1/2}$.
