Integrating factor · Lesson 1

$$y' + f(t)\,y = g(t) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad I = e^{\int f\,dt},\qquad (I y)' = I g,\qquad y = \frac{1}{I}\left(\int I g\,dt + C\right)$$
  1. Divide so that the coefficient of $y'$ is 1
  2. $I = e^{\int f}$ (no constant needed)
  3. $(Iy)' = Ig$
  4. Integrate, add $C$ before dividing by $I$, apply the IC, check by substituting
Useful $\int f$$I$
$f = a/t$$t^{a}$ (for $t>0$)
$f = \tan t$$\sec t$
$f = \dfrac{kt}{t^2+1}$$(t^2+1)^{k/2}$
$f = 2t$$e^{t^2}$

Interval of validity · Lesson 1

  1. Standard form: divide by the leading coefficient ($y'$ or $y''$)
  2. Bad points: zeros of what you divided by, denominators in $g$, $\ln(\le 0)$, $\sqrt{<0}$, $\tan$ asymptotes
  3. Answer: the largest open interval between bad points that contains $t_0$. If $t_0$ is a bad point, there's no guarantee

By parts (needed all the time): $\int t e^{t}\,dt = (t-1)e^t$,   $\int t^2 e^t\,dt = (t^2-2t+2)e^t$.

General solution structure · Lesson 2

$y = y_c + y_p$. Complementary solutions ($Ty_c=0$) form an $n$-dimensional space for an $n$th-order ODE: $y_c = C_1y_1 + C_2y_2$ with $y_1,y_2$ linearly independent. Any one particular solution ($Ty_p = g$) will do: two choices differ by a $y_c$.

Reduction of order · Lesson 2

$$y = uv,\quad w = u':\qquad w' + \Big(\frac{2v'}{v} + f_1\Big)w = \frac{g}{v},\qquad I = v^2e^{\int f_1},\qquad (Iw)' = \frac{Ig}{v}$$
  1. Standard form: divide by the $y''$ coefficient to get $f_1$, $g$
  2. $I = v^2 e^{\int f_1}$, then $Iw = \int \frac{Ig}{v}\,dt + C_1$
  3. $u = \int w\,dt + C_2$, then $y = uv$. Keep both constants; multiply by $v$
  4. Read off $y_c$ and $y_p$, apply ICs, check $y_c$ by substituting

Wronskian & Abel · Lesson 3

$$\operatorname{wr}[y_1,y_2] = y_1y_2' - y_1'y_2, \qquad \operatorname{wr}(t) = \operatorname{wr}(t_0)\,e^{-\int_{t_0}^t f_1} \;\;(\text{standard form; } n\text{th order: use } f_{n-1})$$
  • $\operatorname{wr}\ne0$ at some $t$ ⇒ independent. For two solutions, wr is either never 0 or always 0. (Not for arbitrary functions: $t^2$, $t|t|$.)
  • Second solution: $(y_2/y_1)' = \operatorname{wr}/y_1^2$.   Property proofs: evaluate wr at a clever $t_0$ (shared zero or shared max ⇒ dependent).

Particular solutions · Lesson 7

  • Decide: told "reduction of order", or variable coefficients → machine. Constant-coeff. or Euler with $e^{kt}$ / polynomial / trig / $t^k$ forcing → shortcut. System → diagonalise.
  • Machine: $y = uv$ always. $g$ after dividing. Pick $v$ matching the forcing. The constant-free part of $uv$ is $y_p$.
  • Shortcut: $e^{kt}\to Ae^{kt}$, degree-$m$ poly $\to$ full degree-$m$ poly, $\cos\omega t$ or $\sin\omega t \to A\cos\omega t + B\sin\omega t$. Euler: $t^k\to At^k$.
  • Resonance: exponent is an $s$-fold root → multiply by $t^s$ (Euler: $(\ln t)^s$). If your guess substitutes to $0$, that's the sign.

Systems & eigenvalues · Lesson 4

  • $y'' + by' + cy = g \;\leftrightarrow\; \mathbf y' = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-c&-b\end{pmatrix}\mathbf y + \begin{pmatrix}0\\g\end{pmatrix}$,   $\mathbf y = (y, y')$. Bottom row = negated coefficients.
  • Back to one ODE: $x'' - (\operatorname{tr}A)x' + (\det A)x = 0$. Eigenvalues: $\lambda^2 - (\operatorname{tr}A)\lambda + \det A = 0$.
  • $\mathbf y = C_1\mathbf v_1e^{\lambda_1t} + C_2\mathbf v_2e^{\lambda_2t}$. 2×2 eigenvector: row $(p, q)$ of $A-\lambda I$ ⇒ $\mathbf v = (q, -p)$.
  • Complex $\alpha\pm i\beta$: take one $\lambda$, expand $\mathbf v e^{\alpha t}(\cos\beta t + i\sin\beta t)$, keep Re and Im parts.
  • wr of vector solutions $= \det(\mathbf y_1\ \mathbf y_2)$. ICs: solve $C_1\mathbf v_1 + C_2\mathbf v_2 = \mathbf y_0$.

Inhomogeneous systems & $e^{At}$ · Lesson 6

$$A = SDS^{-1},\ \mathbf z = S^{-1}\mathbf y:\quad \mathbf z' = D\mathbf z + S^{-1}\mathbf b,\quad \mathbf z(0) = S^{-1}\mathbf y_0,\quad \mathbf y = S\mathbf z$$ $$\mathbf y' = A\mathbf y,\ \mathbf y(0) = \mathbf y_0 \Rightarrow \mathbf y = e^{At}\mathbf y_0, \qquad e^{At} = Se^{Dt}S^{-1}$$
  • $S^{-1}$ (2×2) $= \frac{1}{\det S}\begin{pmatrix}s_{22}&-s_{12}\\-s_{21}&s_{11}\end{pmatrix}$. Columns of $S$ in the same order as $D$.
  • Resonance: $z' = \lambda z + e^{\lambda t}$ ⇒ $z = (t + C)e^{\lambda t}$.
  • $e^{(\lambda I + N)t} = e^{\lambda t}(I + Nt + \tfrac{t^2}{2}N^2 + \dots)$; $\;\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&1\\0&\lambda\end{pmatrix}\to e^{\lambda t}\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$; $\;\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&-\beta\\ \beta&\alpha\end{pmatrix}\to e^{\alpha t}\begin{pmatrix}\cos\beta t&-\sin\beta t\\ \sin\beta t&\cos\beta t\end{pmatrix}$.
  • $e^{A+B} = e^Ae^B$ only if $AB = BA$. Check: $e^{A\cdot0} = I$, and $\frac{d}{dt}e^{At}|_0 = A$.
