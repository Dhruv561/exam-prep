Topic 4: Fourier series

Lessons 21–26, lectures 15–19. [S] = on the formula sheet (only the coefficient formulas and product-to-sum identities). memorise = not on the sheet.

Coefficients [S]

$$f(x)\sim\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n\ge1}\Big(a_n\cos\tfrac{n\pi x}{L}+b_n\sin\tfrac{n\pi x}{L}\Big)$$ $$a_n=\frac1L\!\int_{-L}^{L}\!f\cos\tfrac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx,\ \ b_n=\frac1L\!\int_{-L}^{L}\!f\sin\tfrac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx$$

Period $2L$, $k_n=n\pi/L$. $a_0$ comes from the $a_n$ formula with $n=0$, so $\frac{a_0}{2}$ is the average of $f$. Compute $a_0$ separately: the general $a_n$ often has $n$ in a denominator.

Parity shortcuts memorise: $f$ even $\Rightarrow b_n=0$, $a_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx$. $f$ odd $\Rightarrow a_n=0$ (incl. $a_0$), $b_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx$. Say "by parity" in one line, then compute only the non-zero ones.

Extensions & half-range

$f$ given on $(0,L)$. Even extension: $F(x)=f(-x)$ on $(-L,0)$, then $2L$-periodic $\Rightarrow$ cosine series. Odd extension: $F(x)=-f(-x)$ $\Rightarrow$ sine series. Same half-range formulas as above.

  • "$f(-x)=\pm f(x)$, $f(x+2a)=f(x)$" means $L=a$ and the extension is fixed for you.
  • Sketch 3 periods. Mark jumps with an open/closed dot and the midpoint value.
  • Odd ext. jumps at $x=0$ if $f(0^+)\ne0$, and at $x=L$ if $f(L^-)\ne0$. Even ext. has no jumps from reflection (only kinks).
  • Piecewise $f$: split the integral at each break; zero pieces drop out.

Integrals & values memorise

$$\int x\cos ax\,dx=\frac{x\sin ax}{a}+\frac{\cos ax}{a^2}$$ $$\int x\sin ax\,dx=-\frac{x\cos ax}{a}+\frac{\sin ax}{a^2}$$
$\sin n\pi$$\cos n\pi$$1-(-1)^n$$\sin\frac{n\pi}2$, $n=1,2,3,4$$\cos\frac{n\pi}2$
$0$$(-1)^n$$0$ even, $2$ odd$1,0,-1,0$$0,-1,0,1$

Products [S]: $\sin A\cos B=\tfrac12[\sin(A-B)+\sin(A+B)]$, $\cos A\cos B=\tfrac12[\cos(A-B)+\cos(A+B)]$, $\sin A\sin B=\tfrac12[\cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)]$. With $\sin x$ or $\cos x$ in $f$: the $(1-n)$ term divides by $0$ at $n=1$, so do $n=1$ separately. Non-integer frequency ($\cos\frac x2$): no special case, gives $\frac1{n-\frac12}\pm\frac1{n+\frac12}$ terms.

Known on $(-L,L]$: $x=\sum\frac{2L(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}\sin\frac{n\pi x}L$, $\ x^2=\frac{L^2}3+\sum\frac{4L^2(-1)^n}{n^2\pi^2}\cos\frac{n\pi x}L$. On $(-\pi,\pi]$: $|x|$: $a_0=\pi$, $a_n=\frac{2((-1)^n-1)}{\pi n^2}$; $\operatorname{sgn}x$: $b_n=\frac{2(1-(-1)^n)}{n\pi}$.

Pointwise convergence memorise

Dirichlet (lect. 17): $f$ piecewise $C^1$ on $(-L,L]$ $\Rightarrow$ FS converges to the periodic extension $F(x)$ where $F$ is continuous, and to $\tfrac12\big[F(x^-)+F(x^+)\big]$ at a jump.

At the ends: value at $x=\pm L$ is $\tfrac12[f(L^-)+f(-L^+)]$. Answer "what does it converge to" as a piecewise function over one period, then "extended $2L$-periodically".

Evaluating $\sum$: plug in $x$ where cos/sin are simple ($0$, $L$, $L/2$), set series = $F$ (or midpoint), rearrange. Show the line; quoting is not enough.

$\sum\frac1{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}6$$\sum\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{12}$$\sum\frac1{(2k-1)^2}=\frac{\pi^2}8$
$\sum\frac1{n^4}=\frac{\pi^4}{90}$$\sum\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{2k-1}=\frac\pi4$(answers to check against)

Gibbs phenomenon memorise

Occurs iff $F$ has a jump, near each jump (incl. $x=\pm L$ if $f(L^-)\ne f(-L^+)$). Partial sums overshoot by $\approx 9\%$ of the jump size ($0.0895\times$jump) on each side. Adding terms narrows the spike but does not shrink it. No jump $\Rightarrow$ no Gibbs.

Uniform convergence memorise

Theorem (lect. 17): $f'$ piecewise continuous on $(-L,L]$ and $F$ continuous on $\mathbb R$ $\Rightarrow$ FS converges uniformly to $F$. If $F$ has a jump: not uniform (partial sums are continuous; a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous).

M-test write-up: $\big|a_n\cos\frac{n\pi x}L+b_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\big|\le|a_n|+|b_n|\le\frac C{n^2}$ for all $x\in\mathbb R$; $\sum\frac C{n^2}$ converges ($p=2>1$, [S]); so the FS converges uniformly on $\mathbb R$.

Decay rule: $F$ has a jump $\Rightarrow$ coeffs $\sim\frac1n$ (M-test fails, $\sum\frac1n$ diverges; inconclusive alone). $F$ continuous, kink only $\Rightarrow\sim\frac1{n^2}$, uniform. Smoother $\Rightarrow$ faster.

Parseval / average power memorise

$$\frac1L\int_{-L}^{L}|f|^2dx=\frac{a_0^2}{2}+\sum_{n\ge1}\big(a_n^2+b_n^2\big)$$

Complex: $\frac1{2L}\int_{-L}^{L}|f|^2dx=\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}|c_n|^2$.

Average power = $\langle f,f\rangle$ = LHS (lecture uses $\frac1L$). Use: $1/n$ coeffs $\to\sum\frac1{n^2}$; $1/n^2$ coeffs $\to\sum\frac1{n^4}$. E.g. $x^2$ on $(-\pi,\pi]$: $\frac{2\pi^4}5=\frac{2\pi^4}9+16\sum\frac1{n^4}$.

Complex form memorise

$f\sim\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}c_ne^{ik_nx}$, $c_n=\frac1{2L}\int_{-L}^{L}fe^{-ik_nx}dx$. $c_0=\frac{a_0}2$, $c_n=\frac{a_n-ib_n}2$, $c_{-n}=\frac{a_n+ib_n}2=\overline{c_n}$ ($f$ real). Back: $a_n=c_n+c_{-n}$, $b_n=i(c_n-c_{-n})$. Use $e^{\pm in\pi}=(-1)^n$.

Term-by-term calculus memorise

  • Integration: always OK for piecewise continuous $f$ (result converges uniformly). Justify: "$f$ piecewise continuous, so term-by-term integration is valid." $\int_0^x\cos k_n\xi=\frac{\sin k_nx}{k_n}$, $\int_0^x\sin k_n\xi=\frac{1-\cos k_nx}{k_n}$. Trap: $\frac{a_0}2$ integrates to $\frac{a_0}2x$, not a Fourier term. Constant: collect it, or average over a period (lect. 19).
  • Differentiation: needs $F$ continuous on $\mathbb R$ and $f'$ piecewise $C^1$. Fails for $x$ on $(-\pi,\pi]$: $-2\sum(-1)^n\cos nx$ diverges.

Periodic ODE recipe

$y''+ky=s(x)=\frac{a_0}2+\sum(a_n\cos k_nx+b_n\sin k_nx)$, periodic BCs $y(\pm L)$, $y'(\pm L)$ equal.

  1. Trial $y_p=\frac{A_0}2+\sum(A_n\cos k_nx+B_n\sin k_nx)$ (only sines if $s$ odd, cosines if even)
  2. Differentiate term by term: $y_p''=-\sum k_n^2(\dots)$
  3. Match: $kA_0=a_0$, $(k-k_n^2)A_n=a_n$, $(k-k_n^2)B_n=b_n$
  4. Write $y_p$ with the solved $A_n,B_n$. IVP (lect. 19): add $C_1\cos\sqrt k x+C_2\sin\sqrt k x$, fit ICs
Resonance: $k=k_m^2=(m\pi/L)^2$ for some $m$ with $a_m$ or $b_m\ne0$ $\Rightarrow$ no periodic solution; response grows like $t\cos k_mt$. For $y''-ky$ ($k>0$): $-(k+k_n^2)A_n=a_n$, never resonant.

Exam checklist

  1. Even / odd / neither? Test the extension, then sketch 3 periods with midpoints at jumps
  2. Coefficients: state which vanish by parity; $a_0$ separately; simplify with $(-1)^n$, $\sin\frac{n\pi}2$; split odd/even $n$
  3. "Converges to?": $F$ where continuous, averages at jumps (incl. $\pm L$), piecewise answer
  4. "Uniform?": quote theorem (continuous $F$ + $f'$ p.w. continuous), or M-test with $C/n^2$; jump $\Rightarrow$ not uniform
  5. "Gibbs?": yes iff jump; where; $\approx9\%$ of jump
  6. "Evaluate $\sum$": plug in $x$ (pointwise) or use Parseval (squared coefficients); show the calculation
  7. Given two series (e.g. $x^4$, $x^2$): combine at one $x$ to isolate the requested sum
