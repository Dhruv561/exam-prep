MAST20030 · Reference · Topics 5–6

Words → boundary & initial conditions

Every phrase a PDE question can use to hide a BC or IC, what it becomes, and the hidden conditions nobody states. Lecture conventions: $u_t=\alpha u_{xx}$, $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$, $\nabla^2u=0$.

Why: the lecturer lists "text → IC/BC modelling" as examinable, and masked 2026 Q6 (18 marks) has a very long intro. Getting a BC wrong costs the whole question, since the eigenfunctions come from the BCs. Nothing here is on the formula sheet.

1 · The 5-step method

  1. Physics → PDE. Temperature / concentration → heat. Displacement of string / membrane → wave. "Steady", "equilibrium", "potential" → Laplace. "Infinitely long" → Fourier transform.
  2. Domain → list every boundary. Each end, each edge, $r=0$, $r=a$, $\theta$ edges, $\pm\infty$. Write the list before reading for conditions.
  3. Match one sentence to each boundary. Ask what the sentence fixes: the value (Dirichlet), the slope / heat flow (Neumann), a relation between them (Robin).
  4. Fill the gaps with hidden conditions. Boundaries with no sentence: bounded at $r=0$, periodic in $\theta$, decay at $\infty$ (§7).
  5. Time. "Initially / at $t=0$" → IC (heat 1, wave 2). "For $t\gt0$ / from then on / suddenly" → BC. "After a long time" → steady state $u_t=0$.

Then check: right number of conditions (§2), each BC written "for $t\gt0$", each IC "for $0\lt x\lt L$", and whether each BC is homogeneous (RHS $=0$). If not, you need $u=u_S+v$.

2 · Count the conditions first

4 · Heat equation (temperature $u$)

4a · Dirichlet: value fixed

WordsCondition
"end held / kept / maintained at temperature $T_0$"$u(0,t)=T_0$
"quenched in ice", "packed in ice", "in iced water" (°C)$u=0$
"immersed in boiling water" (°C)$u=100$
"in perfect thermal contact with a large bath / reservoir at $T_0$"$u=T_0$
"held at the initial temperature"$u=$ the IC value there
"heated instantaneously and held at $T_0$" (lecture Problem 3)$u(0,t)=T_0$ for $t\gt0$; IC unchanged
"edge $y=b$ has temperature $f(x)$"$u(x,b)=f(x)$
"end temperature rises steadily / oscillates / decays"$u(0,t)=kt$, $\;T_0+A\cos\omega t$, $\;e^{-t}$ (time-dependent, §4f)

4b · Neumann: heat flow fixed

WordsCondition
"insulated end", "perfectly insulated", "no heat flows through", "thermally isolated", "adiabatic", "reflecting", "zero flux"$u_x=0$ at that end
"temperature gradient $c$ at $x=a$"$u_x(a,t)=c$ (no sign game)
"heat supplied / pumped in at rate $q$"$x=0$: $u_x=-q/K$; $\;x=L$: $u_x=q/K$
"heat extracted / escapes at rate $q$"$x=0$: $u_x=q/K$; $\;x=L$: $u_x=-q/K$
insulated edge of a plate / rim of a disc$u_x$, $u_y$ or $u_r=0$ (derivative across the edge)

Flux sign rule (derive, don't memorise). Fourier's law: heat flux in the $+x$ direction is $-Ku_x$. With $n$ the outward normal:

$$K\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=\text{heat flux into the body}$$

At $x=L$ the outward normal is $+x$, at $x=0$ it is $-x$:

$$\begin{aligned} &x=L: && \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=u_x(L,t) &&\Rightarrow\ u_x(L,t)=\frac{q_{\text{in}}}{K}\\ &x=0: && \frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=-u_x(0,t) &&\Rightarrow\ u_x(0,t)=-\frac{q_{\text{in}}}{K} \end{aligned}$$

Sanity check: heat flows hot → cold. Heat entering at $x=0$ means the rod is hottest at $x=0$, so $u$ decreases there: $u_x(0,t)\lt0$ ✓. If no $K$ is given, take $K=1$.

4c · Robin: Newton's law of cooling at an end

"Exposed to / loses heat to surroundings at $T_s$ at a rate proportional to the temperature difference":

$$\begin{aligned} &-K\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=h\,(u-T_s) &&\text{heat out}=h\times\text{excess temperature}\\ &x=L:\quad u_x(L,t)=-\beta\,\big(u(L,t)-T_s\big) &&\beta=h/K\gt0\\ &x=0:\quad u_x(0,t)=\beta\,\big(u(0,t)-T_s\big) &&\text{sign flips at the left end} \end{aligned}$$

Limits: $\beta\to\infty$ gives $u=T_s$ (perfect contact, Dirichlet); $\beta\to0$ gives insulated. Homogeneous only if $T_s=0$. A pure-maths stem may just say "Robin", e.g. $y(0)-y'(0)=0$.

4d · Not BCs: these change the PDE or its dimension

WordsEffect
"insulated except at its ends", "laterally insulated", "curved surface insulated", "thin rod / wire"heat flows only along $x$: 1D, $u_t=\alpha u_{xx}$. Says nothing about the ends.
"faces of the plate insulated", "thin plate"2D: $u_t=\alpha\nabla^2u$ in the plane
"heat generated uniformly at rate $q$"$u_t=\alpha u_{xx}+q$ (parabolic $u_S$)
"heat generated at a rate proportional to $x$"$u_t=\alpha u_{xx}+cx$ (cubic $u_S$; 2021 paper had $-18x$)
"heat absorbed / sink"$-q$ term
"lateral surface loses heat to a medium at $T_s$"$u_t=\alpha u_{xx}-\beta(u-T_s)$

4e · Initial conditions

WordsIC
"initially heated to $f(x)$", "initial temperature distribution $f$"$u(x,0)=f(x)$
"initially at uniform temperature $T_1$", "taken out of an oven at $T_1$"$u(x,0)=T_1$
"initially at zero / at the temperature of ice"$u(x,0)=0$
"initial temperature shown in the figure" (2023)piecewise-linear formula read off the graph
"has been in a steady state with ends at $A$, $B$ for a long time; at $t=0$ …"IC = old steady state: $u(x,0)=A+\frac{(B-A)x}{L}$; BCs = new ones

IC and BC may disagree at a corner (e.g. IC $20$, end in ice). That is allowed: heat smooths it instantly.

4f · Time-dependent BCs

"Machine sets the end temperature to $g(t)$" → $u(0,t)=g(t)$. No steady state exists. Method: subtract a $w(x,t)$, linear in $x$, matching both BCs. Then $v=u-w$ has homogeneous BCs and a source term $-w_t$. Recognise it; older papers used this.

5 · Wave equation (displacement $u$)

5a · BCs

WordsCondition
"fixed / pinned / clamped / tied / fastened at both ends", "held fixed at the boundary" (drum)$u=0$
"end moved / shaken as $h(t)$"$u(0,t)=h(t)$, e.g. $A\sin\omega t$
"free end", "loose end", "attached to a ring that slides without friction on a vertical rod"$u_x=0$
"end attached to a spring (constant $k$), tension $T$"$u_x(L,t)=-\frac{k}{T}u(L,t)$ (Robin, recognise only)

5b · ICs (always two)

WordsIC
"released from rest", "let go", "initially at rest", "not moving", "starts from rest"$u_t(x,0)=0$
"in its equilibrium / rest position", "undisturbed", "flat", "horizontal"$u(x,0)=0$
"pulled / deformed / displaced into the shape $f(x)$"$u(x,0)=f(x)$
"struck / hit by a hammer", "given initial velocity $g(x)$"$u_t(x,0)=g(x)$
"struck sharply at the point $x_0$"$u_t(x,0)=v_0\,\delta(x-x_0)$ (recognise)

"Plucked at $x=\ell$ to height $A$" = two straight segments $(0,0)\to(\ell,A)\to(L,0)$, released from rest:

$$u(x,0)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{Ax}{\ell}, & 0\le x\le\ell\\[6pt] \dfrac{A(L-x)}{L-\ell}, & \ell\le x\le L\end{cases}\qquad u_t(x,0)=0$$

Plucked at the centre: put $\ell=\frac{L}{2}$.

5c · Not BCs

"Air resistance / damping proportional to velocity" → $u_{tt}+\gamma u_t=c^2u_{xx}$. "Gravity" → $-g$ term. "Elastic foundation" → $-ku$ term. Recognise only.

6 · Steady state / Laplace

Trigger words: "steady-state temperature", "equilibrium", "after a long time", "electrostatic potential", "time-independent". Set $u_t=0$; the IC is not used (except Neumann–Neumann, §9).

6a · Rectangle $0\le x\le a$, $0\le y\le b$

left, $x=0$$u(0,y)=f(y)$$u_x(0,y)=0$
right, $x=a$$u(a,y)=f(y)$$u_x(a,y)=0$

"Edge along the $x$-axis" = bottom. Several non-zero edges → superpose, one non-zero edge per sub-problem.

6b · Polar shapes

WordsDomain and conditions
"circular plate / disc of radius $a$, rim at $f(\theta)$"$u(a,\theta)=f(\theta)$, bounded at $r=0$, $2\pi$-periodic
"annulus / ring-shaped plate"$u(a,\theta)$, $u(b,\theta)$ given, periodic, no boundedness
"semicircular plate", "the diameter"$0\le\theta\le\pi$: diameter = $\theta=0$ and $\theta=\pi$; bounded at $r=0$; no periodicity
"quarter-disc", "sector / wedge of angle $\alpha$"edges $\theta=0$, $\theta=\alpha$, arc $r=a$; bounded at $r=0$
"semi-annulus" (2022, 2024)$a\lt r\lt b$, $0\le\theta\le\pi$: four edges
"outside a cylinder of radius $a$"$r\gt a$: bounded as $r\to\infty$, periodic
"axisymmetric", "radially symmetric", "independent of $\theta$"$u=u(r)$ or $u(r,t)$: no $\theta$ conditions

Insulated in polar: arc → $u_r=0$. Straight edge → $\frac1r u_\theta=0$, i.e. $u_\theta=0$. Never $u_r=0$ on a straight edge.

7 · Hidden conditions (never in the words)

  1. Bounded at $r=0$ whenever the centre is in the domain (disc, semicircle, wedge, drum). Kills $\ln r$, $r^{-n}$, $Y_0$. Write $|u(0,t)|\lt\infty$.
  2. $2\pi$-periodic in $\theta$ when $\theta$ goes fully round (disc, annulus, exterior): $u(r,-\pi)=u(r,\pi)$ and $u_\theta(r,-\pi)=u_\theta(r,\pi)$. Forces integer $n$.
  3. Bounded / decays at infinity: exterior ($r\to\infty$), semi-infinite strip ($x\to\infty$), infinite line ($u,u_x\to0$ so the FT exists).
  4. Non-trivial: $T(t)\not\equiv0$, which is what lets $u(0,t)=0$ become $X(0)=0$.

8 · Infinite domains (Topic 6)

WordsSet-up
"infinitely long rod / string", "very long", "effectively infinite", $x\in\mathbb{R}$no end BCs; $u,u_x\to0$ as $|x|\to\infty$; ICs as usual; FT in $x$
"infinite strip $0\lt y\lt 1$", "copper strip of effectively infinite length"$u(x,0)$, $u(x,1)$ given; $u\to0$ as $|x|\to\infty$; FT in $x$
"semi-infinite rod $x\gt0$, end held at 0 / insulated"odd / even extension to $\mathbb{R}$, then FT (recognise)

9 · Rare / recognise only

  • Closed loop / ring of wire, length $2L$ → periodic in $x$: $u(-L,t)=u(L,t)$, $u_x(-L,t)=u_x(L,t)$.
  • Symmetric about the centre → $u_x(\frac L2,t)=0$, so you may solve on half the rod.
  • Two rods joined, perfect contact → $u$ continuous and $K_1u_x=K_2u_x$ at the joint.
  • Both ends insulated → total heat conserved, $u\to\frac1L\int_0^L f\,dx$ (mean of IC). 2023 Q6 asked this.
  • Both ends Neumann, net flux in $\ne0$ → no steady state (heat piles up).
  • All-Neumann Laplace → solution never unique (add any constant) and needs zero net flux, $\oint\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\,ds=0$. An old paper asked exactly "why never unique".

10 · BCs → $X$ conditions → eigenfunctions

Only homogeneous BCs transfer to $X$ (with $u=X(x)T(t)$, $T\not\equiv0$):

$$\begin{aligned} &u(a,t)=0 &&\Rightarrow\ X(a)=0\\ &u_x(a,t)=0 &&\Rightarrow\ X'(a)=0\\ &u_x(L,t)+\beta u(L,t)=0 &&\Rightarrow\ X'(L)+\beta X(L)=0\\ &\text{periodic} &&\Rightarrow\ \Theta(-\pi)=\Theta(\pi),\ \Theta'(-\pi)=\Theta'(\pi)\\ &\text{bounded at }0 &&\Rightarrow\ |R(0)|\lt\infty \end{aligned}$$

Inhomogeneous ($u=T_0\ne0$, $u_x=q\ne0$, $T_s\ne0$): split $u=u_S+v$. Then $v$ gets the same type of BC with RHS $0$, and IC $f-u_S$.

D–D: value, value$\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}$, $n\ge1$
N–N: insulated, insulated$1,\ \cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}$, $n\ge0$
D–N: value at $0$, insulated at $L$$\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}$, $n\ge1$
N–D: insulated at $0$, value at $L$$\cos\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}$, $n\ge1$
periodic on $[-L,L]$$1,\ \cos\frac{n\pi x}{L},\ \sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}$

11 · Traps

Read these twice
  1. "Insulated except at its ends" describes the sides (1D). The ends' BCs are in a different sentence.
  2. "Fixed" string → $u=0$. "Fixed temperature $T_0$" → $u=T_0$, not $0$.
  3. "Released from rest" = zero velocity. "In its rest position" = zero displacement.
  4. Wave needs two ICs. If you see one, the other is a phrase like "from rest" or "from equilibrium".
  5. Flux and Newton-cooling signs flip between $x=0$ and $x=L$. Run the hot → cold sanity check.
  6. "Insulated" → derivative, never value. "Held at 0" → value, never derivative.
  7. Lateral heat loss and heat generation are PDE terms, not BCs.
  8. Full disc: add boundedness and periodicity yourself. Annulus: periodicity only. Semicircle / wedge: boundedness only.
  9. Polar straight edge insulated → $u_\theta=0$, not $u_r=0$.
  10. Check which end is $x=0$. Rod on $[-L,L]$? Re-label before using the eigen-table.
  11. "After a long time" → $u_S$, IC unused, except N–N (mean of IC).
  12. Write domains: BCs "$t\gt0$", ICs "$0\lt x\lt L$". These are often a mark.

12 · Ten worked translations

Cover the answer, write the IBVP, then reveal. Reason on the right = what a marker wants.

E1. A thin rod $0\le x\le2$ has its curved surface insulated and is initially at uniform temperature $20$. From $t=0$ its left end is kept in iced water and heat is pumped into its right end at constant rate $3$ (take $K=1$).

Answer
$$\begin{aligned} &u_t=\alpha u_{xx}, && 0\lt x\lt2,\ t\gt0 &&\text{curved surface insulated}\Rightarrow\text{1D}\\ &u(0,t)=0, && t\gt0 &&\text{iced water}\\ &u_x(2,t)=3, && t\gt0 &&\text{heat in at }x=L\Rightarrow u_x=q/K\\ &u(x,0)=20, && 0\lt x\lt2 &&\text{uniform initial temperature} \end{aligned}$$

Inhomogeneous, so split: $u_S=3x$ (check $u_S(0)=0$, $u_S'(2)=3$).

E2. A laterally insulated rod $0\le x\le1$ is initially at $50$ throughout. Its left end is held at $50$; its right end is exposed to air at $20$ and loses heat at a rate proportional to the temperature difference, constant $\beta=2$.

Answer
$$\begin{aligned} &u(0,t)=50, && t\gt0 &&\text{held}\\ &u_x(1,t)=-2\big(u(1,t)-20\big), && t\gt0 &&\text{Newton cooling at }x=L\\ &u(x,0)=50, && 0\lt x\lt1 &&\text{initial} \end{aligned}$$

Sign check via the steady state $u_S=A+Bx$:

$$\begin{aligned} &u_S(0)=50 &&\Rightarrow\ A=50\\ &B=-2(50+B-20) &&\text{Robin at }x=1\\ &3B=-60 &&\text{rearrange}\\ &u_S=50-20x &&u_S(1)=30\text{, between }20\text{ and }50\ ✓ \end{aligned}$$

E3. A rod $0\le x\le L$, insulated except at its ends, has had its ends at $0$ and $100$ for a long time. At $t=0$ the hot end is suddenly insulated; the cold end stays at $0$.

Answer
$$\begin{aligned} &u(0,t)=0, && t\gt0 &&\text{cold end unchanged}\\ &u_x(L,t)=0, && t\gt0 &&\text{hot end now insulated}\\ &u(x,0)=\frac{100x}{L}, && 0\lt x\lt L &&\text{old steady state (a line)} \end{aligned}$$

D–N eigenfunctions $\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}$; $u\to0$ as $t\to\infty$.

E4. A string of length $\pi$ is tied at $x=0$. Its end $x=\pi$ is attached to a ring that slides without friction on a vertical pole. It is pulled into the shape $\sin\frac x2$ and let go.

Answer
$$\begin{aligned} &u(0,t)=0, && t\gt0 &&\text{tied}\\ &u_x(\pi,t)=0, && t\gt0 &&\text{free end (ring)}\\ &u(x,0)=\sin\tfrac x2, && 0\lt x\lt\pi &&\text{pulled into shape}\\ &u_t(x,0)=0, && 0\lt x\lt\pi &&\text{let go = from rest} \end{aligned}$$

D–N, and the IC is the $n=1$ mode, so $u=\sin\frac x2\cos\frac{ct}{2}$.

E5. A guitar string $0\le x\le L$, pinned at both ends, lies in its equilibrium position. At $t=0$ its middle half is struck so that it moves with speed $v_0$.

Answer
$$\begin{aligned} &u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0, && t\gt0 &&\text{pinned}\\ &u(x,0)=0, && 0\lt x\lt L &&\text{equilibrium position}\\ &u_t(x,0)=\begin{cases}v_0, & \frac L4\lt x\lt\frac{3L}4\\ 0, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} && &&\text{struck middle half} \end{aligned}$$

E6. Steady temperature in the square plate $0\le x,y\le1$, faces insulated. The bottom and left edges are kept at $0$, the top edge is insulated, and the right edge has temperature $y(2-y)$.

Answer
$$\begin{aligned} &u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0, && 0\lt x,y\lt1 &&\text{steady, faces insulated}\Rightarrow\text{2D}\\ &u(x,0)=0 && &&\text{bottom}\\ &u(0,y)=0 && &&\text{left}\\ &u_y(x,1)=0 && &&\text{top insulated}\\ &u(1,y)=y(2-y) && &&\text{right} \end{aligned}$$

Homogeneous pair is in $y$ (D–N), so $Y_n=\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi y}{2}$, $X_n=\sinh\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2}$.

E7. Steady temperature in the quarter-disc $0\le r\le2$, $0\le\theta\le\frac\pi2$. The edge along the $x$-axis is held at $0$, the edge along the $y$-axis is insulated, and the curved edge is at $\sin\theta$.

Answer
$$\begin{aligned} &u(r,0)=0 && &&x\text{-axis edge is }\theta=0\\ &u_\theta\big(r,\tfrac\pi2\big)=0 && &&\text{insulated straight edge}\\ &u(2,\theta)=\sin\theta && &&\text{curved edge}\\ &|u(0,\theta)|\lt\infty && &&\text{hidden: centre in domain} \end{aligned}$$

No periodicity ($\theta$ stops at $\frac\pi2$). $\Theta_n=\sin(2n-1)\theta$, $R=r^{2n-1}$, so $u=\frac r2\sin\theta$.

E8. A circular drumskin of radius $1$ is clamped at its rim and vibrates axisymmetrically. It is initially flat and is struck so that it has velocity $g(r)$.

Answer
$$\begin{aligned} &u_{tt}=c^2\Big(u_{rr}+\frac1r u_r\Big), && 0\le r\lt1,\ t\gt0 &&\text{axisymmetric: no }\theta\\ &u(1,t)=0, && t\gt0 &&\text{clamped rim}\\ &|u(0,t)|\lt\infty, && t\gt0 &&\text{hidden}\\ &u(r,0)=0 && &&\text{initially flat}\\ &u_t(r,0)=g(r) && &&\text{struck} \end{aligned}$$

E9. A very long, thin, insulated wire has initial temperature $e^{-x^2}$.

Answer
$$\begin{aligned} &u_t=\alpha u_{xx}, && x\in\mathbb{R},\ t\gt0 &&\text{"very long"}\Rightarrow\text{whole line}\\ &u(x,0)=e^{-x^2}, && x\in\mathbb{R} &&\text{initial}\\ &u,\ u_x\to0 && \text{as }|x|\to\infty &&\text{hidden: FT must exist} \end{aligned}$$

E10. A rod $0\le x\le1$, insulated except at its ends, is initially at $0$. From $t=0$ the temperature of the left end is raised steadily, $u=t$, and the right end is insulated.

Answer
$$\begin{aligned} &u(0,t)=t, && t\gt0 &&\text{time-dependent Dirichlet}\\ &u_x(1,t)=0, && t\gt0 &&\text{insulated}\\ &u(x,0)=0, && 0\lt x\lt1 &&\text{initial} \end{aligned}$$

No steady state. $w=t$ matches both BCs, so $v=u-t$ solves $v_t=\alpha v_{xx}-1$ with homogeneous D–N BCs.

13 · Drill

D1. "The rod is insulated except at its ends." This gives…

It describes the sides: heat flows only along $x$. The ends need their own sentences.

D2. "Heat is extracted from the end $x=0$ at rate $2$" ($K=1$).

Heat in $=-2=K\,\partial u/\partial n=-u_x(0,t)$, so $u_x(0,t)=2$. Check: heat leaves on the left, so the rod is colder there and $u$ increases with $x$.

D3. "The end $x=L$ is in perfect thermal contact with a large bath at $40$."

Perfect contact with a large reservoir fixes the value: Dirichlet.

D4. "The end $x=1$ cools into air at $15$ by Newton's law, constant $\beta$" ($K=1$).

Right end: $u_x(L)=-\beta(u-T_s)$. If the end is hotter than the air, $u$ must drop towards it, so $u_x\lt0$ ✓.

D5. "The string is released from rest in the shape $f(x)$."

"From rest" = zero velocity; "in the shape" = initial displacement. Wave always needs both.

D6. "The string is in its rest position when it is hit, giving it velocity $g(x)$."

"Rest position" = zero displacement (not zero velocity). Hit → velocity.

D7. "The end $x=L$ is attached to a ring free to slide on a frictionless vertical rod."

No vertical force at a frictionless ring, so the string meets the rod flat: free end, Neumann.

D8. Steady temperature in a disc of radius $a$, rim kept at $f(\theta)$. Besides $u(a,\theta)=f(\theta)$ you need…

Centre in domain → bounded. $\theta$ goes fully round → periodic. Neither is stated.

D9. Steady temperature in the full annulus $1\lt r\lt2$. Hidden conditions:

$r=0$ is not in the domain, so $\ln r$ and $r^{-n}$ stay. $\theta$ goes fully round → periodic.

D10. Semicircular plate $0\le r\le a$, $0\le\theta\le\pi$; "the diameter is held at $0$".

The diameter is the two straight edges $\theta=0,\pi$. Centre in domain → bounded. $\theta$ stops at $\pi$ → no periodicity.

D11. "Heat is generated inside the rod at a rate proportional to the distance from $x=0$."

"Inside" → source term in the PDE, not a BC. Linear source → cubic $u_S$.

D12. Both ends of a rod $[0,L]$ are insulated; initial temperature $f(x)$. After a long time…

No heat enters or leaves, so the total is conserved and spreads evenly: the $n=0$ term $\frac{a_0}{2}$.

D13. "A thin wire is bent into a closed circular loop of length $2L$" (arc length $-L\le x\le L$).

$x=-L$ and $x=L$ are the same physical point: value and flux must match. Periodic.

D14. A rod $[0,L]$ has been at steady state with ends at $0$ and $10$. At $t=0$ the right end is put in ice. The IC is…

IC = the old steady state (a line from $0$ to $10$). New BCs: $u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0$.

D15. The straight edge $\theta=\alpha$ of a wedge is insulated.

Insulated = zero derivative across the edge. Across a ray $\theta=\alpha$ means the $\theta$-direction: $\frac1r u_\theta=0$.

D16. "The lateral surface loses heat to surroundings at temperature $0$ at a rate proportional to its temperature."

Lateral = along the whole length, so it is a loss term in the PDE. Ends still need their own BCs.
