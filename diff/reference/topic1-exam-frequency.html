Roughly a quarter of the paper, and growing. The 2024 paper put Topic 1 first, as its biggest question.

2 · Marks by technique, all four papers

Reduction of order is the only technique in all four papers. Systems (phase portrait, diagonalisation, eigenvalues + type, vector form, $e^{At}$) total about 43 marks, but all of them come from 2022 and 2024.

3 · The shift: scalar ODE theory → systems

Split each paper's Topic 1 marks into scalar ODE theory (reduction of order, Wronskian/Abel, Euler, existence interval, $y_c + y_p$ proof) and systems (vector form, eigenvalues, type/stability, phase portraits, diagonalisation, $e^{At}$):

Older papers were almost all Wronskian and reduction of order. The two recent papers spend most of their Topic 1 marks on systems. Since 2022 and 2024 are closest to the current course, weight them more.

5 · What form the questions take

TechniqueForms seenPriority
Reduction of order · Given one solution $v$: find the general solution, usually inhomogeneous (log or power forcing), then fit ICs.
· Find $v$ first by trialling a form like $(t+a)^b$ or $t^\lambda$, then reduce.
· The repeated-root case of an Euler equation, done by reduction of order.		Near-certain
Phase portrait · Full sketch for a 2×2 system, with a checklist: straight-line orbits and directions, ≥4 other orbits, asymptotics as $t\to\pm\infty$, slopes where orbits cross the axes, loci of max/min in $x$ and $y$, orbits with $y/x$ constant, an IC that sends the orbit to the origin.High
Diagonalisation · "Convert to a vector IVP, then solve by diagonalisation", with constant forcing and ICs at 0.High
Eigenvalues + type/stability · Matrix with a parameter: eigenvalues as functions of it, then type and stability for every case.
· Mid-sem style: classify several systems and match them to direction fields.		High
Wronskian / Abel · Show given functions are linearly independent solutions.
· Use the Wronskian of a 3rd-order ODE to derive the ODE a missing solution satisfies.
· Inside a power-series question: Abel's identity to deduce the form of the second solution (e.g. a log singularity).		Medium
Vector form / system ↔ ODE · Rewrite a system as $\mathbf y' = A\mathbf y + \mathbf b$ (usually the first part of a bigger question).
· Convert a 2×2 system into a single 2nd-order ODE.		Medium
Existence interval · "Largest interval where a unique solution is guaranteed": divide by the leading coefficient, find where coefficients break, pick the piece containing $t_0$. Often a small part tacked onto another question.Medium

6 · What this means for revision

  1. Reduction of order, inhomogeneous, including finding $v$ by trial. It's on every paper.
  2. The systems block as one skill: vector form → eigenvalues → type/stability (with a parameter) → phase portrait checklist, plus diagonalisation with forcing and ICs. Recent papers put 15–24 marks here.
  3. Wronskian/Abel and existence intervals: smaller, but they turn up inside Topic 2 questions too, so they pay twice.
  4. Euler, $e^{At}$ and the $y_c + y_p$ proof: know them well enough for a 2–4 mark short answer.

Not counted: the 2018 Green's function short answer (4 marks), since Green's functions aren't in the current lecture notes. The 2026 format isn't confirmed yet; check Canvas.
