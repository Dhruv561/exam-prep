2026 exam: format and predicted questions

Exam on 5 Nov 2026. The prediction is inferred from the masked paper, the lecturer's notes and past-paper patterns. It is not confirmed.

Lecturer's format notes

  • 3 h writing plus 15 min reading. No calculator, dictionary or notes. Templated answer boxes, 2 extra pages per question plus 2 shared extra pages.
  • 7 questions, 10–18 marks each, 90 marks total, marks proportional to length.
  • Subject mark = 65% exam + 20% assignments + 15% mid-sem test. Pass at 50, no hurdles.
  • Theory: definitions, theorems, proofs (e.g. Weierstrass M-test), derivations of formula-sheet items. Applications: computations, text → IC/BC modelling, phase-plot interpretation.
  • Be fluent in matrix diagonalisation, convergence tests, integration, partial fractions.
  • Consults: Thomas (room 164) 27 Oct 11–12, 28 Oct 13–14, 30 Oct 14–15, 2 Nov 16–17, 4 Nov 13–14. Nick (room 205) 29 Oct 10–12, 2 Nov 12–14. Special exams 11–18 Dec.

Formula sheet

Given: trig/hyperbolic identities, limits, p-series and geometric series tests, reduction-of-order formula, Laplace rules and table, Fourier series coefficients, Fourier transform definition and rules (derivative, multiplication, convolution).

Not given: matrix exponential, separation-of-variables recipe, integrating factors, eigenvalue methods, Euler equations, power-series recurrences.

Action points

  1. Prepare power series and Laplace equally, since the order is unknown.
  2. Practise text → equation/IC/BC translation (the long intros on Q2 and Q6).
  3. Practise proofs and derivations: Weierstrass M-test, Laplace derivative rule, FT derivative rule, reduction of order, Fourier coefficients.
  4. Rehearse phase portraits: classify node, saddle, spiral or centre, state stability, sketch from eigenvalues.
  5. Memorise what is not on the sheet (see above).
  6. Time: about 2 min per mark, roughly 20 / 24 / 22 / 26 / 24 / 36 / 28 min for Q1–Q7 with about 10 min spare. Use reading time on Q6 (BCs and setup).
