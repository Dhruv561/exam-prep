The challenge, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes.

Rod $0\le x\le1$, insulated except at its ends, $u_t=u_{xx}+2$. End $x=0$ held at $1$, end $x=1$ insulated, $u(x,0)=1+2x-x^2+4\sin\frac{\pi x}{2}\cos\pi x$.

(a) BCs. [2] (b) $u_S$. [3] (c) IBVP for $v=u-u_S$. [3] (d) SoV, three cases, general $v$. [6] (e) $u(x,t)$. [3] (f) $u(1,t)$ and its limit. [2] (g) Sketch at $t=0,t_1,t_2,\infty$. [3]

(a) · Boundary conditions

Held at $1$: $u(0,t)=1$. Insulated end: no heat flux, $u_x(1,t)=0$. (Both for $t>0$.)

"Insulated except at its ends" is the lateral surface: it is why the model is 1D. It is not a BC.

(b) · Steady state

$u_S$ is time independent, so $0=u_S''+2$, i.e. $u_S''=-2$, giving $u_S=-x^2+Ax+B$.

$u_S(0)=1\Rightarrow B=1$. $u_S'(1)=-2+A=0\Rightarrow A=2$. $$\boxed{u_S(x)=1+2x-x^2}$$

Check: $u_S(0)=1$ ✓, $u_S'(1)=2-2=0$ ✓. Physically: heat made inside can only leave through $x=0$, so the rod is hottest at the insulated end ($u_S(1)=2$).

(c) · IBVP for the transient

PDE: $v_t=u_t=u_{xx}+2=v_{xx}+u_S''+2=v_{xx}$.

BCs: $v(0,t)=u(0,t)-u_S(0)=1-1=0$; $\;v_x(1,t)=u_x(1,t)-u_S'(1)=0$.

IC: $v(x,0)=u(x,0)-u_S(x)=4\sin\frac{\pi x}2\cos\pi x$. By $\sin a\cos b=\frac12[\sin(a-b)+\sin(a+b)]$: $$v(x,0)=2\sin\big(-\tfrac{\pi x}2\big)+2\sin\tfrac{3\pi x}{2}=2\sin\tfrac{3\pi x}2-2\sin\tfrac{\pi x}2.$$

The product-to-sum identity is on the formula sheet. Doing it here, before SoV, makes (e) pure matching.

(d) · Separation of variables

Let $v=X(x)T(t)$: $X\dot T=X''T\Rightarrow\dfrac{\dot T}{T}=\dfrac{X''}{X}=\lambda$ (constant, since the left side depends on $t$ only and the right on $x$ only). So $$X''-\lambda X=0,\qquad \dot T-\lambda T=0.$$ BCs: $X(0)T(t)=0$ and $X'(1)T(t)=0$ for all $t$; $T\equiv0$ is trivial, so $X(0)=0$, $X'(1)=0$.

$\lambda=k^2>0$: $X=A\cosh kx+B\sinh kx$. $X(0)=A=0$. $X'(1)=kB\cosh k=0$, and $\cosh k\ne0$, $k\ne0$, so $B=0$. Trivial.

$\lambda=0$: $X=Ax+B$. $X(0)=B=0$, $X'(1)=A=0$. Trivial.

$\lambda=-k^2<0$: $X=A\cos kx+B\sin kx$. $X(0)=A=0$. $X'(1)=kB\cos k=0$; non-trivial needs $\cos k=0$: $$k_n=\frac{(2n-1)\pi}{2},\quad \lambda_n=-k_n^2,\quad X_n=\sin k_nx,\quad n=1,2,\dots$$

Then $\dot T_n=-k_n^2T_n\Rightarrow T_n=e^{-k_n^2t}$. Superposing: $$v(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\,e^{-(2n-1)^2\pi^2t/4}\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2}.$$

Marks are for: the separated ODEs with the constant justified, BCs transferred to $X$, each case concluded, $k_n$ with the half-integer, $T_n$, and the sum. Taking $k>0$ loses nothing ($-k$ gives the same functions).

(e) · Full solution

At $t=0$: $\sum b_n\sin k_nx=2\sin\frac{3\pi x}2-2\sin\frac{\pi x}2$. $\frac{\pi}{2}=k_1$, $\frac{3\pi}2=k_2$, so by orthogonality (matching coefficients) $b_1=-2$, $b_2=2$, $b_n=0$ for $n\ge3$. $$\boxed{u(x,t)=1+2x-x^2-2e^{-\pi^2t/4}\sin\frac{\pi x}2+2e^{-9\pi^2t/4}\sin\frac{3\pi x}2}$$

Check all four: PDE ($u_S''+2=0$ and each term solves $v_t=v_{xx}$) · $u(0,t)=1$ · $u_x(1,t)=0$ since $\cos\frac\pi2=\cos\frac{3\pi}2=0$ · $t=0$ gives $f$. A wrong sign in (c) shows up immediately at $x=1$: $f(1)=1+2-1+4(1)(-1)=-2$.

(f) · The insulated end

$\sin\frac\pi2=1$, $\sin\frac{3\pi}2=-1$: $$u(1,t)=2-2e^{-\pi^2t/4}-2e^{-9\pi^2t/4}\;\longrightarrow\;2\quad(t\to\infty),$$ since both exponentials decay. ($u(1,0)=-2=f(1)$ ✓.)

$u(1,t)$ increases monotonically from $-2$ to $2$. Both exponentials are positive and decaying, so no overshoot.

(g) · Sketch

Axes $u$ vs $x\in[0,1]$, label $u=1$ at $x=0$ and $u=\pm2$ at $x=1$.

  • $t=0$: $f$. Starts at $1$, rises to about $2.6$ near $x\approx0.3$, falls to $-2$ at $x=1$, arriving flat.
  • $t_1$: the $\sin\frac{3\pi x}2$ term has mostly gone (decays 9× faster): $u\approx u_S-2e^{-\pi^2t_1/4}\sin\frac{\pi x}2$, a parabola pulled down most at $x=1$.
  • $t_2$: same shape, smaller dip.
  • $t\to\infty$: $u_S=1+2x-x^2$, rising from $1$ to its maximum $2$ at $x=1$.
  • Every $t>0$ curve passes through $(0,1)$ and is horizontal at $x=1$.

Drag the slider: the hump near $x=0.3$ at $t=0$ is the $\sin\frac{3\pi x}{2}$ mode; it is gone by $t\approx0.1$. Your $t_1$ should already be a single smooth dip.
