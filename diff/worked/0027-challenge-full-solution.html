A thin wire is bent into a closed loop of length $1$. Its temperature $u(x,t)$, with $x$ the arc length from a marked point, satisfies $u_t=2u_{xx}$ for $0<x<1$, $t>0$.

(a) Classify the PDE. [2]
(b) Explain the periodic BCs; separate variables; ODEs and conditions on $X$. [3]
(c) Three cases for $\lambda$; all eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. [6]
(d) General series solution; limit as $t\to\infty$ in terms of $f(x)=u(x,0)$. [2]
(e) Solve with $u(x,0)=3+\sin^2(\pi x)$. [2]

Write thisDon't write this

(a) · Classification

Second order, linear, homogeneous (every term contains $u$). Written as $2u_{xx}-u_t=0$ with variables $(x,t)$: $A=2$, $B=0$, $C=0$, so $B^2-4AC=0$: parabolic.

$C$ is the coefficient of $u_{tt}$, which is absent. $u_t$ is lower order and doesn't enter the discriminant.

(b) · Boundary conditions and separation

$x=0$ and $x=1$ are the same physical point of the loop, so the temperature and the heat flux $-\kappa u_x$ agree there: $u(0,t)=u(1,t)$, $u_x(0,t)=u_x(1,t)$.

Try $u=X(x)T(t)$: $XT'=2X''T\Rightarrow\dfrac{T'}{2T}=\dfrac{X''}{X}$. The left side depends only on $t$, the right only on $x$, so both equal a constant $\lambda$: $$X''-\lambda X=0,\qquad T'-2\lambda T=0.$$ The BCs give $\big(X(0)-X(1)\big)T(t)=0$ for all $t$; $T\not\equiv0$ for a non-trivial $u$, so $$X(0)=X(1),\qquad X'(0)=X'(1).$$

(c) · Three cases

Case $\lambda=k^2>0$ ($k>0$): $X=A\cosh kx+B\sinh kx$, $X'=k(A\sinh kx+B\cosh kx)$.
$X(0)=X(1)$: $A=A\cosh k+B\sinh k\ \Rightarrow\ A(\cosh k-1)+B\sinh k=0$.
$X'(0)=X'(1)$: $kB=k(A\sinh k+B\cosh k)\ \Rightarrow\ A\sinh k+B(\cosh k-1)=0$.
Determinant: $(\cosh k-1)^2-\sinh^2k=2-2\cosh k<0$ since $k>0$. Non-zero, so $A=B=0$. Only the trivial solution.

Case $\lambda=0$: $X=Ax+B$. $X(0)=X(1)$: $B=A+B\Rightarrow A=0$. $X'=0$ on both sides ✓. So $X_0=1$ is an eigenfunction, $\lambda_0=0$.

Case $\lambda=-k^2<0$ ($k>0$): $X=A\cos kx+B\sin kx$, $X'=k(-A\sin kx+B\cos kx)$.
$X(0)=X(1)$: $A(1-\cos k)-B\sin k=0$.
$X'(0)=X'(1)$: $A\sin k+B(1-\cos k)=0$.
Determinant: $(1-\cos k)^2+\sin^2k=2-2\cos k$. A non-trivial $(A,B)$ needs this to be $0$: $\cos k=1$, so $k=2n\pi$. Then both equations read $0=0$ and $A,B$ are both free: $$\lambda_n=-4n^2\pi^2,\qquad X_n=\cos(2n\pi x)\ \text{ and }\ \sin(2n\pi x),\qquad n=1,2,\dots$$ together with $\lambda_0=0$, $X_0=1$.

The interval $(0,1)$ isn't symmetric, so the even/odd shortcut from the lesson's $(-\pi,\pi)$ example doesn't apply. Writing the two BCs as a $2\times2$ linear system and taking the determinant is the clean route. You can quote $(\cosh k-1)^2-\sinh^2k=2-2\cosh k$ after one line of expansion using $\cosh^2-\sinh^2=1$. Sanity check: the period is $1=2L$, so the table predicts $k_n=\frac{n\pi}{L}=2n\pi$ ✓.

(d) · General solution and long-time limit

$T_n'=2\lambda_nT_n=-8n^2\pi^2T_n\Rightarrow T_n=e^{-8n^2\pi^2t}$; $T_0=1$. Superposing, $$u(x,t)=\frac{a_0}2+\sum_{n=1}^\infty e^{-8n^2\pi^2t}\big(a_n\cos2n\pi x+b_n\sin2n\pi x\big).$$ As $t\to\infty$ every exponential $\to0$, so $$u\to\frac{a_0}{2}=\int_0^1f(x)\,dx,$$ the average initial temperature, since $u(x,0)=f$ is the full Fourier series of $f$ with period $1$, $a_0=\frac{1}{1/2}\int_0^1f\,dx$.

Period $1$ means $L=\frac12$, so the sheet's $a_0=\frac1L\int f$ becomes $2\int_0^1f$. Physically: a closed loop loses no heat, so it settles to its mean temperature.

(e) · The given IC

$3+\sin^2\pi x=3+\tfrac12-\tfrac12\cos2\pi x=\tfrac72-\tfrac12\cos2\pi x$. Matching with $u(x,0)$: $\frac{a_0}2=\frac72$, $a_1=-\frac12$, all other $a_n,b_n=0$. $$\boxed{u(x,t)=\frac72-\frac12e^{-8\pi^2t}\cos(2\pi x)}$$

Check: $u_t=4\pi^2e^{-8\pi^2t}\cos2\pi x$ and $2u_{xx}=2\cdot\frac12\cdot4\pi^2e^{-8\pi^2t}\cos2\pi x$ ✓; $u(0,t)=u(1,t)$ ✓; $u(x,0)=3+\sin^2\pi x$ ✓. Limit $\frac72=\int_0^1(3+\sin^2\pi x)\,dx$, agreeing with (d). No integrals needed: the IC is already a finite trig sum.
