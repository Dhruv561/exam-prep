The quarter-annulus, written out as an exam answer

Plate $1<r<2$, $0\le\theta\le\frac\pi2$, steady state $u_{rr}+\frac1ru_r+\frac1{r^2}u_{\theta\theta}=0$. Edge $\theta=0$ held at $0$; edge $\theta=\frac\pi2$ insulated; outer arc at $0$; inner arc temperature $\sin\theta-2\sin5\theta$.

(a) BCs [2] (b) separated ODEs [3] (c) eigenvalues/eigenfunctions, three cases [5] (d) $R_n$ [4] (e) $u(r,\theta)$ [4] (f) temperature at $r=\sqrt2$ on the insulated edge [2]

(a) · Boundary conditions

$u(r,0)=0$, $\;\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}(r,\tfrac\pi2)=0$ for $1<r<2$; $\;u(2,\theta)=0$, $\;u(1,\theta)=\sin\theta-2\sin5\theta$ for $0\le\theta\le\frac\pi2$.

"Insulated" = zero normal derivative. On a radial edge the normal is the $\theta$ direction, so it is $\frac1r u_\theta=0$, i.e. $u_\theta=0$.

(b) · Separation

Let $u=R(r)\Theta(\theta)$. Then $R''\Theta+\frac1rR'\Theta+\frac1{r^2}R\Theta''=0$. Multiply by $\dfrac{r^2}{R\Theta}$: $$\frac{r^2R''+rR'}{R}=-\frac{\Theta''}{\Theta}.$$ The left side depends only on $r$, the right only on $\theta$, so both equal a constant $\lambda$: $$r^2R''+rR'-\lambda R=0,\qquad \Theta''+\lambda\Theta=0.$$

The "depends only on … so constant" sentence is the mark. Don't divide by $r^2$ the wrong way round: you need the $r$-terms all on one side.

(c) · Eigenvalue problem in $\theta$

$u(r,0)=R(r)\Theta(0)=0$ for all $r$ ⇒ $\Theta(0)=0$ (else $u\equiv0$). Similarly $\Theta'(\frac\pi2)=0$.

$\lambda=-k^2<0$: $\Theta=A\cosh k\theta+B\sinh k\theta$. $\Theta(0)=0\Rightarrow A=0$; $\Theta'(\frac\pi2)=Bk\cosh\frac{k\pi}2=0\Rightarrow B=0$ since $\cosh>0$. Trivial.

$\lambda=0$: $\Theta=A+B\theta$. $A=0$, $\Theta'=B=0$. Trivial.

$\lambda=k^2>0$: $\Theta=A\cos k\theta+B\sin k\theta$. $A=0$; $\Theta'(\frac\pi2)=Bk\cos\frac{k\pi}2=0\Rightarrow \frac{k\pi}2=(n-\frac12)\pi\Rightarrow k=2n-1$.

$$\lambda_n=(2n-1)^2,\qquad \Theta_n(\theta)=\sin\big((2n-1)\theta\big),\quad n=1,2,3,\dots$$

Check against the table: Dirichlet at $0$, Neumann at $\beta=\frac\pi2$ ⇒ $\sin\frac{(n-\frac12)\pi\theta}{\beta}=\sin(2n-1)\theta$. Both $\sin\theta$ and $\sin5\theta$ in the data are in this family ($n=1,3$), which is your sign the setup is right.

(d) · Radial equation

$r^2R''+rR'-(2n-1)^2R=0$ is an Euler equation. Try $R=r^m$: $m(m-1)+m-(2n-1)^2=0\Rightarrow m=\pm(2n-1)$. So $$R_n=A_nr^{2n-1}+B_nr^{-(2n-1)}.$$ Both are kept: $r=0$ is not in the domain. $u(2,\theta)=0\Rightarrow R_n(2)=0$, so (up to a constant) $$R_n(r)=\Big(\frac2r\Big)^{2n-1}-\Big(\frac r2\Big)^{2n-1}.$$

Long way: $A_n2^{k}+B_n2^{-k}=0\Rightarrow B_n=-4^{k}A_n$, same thing. Writing $(\frac ba)^{k}$-style ratios directly is the polar version of $\sinh(k(b-y))$. Saying why $r^{-k}$ stays (no origin) is worth a line.

(e) · Superpose and match

$$u(r,\theta)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n\Big[\Big(\frac2r\Big)^{2n-1}-\Big(\frac r2\Big)^{2n-1}\Big]\sin\big((2n-1)\theta\big).$$

At $r=1$: $\displaystyle\sum c_n\big(2^{2n-1}-2^{-(2n-1)}\big)\sin(2n-1)\theta=\sin\theta-2\sin5\theta$. Match:

$n=1$: $c_1(2-\frac12)=1\Rightarrow c_1=\frac23$. $\quad n=3$: $c_3(32-\frac1{32})=-2\Rightarrow c_3=-\frac{64}{1023}$. All other $c_n=0$.

$$u(r,\theta)=\frac{4-r^2}{3r}\sin\theta-\frac{2(1024-r^{10})}{1023\,r^5}\sin5\theta.$$

Simplify: $\frac23(\frac2r-\frac r2)=\frac23\cdot\frac{4-r^2}{2r}$, and $-\frac{64}{1023}\cdot\frac{1024-r^{10}}{32r^5}$. Quick check: $r=2$ gives $0$ ✓, $r=1$ gives $1\cdot\sin\theta-\frac{2\cdot1023}{1023}\sin5\theta$ ✓. Verified in sympy: $\nabla^2u=0$ and all four BCs.

(f) · A point value

Insulated edge: $\theta=\frac\pi2$, $\sin\frac\pi2=1$, $\sin\frac{5\pi}2=1$. At $r=\sqrt2$: $r^2=2$, $r^{10}=32$, $r^5=4\sqrt2$. $$u=\frac{2}{3\sqrt2}-\frac{2\cdot992}{1023\cdot4\sqrt2}=\frac{\sqrt2}{3}-\frac{4\sqrt2}{33}=\frac{\sqrt2}{11}\approx0.129.$$

$992/1023=32/33$ (both divisible by 31). If you got a different number, recheck $\sin\frac{5\pi}2=+1$.
