Let $f(x)=\begin{cases}2,&-2<x<0\\ 2-x,&0\le x\le2\end{cases}$ and $f(x+4)=f(x)$.

(a) Even, odd, or neither? Consequence for the coefficients. [1]
(b) Sketch $f$ for $-6<x\le6$. [2]
(c) Compute $a_0$. [1]
(d) Show $a_n=\dfrac{2(1-(-1)^n)}{n^2\pi^2}$, $n\ge1$. [3]
(e) Compute $b_n$. [3]
(f) Write the series with only non-zero cosine terms. [2]
(g) Check $b_n$ via the odd part of $f$. [2]

(a) Parity

$f(-1)=2$ but $f(1)=1$, so $f(-1)\ne\pm f(1)$: $f$ is neither even nor odd. Hence no coefficients vanish by symmetry; compute $a_0$, $a_n$ and $b_n$.

One counterexample point is the cleanest proof of "neither".

(b) Sketch

Marks: correct shape on $(-2,2]$, correct repetition with period 4, axes labelled at the breakpoints. Open/closed dots follow the definition: $f(2)=0$, so the endpoint of each ramp is filled.

(c) $a_0$

$L=2$, so $k_n=\dfrac{n\pi}{2}$.

$$a_0=\frac12\int_{-2}^{2}f\,dx=\frac12\Big(\int_{-2}^{0}2\,dx+\int_0^2(2-x)\,dx\Big)=\frac12(4+2)=3.$$

Check: average over the period $=6/4=3/2=a_0/2$.

(d) $a_n$

Write $k=n\pi/2$, so $\sin2k=\sin n\pi=0$ and $\cos2k=(-1)^n$.

$$\int_{-2}^{0}2\cos kx\,dx=\frac2k\big[\sin kx\big]_{-2}^{0}=\frac2k\sin2k=0.$$

By parts with $u=2-x$, $dv=\cos kx\,dx$:

$$\int_0^2(2-x)\cos kx\,dx=\Big[\frac{(2-x)\sin kx}{k}\Big]_0^2+\frac1k\int_0^2\sin kx\,dx=0+\frac{1-\cos2k}{k^2}=\frac{4\big(1-(-1)^n\big)}{n^2\pi^2}.$$ $$a_n=\frac12\cdot\frac{4\big(1-(-1)^n\big)}{n^2\pi^2}=\frac{2\big(1-(-1)^n\big)}{n^2\pi^2}.\qquad\blacksquare$$

The boundary term vanishes at both ends: $2-x=0$ at $x=2$, and $\sin0=0$. Show the $\cos 2k=(-1)^n$ substitution explicitly; it is the line the marker looks for in a "show that".

(e) $b_n$

$$\int_{-2}^{0}2\sin kx\,dx=-\frac2k\big[\cos kx\big]_{-2}^{0}=-\frac2k\big(1-(-1)^n\big).$$

By parts with $u=2-x$, $dv=\sin kx\,dx$:

$$\int_0^2(2-x)\sin kx\,dx=\Big[-\frac{(2-x)\cos kx}{k}\Big]_0^2-\frac1k\int_0^2\cos kx\,dx=\frac2k-\frac{\sin 2k}{k^2}=\frac2k.$$ $$b_n=\frac12\Big(-\frac2k+\frac{2(-1)^n}{k}+\frac2k\Big)=\frac{(-1)^n}{k}=\frac{2(-1)^n}{n\pi}.$$

Sign check at $n=1$: $b_1=-2/\pi<0$. Plausible: $f$ is larger on the left half, where $\sin(\pi x/2)<0$.

(f) The series

$1-(-1)^n=0$ for even $n$ and $2$ for odd $n$, so only $n=2m-1$ survives in the cosine terms, with $a_{2m-1}=\dfrac{4}{(2m-1)^2\pi^2}$:

$$f(x)\sim\frac32+\frac{4}{\pi^2}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m-1)^2}\cos\frac{(2m-1)\pi x}{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{n}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{2}.$$

Constant term is $a_0/2$, not $a_0$. Numerically, the series gives $2$ at $x=0$, $1$ at $x=1$, and $1$ (the midpoint of the jump) at $x=2$: checked with 20 000 terms.

(g) Check via the odd part

On $(-2,2)$, $f(x)=2-\tfrac12(x+|x|)=\big(2-\tfrac12|x|\big)-\tfrac12x$. The first bracket is even, so it contributes only to $a_n$. For $x$ on $(-L,L]$ with $L=2$,

$$b_n[x]=\frac22\int_0^2x\sin\frac{n\pi x}{2}\,dx=\frac{4(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}\;\Rightarrow\;b_n[f]=-\frac12\cdot\frac{4(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}=\frac{2(-1)^n}{n\pi}\ ✓$$

The coefficient map $f\mapsto(a_n,b_n)$ is linear, so splitting $f$ into familiar pieces is always allowed. Here it also confirms $a_n$ comes only from the even part $2-\tfrac12|x|$.
