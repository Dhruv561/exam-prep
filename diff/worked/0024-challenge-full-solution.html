$f(x)=0$ for $-2<x\le0$, $f(x)=x$ for $0<x\le2$, extended periodically with period $4$.

(a) Using the complex form, show $c_n=\dfrac{i(-1)^n}{n\pi}+\dfrac{(-1)^n-1}{n^2\pi^2}$ for $n\ne0$, and find $c_0$. [4]
(b) Write down $a_0,a_n,b_n$ and verify $c_{-n}=\overline{c_n}$. [2]
(c) State the value of the series at $x=0$ with justification; use it to evaluate $\sum_{m\ge1}\frac1{(2m-1)^2}$. [2]
(d) Use Parseval to evaluate $\sum_{m\ge1}\frac1{(2m-1)^4}$ and hence $\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^4}$, given $\sum1/n^2=\pi^2/6$. [4]

(a) · Complex coefficients [4]

Period $2L=4$, so $L=2$, $k_n=\dfrac{n\pi}2$. Since $f=0$ on $(-2,0]$, $$c_n=\frac1{2L}\int_{-L}^{L}f(x)e^{-ik_nx}dx=\frac14\int_0^2x\,e^{-ik_nx}dx.$$ For $n\ne0$, integrate by parts ($u=x$, $dv=e^{-ik_nx}dx$): $$\int_0^2x\,e^{-ik_nx}dx=\Big[\frac{x\,e^{-ik_nx}}{-ik_n}\Big]_0^2+\frac1{ik_n}\int_0^2e^{-ik_nx}dx=\frac{2e^{-in\pi}}{-ik_n}+\frac{e^{-in\pi}-1}{k_n^2}.$$ With $e^{-in\pi}=(-1)^n$, $\frac1{-i}=i$ and $k_n=\frac{n\pi}2$: $$\int_0^2x\,e^{-ik_nx}dx=\frac{4i(-1)^n}{n\pi}+\frac{4\big((-1)^n-1\big)}{n^2\pi^2}.$$ Multiplying by $\frac14$: $$c_n=\frac{i(-1)^n}{n\pi}+\frac{(-1)^n-1}{n^2\pi^2},\quad n\ne0.$$ For $n=0$: $\;c_0=\dfrac14\displaystyle\int_0^2x\,dx=\dfrac12.$

Second term: $\frac1{ik_n}\cdot\frac{e^{-ik_nx}}{-ik_n}=\frac{e^{-ik_nx}}{k_n^2}$ because $i\cdot(-i)=1$. Marks: setup with $\frac1{2L}$ and the right limits (1), by parts (2), simplify plus $c_0$ separately (1). The formula divides by $n$, so $c_0$ must be done on its own.

(b) · Real coefficients and the symmetry check [2]

For real $f$: $a_0=2c_0$, $a_n=2\,\mathrm{Re}\,c_n$, $b_n=-2\,\mathrm{Im}\,c_n$. So $$a_0=1,\qquad a_n=\frac{2\big((-1)^n-1\big)}{n^2\pi^2},\qquad b_n=-\frac{2(-1)^n}{n\pi}=\frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}.$$ Replacing $n$ by $-n$ and using $(-1)^{-n}=(-1)^n$: $$c_{-n}=-\frac{i(-1)^n}{n\pi}+\frac{(-1)^n-1}{n^2\pi^2}=\overline{c_n}.\;\checkmark$$

Sanity check: $a_0=\frac12\int_0^2x\,dx=1$ ✓. $a_n=0$ for even $n$ and $-\frac4{n^2\pi^2}$ for odd $n$. Neither even nor odd, so both $a_n$ and $b_n$ survive.

(c) · Pointwise value at $x=0$ [2]

$f$ is piecewise smooth and $f(0^-)=0=f(0^+)$, so the periodic extension is continuous at $x=0$ and the series converges to $f(0)=0$. At $x=0$ all sine terms vanish: $$0=\frac{a_0}2+\sum_{n\ge1}a_n=\frac12-\frac4{\pi^2}\sum_{n\text{ odd}}\frac1{n^2}\;\Rightarrow\;\sum_{m\ge1}\frac1{(2m-1)^2}=\frac{\pi^2}8.$$

"Calculation required" means show the substitution; quoting $\pi^2/8$ earns nothing. The jumps are at $x=\pm2$ (value $2$ to $0$), where the series gives the average $1$.

(d) · Parseval [4]

Parseval's identity (Lecture 19): $\displaystyle\frac1L\int_{-L}^{L}|f|^2dx=\frac{a_0^2}2+\sum_{n\ge1}(a_n^2+b_n^2)$.

Left side: $\displaystyle\frac12\int_0^2x^2dx=\frac12\cdot\frac83=\frac43.$

Right side: $\dfrac{a_0^2}2=\dfrac12$; $\;a_n^2=\dfrac{16}{n^4\pi^4}$ for odd $n$ and $0$ for even $n$; $\;b_n^2=\dfrac4{n^2\pi^2}$, so $\displaystyle\sum b_n^2=\frac4{\pi^2}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}6=\frac23$. Hence $$\frac43=\frac12+\frac23+\frac{16}{\pi^4}\sum_{n\text{ odd}}\frac1{n^4}\;\Rightarrow\;\sum_{m\ge1}\frac1{(2m-1)^4}=\frac{\pi^4}{16}\cdot\frac16=\frac{\pi^4}{96}.$$ Splitting into odd and even $n$, with $\sum_{\text{even}}\frac1{n^4}=\frac1{16}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^4}$: $$\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^4}=\frac{\pi^4}{96}+\frac1{16}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^4}\;\Rightarrow\;\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^4}=\frac{16}{15}\cdot\frac{\pi^4}{96}=\frac{\pi^4}{90}.$$

Marks: correct statement with $\frac1L$ and $\frac{a_0^2}2$ (1), left side (1), right side with $(-1)^n$ squared away and odd-only $a_n^2$ (1), both sums (1). Cross-check: $\pi^4/90$ matches lecture Ex 4.11, which got it from $x^2$.
