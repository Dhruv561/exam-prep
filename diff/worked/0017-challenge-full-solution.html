The challenge, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule goes on your paper. Grey notes are commentary. Printing hides them.

Let $y$ solve $\;y''+6y'+13y=g(t),\;y(0)=0,\;y'(0)=2$, where $g$ is a given function with Laplace transform $G$.

(a) Show that $Y(s)=\dfrac{2+G(s)}{(s+3)^2+4}$. [2]
(b) Express $y(t)$ as the sum of a function of $t$ and an integral involving $g$. [3]
(c) Now let $g(t)=e^{-3t}$. Evaluate your integral to find $y(t)$, and verify $y'(0)=2$. [3]
(d) Find the continuous $\varphi$ with $\;\varphi(t)=\sin t+2\displaystyle\int_0^t\cos(t-\tau)\,\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau$. [4]

(a)

Take $\mathcal L$ of both sides, using $\mathcal L\{y''\}=s^2Y-sy(0)-y'(0)=s^2Y-2$ and $\mathcal L\{y'\}=sY-y(0)=sY$:

$$s^2Y-2+6sY+13Y=G\;\Rightarrow\;(s^2+6s+13)Y=2+G.$$

Since $s^2+6s+13=(s+3)^2+4$,

$$Y(s)=\frac{2+G(s)}{(s+3)^2+4}.\;\blacksquare$$

Marks: derivative rule with ICs [1], completing the square [1].

(b)

$$Y=\frac{2}{(s+3)^2+4}+\frac{1}{(s+3)^2+4}\,G(s).$$

From the table with the $s$-shift, $\mathcal L^{-1}\left\{\frac{2}{(s+3)^2+4}\right\}=e^{-3t}\sin2t$ and $h(t):=\mathcal L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{(s+3)^2+4}\right\}=\tfrac12e^{-3t}\sin2t$. By the convolution theorem, $\mathcal L^{-1}\{HG\}=h*g$, so

$$y(t)=e^{-3t}\sin2t+\frac12\int_0^t e^{-3(t-\tau)}\sin\big(2(t-\tau)\big)\,g(\tau)\,d\tau.$$

Marks: first term [1], $h$ [1], convolution [1]. Since $*$ is commutative you may also write $h(\tau)g(t-\tau)$; part (c) uses that order.

(c)

By commutativity the integral is $\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_0^te^{-3\tau}\sin2\tau\;g(t-\tau)\,d\tau$. With $g(t-\tau)=e^{-3(t-\tau)}$:

$$\tfrac12\int_0^te^{-3\tau}\sin2\tau\;e^{-3(t-\tau)}d\tau=\tfrac12e^{-3t}\int_0^t\sin2\tau\,d\tau=\tfrac12e^{-3t}\cdot\frac{1-\cos2t}{2}=\tfrac14e^{-3t}(1-\cos2t).$$ $$\boxed{y(t)=e^{-3t}\Big(\sin2t+\tfrac14-\tfrac14\cos2t\Big)}$$

$y(0)=0$. Differentiating, $y'=-3y+e^{-3t}\big(2\cos2t+\tfrac12\sin2t\big)$, so $y'(0)=-3(0)+2=2$ ✓.

Choosing the order that makes the exponentials cancel saves the integration by parts. Marks: reorder/simplify [1], integral [1], answer + check [1].

(d)

The integral is $\cos*\varphi$, with transform $\dfrac{s}{s^2+1}\Phi(s)$. Transforming:

$$\Phi=\frac1{s^2+1}+\frac{2s}{s^2+1}\Phi\;\Rightarrow\;(s^2+1-2s)\Phi=1\;\Rightarrow\;\Phi=\frac1{(s-1)^2}.$$

Since $\mathcal L\{t\}=1/s^2$, the $s$-shift gives

$$\boxed{\varphi(t)=te^{t}}.$$

Check: $\varphi(0)=0=\sin0+0$ ✓.

Marks: recognise convolution [1], transform equation [1], solve for $\Phi$ [1], invert [1]. Extra check: $\cos*\varphi=\tfrac12(te^t-\sin t)$, which satisfies the equation.
