A damped oscillator satisfies $\;2y''+4y'+10y=20e^{-t}\;$ with $y(0)=1,\ y'(0)=0$.

(a) Show that $\;Y(s)=\dfrac{s+2}{s^2+2s+5}+\dfrac{10}{(s+1)(s^2+2s+5)}.$ [3]
(b) Express $\dfrac{10}{(s+1)(s^2+2s+5)}$ in partial fractions with real constants. [4]
(c) Hence find $y(t)$. [3]
(d) Check against both ICs; describe the motion for large $t$ and why. [2]

Write thisDon't write this

(a) Transform

Let $Y=\mathcal L\{y\}$. Then $\mathcal L\{y'\}=sY-y(0)=sY-1$ and $\mathcal L\{y''\}=s^2Y-sy(0)-y'(0)=s^2Y-s$. Also $\mathcal L\{e^{-t}\}=\dfrac1{s+1}$. Transforming the ODE:

$$2(s^2Y-s)+4(sY-1)+10Y=\frac{20}{s+1}\;\Rightarrow\;(2s^2+4s+10)\,Y=2s+4+\frac{20}{s+1}.$$

Dividing by $2$:

$$Y=\frac{s+2}{s^2+2s+5}+\frac{10}{(s+1)(s^2+2s+5)}.\qquad\blacksquare$$

Carry the 2 through both IC terms of $y''$ (and only the $y$ IC for $4y'$). Dividing by 2 at the end is safe; dividing the ODE first is also fine if the forcing becomes $10e^{-t}$.

(b) Partial fractions

$s^2+2s+5$ has discriminant $4-20<0$, so it is irreducible. Write

$$\frac{10}{(s+1)(s^2+2s+5)}=\frac{A}{s+1}+\frac{Bs+C}{s^2+2s+5}\;\Rightarrow\;10=A(s^2+2s+5)+(Bs+C)(s+1).$$

$s=-1$: $10=4A$, so $A=\tfrac52$. Coefficient of $s^2$: $0=A+B$, so $B=-\tfrac52$. Constant term: $10=5A+C$, so $C=10-\tfrac{25}2=-\tfrac52$. Hence

$$\frac{10}{(s+1)(s^2+2s+5)}=\frac{5/2}{s+1}-\frac52\cdot\frac{s+1}{s^2+2s+5}.$$

Check at $s=0$: LHS $=10/5=2$; RHS $=\tfrac52-\tfrac52\cdot\tfrac15=\tfrac52-\tfrac12=2$ ✓.

(c) Invert

Complete the square: $s^2+2s+5=(s+1)^2+4$. Split $s+2=(s+1)+1=(s+1)+\tfrac12\cdot2$:

$$Y=\frac{5/2}{s+1}+\Big(1-\frac52\Big)\frac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+4}+\frac12\cdot\frac{2}{(s+1)^2+4}=\frac{5/2}{s+1}-\frac32\cdot\frac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+4}+\frac12\cdot\frac{2}{(s+1)^2+4}.$$

From the sheet, $\mathcal L\{e^{at}\}=\frac1{s-a}$, $\cos2t\leftrightarrow\frac{s}{s^2+4}$, $\sin2t\leftrightarrow\frac2{s^2+4}$, and the s-shift $s\to s+1$ gives a factor $e^{-t}$:

$$y(t)=\tfrac52e^{-t}-\tfrac32e^{-t}\cos2t+\tfrac12e^{-t}\sin2t=\tfrac12e^{-t}\big(5-3\cos2t+\sin2t\big).$$

State the s-shift theorem by name. The marks are in the split and the table lookup.

(d) Checks and interpretation

$y(0)=\tfrac12(5-3)=1$ ✓. Let $g=5-3\cos2t+\sin2t$, so $y=\tfrac12e^{-t}g$ and $y'=\tfrac12e^{-t}(g'-g)$. $g'=6\sin2t+2\cos2t$, so $g'(0)=2=g(0)$ and $y'(0)=0$ ✓.

As $t\to\infty$, $y\to0$: $g$ is bounded and $e^{-t}\to0$. The motion is a damped oscillation of angular frequency $2$ (from the roots $-1\pm2i$ of $2s^2+4s+10$) inside the envelope $e^{-t}$, on top of a decaying forced response $\tfrac52e^{-t}$ from the forcing $20e^{-t}$. Every exponent has negative real part, so nothing survives.

The forced part checks independently: $y_p=ke^{-t}$ gives $(2-4+10)k=20$, so $k=\tfrac52$, matching $A$ in (b).
