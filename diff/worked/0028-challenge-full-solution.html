The mixed-end rod, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes.

A rod of length $2$ has its lateral surface insulated and thermal diffusivity $3$. The end $x=0$ is kept in ice and the end $x=2$ is insulated. Initially $u(x,0)=x$.

(a) PDE, BCs, IC. [2] (b) Separate; conditions on $X$. [2] (c) Three cases: eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. [4] (d) $T_n$ and general solution. [2] (e) $u(x,t)$. [4] (f) One-term approximation to $u(2,t)$; sketch. [2] (g) $u$ if $u(x,0)=4\sin\frac{\pi x}{4}-\sin\frac{5\pi x}{4}$. [2]

Write thisDon't write this

(a) · The IBVP [2]

$$u_t=3u_{xx},\qquad 0\lt x\lt2,\ t\gt0,$$ $$u(0,t)=0,\quad u_x(2,t)=0\quad(t\gt0),\qquad u(x,0)=x\quad(0\lt x\lt2).$$

[1] PDE with $\alpha=3$ and ranges; [1] both BCs plus IC. "Lateral surface insulated" only justifies the 1D equation. "Insulated" at $x=2$ is the Neumann condition.

(b) · Separation [2]

Substitute $u=X(x)T(t)$: $XT'=3X''T$. Divide by $3XT$: $$\frac{T'}{3T}=\frac{X''}{X}=\lambda,$$ constant because the left side depends only on $t$ and the right only on $x$. Hence $X''-\lambda X=0$ and $T'-3\lambda T=0$.

$u(0,t)=X(0)T(t)=0$ for all $t\gt0$, and $T\not\equiv0$ (else $u\equiv0$), so $X(0)=0$. Similarly $u_x(2,t)=X'(2)T(t)=0\Rightarrow X'(2)=0$.

The "$T\not\equiv0$" clause is the step that's usually skipped and usually marked.

(c) · Three cases [4]

$\lambda=k^2\gt0$: $X=A\cosh kx+B\sinh kx$. $X(0)=A=0$. $X'(2)=Bk\cosh2k=0$, and $k\cosh2k\ne0$, so $B=0$. Trivial only.

$\lambda=0$: $X=Ax+B$. $X(0)=B=0$, $X'(2)=A=0$. Trivial only.

$\lambda=-k^2\lt0$: $X=A\cos kx+B\sin kx$. $X(0)=A=0$. $X'(2)=Bk\cos2k=0$; for $B\ne0$, $\cos2k=0$, so $2k=\left(n-\tfrac12\right)\pi$: $$k_n=\frac{(2n-1)\pi}{4},\qquad \lambda_n=-k_n^2=-\frac{(2n-1)^2\pi^2}{16},\qquad X_n=\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{4},\quad n=1,2,\dots$$

Using $\cosh,\sinh$ instead of $e^{\pm kx}$ makes $X(0)=0$ kill a term at once. Both forms get full marks. [1] each trivial case, [2] the oscillatory case with $k_n$.

(d) · Time ODE and general solution [2]

$T_n'-3\lambda_nT_n=0\Rightarrow T_n=e^{3\lambda_nt}=e^{-3(2n-1)^2\pi^2t/16}$. By superposition, $$u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty C_n\,e^{-3(2n-1)^2\pi^2t/16}\,\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{4}.$$

(e) · Coefficients [4]

At $t=0$: $x=\sum C_n\sin k_nx$. The $X_n$ are orthogonal on $(0,2)$ with $\int_0^2X_n^2\,dx=1$, so $$C_n=\frac22\int_0^2x\sin k_nx\,dx=\Big[-\frac{x\cos k_nx}{k_n}\Big]_0^2+\frac1{k_n}\int_0^2\cos k_nx\,dx=-\frac{2\cos2k_n}{k_n}+\frac{\sin2k_n}{k_n^2}.$$

$2k_n=\frac{(2n-1)\pi}2$, so $\cos2k_n=0$ and $\sin2k_n=(-1)^{n+1}$. Hence $$C_n=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{k_n^2}=\frac{16(-1)^{n+1}}{(2n-1)^2\pi^2},$$ $$\boxed{u(x,t)=\frac{16}{\pi^2}\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(2n-1)^2}\,e^{-3(2n-1)^2\pi^2t/16}\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{4}.}$$

[1] formula $C_n=\frac2L\int_0^LfX_n$ (not on the formula sheet). [1] integration by parts. [1] evaluating $\cos2k_n=0$, $\sin2k_n=(-1)^{n+1}$. [1] final series. Sanity check: $C_1=\frac{16}{\pi^2}\approx1.6$, about the size of $x$ on $(0,2)$.

(f) · Long time and sketch [2]

Every term decays; $n=1$ decays slowest. For large $t$, $$u(x,t)\approx\frac{16}{\pi^2}e^{-3\pi^2t/16}\sin\frac{\pi x}{4},\qquad u(2,t)\approx\frac{16}{\pi^2}e^{-3\pi^2t/16}.$$

Sketch: $t=0$, the line from $(0,0)$ to $(2,2)$. $t_1$: smooth, through $0$ at $x=0$, increasing, flat at $x=2$, and below the line. $t_2$: a lower quarter-sine. $t=\infty$: $u\equiv0$.

The IC has slope $1$ at $x=2$, but every $t\gt0$ curve has slope $0$ there: the BC wins instantly. Turn on "Exam sketch" for the four curves.

(g) · Matching variant [2]

$\sin\frac{\pi x}4=X_1$ ($k_1=\frac\pi4$) and $\sin\frac{5\pi x}4=X_3$ ($k_3=\frac{5\pi}4$). By linear independence, $C_1=4$, $C_3=-1$, and all other $C_n=0$: $$u(x,t)=4e^{-3\pi^2t/16}\sin\frac{\pi x}{4}-e^{-75\pi^2t/16}\sin\frac{5\pi x}{4}.$$

Check that each term is in the eigen-list for these BCs: $\frac{5\pi}{4}=\frac{(2\cdot3-1)\pi}{4}$ ✓. Something like $\sin\frac{\pi x}{2}$ would not be ($k=\frac{2\pi}4$ is not half-odd).
