Overdispersion and quasi-likelihood

Topic 3 on one printed page. Every past paper's GLM R-output question uses the bottom half. Each heading links to the lesson that teaches it.

Overdispersion · Lesson 10

Definition: the actual \(\operatorname{Var}Y_i\) is larger than the model says (Poisson \(\mu_i\); binomial \(m_ip_i(1-p_i)\)). Causes: dependent trials or events (clustering), a \(p\) or rate that varies between units (missing covariates). Lack of fit can also come from a wrong mean structure or outliers, so check residuals vs \(\hat\eta\) for a trend first. slide 3; troutegg notes

Quantitative evidence (exam: "give a quantitative reason")Overdispersed if
Residual deviance vs residual df\(D \gg n-p\) (e.g. 506 on 42 df); \(D\approx\chi^2_{n-p}\) needs large counts, and Bernoulli data (\(m_i=1\)) can't show it
Fit test pchisq(D, df, lower.tail=FALSE)p-value tiny
\(\hat\phi=X^2/(n-p)\), \(X^2=\sum(y_i-\hat\mu_i)^2/v(\hat\mu_i)\) sum(residuals(m,"pearson")^2)/m$df.residual\(\hat\phi\gg1\)
Ignoring it (model answer): \(\hat\beta\) is unaffected, but \(\operatorname{Var}\hat\beta\) is underestimated by the factor \(\phi\). SEs are too small, so z-values are too large, p-values too small, CIs too narrow and χ² LRTs too significant. You end up keeping predictors that aren't important. slide 13

Quasi-likelihood · Lesson 11

Specify only \(g(\mu_i)=x_i^T\beta\), \(\mathbb EY_i=\mu_i\), \(\operatorname{Var}Y_i=\phi\,v(\mu_i)\). IWLS needs only \(g\) and \(v\). slides 4–8

$$u_i=\frac{Y_i-\mu_i}{\phi v(\mu_i)}:\quad \mathbb Eu_i=0,\quad \operatorname{Var}u_i=\frac1{\phi v(\mu_i)},\quad -\mathbb E\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial\mu_i}=\frac1{\phi v(\mu_i)}=\mathbb Eu_i^2$$ $$Q_i=\int_{y_i}^{\mu_i}\frac{y_i-t}{\phi\,v(t)}\,dt\ \le 0,\qquad Q=\sum_i Q_i,\qquad \frac{D_Q}{\phi}=-2\sum_iQ_i\ \text{(scaled; saturated }Q=0),\qquad D_Q=-2\sum_i\int_{y_i}^{\mu_i}\frac{y_i-t}{v(t)}\,dt$$
\(v(\mu)\)\(\phi\,Q_i\)\(D_Q\) equals
\(\mu\) (quasi-Poisson)\(y\log(\mu/y)-(\mu-y)\)Poisson deviance
\(\mu(m-\mu)/m\) (quasi-binomial)\(y\log(\mu/y)+(m-y)\log\frac{m-\mu}{m-y}\)binomial deviance
\(\mu^2\)\(-\frac{y}{\mu}+1-\log\frac{\mu}{y}\)gamma deviance

Slides 10–11 write \(Q_i\) up to terms free of \(\mu\): quasi-Poisson \(\frac1\phi[y\log\mu-\mu]\). Use the definite integral (as above) for numeric \(Q\) and \(D_Q\). Use \(0\log0=0\).

  • \(1/\phi\) factors out of the quasi-score equations, so \(\hat\beta_{\text{quasi}}=\hat\beta_{\text{MLE}}\) and the deviances match. \(\phi\) is estimated by \(X^2/(n-p)\), not by maximising \(Q\). Asymptotics are as for the MLE, but less efficient when the true distribution is known. slide 7, 12
  • "Can you fit [non-standard] responses with quasi-likelihood? How?" Yes: write the variance as \(\phi\,v(\mu)\) using the known mean–variance relation, pick a link \(g\), fit by IWLS (family=quasi(link=…, variance=…)), set \(\hat\phi=X^2/(n-p)\), and compare models with F tests.

Quasi-Poisson / quasi-binomial R output · Lesson 12

$$\text{Poisson vs quasi-Poisson: } \operatorname{Var}Y=\mu \text{ vs } \phi\mu;\qquad \text{quasi-binomial: } \operatorname{Var}Y=\phi\,m p(1-p)$$ $$\mathrm{SE}_{\text{quasi}}=\sqrt{\hat\phi}\;\mathrm{SE}_{\phi=1},\qquad t=z/\sqrt{\hat\phi},\qquad F=\frac{(D_A-D_B)/s}{\hat\phi_B}\approx F_{s,\;n-p_B}\quad(A\subset B,\ s=p_B-p_A)$$
  • Same \(\hat\beta\), fitted values, predictions and deviances in both fits. Quasi output shows t value, Pr(>|t|), (Dispersion parameter for quasipoisson family taken to be φ̂), and AIC: NA because there's no likelihood.
  • The reverse direction: Poisson SE \(=\) quasi SE \(\div\sqrt{\hat\phi}\).
  • The F test uses \(\hat\phi\) from the bigger model. R: anova(A, B, test="F") on quasi fits, or anova(A, B, dispersion=phihat, test="F") on Poisson/binomial fits (same F, but the p-value uses ∞ denominator df, so it differs slightly). drop1/step don't work for quasi fits (slide 12). slide 9
  • The deviance can't test fit for a quasi model, so don't use χ² on the raw \(\Delta D\). Allowing for \(\phi\), F is smaller than \(\Delta D/s\) and terms often stop being significant.
  • Still asked on the same question: Poisson \(\hat\ell=p-\text{AIC}/2\), saturated \(\ell_s=\hat\ell+D/2\), prediction \(\exp(\hat\eta)\) (plus offset), Wald CI \(\hat\beta\pm1.96\,\mathrm{SE}\).
Traps
The printout gives two \(\hat\phi\) lines: use the big model's for F and the model in question's for its SEs · the F df are \((s,\,n-p_B)\), not \((s,\,n-p_A)\) · don't divide the estimate by anything: only SEs and test statistics change · \(\hat\phi=X^2/(n-p)\) (Pearson), not \(D/(n-p)\) · for a quasi fit R doesn't print an AIC, so get \(\hat\ell\) from the Poisson fit.
"Difference between Poisson and quasi-Poisson" (2–3 sentences): Poisson assumes \(\operatorname{Var}Y_i=\mu_i\) (\(\phi=1\)); quasi-Poisson assumes \(\operatorname{Var}Y_i=\phi\mu_i\) with \(\phi\) estimated by \(X^2/(n-p)\), and only the mean and variance are specified, not a distribution. The estimates are identical, but quasi SEs are \(\sqrt{\hat\phi}\) times larger and nested models are compared with an F test instead of χ².
