Exponential family · Lesson 5

$$f(y;\theta,\phi)=\exp\!\left[\frac{y\theta-b(\theta)}{a(\phi)}+c(y,\phi)\right],\qquad \mathbb EY=\mu=b'(\theta),\qquad \operatorname{Var}Y=a(\phi)\,b''(\theta)=a(\phi)\,v(\mu)$$ $$v=b''\circ(b')^{-1}\ \text{(compute }b''(\theta)\text{, substitute }\theta(\mu)\text{)},\qquad \text{canonical link } g=(b')^{-1},\ \text{i.e. } g(\mu)=\theta .$$

Recipe: take logs · check the support doesn't involve the parameter · the term with \(y\) times the parameter gives \(\theta\) (the parameter must multiply \(y\), not \(y^2\), \(\log y\) or \(e^y\)) · factor any scale out of both the \(y\)-term and the parameter-only term to get \(a(\phi)\) · the parameter-only term is \(-b/a(\phi)\): rewrite \(b\) in \(\theta\) · the rest is \(c(y,\phi)\) · check \(b'(\theta)=\mu\). Proof of the lemma: \(\mathbb E\ell'=0\) and \(\mathbb E\ell''=-\mathbb E(\ell')^2\) for one observation. Exp. families slides 3, 8, 9; GLMs I slide 4

GLM, score, IWLS, \(\operatorname{Var}\hat\beta\) · Lesson 6

GLM = independent \(Y_i\) from one exponential family (common \(\phi\)) + \(\eta_i=x_i^T\beta\) + monotone differentiable \(g(\mu_i)=\eta_i\). \(\phi\) is not modelled. GLMs I slide 3

$$U_j=\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\beta_j}=\sum_i\frac{(y_i-\mu_i)\,x_{ij}}{a(\phi)\,v(\mu_i)\,g'(\mu_i)}\qquad\text{(chain }\beta\to\eta\to\mu\to\theta\text{: }x_{ij},\ 1/g',\ 1/v)$$ $$z_i=\eta_i+(y_i-\mu_i)\,g'(\mu_i),\qquad W_{ii}=\frac{1}{g'(\mu_i)^2\,v(\mu_i)},\qquad \hat\beta^{\text{new}}=(X^TWX)^{-1}X^TWz$$ $$\operatorname{Var}\hat\beta=a(\phi)\,(X^T\hat WX)^{-1}=\mathcal I(\hat\beta)^{-1},\qquad \widehat{a(\phi)}=\hat\phi=\frac{X^2}{n-p},\quad X^2=\sum_i\frac{(y_i-\hat\mu_i)^2}{v(\hat\mu_i)}$$
  • IWLS: start \(\hat\mu^0=y\) (R: Poisson \(y+0.1\), binomial \((y+0.5)/(m+1)\)); iterate to convergence. IWLS = Fisher scoring for every GLM; R's "Number of Fisher Scoring iterations". slides 12–14
  • \(a(\phi)\) cancels in the update (\(\hat\beta\) doesn't depend on it) but not in \(\operatorname{Var}\hat\beta\): SEs scale with \(\sqrt{\hat\phi}\). slides 15–17
  • Canonical link \(\Rightarrow g'=1/v\): \(U=X^T(y-\mu)/a(\phi)\), \(W_{ii}=v(\mu_i)\), Hessian has no \(y\) so \(J=\mathcal I\) and Newton–Raphson = Fisher scoring; \(X^Ty\) sufficient; with an intercept (or a factor) the fitted totals equal the observed totals (Lesson 9 challenge (f)). Non-canonical: \(J\) contains \(y\), \(J\neq\mathcal I\).

Poisson GLMs and rate models · Lesson 7

  • Log link: \(+\Delta\) in \(x_j\) multiplies the mean by \(e^{\beta_j\Delta}\); CI \(\exp\big(\Delta(\hat\beta_j\pm1.96\,\text{se}_j)\big)\). Predictions: CI on \(\hat\eta\) (R's se.fit), then exponentiate.
  • Deviance \(D=2\sum_i\big[y_i\log\frac{y_i}{\hat\mu_i}-(y_i-\hat\mu_i)\big]\) (\(0\log0=0\), so \(y_i=0\) gives \(d_i=2\hat\mu_i\)). GLMs II slide 5
  • \(\phi=1\): fit test \(D\approx\chi^2_{n-p}\) only if every \(\hat\lambda_i\gtrsim5\) (binomial: \(m_ip_i,\ m_i(1-p_i)\ge5\)); LRT \(D_A-D_B\approx\chi^2_s\) needs no such condition; \(\ell=p-\text{AIC}/2\). slides 7–8, 10
  • Rate model: \(\log\lambda_i=\log t_i+x_i^T\beta\), R's offset(log(t)): coefficient fixed at 1, not estimated, not in \(p\); \(e^{\beta_j}\) are rate ratios. To predict a rate per 1000, set \(t=1000\) in newdata. Check proportionality by estimating the coefficient of \(\log t\) and testing \(=1\) (Wald \((\hat\gamma-1)/\text{se}\)).
  • Area-level predictor (e.g. water hardness) is nested in the area factor: \(s=(\#\text{areas}-1)-1\) (Lesson 9 challenge (b)).

Gamma (and any GLM with unknown \(\phi\)) · Lesson 7

  • \(v(\mu)=\mu^2\), \(\operatorname{Var}Y=\phi\mu^2\), CV \(=\sqrt\phi\). Deviance \(D=-2\sum_i\big[\log\frac{y_i}{\hat\mu_i}-\frac{y_i-\hat\mu_i}{\hat\mu_i}\big]\). Default link inverse \(1/\mu\); use log for multiplicative effects.
  • R's "Dispersion parameter … taken to be" is the Pearson \(\hat\phi=X^2/(n-p)\), not \(D/(n-p)\). Gamma Pearson residual \(=(y-\hat\mu)/\hat\mu\). Wald column is t value.
  • No deviance fit test (\(\hat\phi\) comes from the same residuals). slide 7
  • F test, A ⊂ B, \(s=p_B-p_A\): \(\displaystyle F=\frac{(D_A-D_B)/s}{\hat\phi_B}\approx F_{s,\,n-p_B}\), \(\hat\phi_B=X^2_B/(n-p_B)\) from the bigger model; R: anova(A, B, test = "F"). slide 9
  • AIC: R counts \(\phi\) as a parameter for gamma and inverse Gaussian, so \(\ell=(p+1)-\text{AIC}/2\). A continuous \(\ell\) (and so AIC) can be positive or negative.

Reading them: outlier = large jack-knife residual · high leverage = potential to move the fit (large when \(x_i\) is unusual and its IWLS weight is large; a point with \(\hat p\approx0\) has tiny weight) · influential = large Cook's, needs leverage and a residual; confirm by refitting without it. Half-normal plots: look for a jump in the tail, not a straight line. Residuals vs \(\hat\eta\): trend ⇒ wrong systematic part; changing spread ⇒ wrong \(v(\mu)\); curved bands come from discrete \(y\) (\(y=0\) gives \(r_D=-\sqrt{2\hat\mu}\)).
