The prerequisites for Topics 4–6 on one printed page. Taught in Lesson 14; kernels, completing the square and conjugacy are in Lesson 15. The formula sheet gives pdfs and means only; anything marked ★ is not on it.

1 · Joint, marginal, conditional

\(p(x,z)=p(x\mid z)p(z)\) · \(p(x)=\sum_zp(x,z)\) (or \(\int\)) · \(p(z\mid x)=p(x,z)/p(x)\). Conditional independence: iid given the parameters means \(p(x\mid\theta)=\prod_ip(x_i\mid\theta)\), but marginally the \(x_i\) are dependent (\(\operatorname{Cov}=\operatorname{Var}\) of the shared parameter, for Poisson–Gamma).

Mixture: \(Z_i\sim\text{Cat}(\pi_1,\dots,\pi_K)\), \(X_i\mid Z_i=k\sim f_k\). \(P(x_i,Z_i=k)=\pi_kf_k(x_i)\); \(p(x_i)=\sum_k\pi_kf_k(x_i)\); \(\ell(\theta)=\sum_i\log\sum_k\pi_kf_k(x_i)\) (sum inside the log, so no closed form). Mixture slide 12, EM slide 6

$$\text{Complete data: }P(X,Z\mid\theta)=\prod_i\prod_k[\pi_kf_k(x_i)]^{I(Z_i=k)},\qquad \log P(X,Z\mid\theta)=\sum_i\sum_kI(Z_i=k)[\log\pi_k+\log f_k(x_i)].$$

Hierarchical Bayes: \(p(x,\mu,\tau)=\prod_ip(x_i\mid\mu,\tau)\,p(\mu)\,p(\tau)\) (independent priors) or \(\prod_ip(x_i\mid\mu,\tau)\,p(\mu\mid\tau)\,p(\tau)\) (VI example). Each prior appears once. VI slide 20, MC slide 36

2 · Responsibility = Bayes' rule for a label

$$\gamma_{ik}=P(Z_i=k\mid X,\theta^0)=\frac{\pi_k^0f_k(x_i;\theta^0)}{\sum_j\pi_j^0f_j(x_i;\theta^0)}\qquad K=2:\ \gamma_{i1}=\frac{1}{1+e^{d}},\ d=\log\frac{\pi_2f_2(x_i)}{\pi_1f_1(x_i)}=\log\frac{\pi_2\sigma_1}{\pi_1\sigma_2}-\frac{(x_i-\mu_2)^2}{2\sigma_2^2}+\frac{(x_i-\mu_1)^2}{2\sigma_1^2}$$
ComponentsCancelsKeep
Normal, equal \(\sigma\)\(\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\)\(\pi_ke^{-(x-\mu_k)^2/2\sigma^2}\)
Normal, unequal \(\sigma_k\)\(\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\)\(\frac{\pi_k}{\sigma_k}e^{-(x-\mu_k)^2/2\sigma_k^2}\)
Poisson(\(\lambda_k\))\(1/x!\)\(\pi_k\lambda_k^{x}e^{-\lambda_k}\)
Exponential(rate \(\lambda_k\))nothing\(\pi_k\lambda_ke^{-\lambda_kx}\)

Example: \(\pi_1=0.4\), \(N(10,2^2)\) vs \(N(16,3^2)\), \(x=13\): \(\frac{0.4}2e^{-9/8}=0.0649\), \(\frac{0.6}3e^{-1/2}=0.1213\), so \(\gamma_1=0.35\). R: pi1*dnorm(x,m1,s1)/(pi1*dnorm(x,m1,s1)+pi2*dnorm(x,m2,s2)) gives \(\gamma_{i1}\) for every \(x_i\) (dnorm takes the SD). Mixture slide 14

Traps
Only constants common to every term cancel · don't forget \(\pi_k\) · \(K=3\): three terms below the line, so \(1-\gamma_1\) is not \(\gamma_2\) · a known label (an observation known to come from component 1) has \(\gamma_{i1}=1\), \(\gamma_{i2}=0\): don't apply Bayes' rule to it.

3 · Indicator expectation → Q-function

\(\mathbb E[I(A)]=P(A)\), so \(\mathbb E_{Z\mid X,\theta^0}[I(Z_i=k)]=\gamma_{ik}\). The complete log-likelihood is linear in the indicators, so by linearity:

$$Q(\theta,\theta^0)=\mathbb E_{Z\mid X,\theta^0}[\log P(X,Z\mid\theta)]=\sum_i\sum_k\gamma_{ik}\big[\log\pi_k+\log f_k(x_i;\theta)\big],\qquad \mathbb E[N_k\mid X]=\sum_i\gamma_{ik}.$$

E-step: indicators → \(\gamma_{ik}\) (at \(\theta^0\), then fixed). This can't be done to \(\log\sum_k\), where \(Z\) is already summed out inside the log. EM slides 18–19

4 · Adam's and Eve's laws

$$\mathbb EX=\mathbb E[\mathbb E(X\mid Z)],\qquad \operatorname{Var}X=\mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(X\mid Z)]+\operatorname{Var}[\mathbb E(X\mid Z)]\quad(\text{within}+\text{between})$$
UseResult
Mixture, \(\bar\mu=\sum\pi_k\mu_k\)\(\operatorname{Var}X=\sum\pi_k\sigma_k^2+\sum\pi_k(\mu_k-\bar\mu)^2\); for \(K=2\) the between part is \(\pi_1\pi_2(\mu_1-\mu_2)^2\). Not the same as \(\operatorname{Var}(\pi_1X_1+\pi_2X_2)\)
Poisson–Gamma, \(\lambda\sim\text{Gamma}(a,b)\) (rate)\(\mathbb EX=a/b=\mu\); \(\operatorname{Var}X=a/b+a/b^2=\mu+\mu^2/a>\mu\): overdispersion, marginally negative binomial. Fix for a Bayesian model: unit-level \(\lambda_i\) (random effect)
Posterior predictive\(\operatorname{Var}\tilde x=\operatorname{Var}(\theta\mid x)+\sigma^2\), mean \(\mathbb E(\theta\mid x)\) Intro Bayes slide 20
Beta-binomial, \(X\mid\theta\sim\text{Bin}(n,\theta)\)\(\mathbb EX=n\mathbb E\theta\); \(\operatorname{Var}X=n(\mathbb E\theta-\mathbb E\theta^2)+n^2\operatorname{Var}\theta\)

★ Variances not on the formula sheet: Gamma \(a/b^2\) · Inv-Gamma \(b^2/[(a-1)^2(a-2)]\) · Exponential \(1/\lambda^2\) · Poisson \(\lambda\) · Beta(\(\alpha,\beta\)) mean \(\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}\), variance \(\frac{\alpha\beta}{(\alpha+\beta)^2(\alpha+\beta+1)}\).

5 · Expectations of functions: CAVI, Monte Carlo, rejection

$$\mathbb E[(c-\mu)^2]=(c-\mathbb E\mu)^2+\operatorname{Var}\mu\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\mathbb E_{q_\mu}[(x_i-\mu)^2]=(x_i-m)^2+s^2,\quad \mathbb E_{q_\mu}[(\mu-\mu_0)^2]=(m-\mu_0)^2+s^2$$

\(q_\tau=\text{Gamma}(a,b)\) (rate): \(\mathbb E\tau=a/b\), not \(b/a\). \(\mathbb E[\log\tau]\ne\log\mathbb E\tau\). When updating \(q_\tau\), its own \(\log\tau\) and \(\tau\) terms stay as they are. VI slides 21–22, CAVI notes (43)–(45)

  • Monte Carlo: \(\frac1N\sum_jg(X_j)\approx\mathbb E\,g(X)\); with \(g=I(X\in A)\) it estimates \(P(A)\). mean(rgamma(1e5, 3, 2)) \(\approx3/2\) (R's order is shape, rate); mean(lam > 4) \(\approx P(\lambda>4)\).
  • Rejection with envelope \(kh\ge f\): \(P(\text{accept})=1/k\) (area under \(f\) over area under \(kh\)); the number of tries is Geometric with mean \(k\). Uniform envelope on \([a,b]\): \(k=(b-a)\max f\). MC slide 25
  • Log scale: compute \(\ell_k=\log\pi_k+\log f_k(x_i)\), subtract \(\max_k\ell_k\), exponentiate and normalise. Exponentiating only differences means nothing underflows.
