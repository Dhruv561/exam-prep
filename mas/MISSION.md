# Mission: Modern Applied Statistics (MAST30027)

## Why
Do well in the MAST30027 Modern Applied Statistics exam on **10 November 2026**, starting from scratch on 12 October 2026 (about 4 weeks, with DE and DS exams on 5 Nov taking priority until then).

## Success looks like
- Can do the derivations that come up every year by hand: log-likelihood → MLE → Fisher information → asymptotic distribution; exponential-family form, canonical link and variance function; EM Q-function and M-step for mixtures; Gibbs full conditionals; Metropolis–Hastings acceptance probabilities; CAVI updates
- Can read R `glm` output (binomial, Poisson, quasi-Poisson) and compute what's asked from it: predictions and Wald CIs, deviance/LRT and F tests, dispersion, AIC/log-likelihood, and say which model to prefer and why
- Can explain the consequences of modelling choices (overdispersion, link choice, priors) in 2–3 sentences

## Constraints
- About 4 weeks total, but study time is shared with DE and DS until 5 Nov, so MAS gets most of 6–10 Nov
- Course materials: `lectures/`, `annotated-slides/`, `extra-notes/`, `tutes/` (Quarto/HTML, some with solutions)
- Past exams in `exams/` (2016–2022, with some official solutions) are reserved for the user to sit timed. Lessons copy their patterns, tricks and difficulty but never their questions
- Format seen in 2022: 2 h, 5 questions, 50 marks, Casio FX-82, one double-sided handwritten A4 sheet. Lecturer (Oct 2026): all exercises, no MCQ, show calculations and explanations, a formula sheet is provided (first page still to be released)

## Out of scope
- Writing R code from scratch (the exam reads R output; it doesn't ask you to write code)
- Measure-theoretic proofs of the MLE regularity conditions
- Most Markov chain theory (lecturer: not examinable)
