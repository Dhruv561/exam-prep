\topic{PREREQUISITES}
\hd{Probability toolkit:} \bh{Total prob:} $P(A)=\sum_k P(A|B_k)P(B_k)$. \bh{Bayes:} \m{P(B_k|A)=P(A|B_k)P(B_k)/\sum_j P(A|B_j)P(B_j)}. \bh{Joint:} $p(x,z)=p(x|z)p(z)$. \bh{Marginal:} $p(x)=\int p(x,z)\,dz$ (or $\sum_z$). \bh{Conditional indep.} given $\theta$: $p(\tilde x|\theta,x)=p(\tilde x|\theta)$. \bh{Indicator:} \m{\E[I(A)]=P(A)}.
\sk\bh{Tower:} $\E X=\E[\E(X|Z)]$. \bh{Total variance:} $\Var X=\E[\Var(X|Z)]+\Var[\E(X|Z)]$. \bh{Linear:} $\Var(aX+bY)=a^2\Var X+b^2\Var Y+2ab\Cov(X,Y)$; \m{\Var(t\T\hth)=t\T Vt}; $\Var(AY)=A\Var(Y)A\T$.
\sk\bh{Square trick (CAVI):} \m{\E[(c-\mu)^2]=(c-\E\mu)^2+\Var\mu}, so $\E[\mu^2]=(\E\mu)^2+\Var\mu$.
\sk\bh{Jensen:} $f$ convex $\Rightarrow\E f(X)\ge f(\E X)$. Log is concave: $\log\E Y\ge\E\log Y$, equal iff $Y$ is constant.
\sk\bh{Invariance:} MLE of $g(\theta)$ is $g(\hth)$. \bh{Delta:} $g(\hth)\approx\Nor(g(\theta),g'(\theta)^2I(\theta)\inv)$. \bh{Taylor:} $g(Y)\approx g(\mu)+(Y-\mu)g'(\mu)$.
\sk\bh{Integrals:} $\int_0^\infty x^{a-1}e^{-bx}dx=\Gamma(a)/b^a$; $\int_0^1x^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1}dx=B(a,b)=\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)/\Gamma(a+b)$; $\Gamma(a+1)=a\Gamma(a)$; $\Gamma(n)=(n-1)!$; $\Gamma(\tfrac12)=\sqrt\pi$. \bh{Normal integral:} $\int e^{-\frac A2\mu^2+B\mu}d\mu=\sqrt{2\pi/A}\,e^{B^2/2A}$.
\sk\bh{Inverse:} $\begin{psmallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{psmallmatrix}\inv=\frac1{ad-bc}\begin{psmallmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{psmallmatrix}$. \bh{Odds:} $o=\frac p{1-p}$, $p=\frac o{1+o}$.
\sk\bh{Complete the square:} $-\frac A2\mu^2+B\mu$ is the kernel of \m{\Nor(B/A,\ 1/A)}. \warn{Course log = ln: press \textbf{ln} on the FX-82, not log.} Round final answers to 2dp. FX-82: type $(-0.5)^2$ with brackets; $\sum\log a_i$ is one \textbf{ln} of the product.

\sk\bh{Variances the sheet omits:} Exp(rate $\lambda$) $1/\lambda^2$; Gamma$(a,\text{rate }b)$ $a/b^2$; InvGamma$(a,b)$ $\frac{b^2}{(a-1)^2(a-2)}$ ($a>2$); Poisson $\lambda$.
\sk\bh{Gamma extras:} $\E[1/X]=b/(a-1)$ ($a>1$); \m{\E[\log X]=\psi(a)-\log b}, $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$. \warn{Scale form:} $x^{a-1}e^{-x/b}/(\Gamma(a)b^a)$ has mean $ab$. \m{\tau\sim\Ga(a,b)\iff1/\tau\sim\IG(a,b)}; if $\sigma^2\sim\IG(a,b)$, $\E[1/\sigma^2]=a/b$.
\sk\bh{Bivariate Normal:} \m{X_1|X_2{=}x_2\sim\Nor(\mu_1+(x_2-\mu_2)\sigma_{12}/\sigma_2^2,\ \sigma_1^2-\sigma_{12}^2/\sigma_2^2)}.
\sk\bh{Facts:} $Z^2\sim\chi^2_1$; sum of $k$ indep. $Z^2\sim\chi^2_k$ (mean $k$, var $2k$); $Z/\sqrt{U/r}\sim t_r$; $(U/m)/(V/n)\sim F_{m,n}$; $t_\nu^2\sim F_{1,\nu}$. $\sum$ Pois is Pois; $\sum_1^n$Exp$(\lambda)$ is Gamma$(n,\lambda)$.
\sk\bh{Critical values:} $z$: 1.645 (90\%), \textbf{1.96} (95\%), 2.576 (99\%). $\chi^2_{df}(.95)$: 1: \textbf{3.84}, 2: 5.99, 3: 7.81, 4: 9.49, 5: 11.07, 6: 12.59, 10: 18.31, 30: 43.77. $\chi^2_1(.99)$: 6.63.
\bx
\hd{Confidence intervals:} estimate $\pm$ critical value $\times$ SE. \bh{Say:} “if we repeated this, 95\% of such intervals would contain the true value”; \warn{not} “95\% chance $\theta$ is here” (that is a credible interval). CI excludes 0 (1 for a ratio) $\iff$ reject $H_0$ at 5\%.
\bx
\hd{Tests:} $H_0$ no effect. Reject if statistic beyond the cutoff, or $p<\alpha$. \bh{p-value:} P(this extreme or more $|\ H_0$), \warn{not} P($H_0$ true). \bh{Type I:} reject true $H_0$ ($\alpha$). \bh{Type II:} keep false $H_0$ ($\beta$). \bh{Power:} $1-\beta$. Write “reject” or “insufficient evidence”, \warn{never “accept”}. $\chi^2$, $F$ right-tailed.
\bx
\hd{Estimators:} \bh{Bias} $=\E\hth-\theta$. \bh{MSE} $=\Var\hth+$ Bias$^2$. \bh{Consistent:} $\hth\to\theta$. \bh{SE:} estimated sd of $\hth$. \bh{Precision:} 1/variance. $\bar X\sim\Nor(\mu,\sigma^2/n)$ (CLT).
\bx
\hd{Model terms:} \bh{Nested:} A is B with parameters constrained (usually to 0). \bh{Factor}, $L$ levels: $L-1$ dummies vs a reference level. `x1*x2` $=$ `x1+x2+x1:x2`. `I(x^2)`: quadratic. Coefficients mean “holding other predictors fixed”. A predictor can flip sign when correlated with others.
% ===================== LIKELIHOOD, BINOMIAL =====================
\topic{LIKELIHOOD \& BINOMIAL REGRESSION}
\hd{Likelihood:} independent $Y_i$ with densities $f_i(\cdot;\theta)$ (may depend on covariates): \m{l(\theta)=\sumi\log f_i(y_i;\theta)}. MLE $\hth$ maximises $l$.
\sk\bh{Score:} $U(\theta)=\partial l/\partial\theta$. \bh{Observed information:} \m{J(\theta)=-\partial^2l/\partial\theta\,\partial\theta\T=-H}, depends on $y$. \bh{Fisher information:} \m{I(\theta)=\E J(\theta;Y)}, no $y$. This is the whole sample ($n\times$ one-obs info if iid). Bigger information = sharper curvature = smaller variance.
\sk\bh{Asymptotics} ($n\to\infty$, $\theta^*$ true): \bh{Consistent:} $P(|\hth-\theta^*|>\epsilon)\to0$. \bh{Normal:} \m{\hth\approx\Nor(\theta^*,I(\theta^*)\inv)}. \bh{Efficient:} asymptotically unbiased with smallest variance $I(\theta^*)\inv$.
\sk\bh{Regularity:} $l$ smooth (3rd derivatives exist, continuous, bounded expectation); support of $Y$ free of $\theta$; parameter space finite-dimensional, free of $Y$; $\theta^*$ not on boundary.
\sk\kw{Q1 recipe:} (a) $l$. (b) $l'$, $l''$; \m{I=-\E[l'']}, replace $\sum x_i$ by $n\E X$ if data remain. (c) Solve $l'=0$, check $l''<0$, state $\hth\approx\Nor(\theta,1/I(\theta))$.
\sk\bh{Marks:} no data in $l''$: write “$J=I$, no expectation needed”. \bh{New parameter:} the MLE plugs in (invariance), but \warn{$I$ must be redone}: one Bin: $I(p)=\frac m{p(1-p)}$, $I(\eta)=mp(1-p)$. Course $I$ is the whole sample: never divide by $n$ again.

\bx
\hd{Wald CI for $t\T\theta$:} \m{\hth\approx\Nor(\theta^*,I(\hth)\inv)}, so \m{t\T\hth\pm z_\alpha\sqrt{t\T I(\hth)\inv t}}, $\Phi(z_\alpha)=1-\alpha/2$. Unit vector $t$: $\hth_i\pm z_\alpha\sqrt{[I(\hth)\inv]_{ii}}$. No $I$? Use $J(\hth;y)$.
\sk\hd{LR confidence region:} $\{\theta:2l(\hth)-2l(\theta)\le\chi^2_k(1-\alpha)\}$, $k=\dim\theta$. Better than the Normal approximation for small $n$. $\theta_0$ is inside iff $2[l(\hth)-l(\theta_0)]\le\chi^2_k(.95)$. The Wald box ignores correlation: a point can sit inside both intervals yet outside the region.
\sk\hd{Wald test:} $H_0:\beta_j=0$. \m{z^*=\hb_j/\mathrm{se}(\hb_j)\sim\Nor(0,1)} under $H_0$. Reject if $|z^*|>1.96$.
\sk\bh{Null not 0:} $z=(\hb-b_0)/\mathrm{se}$; R's $z$ column only tests 0.
\sk\hd{LRT:} Reduced R nested in Full F, $k$ fewer parameters. \m{LR^*=-2[\log L(\hth^R)-\log L(\hth^F)]\sim\chi^2_k} under R. If $LR^*>\chi^2_k(1-\alpha)$ reject R, pick F.
\sk\bh{Wald vs LRT:} asymptotically equivalent ($z^{*2}\approx\chi^2_1$), but the $\chi^2$ approximation to the LR is generally better, so \kw{prefer LRT}.
\bx
\hd{Binomial regression:} $Y_i\sim\mathrm{bin}(m_i,p_i)$ independent, $m_i$ known (binary: $m_i=1$), \m{g(p_i)=\eta_i=x_i\T\beta}. Linear $p=\beta_0+\beta_1t$ fails because $0\le p\le1$ is needed.
\sk\bh{Logit:} \m{\eta=\log\frac p{1-p}}, $p=\frac{e^\eta}{1+e^\eta}=\frac1{1+e^{-\eta}}$. \bh{Probit:} $\eta=\Phi\inv(p)$, $p=\Phi(\eta)$. \bh{Cloglog:} $\eta=\log(-\log(1-p))$, $p=1-e^{-e^\eta}$.
\sk\bh{Logistic curve} $\eta=\beta_0+\beta_1t$: $p=\frac12$ at $t=-\beta_0/\beta_1$ (location), slope there $\beta_1/4$ (steepness).
\dm{l(\beta)=c+\sumi\big[y_i\log\tfrac{p_i}{1-p_i}+m_i\log(1-p_i)\big]}
Logit: $l=c+\sumi[y_i\eta_i-m_i\log(1+e^{\eta_i})]$. No closed form: use `optim` or IWLS.
\sk\bh{Logit information:} $J=I=X\T WX$, $W=\mathrm{diag}(m_ip_i(1-p_i))$ (no $y_i$ left). One predictor: entries $\sum m_ip_iq_i$, $\sum m_ix_ip_iq_i$, $\sum m_ix_i^2p_iq_i$, $q_i=1-p_i$.
\sk\bh{Predict at $x_0$:} $\heta_0=x_0\T\hb$, $\hat p_0=g\inv(\heta_0)$.
\sk\kw{CI for $p$:} $p$ is not linear in $\beta$, so (1) CI for $\eta$: \m{\heta_0\pm1.96\sqrt{x_0\T Vx_0}}, $V=I(\hb)\inv$; one predictor $x_0\T Vx_0=V_{11}+2x_0V_{12}+x_0^2V_{22}$. (2) Apply $g\inv$ to both ends ($g$ monotone).
\sk\bh{Two predictors,} $x_0=(1,a,b)$: \m{x_0\T Vx_0=V_{00}+a^2V_{aa}+b^2V_{bb}+2aV_{0a}+2bV_{0b}+2abV_{ab}}. Keep every digit of `vcov`: terms nearly cancel; drop the covariances and se is far too big. Smallest near the centre of the $x$s, wide when extrapolating.
\sk\kw{Odds:} $\log o=\eta$. Change $x_j$ by $\Delta$: \m{OR=e^{\Delta\beta_j}}, CI \m{\exp\{\Delta(\hb_j\pm1.96\,\mathrm{se})\}} (swap ends if $\Delta<0$). Several $x$ change by vector $d$: $\log OR=d\T\hb$, se $=\sqrt{d\T Vd}$.
\sk\bh{OR between two non-baseline levels:} $\log OR=\hb_a-\hb_b$, \m{\mathrm{se}=\sqrt{V_{aa}+V_{bb}-2V_{ab}}}.
\sk\bh{Classify:} $\hat p\ge$ cutoff; trade off false positives and negatives by cost (screening: fewer false negatives).
\sk\bh{Score (logit):} \m{\sumi(y_i-m_ip_i)x_{ij}=0}: observed minus fitted.
\sk\bh{Links:} same predictors with different links are not nested: no LRT, use AIC. They agree mid-range, differ in the tails. Cloglog leaves 0 slowly, nears 1 fast; natural if $p=1-e^{-\lambda}$ (at least one Poisson event). Probit, cloglog: $e^\beta$ is not an OR; $y$ stays in the Hessian, so $J\ne I$.
\sk\bh{Case-control:} logit slopes are still valid log OR; only the intercept shifts, by $\log(\pi_1/\pi_0)$, so no population risks. Not true for probit.
\sk\bh{Sparse data (Hauck-Donner):} se too big, $z$ too small, a real effect missed: trust $\Delta D$.
% ===================== GLM =====================
\topic{GLMs: DEVIANCE, IWLS, DIAGNOSTICS}
\hd{Deviance:} \bh{Saturated S:} one parameter per observation, $\hmu_i=y_i$. \bh{Null:} intercept only. \bh{Scaled deviance:} \m{D^A/\phi=-2\log[L(\hb_A)/L(S)]=2(l_S-l_A)}. \bh{Deviance} is $D^A=\sum d_i$. Binomial and Poisson: $\phi=1$, same thing. $D_{null}-D_{res}$ = improvement from predictors.
\sk\bh{$y=0$:} Poisson $d_i=2\hmu_i$; binomial $d_i=-2m_i\log(1-\hat p_i)$.

\par
\kw{Adequacy (goodness of fit), $\phi=1$ only:} $H_0$: fitted model adequate. If $m_ip_i,\ m_i(1-p_i)\ge5$ (binomial) or $\lambda_i\ge5$ (Poisson) then \m{D\approx\chi^2_{n-p}}, $p$ including $\beta_0$. Reject if $D>\chi^2_{n-p}(.95)$; rough check $D\approx df$. \warn{Saturated model has $n$ parameters (not fixed), so ML theory does not apply: $D$ need not be $\chi^2$.} Small $m_i$ or counts (binary $m_i=1$ especially): no adequacy test, $\Delta D$ still fine. Normal, Gamma, IG: $\phi$ estimated, cannot test adequacy. $D$ far above the cutoff: misfit is real even if a few counts are small. 0/1 data: $D$ depends only on $\hat p_i$, so says nothing about fit.
\sk\bh{Lack of fit} may be overdispersion, wrong mean or missing interaction: check residuals vs $\heta$ first.
\sk\kw{Nested:} A in B, $s$ extra parameters. \m{(D^A-D^B)/\phi=-2[l_A-l_B]}, $l_S$ cancels. $\phi=1$: $D^A-D^B\approx\chi^2_s$. $\phi$ unknown: $F$ test (see Overdispersion). \warn{Subtract residual deviances, not null deviances.} $D_{null}-D_{res}$ on $p-1$ df tests all predictors at once. \bh{Numeric score vs factor} ($L$ levels), or a group-level covariate vs a group factor: nested, $s=L-2$.
\sk\kw{AIC (non-nested too):} \m{AIC=2p-2\hat l}, smaller is better, same data only, not a test. B has $s$ more parameters: \m{AIC_B-AIC_A=2s-(D_A-D_B)/\phi} (exact for $\phi=1$). An extra parameter pays only if it cuts $D$ by more than 2.
\sk\kw{From R output:} \m{p=n-}residual df; $n=$ null df $+1$. $\phi=1$: \m{\hat l=p-AIC/2}, \m{l_S=\hat l+D/2} (same $l_S$ for every model on those data). Gamma, Normal: R's AIC counts $\phi$, so $\hat l=(p+1)-AIC/2$. Check: two models on the same data give the same $l_S$. Continuous data: $\hat l>0$, AIC $<0$ are fine.
\bx
\hd{Exponential family:} \m{f(y;\theta,\phi)=\exp\big[\frac{y\theta-b(\theta)}{a(\phi)}+c(y,\phi)\big]}. $\theta$ canonical (location), $\phi$ dispersion (scale). Form not unique. Convention $a(\phi)=\phi$.
\sk\m{\E Y=b'(\theta)}, \m{\Var Y=b''(\theta)a(\phi)=v(\mu)a(\phi)}. \bh{Variance function} \m{v(\mu)=b''((b')\inv(\mu))}: how spread changes with mean. \bh{Canonical link} \m{g=(b')\inv}, $g(\mu)=\theta$. Canonical: $g'(\mu)=1/v(\mu)$.
\sk\bh{Proof:} $\int f\,dy=1$. Differentiate in $\theta$: $\E[(Y-b')/a]=0$, so $\E Y=b'$. Again: $\E[(Y-b')^2/a^2]-b''/a=0$, so $\Var Y=b''a$.
\sk\kw{Recipe:} write $\log f$; find $y\times$(something) $=\theta/a$; the term with no $y$ gives $-b/a$; leftovers $c$. Write $b$ in $\theta$ only. \warn{Check $b'(\theta)=\mu$.} State all of $\theta,\phi,a,b,c$ and write “support free of $\theta$”. \warn{Not exp family} if the support depends on $\theta$, or the parameter multiplies $y^2$, $\log y$ or $e^y$ rather than $y$.
\li\bh{Normal:} $\theta=\mu$, $\phi=\sigma^2$, $b=\theta^2/2$, $c=-\frac12(\frac{y^2}\phi+\log2\pi\phi)$; $g=\mu$, $v=1$.
\li\bh{Poisson:} $\theta=\log\lambda$, $\phi=1$, $b=e^\theta$, $c=-\log y!$; $g=\log\mu$, $v=\mu$.
\li\bh{Binomial:} $\theta=\log\frac p{1-p}$, $\phi=1$, $b=m\log(1+e^\theta)$, $c=\log\binom my$; $g=\log\frac\mu{m-\mu}$, $v=\mu(1-\mu/m)$.
\li\bh{Gamma$(\nu,\lambda)$:} $\theta=-\lambda/\nu=-1/\mu$, $\phi=1/\nu$, $b=-\log(-\theta)$, $c=(\frac1\phi-1)\log y-\frac{\log\phi}\phi-\log\Gamma(\frac1\phi)$; $g=-1/\mu$ (R: $1/\mu$), $v=\mu^2$, $\Var Y=\phi\mu^2$.
\li\bh{Exponential$(\lambda)$:} $\theta=-\lambda$, $\phi=1$, $b=-\log(-\theta)$, $c=0$; $g=-1/\mu$, $v=\mu^2$. $\mu=-1/\eta$ needs $\eta<0$, so canonical link can fail: use log. $\phi$ known: $\chi^2$ tests.
\li\bh{Inverse Gaussian:} $\theta=-\frac1{2\mu^2}$, $\phi=1/\lambda$, $b=-\sqrt{-2\theta}$, $c=-\frac12[\log(2\pi\phi y^3)+\frac1{\phi y}]$; $g=-\frac1{2\mu^2}$ (R: $1/\mu^2$), $v=\mu^3$.
\li\bh{Weibull,} $k$ known: $Y=X^k\sim$ Exp(rate $\lambda^k$).
\li\bh{Geometric,} $y\ge0$: $\theta=\log(1-p)$, $\phi=1$, $b=-\log(1-e^\theta)$, $c=0$; $v=\mu+\mu^2$.
\li\bh{NegBin,} $r$ known: $\theta=\log\frac\mu{\mu+r}$, $\phi=1$, $b=-r\log(1-e^\theta)$, $c=\log\frac{\Gamma(y+r)}{\Gamma(r)y!}$; $v=\mu+\mu^2/r$. $r$ unknown: not exp family.
\li\bh{Proportion} $Y=X/m$: $\theta$ logit, $b=\log(1+e^\theta)$, $a(\phi)=1/m$, $v=\mu(1-\mu)$ (R's scale). \bh{Mean of $k$ Poissons:} $b=e^\theta$, $a(\phi)=1/k$, $\Var Y=\mu/k$; $\phi$ known from the design.
\bx
\hd{GLM:} $Y$ from exponential family and \m{\mu=\E Y=g\inv(x\T\beta)}; $g$ monotone differentiable link. We model location $\eta=x\T\beta$; scale sorts itself out. Canonical link: $\theta_i=x_i\T\beta$.
\sk\bh{Fitted mean:} log link $e^\eta$; logit $me^\eta/(1+e^\eta)$; identity $\eta$; inverse $1/\eta$.
\sk\bh{Newton-Raphson:} $x_{n+1}=x_n-f'(x_n)/f''(x_n)$; \m{\theta^{n+1}=\theta^n-H(\theta^n)\inv U(\theta^n)}. \bh{Fisher scoring:} replace $J$ by $I$ (positive definite): \m{\theta^{n+1}=\theta^n+I(\theta^n)\inv U(\theta^n)}. Equal under a canonical link.
\sk\bh{Score:} $U_j=\sumi\frac{(y_i-\mu_i)x_{ij}}{a(\phi)v(\mu_i)g'(\mu_i)}$. Canonical link: $X\T(y-\hmu)=0$, so $\sum\hmu_i=\sum y_i$ with an intercept.
\sk\bh{Canonical link:} $W=v(\mu)$, $J=I$, NR = Fisher scoring = IWLS, $X\T y$ sufficient, and fitted totals match the data: group totals, $\sum x_{ij}\hmu_i=\sum x_{ij}y_i$. Non-canonical: $J$ contains $y$; same $\hb$.
\sk\bh{WLS:} $Y=X\beta+\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\sim\Nor(0,\Sigma)$: $\hb=(X\T\Sigma\inv X)\inv X\T\Sigma\inv y$. \bh{Idea:} $g(Y_i)\approx Z_i=g(\mu_i)+(Y_i-\mu_i)g'(\mu_i)$ with $\Var=g'(\mu_i)^2v(\mu_i)a(\phi)$; depends on $\beta$, so iterate.
\sk\kw{IWLS:} (1) $\hmu^0=y$. (2) \m{z_i=g(\hmu_i)+(y_i-\hmu_i)g'(\hmu_i)}, \m{W_{ii}=1/[g'(\hmu_i)^2v(\hmu_i)]}. (3) \m{\hb=(X\T WX)\inv X\T Wz}, $\hmu_i=g\inv(x_i\T\hb)$. (4) Repeat until $\hb$ stable. $a(\phi)$ cancels. Same as Fisher scoring. Needs only $g$, $v$.
\sk\bh{One step by hand:} $\hmu^0=y$, so $z_i=g(y_i)$, $W_{ii}=1/[g'(y_i)^2v(y_i)]$ (Poisson log: $\log y$, $y$). \m{X\T WX=\begin{psmallmatrix}\sum W&\sum Wx\\\sum Wx&\sum Wx^2\end{psmallmatrix}}, \m{X\T Wz=\begin{psmallmatrix}\sum Wz\\\sum Wxz\end{psmallmatrix}}. $y=0$ breaks the start: R uses $y+.1$ (Poisson), $(y+.5)/(m+1)$ (binomial).

\end{tabular}}
\sk\bh{Other $W$:} Poisson $\sqrt\mu$: 4. Gamma $1/\mu$: $\mu^2$. Gamma identity: $1/\mu^2$ ($z=y$). IG $1/\mu$: $\mu$. Cloglog: $m(1-p)L^2/p$, $L=e^\eta$.
\sk\bh{Variance:} \m{\Var\hb=(X\T\hat\Sigma\inv X)\inv}, $\hat\Sigma_{ii}=g'(\hmu_i)^2v(\hmu_i)a(\hphi)$. Same as $a(\hphi)(X\T\hat WX)\inv$: $a(\phi)$ does \warn{not} cancel here. Gamma log: $\hphi(X\T X)\inv$. $\phi$ known but R took 1: SE$\times\sqrt{a(\phi)}$.
\sk\bh{Estimate $\phi$:} \m{\hphi=X^2/(n-p)}, Pearson \m{X^2=\sumi(y_i-\hmu_i)^2/v(\hmu_i)}. Consistent. \warn{Not} $D/(n-p)$. If $\phi=1$ and the model is adequate, $X^2\approx\chi^2_{n-p}$.
\sk\kw{Rate model:} $Y_i\sim$ Pois$(\lambda_i)$, $\lambda_i\propto t_i$: \m{\log\lambda_i=\log t_i+x_i\T\beta}. `offset(log(t))` fixes the coefficient of $\log t$ at 1 (uses no df). $e^{\beta}$ = rate ratio; $\hat\lambda=t\,e^{x\T\hb}$. \bh{Check it:} free coefficient of $\log t$, $z=(\hb-1)/\mathrm{se}$, or $\Delta D$ on 1 df. Forget $\log t$ in $\heta$ and you get the rate, not the count.
\sk\kw{Gamma GLM:} `Gamma(link="log")` etc. $\phi$ estimated, so $t$ and $F$. $\mathrm{sd}/\mu=\sqrt\phi$. Pick link by plotting $g(y)$ vs $x$.
\sk\bh{Intercept only:} $\hmu=\bar y$, $\hb_0=g(\bar y)$; Poisson log se $=1/\sqrt{\sum y_i}$. \bh{Quadratic:} turning point $x=-\hb_1/(2\hb_2)$. \bh{Log link with $\log x$:} $\mu=e^{\beta_0}x_1^{\beta_1}x_2^{\beta_2}$; test a theory value by $(\hb-b_0)/\mathrm{se}$. \bh{Identity link:} CI directly on the response scale. \bh{Inverse link:} larger $\eta$ means smaller $\mu$.
\bx
\hd{Residuals:} \bh{Response:} $y_i-\hmu_i$; not homoskedastic unless $v$ constant. \bh{Pearson:} \m{r_P(i)=(y_i-\hmu_i)/\sqrt{v(\hmu_i)}}, $\sum r_P^2=X^2$. \bh{Deviance:} \m{r_D(i)=\mathrm{sign}(y_i-\hmu_i)\sqrt{d_i}}, $\sum r_D^2=D$ (R default). Binomial $v=m\hat p\hat q$. $y=0$: $r_D=-\sqrt{2\hmu}$, a smooth curve on the plot. `type="response"` on `cbind` gives $y/m-\hat p$.
\sk\bh{Leverage:} \m{H=X(X\T\hat\Sigma\inv X)\inv X\T\hat\Sigma\inv}, $H_{ii}$ measures potential influence (unusual $x$; also depends on IWLS weight), no $\phi$. $\sum H_{ii}=p$: rule of thumb, flag $>2p/n$. Large leverage need not mean actual influence. By hand: \m{H_{ii}=w_ix_i\T\widehat{\Var}(\hb)x_i} ($\phi=1$), $w_i$ the IWLS weight: $m\hat p\hat q$ (logit), $\hmu$ (Poisson log), 1 (Gamma log: $H$ depends on $x$ only). Mean leverage $p/n$. Tiny $\hmu$: small $H$ even at extreme $x$.
\sk\bh{Studentised:} $\Var(Z-\hat Z)=(I-H)\Sigma$, so \m{r_{SP}=r_P/\sqrt{(1-H_{ii})\hphi}}, \m{r_{SD}=r_D/\sqrt{(1-H_{ii})\hphi}}. Unit variance after adjusting for leverage. $\pm$: above/below fit.
\sk\bh{Jack-knife:} change in $\hmu_i$ when $y_i$ removed (outliers): \m{\mathrm{sign}(y_i-\hmu_i)\sqrt{(1-H_{ii})r_{SD}^2+H_{ii}r_{SP}^2}}. $\phi=1$: $\mathrm{sign}\cdot\sqrt{r_D^2+H_{ii}r_P^2/(1-H_{ii})}$.
\sk\bh{Cook:} change in the whole fit when $i$ removed: \m{(\hb_{(i)}-\hb)\T X\T\hat\Sigma\inv X(\hb_{(i)}-\hb)/p\approx r_{SP}^2H_{ii}/[p(1-H_{ii})]}. Same as $r_P^2H_{ii}/[p\hphi(1-H_{ii})^2]$. Flag near 1 or a clear jump. Gamma: keep $\hphi$.
\sk\bh{Half-normal plot:} ordered $|$values$|$ vs $\Phi\inv((n+i)/(2n+1))$. Smooth = fine; \warn{jump or kink in tail = problem}. Need not be straight.
\sk\bh{$r_D$ vs $\heta$:} no trend, even spread is good. Trend or curve: wrong mean. Fan: wrong variance. \bh{Banding:} discrete counts, not a problem; use a smoother. 0/1 data: two curves, nothing to read.
\sk\bh{Linearity:} plot $g(y)$ or $z$ vs each $x_j$: want straight. Fix by transforming $x$, \warn{not $y$} (breaks distribution of $Y$).
\sk\bh{Influential point:} refit without it, compare $\hb$, $D$, $\hphi$. Typically keep unless a recording error.
\sk\kw{Classify:} \bh{outlier:} big jack-knife, ordinary $H$, coefficients barely move. \bh{High leverage only:} big $H$, tiny residual, small Cook. \bh{Influential:} big Cook, coefficients move when dropped (needs both leverage and residual).
% ===================== OVERDISPERSION =====================
\topic{OVERDISPERSION \& QUASI-LIKELIHOOD}
\hd{Overdispersion:} actual $\Var Y_i$ larger than the model says. \bh{Causes:} binomial: dependent trials or $p$ changing trial to trial. Poisson: dependent events or non-constant rate. Random rate: $\Var Y=\E\lambda+\Var\lambda>\E\lambda$. Clusters of size $k$: $\Var Y=(1+(k-1)\tau^2)mp(1-p)$.
\sk\bh{Detect:} $D\gg df$, GOF $p$ tiny, $\hphi\gg1$ (no universal cutoff). Rule out wrong mean and outliers first. $D/df$ is close to $\hphi$, but quote Pearson. A free mean for every group and $D$ still big: must be overdispersion. \warn{0/1 data: cannot be detected.}
\sk\kw{If ignored:} $\hb$ unaffected, but SEs too small, $z$ too big, $p$-values too small, CIs too narrow: spurious significant predictors, too big a model. True se $\approx\sqrt{\hphi}\,$se, $z\to z/\sqrt{\hphi}$, $\Delta D\to\Delta D/\hphi$. $\hphi>1$ only ever makes terms less significant; $\hphi<1$: SEs too large.
\sk\bh{Fixes:} quasi-likelihood; different model (Poisson with Gamma rate gives NegBin, $\Var=\mu+\mu^2/\nu$); Bayes: random effect in $\eta$.
\sk\hd{Quasi-likelihood:} specify only $g(\mu_i)=x_i\T\beta$, $\E Y_i=\mu_i$, $\Var Y_i=\phi v(\mu_i)$; no distribution. Score \m{u_i=\frac{Y_i-\mu_i}{\phi v(\mu_i)}}: $\E u=0$, $\Var u=-\E\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mu}=\frac1{\phi v}$, as for a real score.
\dm{Q_i=\int_{y_i}^{\mu_i}\frac{y_i-t}{\phi v(t)}dt,\quad Q=\sumi Q_i}
$\partial Q_i/\partial\mu_i=u_i$. $Q$ need not be a real log-density. $\hb$ is consistent and asymptotically Normal as for MLE, but less efficient: use the distribution if known.
\sk\bh{Quasi-deviance:} saturated $Q=0$, so scaled $D_Q/\phi=-2\sum Q_i$, \m{D_Q=-2\sumi\int_{y_i}^{\mu_i}\frac{y_i-t}{v(t)}dt}.
\sk\bh{Quasi-Poisson:} $v=\mu$, $Q_i=\frac1\phi[y_i\log\mu_i-\mu_i]$ + const. \bh{Quasi-binomial:} $v=\mu(m-\mu)/m$, $Q_i=\frac1\phi[y_i\log\frac{\mu_i}{m_i-\mu_i}+m_i\log(m_i-\mu_i)]$ + const. $\phi$ cancels in IWLS, so \kw{same $\hb$, $\hmu$, $D$} as Poisson/binomial.
\sk\bh{Numeric $Q_i$ (definite integral, $\le0$):} $v=\mu$: $\phi Q_i=y\log\frac\mu y-(\mu-y)$. $v=\mu(m-\mu)/m$: $y\log\frac\mu y+(m-y)\log\frac{m-\mu}{m-y}$. $v=\mu^2$: $1-\frac y\mu+\log\frac y\mu$. $v=\mu^3$: $-\frac{(y-\mu)^2}{2y\mu^2}$.
\sk\bh{Other $v$:} $v=1$: $\phi Q_i=-\frac12(y-\mu)^2$, $D_Q$ = RSS, $\hphi=\hat\sigma^2$. $D_Q$ for $v=\mu$, $\mu(m-\mu)/m$, $\mu^2$, $\mu^3$ is the Poisson, binomial, Gamma, IG deviance. $Q_i\le0$: integrand opposes the direction of integration. \bh{Why Pearson, not max $Q$:} $Q=\frac1\phi\times(\le0)$ rises to 0 as $\phi\to\infty$. \bh{Scaled:} $D_Q/\hphi=-2\sum Q_i\approx n-p$ by construction, so no fit test.
\sk\kw{SE:} \m{\mathrm{se}_Q=\sqrt{\hphi}\ \mathrm{se}_P}, $\hphi$ of \warn{that same model}; reverse: $\mathrm{se}_P=\mathrm{se}_Q/\sqrt{\hphi}$. Estimate never changes. $t=z/\sqrt{\hphi}$, $z=t\sqrt{\hphi}$. One point: $\Var Y_i=\hphi m\hat p\hat q$.
\sk\bh{Coefficient:} \m{t^*=\hb/\mathrm{se}_Q\approx t_{n-p}}; CI $\hb\pm t_{n-p}(.975)\mathrm{se}_Q$ (1.96 if no $t$ given), exp for ratio. Prediction is unchanged, only its CI widens: `predict` on the quasi fit includes $\hphi$ (`residual.scale` $=\sqrt{\hphi}$); on the Poisson fit multiply se.fit by $\sqrt{\hphi}$.
\sk\kw{Nested $F$:} \m{F=\frac{(D_A-D_B)/s}{\hphi}\approx F_{s,\,n-p_B}}, \warn{$\hphi$ from the BIG model B}. No adequacy test, no $\chi^2$, AIC is NA (no likelihood), `step`/`drop1` do not work. Exact for Normal (LM $F$ test), approximate otherwise. Big model's $\hphi$ not printed: use $D_B/(n-p_B)$. $X^2$ can fall while $\hphi$ rises: df fell, the extra terms explain nothing.
\sk\bh{New data type:} choose link and $v$ from mean-variance (e.g. proportions: logit, $\mu(1-\mu)$), fit IWLS, $\hphi$ from Pearson. $\hphi<1$ (underdispersion) is allowed. iid mean $\lambda$, var $\phi\lambda$: $\hat\lambda=\bar y$, $\hphi=\frac{\sum(y_i-\bar y)^2}{\bar y(n-1)}$. Non-integer fractions work in `quasibinomial`. Lognormal: $v=\mu^2$, $\phi=e^{\sigma^2}-1$.

\topic{READING R OUTPUT}
\hd{\rr{summary(glm)} line by line:}
\li\bh{Deviance Residuals} (old R): 5-number summary of $r_D$.
\li\bh{Estimate} $\hb_j$: change in $\eta$ per unit of $x_j$, others fixed. Row `schoolB` = level B vs baseline; `a:b` = interaction; `(Intercept)` = $\eta$ at $x=0$, baseline levels. \bh{Std. Error:} $\sqrt{V_{jj}}$.
\li\bh{z value} (t if $\phi$ estimated) = Est/SE: tests $\beta_j=0$ \warn{with every other term kept in}. \bh{Pr(>|z|):} two-sided $p$. Stars `***` $<.001$, `**` $<.01$, `*` $<.05$, `.` $<.1$.
\li\bh{Dispersion line:} “taken to be 1” is an assumption, not an estimate. Other value: $\hphi=X^2/(n-p)$.
\li\bh{Null deviance} on $n-1$: intercept-only fit, same for every model on those data. \bh{Residual deviance} on $n-p$: your model.
\li\bh{AIC:} $2p-2\hat l$; NA for quasi. \bh{Fisher Scoring iterations:} IWLS steps. \bh{“$k$ observations deleted”:} $n$ is smaller than the data.
\bx
\hd{Recover anything hidden:} $n=$ null df $+1$. $p=n-$ res df. Non-intercept terms $=$ null df $-$ res df. SE $=$ Est$/z$; Est $=z\,$SE. $\hat l=p-AIC/2$ (Gamma $p+1$); $l_S=\hat l+D/2$; $\hat l_B=\hat l_A+\Delta D/2$. $s=$ res df$_A-$ res df$_B$. \m{\Delta D=D_A-D_B}. $AIC_B-AIC_A=2s-\Delta D$. $\hphi=X^2/(n-p)=(\mathrm{se}_Q/\mathrm{se}_P)^2=(z/t)^2$; $X^2=\hphi(n-p)$. \m{F=(\Delta D/s)/\hphi}, so $\hphi=(\Delta D/s)/F$. $z^2\approx\Delta D$ and $t^2\approx F$ on 1 df. Logit $\hat p=1/(1+e^{-\heta})$; log link $\hmu=e^{\heta}$. $|z|>1.96$: $p<.05$; $|z|>2.58$: $p<.01$.
\bx
\hd{\rr{anova(A,B,test="Chisq")}, two models:} row 1 is the smaller model A, row 2 the bigger B.
\li\bh{Resid. Df:} $n-p_A$, $n-p_B$. \bh{Resid. Dev:} $D_A$, $D_B$.
\li\bh{Df} (row 2): $s$. \bh{Deviance:} $\Delta D=D_A-D_B\ge0$.
\li\bh{Pr(>Chi):} $P(\chi^2_s>\Delta D)$. Small: reject A, keep B. Large: keep A.
\li`test="F"` adds \bh{F} $=(\Delta D/s)/\hphi_B$ and \bh{Pr(>F)} on $(s,n-p_B)$.
\sk\hd{\rr{anova(m,test="Chisq")}, one model:} “terms added sequentially (first to last)”.
\li\bh{NULL row:} Resid. Df $n-1$, Resid. Dev $D_{null}$.
\li\bh{Term rows:} that term added \warn{after those above it}. Df its df; Deviance the drop in $D$; Resid. Df, Resid. Dev running totals.
\li\warn{Order matters:} only the last row tests a term given all the others.
\li\bh{Checks:} Df column sums to $p-1$; Deviance column to $D_{null}-D_{res}$; last Resid. Dev is the summary's.
\li Read upward: interaction first. If `A:B` is significant keep `A` and `B`, whatever their rows say.
\sk\hd{\rr{drop1(m,test="Chisq")}:} `<none>` is $m$; each row drops \warn{only that term}, so order is irrelevant. Deviance = $D$ after dropping; LRT = rise in $D$; 1-df term: LRT $\approx z^2$.
\sk\hd{\rr{step(m)}:} `Start: AIC=` is the current model. Rows sorted by AIC, lowest first: `- x` drops x, `<none>` changes nothing. Top row wins; stops when `<none>` is on top. Last block's formula is the final model. Fails on quasi (AIC NA).

\hd{Degrees of freedom:} \bh{null} $n-1$. \bh{Residual, GOF $\chi^2$, Wald $t$, $\hphi$:} $n-p$, $p$ counting the intercept. \bh{All terms:} $p-1$. \bh{LRT $\chi^2$:} $s$ = difference in residual df. \bh{Quasi, Gamma $F$:} $(s,n-p_B)$. \bh{Numeric term} or `I(x^2)`: 1. \bh{Offset:} 0. \bh{Factor:} $L-1$. \bh{Two factors} `A:B`: $(L_A-1)(L_B-1)$. \bh{Factor by numeric} `A:x`: $L-1$. `A*B`: main effects plus interaction. \bh{Numeric to factor:} $L-2$. \bh{LR region:} $\dim\theta$. \bh{Saturated:} 0 residual df. \bh{Log-linear table:} cells $-$ parameters; `A+B+C` = mutual independence, `A*B` = interaction. \bh{Mixture:} $K-1$ + component parameters.

\hd{Interpretation lines:} \bh{Log link:} “Holding other predictors fixed, a 1-unit rise in $x$ multiplies the expected count by $e^{\hb}$, a $100(e^{\hb}-1)$\% change.” \bh{Logit:} same for the \warn{odds}, not the probability. \bh{Identity:} adds $\hb$. \bh{Inverse:} $1/\mu$ rises by $\hb$, so $\hb>0$ lowers $\mu$. \bh{Factor:} “relative to the reference level”. \bh{$\log x$ with log link:} doubling $x$ multiplies $\mu$ by $2^{\hb}$. \bh{Interaction:} the effect of one predictor depends on the other; a cell vs baseline is $\exp$(its main effects + interaction); add the intercept for the cell mean. \bh{Intercept:} $e^{\hb_0}$ = baseline mean, rate or odds at $x=0$. \bh{$\Delta$ units:} $e^{\Delta\hb}$, \warn{not} $\Delta e^{\hb}$. CI: exponentiate the ends, never the se. Observational: “associated with”, not “causes”.
\bx
\hd{Hard subquestions (R output):}
\li\bh{Which model, 2 quantitative reasons:} smaller AIC, and $\Delta D>\chi^2_s(.95)$ rejects the smaller one.
\li\bh{2 reasons overdispersed:} $D\gg df$ and $\hphi\gg1$.
\li\bh{Why $F$, $t$ not $\chi^2$, $z$:} $\phi$ unknown, estimated by $\hphi$.
\li\bh{Poisson vs quasi-Poisson:} Poisson fixes $\Var Y=\mu$. Quasi keeps the same mean model, sets $\Var Y=\phi\mu$ with $\phi$ estimated from Pearson and assumes no distribution. Same $\hb$; SEs $\times\sqrt{\hphi}$; tests become $t$, $F$; no AIC.
\li\bh{Linear assumption evidence:} $\log(y/t)$ vs $x$ straight; interaction plot parallel lines = no interaction.
\li\bh{Outlier:} clear point in half-normal Cook/jack-knife plot. Refit, re-check $\hphi$: overdispersion typically remains.
\li\bh{GOF valid?} Check first: `range(fitted(m))` all $\ge5$; never for 0/1.
\li\bh{$\chi^2$ says keep, $F$ says drop:} believe $F$, $\phi=1$ is false: prefer the smaller model.
\li\bh{Which $\hphi$?} SE: the model holding that coefficient. $F$: the bigger model.
\li\bh{Factor with no significant dummy:} can still matter: one LRT on $L-1$ df.
\li\bh{$z^2\ne\Delta D$, $t^2\ne F$:} curvature at $\hb$ vs the actual drop; close, not equal. Trust $\Delta D$.
