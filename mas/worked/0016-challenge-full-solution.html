Poisson mixture, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

Setup (written in the shape of a past paper; fresh numbers). Counts \(x=(0,\ 3,\ 5,\ 1)\) follow a two-component Poisson mixture: \(Z_i\sim\text{Categorical}(\pi,1-\pi)\), \(X_i\mid Z_i=1\sim\text{Poisson}(1)\), \(X_i\mid Z_i=2\sim\text{Poisson}(4)\). Only \(\pi\) is unknown. Two EM runs from different starting values (one stopped early) report \(\hat\pi=0.3\) and \(\hat\pi=0.6\). Pick the one with the higher \(\ell(\pi)\).

The component pmf values you'll need, which don't depend on \(\pi\):

Here you can't cancel \(x!\): the log-likelihood needs the actual densities, not ratios. At \(\pi=0.3\) the four terms \(\log[0.3f_1+0.7f_2]\) are \(-2.0941,\ -1.8634,\ -2.2043,\ -1.8223\), so \(\ell(0.3)=-7.98\).

Compute \(\ell(0.6)\) and pick the run

The log goes outside the inner sum:

$$\begin{aligned} \ell(\pi)&=\sum_i\log\big[\pi f_1(x_i)+(1-\pi)f_2(x_i)\big] \end{aligned}$$

\(-7.77\gt-7.98\).

Use \(\hat\pi=0.6\)

Classify \(x_2=3\)

Using the \(x=3\) line of the table:

$$\begin{aligned} P(Z_2=1\mid X_2=3)&=\frac{0.6(0.061313)}{0.6(0.061313)+0.4(0.195367)}\\ &=\frac{0.036788}{0.114935}\\ &=0.32\\ P(Z_2=2\mid X_2=3)&=1-0.32\\ &=0.68 \end{aligned}$$

\(0.68\gt0.5\): assign \(x_2=3\) to cluster 2

Why choose the larger \(\ell\)?

The MLE maximises the observed-data log-likelihood \(\ell(\theta)\). EM only guarantees \(\ell\) never decreases, so different starts can stop at different local maxima, or early. So compare runs on \(\ell\) and keep the higher: here \(\hat\pi=0.6\).
