Lead in tap water, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

A lab measures lead concentration \(Y_i\) (μg/L) in tap-water samples. Concentrations are lognormal: \(\log Y_i\sim N(m_i,\sigma^2)\) independently, where \(\sigma=0.4\) is known from the lab's repeat-measurement studies. You want to model \(\mu_i=\mathbb EY_i\) by pipe type, \(\log\mu_i=\beta_0+\beta_1x_i\) (\(x_i=1\) for old solder joints). You may use: if \(\log Y\sim N(m,\sigma^2)\) then \(\mathbb E(Y^k)=e^{km+k^2\sigma^2/2}\).

(a) Show that \(\operatorname{Var}Y_i=\phi\,v(\mu_i)\) and identify \(v\) and \(\phi\) (give \(\phi\) numerically). [2]
(b) Could you fit this regression using quasi-likelihood? If so, describe how (link, variance function, fitting, \(\phi\), testing). [2]
(c) Derive \(Q_i\) for your \(v\), and hence the quasideviance \(D_Q\). [2]
(d) Four samples: \(y=(2,\,6)\) for new pipes and \(y=(5,\,15)\) for old, with fitted means \(\hat\mu=(4,\,4,\,10,\,10)\). Compute \(\hat\phi\) (Pearson), \(D_Q\), \(Q\) and the scaled quasideviance using \(\hat\phi\). [3]
(e) Is the quasi-likelihood \(\hat\beta\) the MLE under the lognormal model? Comment on its efficiency, and on \(\hat\phi\) versus the \(\phi\) in (a). [2]

(a) · Variance function [2]

Using \(\mathbb E(Y^k)=e^{km+k^2\sigma^2/2}\):

$$\begin{aligned} \mu_i&=\mathbb EY_i=e^{m_i+\sigma^2/2}\\ \mathbb EY_i^2&=e^{2m_i+2\sigma^2}\\ \operatorname{Var}Y_i&=e^{2m_i+2\sigma^2}-e^{2m_i+\sigma^2}\\ &=e^{2m_i+\sigma^2}\big(e^{\sigma^2}-1\big)\\ &=\mu_i^2\big(e^{\sigma^2}-1\big) \end{aligned}$$

So:

$$\begin{aligned} v(\mu)&=\mu^2\\ \phi&=e^{\sigma^2}-1\\ &=e^{0.16}-1\\ &=0.17 \end{aligned}$$

\(v(\mu)=\mu^2\), \(\phi=0.17\)

(b) · Quasi-likelihood? [2]

Yes: quasi-likelihood needs only the mean and the variance as a function of the mean, and (a) gives both.

  • Link \(g(\mu)=\log\mu\), variance function \(v(\mu)=\mu^2\), fitted by IWLS.
  • \(\hat\phi=X^2/(n-p)\) with \(X^2=\sum_i(y_i-\hat\mu_i)^2/\hat\mu_i^2\); SEs from \(\hat\phi(X^TWX)^{-1}\).
  • Nested models: \(F=\dfrac{(D_Q^A-D_Q^B)/s}{\hat\phi}\sim F_{s,n-p}\).

(c) · \(Q_i\) and \(D_Q\) [2]

$$\begin{aligned} Q_i&=\int_{y_i}^{\mu_i}\frac{y_i-t}{\phi\,t^2}\,dt\\ &=\frac1\phi\int_{y_i}^{\mu_i}\Big(\frac{y_i}{t^2}-\frac1t\Big)dt\\ &=\frac1\phi\Big[-\frac{y_i}t-\log t\Big]_{y_i}^{\mu_i}\\ &=\frac1\phi\Big[-\frac{y_i}{\mu_i}-\log\mu_i+1+\log y_i\Big]\\ &=\frac1\phi\Big[1-\frac{y_i}{\mu_i}+\log\frac{y_i}{\mu_i}\Big] \end{aligned}$$

Then, with saturated \(Q=0\):

$$\begin{aligned} D_Q&=-2\sum_i\phi Q_i\\ &=2\sum_i\Big[\frac{y_i-\mu_i}{\mu_i}-\log\frac{y_i}{\mu_i}\Big] \end{aligned}$$

This is the gamma deviance: same \(v\), same answer.

(d) · Numbers [3]

Pearson:

$$\begin{aligned} X^2&=\frac{(2-4)^2+(6-4)^2}{4^2}+\frac{(5-10)^2+(15-10)^2}{10^2}\\ &=\frac{8}{16}+\frac{50}{100}\\ &=1\\ \hat\phi&=\frac{X^2}{n-p}\\ &=\frac1{4-2}\\ &=0.50 \end{aligned}$$

Each term \(\phi Q_i=1-r+\log r\) with \(r=y_i/\hat\mu_i\):

$$\begin{aligned} r=0.5:\quad&1-0.5+\log0.5=-0.1931\quad\text{(samples 1, 3)}\\ r=1.5:\quad&1-1.5+\log1.5=-0.0945\quad\text{(samples 2, 4)}\\ \sum_i\phi Q_i&=2(-0.1931)+2(-0.0945)\\ &=-0.5754 \end{aligned}$$

So:

$$\begin{aligned} D_Q&=-2(-0.5754)\\ &=1.15\\ Q&=\frac{-0.5754}{\hat\phi}\\ &=\frac{-0.5754}{0.5}\\ &=-1.15\\ \text{scaled quasideviance}&=\frac{D_Q}{\hat\phi}\\ &=\frac{1.1507}{0.5}\\ &=2.30 \end{aligned}$$

\(\hat\phi=0.50\), \(D_Q=1.15\), \(Q=-1.15\), scaled quasideviance \(2.30\)

(e) · Efficiency [2]

No. The quasi \(\hat\beta\) is the gamma MLE (same \(v\), same link). Under the lognormal model the MLE comes from least squares on \(\log y_i\), so the quasi estimator is consistent but less efficient.

\(\hat\phi=0.50\) is far from the true \(0.17\): it rests on only \(n-p=2\) df, so it is very noisy.

Lognormal MLE means here: group geometric means times \(e^{\sigma^2/2}\), i.e. 3.75 and 9.38, not 4 and 10.
