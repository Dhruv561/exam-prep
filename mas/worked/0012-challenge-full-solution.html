Warp breaks, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

(a) Find the maximised log-likelihood of modelB and of the saturated model. [3]
(b) Ignoring overdispersion, which Poisson model is better? Give two quantitative reasons. [2]
(c) Give two quantitative reasons to suspect modelB is overdispersed. [2]
(d) In two or three sentences, explain how modelD differs from modelB. [2]
(e) Give the standard error of the woolB coefficient allowing for overdispersion, and the t value R prints for it in summary(modelD). [2]
(f) Compute the F statistic hidden in the last anova. Give its df, state the formula used, and say which model you prefer allowing for overdispersion, and why. [4]
(g) Give a 95% CI for the ratio of mean breaks at high vs low tension (same wool), allowing for overdispersion. Interpret it. [2]
(h) Using modelB, predict the mean number of breaks for wool B at medium tension, with a 95% CI allowing for overdispersion. [3]

(a) · Log-likelihoods [3]

$$\begin{aligned} n&=53+1=54\\ p_B&=54-50=4\\ \hat\ell_B&=p_B-\frac{\text{AIC}}2\\ &=4-\frac{493.06}2\\ &=-242.53\\ \ell_{\text{sat}}&=\hat\ell_B+\frac{D_B}2\\ &=-242.53+\frac{210.39}2\\ &=-137.34 \end{aligned}$$

Check from modelA:

$$\begin{aligned} 3-\frac{507.09}2+\frac{226.43}2&=-137.33\quad\checkmark \end{aligned}$$

\(\hat\ell_B=-242.53\), \(\ell_{\text{sat}}=-137.34\)

(b) · Ignoring overdispersion [2]

  1. AIC: \(493.06\lt507.09\).
  2. LRT: \(226.43-210.39=16.04\) on 1 df, above \(3.84\) (p = 0.00006), so reject \(H_0:\beta_{\text{woolB}}=0\).

modelB is better

(c) · Overdispersion evidence [2]

  1. \(D=210.39\) on 50 df, far above \(\chi^2_{50}(0.95)=67.50\), and all fitted means are at least 19.44, so the \(\chi^2\) approximation is sound.
  2. \(\hat\phi=X^2/(n-p)=4.26\gg1\).

(d) · modelD vs modelB [2]

modelB assumes \(\operatorname{Var}Y_i=\mu_i\). modelD assumes only \(\mathbb EY_i=\mu_i\) and \(\operatorname{Var}Y_i=\phi\mu_i\), with \(\phi\) estimated by \(X^2/(n-p)\), so it has no likelihood and no AIC. Estimates and deviances are identical, but modelD's SEs are \(\sqrt{4.26}=2.06\) times larger and nested models are compared by F tests.

(e) · SE of woolB [2]

Use modelB's \(\hat\phi\), since modelD has modelB's formula:

$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{\hat\phi_B}&=\sqrt{4.261522}=2.0643\\ \text{SE}&=0.05157(2.0643)\\ &=0.11\\ t&=\frac{-0.20599}{0.10646}\\ &=-1.93 \end{aligned}$$

SE \(=0.11\), \(t=-1.93\)

(f) · The hidden F [4]

$$\begin{aligned} F&=\frac{(D_C-D_D)/s}{\hat\phi_B}\\ &=\frac{(226.43-210.39)/1}{4.261522}\\ &=\frac{16.039}{4.2615}\\ &=3.76 \end{aligned}$$

df:

$$\begin{aligned} s&=51-50=1\\ n-p_B&=54-4=50 \end{aligned}$$

So \(F\sim F_{1,50}\) under \(H_0\), and \(3.76\lt F_{1,50}(0.95)=4.03\): no significant wool effect.

\(F=3.76\) on \((1,50)\) df. Prefer the smaller modelC

This reverses (b): the \(\chi^2\) test assumed \(\phi=1\). \(\hat\phi\) comes from the bigger model because it is valid whether or not \(H_0\) holds. With 4.602 you'd get 3.49: wrong.

(g) · CI for high vs low tension [2]

$$\begin{aligned} \text{SE}&=0.06396(2.0643)\\ &=0.1320\\ \text{CI for }\beta_H&=-0.51849\pm1.96(0.1320)\\ &=-0.51849\pm0.2588\\ &=(-0.7773,\ -0.2597)\\ e^{-0.51849}&=0.60\\ \text{exp}&\Rightarrow(0.46,\ 0.77) \end{aligned}$$

Ratio 0.60, 95% CI \((0.46,\ 0.77)\): high tension cuts mean breaks by about 40% for the same wool, and the CI excludes 1

(h) · Prediction [3]

$$\begin{aligned} \hat\eta&=3.69196-0.20599-0.32132\\ &=3.16465\quad\checkmark\\ \hat\mu&=e^{3.164654}\\ &=23.68 \end{aligned}$$

The printed se.fit is Poisson, so scale it:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{se}&=0.05397811(2.0643)\\ &=0.1114\\ \text{CI}&=3.164654\pm1.96(0.1114)\\ &=3.164654\pm0.2184\\ &=(2.9463,\ 3.3831)\\ \text{exp}&\Rightarrow(19.03,\ 29.46) \end{aligned}$$

\(\hat\mu=23.68\) breaks, 95% CI \((19.03,\ 29.46)\)

Ignoring overdispersion gives \((21.30,\ 26.32)\): too narrow.
