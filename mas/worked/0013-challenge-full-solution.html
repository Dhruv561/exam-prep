Question 2 (4 marks). A colleague fits a Poisson regression to overdispersed counts and reports the summary as is. What misleading results can this give, and why?

Question 1

(a) · Log-likelihoods [2]

\(p=4\), and \(\phi=1\) for Poisson:

$$\begin{aligned} \hat\ell_{m2}&=4-\frac{847.66}2\\ &=-419.83\\ \ell_{\text{sat}}&=-419.83+\frac{556.39}2\\ &=-141.64 \end{aligned}$$

\(\hat\ell_{m2}=-419.83\), \(\ell_{\text{sat}}=-141.64\)

Check with m1: \(3-874.11/2+584.85/2=-141.63\) ✓.

(b) · Which Poisson model [3]

m2:

  1. LRT, \(H_0\): log(age) not needed. \(584.85-556.39=28.46\) on 1 df, p \(=9.6\times10^{-8}\), so reject \(H_0\).
  2. AIC: \(847.66\lt874.11\).

(c) · Prediction [2]

The predictors enter as logs:

$$\begin{aligned} \hat\eta&=-2.72002+1.22522\log20-0.01759+0.58778\log25\\ &=-2.72002+1.22522(2.99573)-0.01759+0.58778(3.21888)\\ &=-2.72002+3.67043-0.01759+1.89199\\ &=2.8248\\ \hat\mu&=e^{2.8248}\\ &=16.86 \end{aligned}$$

16.86 seizures

(d) · Overdispersion evidence [2]

  1. Residual deviance 556.39 on 55 df, about 10 times its df (mean of \(\chi^2_{55}\) is 55).
  2. \(\hat\phi=X^2/(n-p)=11.05\gg1\).

Fitted counts run 3.9 to 186, mostly well above 5, so the \(\chi^2\) comparison is reasonable. Wrong mean structure or outliers can also inflate \(D\).

(e) · Poisson vs quasi-Poisson [3]

Poisson assumes \(\operatorname{Var}Y_i=\mu_i\). Quasi-Poisson assumes \(\operatorname{Var}Y_i=\phi\mu_i\) with \(\phi\) estimated by \(X^2/(n-p)\), and specifies only the mean and variance, not a distribution.

The estimates \(\hat\beta\), fitted values and deviance are identical, because the quasi-score equations are the Poisson ones times \(1/\phi\).

The SEs are \(\sqrt{\hat\phi}\) times larger, nested models use F tests rather than \(\chi^2\), and there is no likelihood, so no AIC.

(f) · SE allowing for overdispersion [2]

Use phi2, the \(\hat\phi\) for m2:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{SE}&=0.04818\sqrt{11.05488}\\ &=0.04818(3.32489)\\ &=0.16\\ t&=\frac{-0.01759}{0.16019}\\ &=-0.11 \end{aligned}$$

SE \(=0.16\). \(|t|=0.11\), so no evidence of a treatment effect

(g) · The F statistic [3]

$$\begin{aligned} F&=\frac{(D_{m1}-D_{m2})/s}{\hat\phi_{m2}}\\ &=\frac{28.456/1}{11.05488}\\ &=2.57 \end{aligned}$$

\(F=2.57\) on \((1,\ 55)\) df

Using phi1 gives 2.53: lose the mark.

(h) · Which quasi model [2]

\(2.57\lt F_{1,55}(0.95)=4.02\), so don't reject \(H_0\): prefer the smaller q1, without log(age).

This differs from (b) because the \(\chi^2\) test assumed \(\phi=1\). With \(\phi\approx11\), a change in deviance of 28 is about what extra-Poisson noise alone produces.

Check: quasi \(t=5.347/3.325=1.61\), and \(1.61^2\approx2.57\).

(i) · Scaled quasideviance and \(\sum Q_i\) [2]

For quasi-Poisson, \(D_Q\) is the Poisson deviance:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{D_Q}{\hat\phi}&=\frac{556.39}{11.05488}\\ &=50.33\\ \sum_iQ_i&=-\frac12\cdot\frac{D_Q}{\hat\phi}\\ &=-\frac{50.33}2\\ &=-25.16 \end{aligned}$$

Scaled quasideviance \(50.33\), \(\sum_iQ_i=-25.16\)

50.33 is near \(n-p=55\) by construction, since \(\hat\phi\) came from the same residuals. It isn't a fit test.

Question 2

Ignoring overdispersion [4]

\(\hat\beta\) and the fitted values are still fine, because the score equations don't depend on \(\phi\).

But \(\operatorname{Var}\hat\beta=\phi(X^TWX)^{-1}\), and the Poisson fit sets \(\phi=1\), so variances are underestimated by \(\phi\) and SEs by \(\sqrt\phi\).

So CIs are too narrow, \(z\) values too large, p-values too small, and \(\chi^2\) tests on unscaled deviances too significant.

The result: predictors that don't matter look significant, and the chosen model is too complex (trt in m1: p = 0.020 as Poisson, 0.49 allowing for \(\phi\)).
