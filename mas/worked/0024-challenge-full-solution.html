Metropolis–Hastings acceptance, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

Setup (fresh model; 12 marks, about 28 minutes, FX-82, 2 d.p.).

Model: \(x_1,\dots,x_5\) are independent, \(x_i\mid\mu,\tau\sim\text{Normal}(\mu,\ 1/\tau)\), with observed values \(0.5,\ 1.0,\ 1.5,\ 2.0,\ 1.0\) (so \(\bar x=1.2\) and \(\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2=1.30\)).

Prior: \(\mu\) and \(\tau\) independent, \(\mu\sim\text{Normal}(0,\ 4)\), \(\tau\sim\text{Gamma}(3,\ 2)\) (shape, rate).

Metropolis–Hastings: For the current values \((\mu^c,\tau^c)\), propose new values \((\mu^*,\tau^*)\) as follows: \(\tau^*\sim\text{Gamma}(\text{shape}=2,\ \text{rate}=2/\tau^c)\) and, independently, \(\mu^*\sim\text{Normal}(\text{mean}=\mu^c,\ \text{variance}=0.5)\).

  1. Write down the target \(\pi(\mu,\tau)\) for the algorithm, up to a constant of proportionality. [3]
  2. Compute the acceptance probability when \((\mu^c,\tau^c)=(1,\ 1.5)\) and \((\mu^*,\tau^*)=(1.5,\ 1)\). Show your work. [6]
  3. Briefly explain why the proposal for \(\mu\) did not need to appear in your answer to (b). [1]
  4. In a long run the acceptance rate is 2%. Suggest one change to the algorithm, and say what you expect it to do to the acceptance rate and to the quality of the draws. [2]

Note: the Gamma(shape \(\nu\), rate \(\beta\)) density is \(\frac{\beta^\nu}{\Gamma(\nu)}x^{\nu-1}e^{-\beta x}\) for \(x>0\), and \(\Gamma(2)=1\).

(a) · The target [3]

\(\sum_i(x_i-\mu)^2=S+n(\bar x-\mu)^2=1.30+5(1.2-\mu)^2=:Q(\mu)\), and \(\pi\) is likelihood × priors:

$$\begin{aligned} \pi(\mu,\tau)&=p(x\mid\mu,\tau)\,p(\mu)\,p(\tau)\\ &\propto\tau^{5/2}\exp\Big\{-\frac\tau2Q(\mu)\Big\}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\mu^2}{8}\Big\}\tau^{3-1}e^{-2\tau} \end{aligned}$$

\(\pi(\mu,\tau)\propto\tau^{9/2}\exp\Big\{-\dfrac\tau2\big[1.30+5(1.2-\mu)^2\big]-\dfrac{\mu^2}8-2\tau\Big\}\)

(b) · Acceptance probability [6]

$$\begin{aligned} \alpha&=\min\Big\{1,\ \frac{\pi(\mu^*,\tau^*)}{\pi(\mu^c,\tau^c)}\cdot\frac{q(\mu^c,\tau^c\mid\mu^*,\tau^*)}{q(\mu^*,\tau^*\mid\mu^c,\tau^c)}\Big\} \end{aligned}$$

\(Q\) at both points:

$$\begin{aligned} Q(\mu^c)&=1.30+5(1.2-1)^2\\ &=1.50\\ Q(\mu^*)&=1.30+5(1.2-1.5)^2\\ &=1.75 \end{aligned}$$

Likelihood ratio, new over current:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{(i)}&=\Big(\frac{\tau^*}{\tau^c}\Big)^{5/2}\exp\Big\{-\tfrac12\big[\tau^*Q(\mu^*)-\tau^cQ(\mu^c)\big]\Big\}\\ &=\Big(\frac1{1.5}\Big)^{2.5}\exp\Big\{-\tfrac12\big[1.75-2.25\big]\Big\}\\ &=0.3629\,e^{0.25}\\ &=0.3629(1.2840)\\ &=0.4660 \end{aligned}$$

Prior ratios:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{(ii) }\mu&=\exp\Big\{-\frac{1.5^2-1^2}8\Big\}\\ &=e^{-0.15625}\\ &=0.8553\\ \text{(iii) }\tau&=\Big(\frac{\tau^*}{\tau^c}\Big)^2e^{-2(\tau^*-\tau^c)}\\ &=\Big(\frac1{1.5}\Big)^2e^{1}\\ &=0.4444(2.7183)\\ &=1.2081 \end{aligned}$$

Proposal for \(\tau\). Rates: forward \(2/\tau^c=1.3333\), reverse \(2/\tau^*=2\). The density is \(\beta^2xe^{-\beta x}\):

$$\begin{aligned} q_\tau(\tau^*\mid\tau^c)&=(1.3333)^2(1)e^{-1.3333}\\ &=1.7778(0.2636)\\ &=0.4686\\ q_\tau(\tau^c\mid\tau^*)&=2^2(1.5)e^{-3}\\ &=6(0.049787)\\ &=0.2987\\ \text{(iv) ratio}&=\frac{0.2987}{0.4686}\\ &=0.6375 \end{aligned}$$

The \(\mu\) proposal ratio is 1 (see (c)). Multiply:

$$\begin{aligned} 0.4660\times0.8553&=0.3986\\ 0.3986\times1.2081&=0.4815\\ 0.4815\times0.6375&=0.3070\\ \alpha&=\min\{1,\ 0.3070\}\\ &=0.31 \end{aligned}$$

\(\alpha=0.31\)

Common slips: no proposal ratio gives 0.48; forward over reverse gives 0.76; shape 3 instead of \(3-1\) in the prior.

(c) · Why the \(\mu\) proposal vanishes [1]

It is a Normal centred at the current value with the same variance 0.5 both ways, so \(q_\mu(\mu^*\mid\mu^c)=q_\mu(\mu^c\mid\mu^*)\): a symmetric random walk, so its ratio is 1.

(d) · 2% acceptance [2]

Steps are too large, so proposals mostly land where \(\pi\) is much smaller. Reduce the proposal spread (e.g. \(\mu\) variance from 0.5 to 0.05, and a larger Gamma shape for \(\tau\) so it concentrates near \(\tau^c\)).

That raises the acceptance rate and gives more distinct draws. Don't overdo it: very small steps are accepted nearly always but move slowly, giving high autocorrelation and a small effective sample size.
