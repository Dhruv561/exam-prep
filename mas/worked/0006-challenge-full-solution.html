Poisson with a square-root link, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

A café counts customers \(y_i\) arriving in 12 half-hour windows, two windows at each of six staffing levels \(x_i\in\{1,\dots,6\}\). (Simulated data.) Model: \(Y_i\sim\text{Poisson}(\mu_i)\) independently, \(\sqrt{\mu_i}=\beta_0+\beta_1x_i\).

(a) State the three components of this GLM. Is the link canonical? Justify.
(b) Write the log-likelihood as a function of \(\beta\) and derive the score equations.
(c) Give the IWLS working response \(z_i\) and weight \(W_{ii}\). Simplify \(W_{ii}\) as far as possible.
(d) Find the Fisher information \(\mathcal I(\beta)\). Use it, with \(\sum x_i=42\) and \(\sum x_i^2=182\), to reproduce both standard errors of fit1. Why don't you need \(\hat\beta\)?
(e) For the intercept-only model fit0, show that \(\hat\beta_0=\sqrt{\bar y}\) and \(\text{SE}(\hat\beta_0)=1/(2\sqrt n)\), and check against the printout. Starting from \(\beta_0^{0}=2\), carry out one IWLS step.
(f) Find the observed information \(J(\beta)\). Is Newton–Raphson the same algorithm as Fisher scoring here? Explain in 2–3 sentences.

(a) · The three components

  1. Random: \(Y_i\sim\text{Poisson}(\mu_i)\) independently, an exponential family with \(\theta=\log\mu\), \(b(\theta)=e^\theta\), \(a(\phi)=1\).
  2. Linear predictor: \(\eta_i=\beta_0+\beta_1x_i\).
  3. Link: \(g(\mu_i)=\sqrt{\mu_i}=\eta_i\).

The canonical link is \((b')^{-1}\):

$$\begin{aligned} b'(\theta)&=e^\theta=\mu\\ (b')^{-1}(\mu)&=\log\mu\ne\sqrt\mu \end{aligned}$$

Not canonical

(b) · Log-likelihood and score equations

\(\mu_i=\eta_i^2\), so

$$\begin{aligned} \ell(\beta)&=\sum_i\big[y_i\log\mu_i-\mu_i-\log y_i!\big]\\ &=\sum_i\big[2y_i\log\eta_i-\eta_i^2-\log y_i!\big] \end{aligned}$$

Differentiate with \(\partial\eta_i/\partial\beta_j=x_{ij}\):

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\beta_j}&=\sum_i\Big(\frac{2y_i}{\eta_i}-2\eta_i\Big)x_{ij}\\ &=\sum_i\frac{2(y_i-\eta_i^2)}{\eta_i}\,x_{ij}\\ &=\sum_i\frac{2(y_i-\mu_i)}{\sqrt{\mu_i}}\,x_{ij} \end{aligned}$$

\(\displaystyle\sum_i\frac{y_i-\hat\mu_i}{\sqrt{\hat\mu_i}}=0\) and \(\displaystyle\sum_i\frac{(y_i-\hat\mu_i)x_i}{\sqrt{\hat\mu_i}}=0\)

(c) · Working response and weight

$$\begin{aligned} g'(\mu)&=\frac1{2\sqrt\mu}\\ z_i&=\eta_i+(y_i-\mu_i)g'(\mu_i)\\ &=\eta_i+\frac{y_i-\mu_i}{2\sqrt{\mu_i}}\\ &=\eta_i+\frac{y_i-\eta_i^2}{2\eta_i}\\ &=\frac{\eta_i}2+\frac{y_i}{2\eta_i}\\[6pt] W_{ii}&=\frac1{g'(\mu_i)^2\,v(\mu_i)}\\ &=\frac1{\frac1{4\mu_i}\cdot\mu_i}\\ &=4 \end{aligned}$$

\(z_i=\dfrac{\eta_i}2+\dfrac{y_i}{2\eta_i}\), \(W_{ii}=4\) for every \(i\) and every iteration

(d) · Fisher information and SEs

$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal I(\beta)&=\frac{X^TWX}{a(\phi)}\\ &=4X^TX\\ X^TX&=\begin{pmatrix}12&42\\42&182\end{pmatrix}\\ \det(X^TX)&=12(182)-42^2\\ &=2184-1764\\ &=420\\ \mathcal I^{-1}&=\frac1{4(420)}\begin{pmatrix}182&-42\\-42&12\end{pmatrix}\\ &=\frac1{1680}\begin{pmatrix}182&-42\\-42&12\end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}$$

SEs are the square roots of the diagonal:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{SE}(\hat\beta_0)&=\sqrt{\frac{182}{1680}}\\ &=\sqrt{0.10833}\\ &=0.3291\quad\checkmark\\ \text{SE}(\hat\beta_1)&=\sqrt{\frac{12}{1680}}\\ &=\sqrt{0.007143}\\ &=0.0845\quad\checkmark \end{aligned}$$

\(\hat\beta\) isn't needed because \(W=4I\) doesn't depend on \(\mu\), so \(\mathcal I\) is the same at every \(\beta\).

(e) · Intercept-only model

\(\eta_i=\beta_0\), so \(\mu_i=\beta_0^2\) for all \(i\). The score equation:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_i\frac{2(y_i-\beta_0^2)}{\beta_0}&=0\\ \sum_iy_i&=n\beta_0^2\\ \hat\beta_0&=\sqrt{\bar y}\\ &=\sqrt{108/12}\\ &=\sqrt9\\ &=3\quad\checkmark \end{aligned}$$

With \(X=\mathbf 1\):

$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal I&=4n\\ \text{SE}(\hat\beta_0)&=\frac1{\sqrt{4n}}\\ &=\frac1{2\sqrt n}\\ &=\frac1{2\sqrt{12}}\\ &=0.1443\quad\checkmark \end{aligned}$$

IWLS with constant weights and only an intercept: the update is the mean of \(z_i\).

$$\begin{aligned} \beta_0^{1}&=\bar z\\ &=\frac{\beta_0^0}2+\frac{\bar y}{2\beta_0^0}\\ &=\frac22+\frac9{2(2)}\\ &=1+2.25\\ &=3.25 \end{aligned}$$

\(\beta_0^1=3.25\)

Next steps: 3.0096, 3.00002. It is Newton's method for \(\sqrt9\).

(f) · Observed information

From the score in (b):

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial^2\ell}{\partial\beta_j\partial\beta_k}&=\sum_i\Big(-\frac{2y_i}{\eta_i^2}-2\Big)x_{ij}x_{ik}\\ J(\beta)&=X^T\operatorname{diag}\Big(2+\frac{2y_i}{\eta_i^2}\Big)X \end{aligned}$$

Check by expectation, using \(\mathbb E[y_i]=\eta_i^2\):

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E\Big[2+\frac{2y_i}{\eta_i^2}\Big]&=2+2=4\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathcal I=4X^TX\ \checkmark \end{aligned}$$

\(J\) depends on the data through \(y_i\), so \(J\ne\mathcal I\) in general, and Newton–Raphson takes different steps from Fisher scoring. This happens because the link isn't canonical: the \(y\) term of \(\ell\) is \(2y_i\log\eta_i\), not linear in \(\beta\). Both reach the same \(\hat\beta\).
