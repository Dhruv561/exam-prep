Tilt sensors, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

Setup (fresh model, past-paper shape; 12 marks). A tilt sensor is bolted to each of \(n\) machines. If it was mounted the right way round, it reads \(+\mu\) plus noise. If it was mounted reversed, it reads \(-\mu\) plus noise. Nobody recorded which. For \(i=1,\dots,n\):

$$\begin{aligned} Z_i&\sim\text{Categorical}(\pi,1-\pi)\\ X_i\mid Z_i=1&\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\\ X_i\mid Z_i=2&\sim N(-\mu,\sigma^2) \end{aligned}$$

with \(\mu>0\) and \(\theta=(\pi,\mu,\sigma^2)\). Twist: \(\mu\) and \(\sigma^2\) are both shared by the two components, with opposite signs on \(\mu\).

  1. Derive \(Q(\theta,\theta^0)\), with the log densities expanded. [3]
  2. Show that the E-step simplifies to \(P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)=\dfrac{\pi^0}{\pi^0+(1-\pi^0)\,e^{-2\mu^0x_i/(\sigma^2)^0}}\). [2]
  3. Derive the M-step updates \(\hat\pi\), \(\hat\mu\) and \(\hat\sigma^2\). Show that \(\hat\sigma^2=\frac1n\sum_ix_i^2-\hat\mu^2\). [5]
  4. With \(x=(2.5,\ -1.0,\ 0.5,\ 3.0,\ -2.0)\) and \(\theta^0=(0.6,\ 2,\ 4)\), carry out one EM iteration. Give \(\hat\pi\), \(\hat\mu\) and \(\hat\sigma^2\) to 2 d.p. [2]

(a) · \(Q(\theta,\theta^0)\) [3]

Complete-data likelihood:

$$\begin{aligned} P(X,Z\mid\theta)&=\prod_{i=1}^n\big[\pi f(x_i;\mu,\sigma^2)\big]^{I(Z_i=1)}\big[(1-\pi)f(x_i;-\mu,\sigma^2)\big]^{I(Z_i=2)} \end{aligned}$$

Take logs:

$$\begin{aligned} \log P(X,Z\mid\theta)&=\sum_{i=1}^n\Big\{I(Z_i=1)\Big[\log\pi-\tfrac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)-\frac{(x_i-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big]\\ &\qquad+I(Z_i=2)\Big[\log(1-\pi)-\tfrac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)-\frac{(x_i+\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big]\Big\} \end{aligned}$$

Only the indicators are random, and \(E_{Z\mid X,\theta^0}[I(Z_i=k)]=P(Z_i=k\mid X_i,\theta^0)=:\gamma_{ik}\):

$$\begin{aligned} Q(\theta,\theta^0)&=\sum_{i=1}^n\Big\{\gamma_{i1}\Big[\log\pi-\tfrac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)-\frac{(x_i-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big]\\ &\qquad+\gamma_{i2}\Big[\log(1-\pi)-\tfrac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)-\frac{(x_i+\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big]\Big\} \end{aligned}$$

(b) · E-step [2]

Equal normalising constants cancel:

$$\begin{aligned} \gamma_{i1}&=\frac{\pi^0e^{-(x_i-\mu^0)^2/(2(\sigma^2)^0)}}{\pi^0e^{-(x_i-\mu^0)^2/(2(\sigma^2)^0)}+(1-\pi^0)e^{-(x_i+\mu^0)^2/(2(\sigma^2)^0)}} \end{aligned}$$

Divide top and bottom by \(e^{-(x_i-\mu^0)^2/(2(\sigma^2)^0)}\):

$$\begin{aligned} \gamma_{i1}&=\frac{\pi^0}{\pi^0+(1-\pi^0)e^{-[(x_i+\mu^0)^2-(x_i-\mu^0)^2]/(2(\sigma^2)^0)}} \end{aligned}$$

Use \((x+\mu)^2-(x-\mu)^2=4\mu x\):

$$\begin{aligned} \gamma_{i1}&=\frac{\pi^0}{\pi^0+(1-\pi^0)e^{-4\mu^0x_i/(2(\sigma^2)^0)}}\\ &=\frac{\pi^0}{\pi^0+(1-\pi^0)e^{-2\mu^0x_i/(\sigma^2)^0}} \end{aligned}$$

(c) · M-step [5]

\(\pi\). Terms in \(\pi\): \(\sum_i[\gamma_{i1}\log\pi+\gamma_{i2}\log(1-\pi)]\).

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial Q}{\partial\pi}&=\sum_i\Big[\frac{\gamma_{i1}}\pi-\frac{\gamma_{i2}}{1-\pi}\Big]=0\\ (1-\pi)\sum_i\gamma_{i1}&=\pi\sum_i\gamma_{i2}\\ \sum_i\gamma_{i1}&=\pi\sum_i(\gamma_{i1}+\gamma_{i2})=n\pi\\ \hat\pi&=\frac1n\sum_i\gamma_{i1} \end{aligned}$$

\(\mu\). Both components contribute:

$$\begin{aligned} Q_\mu&=-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\sum_i\big[\gamma_{i1}(x_i-\mu)^2+\gamma_{i2}(x_i+\mu)^2\big]\\ \frac{\partial Q}{\partial\mu}&=\frac1{\sigma^2}\sum_i\big[\gamma_{i1}(x_i-\mu)-\gamma_{i2}(x_i+\mu)\big]\\ &=\frac1{\sigma^2}\Big[\sum_i(\gamma_{i1}-\gamma_{i2})x_i-\mu\sum_i(\gamma_{i1}+\gamma_{i2})\Big]\\ &=\frac1{\sigma^2}\Big[\sum_i(\gamma_{i1}-\gamma_{i2})x_i-n\mu\Big]=0\\ \hat\mu&=\frac1n\sum_i(\gamma_{i1}-\gamma_{i2})x_i\\ &=\frac1n\sum_i(2\gamma_{i1}-1)x_i \end{aligned}$$

\(\sigma^2\). Using \(\gamma_{i1}+\gamma_{i2}=1\):

$$\begin{aligned} Q_{\sigma^2}&=-\frac12\sum_i\Big[\log\sigma^2+\frac{\gamma_{i1}(x_i-\mu)^2+\gamma_{i2}(x_i+\mu)^2}{\sigma^2}\Big]\\ \frac{\partial Q}{\partial\sigma^2}&=-\frac12\Big[\frac n{\sigma^2}-\frac{\sum_i[\gamma_{i1}(x_i-\mu)^2+\gamma_{i2}(x_i+\mu)^2]}{(\sigma^2)^2}\Big]=0\\ \hat\sigma^2&=\frac1n\sum_i\big[\gamma_{i1}(x_i-\hat\mu)^2+\gamma_{i2}(x_i+\hat\mu)^2\big] \end{aligned}$$

Simplify, expanding one summand:

$$\begin{aligned} \gamma_{i1}(x_i-\hat\mu)^2+\gamma_{i2}(x_i+\hat\mu)^2&=x_i^2-2\hat\mu(\gamma_{i1}-\gamma_{i2})x_i+\hat\mu^2\\ \sum_i[\cdots]&=\sum_ix_i^2-2\hat\mu\sum_i(\gamma_{i1}-\gamma_{i2})x_i+n\hat\mu^2\\ &=\sum_ix_i^2-2\hat\mu(n\hat\mu)+n\hat\mu^2\quad(\text{the }\hat\mu\text{ equation})\\ &=\sum_ix_i^2-n\hat\mu^2\\ \hat\sigma^2&=\frac1n\sum_ix_i^2-\hat\mu^2 \end{aligned}$$

\(2\mu^0/(\sigma^2)^0=4/4=1\), so \(\gamma_{i1}=\dfrac{0.6}{0.6+0.4e^{-x_i}}\).
