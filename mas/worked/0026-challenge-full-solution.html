Setup (8 marks, about 18 minutes, FX-82, 2 d.p.).

Model: a random-walk Metropolis sampler targets the posterior of a Poisson rate \(\lambda>0\), known up to a constant: \(p(\lambda\mid y)\propto\lambda^{19}e^{-4\lambda}\). Proposal: \(\lambda'\mid\lambda\sim N(\lambda,\,0.5^2)\), with negative proposals rejected. Two chains, c1 and c2, of 600 iterations, started at \(\lambda^{(0)}=0.5\) and \(12\). The trace plots and the ACF of chain 1 over iterations 201–600 are below, with R output.

  1. From the trace plot, how many initial iterations would you discard as burn-in? Give the evidence. [2]
  2. The BGR diagnostic is \(\hat R=B/W\), with \(B\) the sd of the pooled sample and \(W\) the average within-chain sd. Compute \(\hat R\) for iterations 1–100 and for iterations 201–600. Using the threshold 1.05, what do you conclude? [2]
  3. The analyst uses the mean of chain 1 over iterations 201–600 as the point estimate of the posterior mean. Is this reasonable? Give its standard error allowing for autocorrelation, and say how it compares with the naive \(s/\sqrt n\). [2]
  4. (i) The acceptance rates are 0.89 and 0.84. Is the step size 0.5 too small, about right or too large, and what change would you make? (ii) Name one informal and one formal check of convergence. [2]

(a) · Burn-in [2]

Chain 1 (start 0.5) reaches the band around 5 by about iteration 30, and chain 2 (start 12) by about iteration 60–70. After about iteration 100 the chains overlap with no trend.

Discard about the first 100 iterations of each chain

Any value in 70–150 with this reasoning earns the marks.

(b) · BGR [2]

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Iterations 1–100:}\quad\hat R&=\frac BW\\ &=\frac{2.20}{1.82}\\ &=1.21\\ \text{Iterations 201–600:}\quad\hat R&=\frac{1.38}{1.34}\\ &=1.03 \end{aligned}$$

\(1.21\gt1.05\): the first 100 iterations aren't stationary. \(1.03\lt1.05\): iterations 201–600 look stationary and can be pooled.

\(\hat R=1.21\) then \(1.03\)

(c) · Point estimate and SE [2]

Yes: after burn-in the chain is approximately stationary, so the mean \(\bar\lambda=5.62\) is a reasonable estimate. But the draws are correlated (\(\hat\rho_1=0.91\)):

$$\begin{aligned} \text{ESS}&=n\,\frac{1-\hat\rho_1}{1+\hat\rho_1}\\ &=400\times\frac{0.09}{1.91}\\ &=18.85\\ \text{SE}&=\frac s{\sqrt{\text{ESS}}}\\ &=\frac{1.02}{\sqrt{18.85}}\\ &=\frac{1.02}{4.34}\\ &=0.23\\ \text{SE}_{\text{naive}}&=\frac{1.02}{\sqrt{400}}\\ &=0.05 \end{aligned}$$

SE \(=0.23\), about 4.6 times the naive \(0.05\). 400 draws are worth about 19 independent ones

(d) · Step size and checks [2]

(i) Acceptance 0.89 and 0.84 are very high and the ACF falls slowly (\(\hat\rho_1=0.91\)): steps are too small, so increase the proposal sd.

(ii) Informal: trace plots of several chains from overdispersed starts, which should overlap and look stationary. Formal: BGR \(\hat R\lt1.05\) on the post-burn-in draws.
