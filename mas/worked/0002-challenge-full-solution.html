Hospital readmissions, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

A hospital records 30-day readmissions. Patients are grouped by age (years) and whether they got a follow-up phone call after discharge (call = 1) or not (0). In each group \(m_i = 60\) and \(Y_i\) is the number readmitted.

> model <- glm(cbind(readmit, 60 - readmit) ~ age + call, family = binomial, data = rd)
Coefficients:

age          0.04422    0.01012   4.367 1.26e-05 ***
call        -0.92544    0.22463  -4.120 3.79e-05 ***

            (Intercept)           age          call

age         -0.0068487  0.00010251  -0.00017631
call        -0.0079756 -0.00017631   0.050459

(a) Write down the model and its log-likelihood \(\ell(\beta)\).
(b) Estimate the readmission probability for a 70-year-old who got a call.
(c) Give a 95% CI for that probability.
(d) By what percentage do the odds of readmission change for a patient 15 years older, all else equal? Give a 95% CI.
(e) Interpret the call coefficient as an odds ratio with a 95% CI, in one sentence.

(a) · Model and log-likelihood

\(Y_i\sim\text{Bin}(60,p_i)\) independently, with logit link:

$$\begin{aligned} \log\frac{p_i}{1-p_i}&=\eta_i\\ &=\beta_0+\beta_1\text{age}_i+\beta_2\text{call}_i \end{aligned}$$

Log-likelihood, using \(\log\frac{p_i}{1-p_i}=\eta_i\) and \(\log(1-p_i)=-\log(1+e^{\eta_i})\):

$$\begin{aligned} \ell(\beta)&=c+\sum_i\big[y_i\log p_i+(60-y_i)\log(1-p_i)\big]\\ &=c+\sum_i\big[y_i\eta_i+60\log(1-p_i)\big]\\ &=c+\sum_i\big[y_i\eta_i-60\log(1+e^{\eta_i})\big] \end{aligned}$$

Distribution, independence, link and linear predictor: each is usually a mark.

(b) · Point estimate

With \(x_0=(1,\ 70,\ 1)\):

$$\begin{aligned} \hat\eta_0&=-3.60777+70(0.04422)-0.92544\\ &=-3.60777+3.09540-0.92544\\ &=-1.4378\\ \hat p_0&=\frac{e^{-1.4378}}{1+e^{-1.4378}}\\ &=0.19 \end{aligned}$$

\(\hat p_0=0.19\)

(c) · CI for that probability

\(x_0^TVx_0\) is the three variances plus twice each covariance:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}\hat\eta_0&=V_{00}+70^2V_{11}+V_{22}+2(70)V_{01}+2V_{02}+2(70)V_{12}\\ &=0.47733+4900(0.00010251)+0.050459\\ &\quad+140(-0.0068487)+2(-0.0079756)+140(-0.00017631)\\ &=0.47733+0.50230+0.05046-0.95882-0.01595-0.02468\\ &=0.03064\\ \text{se}(\hat\eta_0)&=\sqrt{0.03064}=0.1750 \end{aligned}$$

CI on the \(\eta\) scale:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{CI}&=-1.4378\pm1.96(0.1750)\\ &=-1.4378\pm0.3431\\ &=(-1.7809,\ -1.0948) \end{aligned}$$

Transform each end:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{e^{-1.7809}}{1+e^{-1.7809}}&=0.14\\ \frac{e^{-1.0948}}{1+e^{-1.0948}}&=0.25 \end{aligned}$$

95% CI for \(p_0\): \((0.14,\ 0.25)\)

Don't drop the covariances: without them se = 1.02 and the CI is about (0.03, 0.63).

(d) · 15 years older

The log-odds change by \(15\beta_1\):

$$\begin{aligned} 15\hat\beta_1&=15(0.04422)=0.6633\\ e^{0.6633}&=1.94\\ \text{se}(15\hat\beta_1)&=15(0.01012)=0.1518\\ \text{CI}&=0.6633\pm1.96(0.1518)\\ &=(0.3658,\ 0.9608)\\ \text{exp}&\Rightarrow(1.44,\ 2.61) \end{aligned}$$

Odds rise by 94% (95% CI: 44% to 161%)

(e) · The call coefficient

$$\begin{aligned} e^{-0.92544}&=0.40\\ \text{CI}&=-0.92544\pm1.96(0.22463)\\ &=(-1.3657,\ -0.4852)\\ \text{exp}&\Rightarrow(0.26,\ 0.62) \end{aligned}$$

Holding age fixed, a follow-up call multiplies the odds of readmission by an estimated 0.40 (95% CI 0.26 to 0.62), i.e. about 60% lower odds.

The CI excludes 1, consistent with \(|z|=4.12\gt1.96\).
