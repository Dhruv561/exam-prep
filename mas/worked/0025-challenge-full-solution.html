Gibbs with two data sources, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

Setup (fresh model; 11 marks, about 25 minutes, FX-82).

Model: given \((\mu,\tau)\), the observations \(x_1,\dots,x_n\) and \(y_1,\dots,y_m\) are all independent with

$$x_i\mid\mu,\tau\sim\text{Normal}\Big(\mu,\ \frac1\tau\Big),\qquad y_j\mid\mu,\tau\sim\text{Normal}\Big(2\mu,\ \frac1\tau\Big).$$

Prior: \(\mu\) and \(\tau\) are independent, with \(\mu\sim\text{Normal}(1,\ 2)\) and \(\tau\sim\text{Gamma}(3,\ 2)\).

  1. Derive the conditional distribution \(p(\mu\mid\tau,x,y)\). If it is a known distribution, identify the distribution name and its parameters. [4]
  2. Derive the conditional distribution \(p(\tau\mid\mu,x,y)\) and identify it. [3]
  3. Briefly describe a Gibbs sampling scheme for \((\mu,\tau)\), and explain how you would use its output to estimate \(P(\mu>1\mid x,y)\). [3]
  4. Briefly explain why this sampler needs no proposal distribution and no accept/reject step. [1]

(a) · \(p(\mu\mid\tau,x,y)\) [4]

Prior variance 2 gives \(e^{-(\mu-1)^2/4}\). The \(y\) mean is \(2\mu\):

$$\begin{aligned} p(\mu\mid\tau,x,y)&\propto\exp\Big\{-\frac\tau2\sum_i(x_i-\mu)^2\Big\}\exp\Big\{-\frac\tau2\sum_j(y_j-2\mu)^2\Big\}\\ &\qquad\times\exp\Big\{-\frac14(\mu-1)^2\Big\} \end{aligned}$$

Expand. \((y_j-2\mu)^2=y_j^2-4\mu y_j+4\mu^2\):

$$\begin{aligned} \text{exponent}&=-\frac\tau2\Big[n\mu^2-2\mu\sum_ix_i\Big]-\frac\tau2\Big[4m\mu^2-4\mu\sum_jy_j\Big]\\ &\qquad-\frac14\big[\mu^2-2\mu\big]+\text{const}\\ &=-\frac12\Big[(n+4m)\tau+\tfrac12\Big]\mu^2\\ &\qquad+\Big[\tau\sum_ix_i+2\tau\sum_jy_j+\tfrac12\Big]\mu+\text{const}\\ &=-\frac A2\mu^2+B\mu+\text{const} \end{aligned}$$

where

$$\begin{aligned} A&=\tfrac12+(n+4m)\tau\\ B&=\tfrac12+\tau\Big(\sum_ix_i+2\sum_jy_j\Big) \end{aligned}$$

Complete the square:

$$\begin{aligned} p(\mu\mid\tau,x,y)&\propto\exp\Big\{-\frac A2\Big(\mu-\frac BA\Big)^2\Big\} \end{aligned}$$

\(\mu\mid\tau,x,y\sim\text{Normal}\Big(\dfrac{\frac12+\tau(\sum_ix_i+2\sum_jy_j)}{\frac12+(n+4m)\tau},\ \dfrac1{\frac12+(n+4m)\tau}\Big)\)

(b) · \(p(\tau\mid\mu,x,y)\) [3]

Keep the \(\tau\) powers from both likelihoods and the Gamma prior. The Normal prior of \(\mu\) has no \(\tau\):

$$\begin{aligned} p(\tau\mid\mu,x,y)&\propto\tau^{n/2}e^{-\frac\tau2\sum_i(x_i-\mu)^2}\cdot\tau^{m/2}e^{-\frac\tau2\sum_j(y_j-2\mu)^2}\\ &\qquad\times\tau^{3-1}e^{-2\tau}\\ &=\tau^{3+\frac{n+m}2-1}\\ &\qquad\times\exp\Big\{-\tau\Big(2+\frac12\Big[\sum_i(x_i-\mu)^2+\sum_j(y_j-2\mu)^2\Big]\Big)\Big\} \end{aligned}$$

\(\tau\mid\mu,x,y\sim\text{Gamma}\Big(3+\dfrac{n+m}2,\ 2+\dfrac12\Big[\sum_i(x_i-\mu)^2+\sum_j(y_j-2\mu)^2\Big]\Big)\)

(c) · Gibbs scheme [3]

  1. Choose \(\mu^{(0)}\) and \(\tau^{(0)}\gt0\).
  2. For \(t=1,\dots,T\): draw \(\mu^{(t)}\) from (a) with \(\tau=\tau^{(t-1)}\), then draw \(\tau^{(t)}\) from (b) with \(\mu=\mu^{(t)}\).
  3. Discard the first \(T_0\) draws as burn-in.

With \(N=T-T_0\) kept draws:

$$\begin{aligned} P(\mu\gt1\mid x,y)&\approx\frac1N\sum_{t=T_0+1}^T\mathbf 1\{\mu^{(t)}\gt1\} \end{aligned}$$

(d) · No proposal needed [1]

Each draw is from an exact full conditional. Treated as an MH proposal, a full conditional makes the acceptance probability exactly 1, so every draw is accepted and nothing needs tuning.
