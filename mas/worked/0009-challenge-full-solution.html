(a) Explain what the offset does in w1. Interpret the WorkNonManual coefficient as a rate ratio with a 95% CI, in one sentence. [3]
(b) Explain why w1 is nested in w2, even though Water does not appear in w2. Test w1 against w2: state \(H_0\), the statistic, its df and your conclusion. Does AIC agree? [3]
(c) Find the maximised log-likelihoods of w1 and w2, and check that they give the same \(\ell_{\text{sat}}\). [2]
(d) Estimate the malformation rate per 1000 births for families of manual workers in an area with water hardness 40, with a 95% CI. Check \(\hat\eta\) by hand. [2]
(e) For row 10, compute \(r_P\), \(r_D\) and \(r_{SD}\). Compute Cook's distance for row 14. Classify rows 10, 12 and 14 as outlier, high leverage only, or influential, one sentence each. [3]
(f) Explain why the two tapply lines print identical numbers. Name one other sum that must match in the same way. [2]

(a) · Offset and rate ratio [3]

The expected count is proportional to births:

$$\begin{aligned} \lambda_i&=\text{births}_i\times\text{rate}_i\\ \log\lambda_i&=\log(\text{births}_i)+x_i^T\beta \end{aligned}$$

offset(log(births)) adds \(\log(\text{births}_i)\) to \(\eta_i\) with coefficient fixed at 1 (not estimated, not counted in \(p\)). So the model is for the rate per birth, and each \(e^{\beta_j}\) is a rate ratio.

$$\begin{aligned} e^{-0.33653}&=0.71\\ \text{CI}&=-0.33653\pm1.96(0.09677)\\ &=-0.33653\pm0.18966\\ &=(-0.5262,\ -0.1469)\\ \text{exp}&\Rightarrow(0.59,\ 0.86) \end{aligned}$$

For the same water hardness, the malformation rate for non-manual workers' families is estimated at 0.71 times that of manual workers (95% CI 0.59 to 0.86).

(b) · Nesting and LRT [3]

Water takes one value per area, so \(\beta_W\times\) Water is one particular set of area effects. w1 is w2 with the 8 area effects forced onto a straight line in hardness.

$$\begin{aligned} p_{w2}&=16-7=9\\ p_{w1}&=16-13=3\\ s&=9-3=6 \end{aligned}$$

\(H_0\): area differences are explained by water hardness alone.

$$\begin{aligned} \text{LR}&=12.271-3.060\\ &=9.21\\ \chi^2_6(0.95)&=12.59 \end{aligned}$$

\(9.21\lt12.59\), so don't reject. AIC agrees: \(102.52\lt105.31\).

The simpler w1 is enough

(c) · Log-likelihoods [2]

Poisson: \(\phi=1\), so use \(p\).

$$\begin{aligned} \hat\ell_{w1}&=3-\frac{102.52}2=-48.26\\ \hat\ell_{w2}&=9-\frac{105.31}2=-43.66\\ \ell_{\text{sat}}&=-48.26+\frac{12.271}2=-42.12\\ \ell_{\text{sat}}&=-43.655+\frac{3.0604}2=-42.12\quad\checkmark \end{aligned}$$

\(\hat\ell_{w1}=-48.26\), \(\hat\ell_{w2}=-43.66\), both give \(\ell_{\text{sat}}=-42.12\)

(d) · Rate per 1000 births [2]

$$\begin{aligned} \hat\eta&=-4.44391+40(-0.0032376)+\log1000\\ &=-4.44391-0.12950+6.90776\\ &=2.33435\quad\checkmark\\ e^{\hat\eta}&=e^{2.334347}\\ &=10.32 \end{aligned}$$

With births = 1000, \(e^{\hat\eta}\) is the expected count per 1000 births.

$$\begin{aligned} \text{CI}&=2.334347\pm1.96(0.05832)\\ &=2.334347\pm0.11431\\ &=(2.2200,\ 2.4487)\\ \text{exp}&\Rightarrow(9.21,\ 11.57) \end{aligned}$$

10.32 per 1000 births, 95% CI \((9.21,\ 11.57)\)

(e) · Diagnostics [3]

Row 10:

$$\begin{aligned} r_P&=\frac{24-39.390}{\sqrt{39.390}}\\ &=\frac{-15.390}{6.2761}\\ &=-2.45\\ d&=2\Big[24\log\frac{24}{39.390}-(24-39.390)\Big]\\ &=2\big[-11.891+15.390\big]\\ &=6.998\\ r_D&=-\sqrt{6.998}\quad(y\lt\hat\mu)\\ &=-2.65\\ r_{SD}&=\frac{-2.6454}{\sqrt{1-0.0828}}\\ &=\frac{-2.6454}{0.9577}\\ &=-2.76 \end{aligned}$$

Row 14:

$$\begin{aligned} C_{14}&=\frac{r_P^2\,h}{p(1-h)^2}\\ &=\frac{(1.3640)^2(0.4198)}{3(1-0.4198)^2}\\ &=\frac{0.7810}{1.0099}\\ &=0.77 \end{aligned}$$

Leverage threshold \(2p/n=6/16=0.375\), exceeded by rows 12 and 14.

  • Row 10: outlier. Jack-knife residual −2.75 is large but leverage 0.08 is ordinary, and dropping it barely moves Water (−0.00324 → −0.00296).
  • Row 12: high leverage only. \(h=0.45\) but \(r_P=0.68\) is small, so it pulls little.
  • Row 14: influential. High leverage and a sizeable residual, Cook's 0.77, and dropping it strengthens the Water effect by 40% (−0.00324 → −0.00454).

(f) · Why the totals match [2]

Poisson with canonical log link: the score equations are

$$\begin{aligned} X^T(y-\hat\mu)&=0 \end{aligned}$$

The offset sits in \(\hat\mu\), not \(X\), so this still holds. Intercept column and WorkNonManual column:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_i\hat\mu_i&=\sum_iy_i\\ \sum_{\text{NonManual}}\hat\mu_i&=\sum_{\text{NonManual}}y_i \end{aligned}$$

Subtracting gives the Manual totals too.

Another matching sum, from the Water column: \(\sum_i\text{Water}_i\,\hat\mu_i=\sum_i\text{Water}_i\,y_i\)
