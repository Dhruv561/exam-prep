Negative binomial family, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

Let \(Y\) have the negative binomial distribution with mean \(\mu>0\) and known size \(r>0\):

$$P(Y=y) = \frac{\Gamma(y+r)}{\Gamma(r)\,y!}\left(\frac{r}{r+\mu}\right)^{\!r}\left(\frac{\mu}{r+\mu}\right)^{\!y},\qquad y = 0,1,2,\dots$$

(a) Show that \(Y\) is from an exponential family, identifying \(\theta\), \(\phi\), \(a(\phi)\), \(b(\theta)\) and \(c(y,\phi)\). [4]
(b) Use the exponential-family lemma to find \(\mathbb EY\) and \(\operatorname{Var}Y\). [2]
(c) Find the variance function. With \(r = 3\) and \(\mu = 6\), compute \(\operatorname{Var}Y\) and compare it with a Poisson of the same mean. [2]
(d) Obtain the canonical link. Why might a log link be preferred in practice? [2]
(e) For independent \(Y_1,\dots,Y_n\) with the canonical link \(\theta_i = x_i^T\beta\), show that the score for \(\beta\) is \(X^T(y-\mu)\). [2]
(f) Faraway (§8.1) says the negative binomial is not a member of the exponential family. Reconcile this with (a) in 2–3 sentences. [1]

(a) · Exponential-family form [4]

The support does not depend on \(\mu\). Taking logs:

$$\begin{aligned} \log f(y)&=y\log\frac{\mu}{\mu+r}+r\log\frac{r}{\mu+r}+\log\Gamma(y+r)-\log\Gamma(r)-\log y! \end{aligned}$$

Match to \(\log f=\dfrac{y\theta-b(\theta)}{a(\phi)}+c(y,\phi)\). The coefficient of \(y\) gives

$$\begin{aligned} \theta&=\log\frac{\mu}{\mu+r}\\ \phi&=1,\quad a(\phi)=\phi \end{aligned}$$

Rewrite the parameter-only term in \(\theta\):

$$\begin{aligned} e^\theta&=\frac{\mu}{\mu+r}\\ 1-e^\theta&=\frac{r}{\mu+r}\\ r\log\frac{r}{\mu+r}&=r\log(1-e^\theta)=-b(\theta) \end{aligned}$$

\(\theta=\log\dfrac{\mu}{\mu+r}\), \(\phi=1\), \(a(\phi)=\phi\), \(b(\theta)=-r\log(1-e^\theta)\), \(c(y,\phi)=\log\Gamma(y+r)-\log\Gamma(r)-\log y!\)

\(\theta\lt0\). \(c\) contains \(r\), which is fine because \(r\) is known.

(b) · Mean and variance [2]

$$\begin{aligned} b'(\theta)&=\frac{re^\theta}{1-e^\theta}\\ &=r\cdot\frac{\mu/(\mu+r)}{r/(\mu+r)}\\ &=\mu\\[6pt] b''(\theta)&=\frac{re^\theta(1-e^\theta)+re^{2\theta}}{(1-e^\theta)^2}\\ &=\frac{re^\theta}{(1-e^\theta)^2}\\ &=\frac{re^\theta}{1-e^\theta}\cdot\frac1{1-e^\theta}\\ &=\mu\cdot\frac{\mu+r}{r}\\ &=\mu+\frac{\mu^2}r \end{aligned}$$

\(\mathbb EY=b'(\theta)=\mu\), \(\operatorname{Var}Y=a(\phi)b''(\theta)=\mu+\mu^2/r\)

(c) · Variance function [2]

$$\begin{aligned} v(\mu)&=b''(\theta)\text{ in terms of }\mu=\mu+\frac{\mu^2}r\\ \operatorname{Var}Y&=6+\frac{6^2}3\\ &=6+12\\ &=18 \end{aligned}$$

\(v(\mu)=\mu+\mu^2/r\). \(\operatorname{Var}Y=18\), three times the Poisson's 6

(d) · Canonical link [2]

The canonical link sets \(g(\mu)=\theta\):

$$\begin{aligned} g(\mu)&=\log\frac{\mu}{\mu+r} \end{aligned}$$

This \(g\) only takes negative values, so \(x^T\beta\lt0\) would be needed for every \(x\), which fitting can't guarantee. A log link maps \((0,\infty)\) onto all of \(\mathbb R\), so any \(\beta\) is valid, and \(e^{\beta_j}\) is a multiplicative effect on the mean.

(e) · Score under the canonical link [2]

\(\phi=1\) and \(\theta_i=x_i^T\beta\):

$$\begin{aligned} \ell(\beta)&=\sum_i\big[y_ix_i^T\beta-b(x_i^T\beta)+c(y_i)\big]\\ \frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\beta}&=\sum_i\big[y_ix_i-b'(x_i^T\beta)x_i\big]\\ &=\sum_i(y_i-\mu_i)x_i\\ &=X^T(y-\mu) \end{aligned}$$

(f) · Reconciling with Faraway [1]

Faraway means the negative binomial with \(r\) unknown. Then \(r\) sits inside \(b\) and inside \(c\) and can't be split off as a dispersion \(a(\phi)\) dividing \(y\theta-b(\theta)\), so the form fails. With \(r\) known, as here, it is a one-parameter exponential family with \(\phi=1\).
