Normal mean with a joint prior, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

Setup (fresh model; 12 marks, about 28 minutes, FX-82, 2 d.p.).

Model: \(x_1,\dots,x_n\) are independent, \(x_i\mid\mu,\tau\sim\text{Normal}(\mu,\ 1/\tau)\).

Prior: \(p(\mu,\tau)=p(\mu\mid\tau)\,p(\tau)\) with

$$\mu\mid\tau\sim\text{Normal}\Big(1,\ \frac1{2\tau}\Big),\qquad\tau\sim\text{Gamma}(3,\ 2).$$
  1. Derive the conditional distribution \(p(\mu\mid\tau,x)\). If it is a known distribution, identify the distribution name and its parameters. [4]
  2. Suppose \(n=6\) and \(\bar x=3.4\). For \(\tau=2\), find the posterior mean of \(\mu\) and a 95% credible interval for \(\mu\). Show your work. [3]
  3. Write the posterior mean in (a) as a weighted average of the prior mean and \(\bar x\), and give the two weights. Briefly explain what happens to the weights as \(n\to\infty\). [2]
  4. An analyst instead uses the priors \(p(\mu\mid\tau)\propto1\) and \(p(\tau)\propto1/\tau\). Are these priors proper? Derive \(p(\tau\mid x)\), identify it, and state when it is a proper distribution. Let \(S=\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2\). [3]

(a) · \(p(\mu\mid\tau,x)\) [4]

Keep only the \(\mu\) terms of likelihood × prior. Prior variance \(\frac1{2\tau}\) gives \(e^{-\tau(\mu-1)^2}\):

$$\begin{aligned} p(\mu\mid\tau,x)&\propto p(x\mid\mu,\tau)\,p(\mu\mid\tau)\\ &\propto\exp\Big\{-\frac\tau2\sum_i(x_i-\mu)^2\Big\}\exp\big\{-\tau(\mu-1)^2\big\} \end{aligned}$$

Expand and collect:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{exponent}&=-\frac\tau2\Big[n\mu^2-2\mu\sum_ix_i\Big]-\tau\big[\mu^2-2\mu\big]+\text{const}\\ &=-\frac\tau2(n+2)\mu^2+\tau\big(n\bar x+2\big)\mu+\text{const}\\ &=-\frac A2\mu^2+B\mu+\text{const} \end{aligned}$$

where

$$\begin{aligned} A&=(n+2)\tau\\ B&=\tau(n\bar x+2) \end{aligned}$$

Complete the square:

$$\begin{aligned} p(\mu\mid\tau,x)&\propto\exp\Big\{-\frac A2\Big(\mu-\frac BA\Big)^2\Big\}\\ \frac BA&=\frac{\tau(n\bar x+2)}{(n+2)\tau}=\frac{n\bar x+2}{n+2}\\ \frac1A&=\frac1{(n+2)\tau} \end{aligned}$$

\(\mu\mid\tau,x\sim\text{Normal}\Big(\dfrac{n\bar x+2}{n+2},\ \dfrac1{(n+2)\tau}\Big)\)

(b) · Numbers at \(\tau=2\) [3]

$$\begin{aligned} E[\mu\mid\tau,x]&=\frac{6(3.4)+2}{6+2}\\ &=\frac{22.4}8\\ &=2.80\\ \operatorname{Var}(\mu\mid\tau,x)&=\frac1{8(2)}\\ &=0.0625\\ \text{sd}&=0.25\\ \text{95\% interval}&=2.80\pm1.96(0.25)\\ &=2.80\pm0.49 \end{aligned}$$

Posterior mean \(2.80\), 95% credible interval \((2.31,\ 3.29)\)

(c) · Weighted average [2]

$$\begin{aligned} E[\mu\mid\tau,x]&=\frac{n\bar x+2}{n+2}\\ &=\frac2{n+2}\cdot1+\frac n{n+2}\cdot\bar x \end{aligned}$$

Weight \(\frac2{n+2}\) on the prior mean 1 and \(\frac n{n+2}\) on \(\bar x\) (0.25 and 0.75 at \(n=6\)). The prior acts like 2 extra observations at 1. As \(n\to\infty\), \(\frac2{n+2}\to0\) and \(\frac n{n+2}\to1\): the posterior mean tends to \(\bar x\) and the prior is washed out.

Check: \(0.25(1)+0.75(3.4)=2.80\) ✓.

(d) · Improper priors [3]

Both are improper:

$$\begin{aligned} \int_{-\infty}^\infty1\,d\mu&=\infty\\ \int_0^\infty\tau^{-1}\,d\tau&=\infty \end{aligned}$$

Use \(\sum_i(x_i-\mu)^2=S+n(\bar x-\mu)^2\):

$$\begin{aligned} p(\mu,\tau\mid x)&\propto\tau^{n/2}e^{-\frac\tau2[S+n(\bar x-\mu)^2]}\tau^{-1} \end{aligned}$$

Integrate out \(\mu\) with the Normal normalising constant:

$$\begin{aligned} p(\tau\mid x)&\propto\tau^{\frac n2-1}e^{-\frac{\tau S}2}\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-\frac{n\tau}2(\mu-\bar x)^2}\,d\mu\\ &=\tau^{\frac n2-1}e^{-\frac{\tau S}2}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{n\tau}}\\ &\propto\tau^{\frac{n-1}2-1}e^{-\frac S2\tau} \end{aligned}$$

\(\tau\mid x\sim\text{Gamma}\Big(\dfrac{n-1}2,\ \dfrac S2\Big)\), proper iff \(n\ge2\) and \(S\gt0\)

Lines a marker looks for
\(\tau\) kept in the prior variance · \(A\), \(B\) · Normal(mean, variance) · 0.25 as sd, not 0.0625 · weights and \(n\to\infty\) · both improper · \(\tau^{-1/2}\) from the integral, so shape \((n-1)/2\).
