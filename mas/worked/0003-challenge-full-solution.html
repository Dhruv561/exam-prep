Turtle nests, written out as an exam answer

(a) For model1, what is the log-likelihood of the fitted model, and of the saturated model?
(b) Assuming binomial responses, which is better, model1 or model2? Give two quantitative reasons.
(c) Is model1 an adequate fit? State what you checked first. (The fitted \(\hat p_i\) range from 0.69 to 0.85.)
(d) Give a 95% CI for the factor by which the odds of hatching change when sand temperature rises by 1 °C, under model1.
(e) What does phi1 suggest, and which of your conclusions in (b) is most at risk because of it?

(a) · Log-likelihoods

\(n=35+1=36\) and \(p=36-30=6\) (intercept, temp, three beach dummies, depth).

$$\begin{aligned} \text{AIC}&=-2\hat\ell+2p\\ \hat\ell_1&=p-\frac{\text{AIC}}2\\ &=6-\frac{224.98}{2}\\ &=-106.49 \end{aligned}$$

From \(D=2(\ell_{\text{sat}}-\hat\ell)\):

$$\begin{aligned} \ell_{\text{sat}}&=\hat\ell_1+\frac D2\\ &=-106.49+\frac{48.207}2\\ &=-82.39 \end{aligned}$$

\(\hat\ell_1=-106.49\), \(\ell_{\text{sat}}=-82.39\)

Check with model2: \(3-228.95/2+58.173/2=-82.39\) ✓.

(b) · model1 or model2?

Reason 1: LRT. model2 is nested in model1. \(H_0\): all three beach coefficients are 0.

$$\begin{aligned} \text{LR}&=D_2-D_1\\ &=58.173-48.207\\ &=9.97\\ \text{df}&=33-30=3\\ \chi^2_3(0.95)&=7.81 \end{aligned}$$

\(9.97\gt7.81\), so reject \(H_0\): beach is needed.

Reason 2: AIC. \(224.98\lt228.95\), so model1 has the lower AIC.

Under the binomial assumption, model1 is better

No single beach dummy is significant (p = 0.12, 0.10, 0.81), yet the factor is. Test factors as a block.

(c) · Goodness of fit

First check the condition. \(m_i\ge60\) and \(0.69\le\hat p_i\le0.85\), so

$$\begin{aligned} \min_im_i(1-\hat p_i)&\approx60(0.15)=9\ge5\\ \min_im_i\hat p_i&\approx60(0.69)=41.4\ge5 \end{aligned}$$

So \(D\approx\chi^2_{30}\) if model1 is adequate.

$$\begin{aligned} D&=48.21\gt\chi^2_{30}(0.95)=43.77 \end{aligned}$$

Reject adequacy: model1 does not fit well

(d) · Odds multiplier for +1 °C

$$\begin{aligned} \text{CI for }\beta_{\text{temp}}&=-0.127696\pm1.96(0.029272)\\ &=-0.127696\pm0.05737\\ &=(-0.1851,\ -0.0703)\\ e^{-0.1851}&=0.83\\ e^{-0.0703}&=0.93 \end{aligned}$$

95% CI for the odds multiplier: \((0.83,\ 0.93)\)

Point estimate \(e^{-0.1277}=0.88\): each extra degree cuts the odds of hatching by about 12%.

(e) · What phi1 says

phi1 \(=X^2/(n-p)=1.58\gt1\), which together with the failed fit test in (c) suggests overdispersion. Under \(\phi=1\) the SEs are too small by a factor of about:

$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{1.584}&=1.26 \end{aligned}$$

The LRT for beach in (b) is most at risk, because a \(\chi^2\) test on an unscaled deviance difference overstates significance.

Allowing for it:

$$\begin{aligned} F&=\frac{9.966/3}{1.584}\\ &=\frac{3.322}{1.584}\\ &=2.10 \end{aligned}$$

Compared with \(F_{3,30}(0.95)=2.92\): not significant, so the conclusion in (b) flips.

The F value wasn't asked for here (no F table in the output); it's the Lesson 12 method, shown so you see the flip.
