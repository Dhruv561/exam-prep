Call centre windows, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

Setup (fresh model; 12 marks, about 28 minutes). A call centre records the number of calls \(X_i\) in each of \(n\) ten-minute windows. Busy windows have three times the call rate of quiet ones. For windows \(i=1,\dots,m\) the type was not recorded:

$$\begin{aligned} Z_i&\sim\text{Categorical}(\pi,1-\pi)\\ X_i\mid Z_i=1&\sim\text{Poisson}(\lambda)\quad\text{(quiet)}\\ X_i\mid Z_i=2&\sim\text{Poisson}(3\lambda)\quad\text{(busy)} \end{aligned}$$

Windows \(i=m+1,\dots,n\) fell during an advertised promotion and are known to be busy: \(X_i\sim\text{Poisson}(3\lambda)\), with no \(Z_i\). All \(X_i\) are independent, \(\theta=(\pi,\lambda)\), \(X=(X_1,\dots,X_n)\), \(Z=(Z_1,\dots,Z_m)\). Your answers may contain \(P(Z_i=k\mid X_i,\theta^0)\).

  1. Derive \(Q(\theta,\theta^0)=E_{Z\mid X,\theta^0}[\log P(X,Z\mid\theta)]\), with the log pmfs expanded. [4]
  2. Derive the M-step for \(\pi\). [2]
  3. Derive the M-step for \(\lambda\). [3]
  4. \(m=4\) with \(x_{1:4}=(1,\ 4,\ 2,\ 6)\), and \(n-m=2\) promotion windows with \(x_5=5\), \(x_6=7\). With \(\theta^0=(0.5,\ 1.5)\), compute \(P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)\) for \(i=1,\dots,4\), then \(\hat\pi\) and \(\hat\lambda\) (2 d.p.). [3]

(a) · \(Q(\theta,\theta^0)\) [4]

Mixture windows get indicators; promotion windows contribute their Poisson\((3\lambda)\) pmf with no \(\pi\):

$$\begin{aligned} P(X,Z\mid\theta)&=\prod_{i=1}^m\Big[\pi\frac{\lambda^{x_i}e^{-\lambda}}{x_i!}\Big]^{I(Z_i=1)}\Big[(1-\pi)\frac{(3\lambda)^{x_i}e^{-3\lambda}}{x_i!}\Big]^{I(Z_i=2)}\\ &\qquad\times\prod_{i=m+1}^n\frac{(3\lambda)^{x_i}e^{-3\lambda}}{x_i!} \end{aligned}$$

Take logs, with \(\log(3\lambda)^{x_i}=x_i\log3+x_i\log\lambda\):

$$\begin{aligned} \log P(X,Z\mid\theta)&=\sum_{i=1}^m\Big\{I(Z_i=1)\big[\log\pi+x_i\log\lambda-\lambda-\log x_i!\big]\\ &\qquad+I(Z_i=2)\big[\log(1-\pi)+x_i\log3+x_i\log\lambda\\ &\qquad\qquad-3\lambda-\log x_i!\big]\Big\}\\ &\quad+\sum_{i=m+1}^n\big[x_i\log3+x_i\log\lambda-3\lambda-\log x_i!\big] \end{aligned}$$

Take the expectation with \(E_{Z\mid X,\theta^0}[I(Z_i=k)]=P(Z_i=k\mid X_i,\theta^0)=:\gamma_{ik}\). The last sum isn't random:

$$\begin{aligned} Q(\theta,\theta^0)&=\sum_{i=1}^m\Big\{\gamma_{i1}\big[\log\pi+x_i\log\lambda-\lambda-\log x_i!\big]\\ &\qquad+\gamma_{i2}\big[\log(1-\pi)+x_i\log3+x_i\log\lambda-3\lambda-\log x_i!\big]\Big\}\\ &\quad+\sum_{i=m+1}^n\big[x_i\log3+x_i\log\lambda-3\lambda-\log x_i!\big] \end{aligned}$$

with, by Bayes' rule,

$$\begin{aligned} \gamma_{i1}&=\frac{\pi^0(\lambda^0)^{x_i}e^{-\lambda^0}}{\pi^0(\lambda^0)^{x_i}e^{-\lambda^0}+(1-\pi^0)(3\lambda^0)^{x_i}e^{-3\lambda^0}}\\ \gamma_{i2}&=1-\gamma_{i1} \end{aligned}$$

(b) · M-step for \(\pi\) [2]

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial Q}{\partial\pi}&=\sum_{i=1}^m\Big[\frac{\gamma_{i1}}\pi-\frac{\gamma_{i2}}{1-\pi}\Big]=0\\ (1-\pi)\sum_{i=1}^m\gamma_{i1}&=\pi\sum_{i=1}^m\gamma_{i2}\\ \sum_{i=1}^m\gamma_{i1}&=\pi\sum_{i=1}^m(\gamma_{i1}+\gamma_{i2})\\ &=\pi m\\ \hat\pi&=\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m\gamma_{i1} \end{aligned}$$

Divide by \(m\), not \(n\): promotion windows have no \(Z\), so they carry no information on \(\pi\).

(c) · M-step for \(\lambda\) [3]

\(\lambda\) is in both components and in the promotion windows:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial Q}{\partial\lambda}&=\sum_{i=1}^m\Big[\gamma_{i1}\Big(\frac{x_i}\lambda-1\Big)+\gamma_{i2}\Big(\frac{x_i}\lambda-3\Big)\Big]+\sum_{i=m+1}^n\Big(\frac{x_i}\lambda-3\Big)\\ &=\frac1\lambda\sum_{i=1}^m(\gamma_{i1}+\gamma_{i2})x_i+\frac1\lambda\sum_{i=m+1}^nx_i-\sum_{i=1}^m(\gamma_{i1}+3\gamma_{i2})-3(n-m)\\ &=\frac1\lambda\sum_{i=1}^nx_i-\sum_{i=1}^m(\gamma_{i1}+3\gamma_{i2})-3(n-m)=0\\ \hat\lambda&=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^nx_i}{\sum_{i=1}^m(\gamma_{i1}+3\gamma_{i2})+3(n-m)} \end{aligned}$$

Maximum: \(\partial^2Q/\partial\lambda^2=-\sum_ix_i/\lambda^2\lt0\).

(d) · One iteration [3]

Dividing top and bottom of \(\gamma_{i1}\) by its numerator:

$$\begin{aligned} \gamma_{i1}&=\frac1{1+\frac{1-\pi^0}{\pi^0}3^{x_i}e^{-2\lambda^0}}\\ &=\frac1{1+3^{x_i}e^{-3}}\quad(\pi^0=0.5,\ \lambda^0=1.5) \end{aligned}$$

With \(e^{-3}=0.049787\):

$$\begin{aligned} x_1=1:\quad&\frac1{1+3(0.049787)}=\frac1{1.149361}=0.87\\ x_2=4:\quad&\frac1{1+81(0.049787)}=\frac1{5.032753}=0.20\\ x_3=2:\quad&\frac1{1+9(0.049787)}=\frac1{1.448084}=0.69\\ x_4=6:\quad&\frac1{1+729(0.049787)}=\frac1{37.294773}=0.03 \end{aligned}$$

Then, using \(\gamma_{i1}+3\gamma_{i2}=3-2\gamma_{i1}\):

$$\begin{aligned} \hat\pi&=\frac{0.8700+0.1987+0.6906+0.0268}4\\ &=\frac{1.7861}4\\ &=0.45\\ \textstyle\sum_{i\le4}(3-2\gamma_{i1})&=12-2(1.7861)\\ &=8.4277\\ \hat\lambda&=\frac{1+4+2+6+5+7}{8.4277+3(2)}\\ &=\frac{25}{14.4277}\\ &=1.73 \end{aligned}$$

\(\gamma_{i1}=(0.87,\ 0.20,\ 0.69,\ 0.03)\), \(\hat\pi=0.45\), \(\hat\lambda=1.73\)
