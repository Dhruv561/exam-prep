Burst water mains, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

A water utility records the time (hours) to repair 50 burst mains. Predictors: diam (pipe diameter, cm), soil (factor: clay, loam, sand; baseline clay) and depth (m). Repair times are positive and right-skewed, with spread growing with the mean, so a gamma GLM with log link is fitted. (Simulated data.)

(a) What are \(n\) and \(p\) for model1? Show that the printed dispersion 0.1578778 is the Pearson estimate, and say what it implies about the coefficient of variation of repair times.
(b) Interpret the soilloam coefficient as a multiplicative effect, with a 95% CI.
(c) Test whether soil type is needed, given diameter and depth. State the statistic, its distribution and df, and your conclusion.
(d) Explain why the test in (c) is not \(\chi^2\), and why the residual deviance 7.1464 on 45 df cannot be used to check model1's fit.
(e) Find the maximised log-likelihood of each model. Which model do you prefer? Give two quantitative reasons.
(f) Predict the mean repair time for a 40 cm main in sandy soil at 1.5 m depth, with a 95% CI. Check \(\hat\eta\) by hand.

(a) · \(n\), \(p\), dispersion

$$\begin{aligned} n&=49+1=50\\ p&=50-45=5\quad\text{(intercept, diam, 2 soil dummies, depth)}\\ \hat\phi&=\frac{X^2}{n-p}\\ &=\frac{7.104503}{45}\\ &=0.157878\quad\checkmark \end{aligned}$$

So the printed dispersion is the Pearson estimate. For a gamma GLM \(\operatorname{Var}Y=\phi\mu^2\), so

$$\begin{aligned} \text{CV}&=\frac{\text{sd}(Y)}{\mu}\\ &=\sqrt\phi\\ &=\sqrt{0.1579}\\ &=0.40 \end{aligned}$$

\(n=50\), \(p=5\). The sd of repair time is about 40% of its mean, whatever the mean

(b) · soilloam as a multiplier

Log link, so \(e^{\beta}\) multiplies the mean:

$$\begin{aligned} e^{-0.487491}&=0.61\\ \text{CI for }\beta&=-0.487491\pm1.96(0.164317)\\ &=-0.487491\pm0.3221\\ &=(-0.8096,\ -0.1654)\\ \text{exp}&\Rightarrow(0.45,\ 0.85) \end{aligned}$$

Mean repair time in loam is about 0.61 times that in clay (95% CI 0.45 to 0.85), same diameter and depth

\(t_{45}(0.975)=2.01\) in place of 1.96 is also fine, since \(\phi\) is estimated.

(c) · Is soil needed?

\(H_0\): both soil coefficients are 0 (model2 nested in model1), \(s=47-45=2\).

$$\begin{aligned} F&=\frac{(D_2-D_1)/s}{\hat\phi_1}\\ &=\frac{(8.4640-7.1464)/2}{0.1578778}\\ &=\frac{0.6588}{0.1578778}\\ &=4.17 \end{aligned}$$

Under \(H_0\), \(F\sim F_{2,45}\), and \(4.17\gt F_{2,45}(0.95)=3.20\).

Reject \(H_0\): soil type is needed

\(\hat\phi\) is the bigger model's, and the denominator df is the bigger model's residual df.

(d) · Why not \(\chi^2\), and why no fit test

\(\phi\) is unknown and is replaced by \(\hat\phi\), which has its own sampling error. Dividing by \(\hat\phi\) gives an approximate \(F\), not \(\chi^2_s/s\); treating \(\hat\phi\) as known would overstate significance.

For fit, \(D/\phi\approx\chi^2_{n-p}\) needs \(\phi\) known. Here \(\hat\phi\) comes from the same residuals, so any lack of fit is absorbed into it and there is no independent yardstick:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{D}{\hat\phi}&=\frac{7.1464}{0.1579}=45.27\approx45\text{ by construction} \end{aligned}$$

(e) · Log-likelihoods and choice

The gamma AIC counts \(\phi\) as a parameter, so use \(p+1\):

$$\begin{aligned} \hat\ell_1&=(5+1)-\frac{231.49}2\\ &=6-115.745\\ &=-109.75\\ \hat\ell_2&=(3+1)-\frac{236.17}2\\ &=4-118.085\\ &=-114.09 \end{aligned}$$

Prefer model1:

  1. The F test in (c) rejects dropping soil (\(4.17\gt3.20\)).
  2. AIC: \(231.49\lt236.17\).

\(\hat\ell_1=-109.75\), \(\hat\ell_2=-114.09\). Prefer model1

(f) · Prediction with CI

$$\begin{aligned} \hat\eta&=1.128416+40(0.025575)-0.158320+1.5(-0.037300)\\ &=1.128416+1.023000-0.158320-0.055950\\ &=1.9371\quad\checkmark\ \text{(R: 1.937164)}\\ \hat\mu&=e^{1.937164}\\ &=6.94 \end{aligned}$$

CI on the \(\eta\) scale with R's se.fit:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{CI}&=1.937164\pm1.96(0.08737868)\\ &=1.937164\pm0.1713\\ &=(1.7659,\ 2.1084)\\ \text{exp}&\Rightarrow(5.85,\ 8.24) \end{aligned}$$

\(\hat\mu=6.94\) hours, 95% CI \((5.85,\ 8.24)\)
