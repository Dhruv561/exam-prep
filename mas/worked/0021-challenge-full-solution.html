Part A

Question A (12 marks). A hospital network monitors \(n\) wards for bloodstream infections. Ward \(i\) records \(X_i\) infections over \(t_i\) hundred patient-days. The exposures \(t_i\) are known. Some wards are standard and some are high-risk, but the type is not recorded. For \(i=1,\dots,n\), independently,

$$\begin{aligned} Z_i&\sim\text{Categorical}(\pi,1-\pi)\\ X_i\mid Z_i=1&\sim\text{Poisson}(t_i\lambda_1)\quad\text{(standard)}\\ X_i\mid Z_i=2&\sim\text{Poisson}(t_i\lambda_2)\quad\text{(high-risk)} \end{aligned}$$

where \(\lambda_k\) is the infection rate per hundred patient-days. The Poisson\((\mu)\) pmf is \(f(x;\mu)=\mu^xe^{-\mu}/x!\). We aim to obtain the MLE of \(\theta=(\pi,\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\) by EM. Let \(X=(X_1,\dots,X_n)\) and \(Z=(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)\). Your answers may contain \(P(Z_i=k\mid X_i,\theta^0)\) once you have given its formula.

(a) Write down the complete-data likelihood \(P(X,Z\mid\theta)\) and the complete-data log-likelihood, with the log pmfs expanded. [2]
(b) Derive \(Q(\theta,\theta^0)=E_{Z\mid X,\theta^0}[\log P(X,Z\mid\theta)]\). [2]
(c) Give the E-step. Show that \(P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)\) does not depend on the factor \(t_i^{x_i}/x_i!\), and simplify it. [2]
(d) Derive the M-step updates for \(\pi\), \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\). [4]
(e) Six wards give

ward \(i\)123456
\(t_i\)241532
\(x_i\)190472

With \(\theta^0=(\pi^0,\lambda_1^0,\lambda_2^0)=(0.6,\ 0.5,\ 2)\), carry out one EM iteration. Give the six \(P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)\) and \(\hat\pi\), \(\hat\lambda_1\), \(\hat\lambda_2\), each to 2 d.p. [2]

(a) · Complete-data likelihood and log-likelihood [2]

$$\begin{aligned} P(X,Z\mid\theta)&=\prod_{i=1}^n\Big[\pi\frac{(t_i\lambda_1)^{x_i}e^{-t_i\lambda_1}}{x_i!}\Big]^{I(Z_i=1)}\Big[(1-\pi)\frac{(t_i\lambda_2)^{x_i}e^{-t_i\lambda_2}}{x_i!}\Big]^{I(Z_i=2)} \end{aligned}$$

Take logs, with \(\log(t_i\lambda_k)^{x_i}=x_i\log t_i+x_i\log\lambda_k\):

$$\begin{aligned} \log P(X,Z\mid\theta)&=\sum_{i=1}^n\Big\{I(Z_i=1)\big[\log\pi+x_i\log t_i\\ &\qquad\qquad+x_i\log\lambda_1-t_i\lambda_1-\log x_i!\big]\\ &\qquad+I(Z_i=2)\big[\log(1-\pi)+x_i\log t_i\\ &\qquad\qquad+x_i\log\lambda_2-t_i\lambda_2-\log x_i!\big]\Big\} \end{aligned}$$

(b) · \(Q(\theta,\theta^0)\) [2]

Given \(X\) only the indicators are random:

$$\begin{aligned} E_{Z\mid X,\theta^0}[I(Z_i=k)]&=P(Z_i=k\mid X_i,\theta^0) \end{aligned}$$

So, by linearity:

$$\begin{aligned} Q(\theta,\theta^0)&=\sum_{i=1}^n\Big\{P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)\big[\log\pi+x_i\log t_i\\ &\qquad\qquad+x_i\log\lambda_1-t_i\lambda_1-\log x_i!\big]\\ &\qquad+P(Z_i=2\mid X_i,\theta^0)\big[\log(1-\pi)+x_i\log t_i\\ &\qquad\qquad+x_i\log\lambda_2-t_i\lambda_2-\log x_i!\big]\Big\} \end{aligned}$$

(c) · E-step [2]

Bayes' rule at \(\theta^0\), with \(f\) the Poisson pmf:

$$\begin{aligned} P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)&=\frac{\pi^0f(x_i;t_i\lambda_1^0)}{\pi^0f(x_i;t_i\lambda_1^0)+(1-\pi^0)f(x_i;t_i\lambda_2^0)} \end{aligned}$$

Each density contains the same factor:

$$\begin{aligned} f(x_i;t_i\lambda_k^0)&=\frac{t_i^{x_i}}{x_i!}(\lambda_k^0)^{x_i}e^{-t_i\lambda_k^0} \end{aligned}$$

So \(t_i^{x_i}/x_i!\) cancels:

$$\begin{aligned} P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)&=\frac{\pi^0(\lambda_1^0)^{x_i}e^{-t_i\lambda_1^0}}{\pi^0(\lambda_1^0)^{x_i}e^{-t_i\lambda_1^0}+(1-\pi^0)(\lambda_2^0)^{x_i}e^{-t_i\lambda_2^0}}\\ &=\frac1{1+\dfrac{1-\pi^0}{\pi^0}\Big(\dfrac{\lambda_2^0}{\lambda_1^0}\Big)^{x_i}e^{-t_i(\lambda_2^0-\lambda_1^0)}} \end{aligned}$$

and \(P(Z_i=2\mid X_i,\theta^0)=1-P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)\).

(d) · M-step [4]

Write \(\gamma_{ik}=P(Z_i=k\mid X_i,\theta^0)\), fixed numbers here.

\(\pi\):

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial Q}{\partial\pi}&=\sum_i\Big[\frac{\gamma_{i1}}\pi-\frac{\gamma_{i2}}{1-\pi}\Big]=0\\ (1-\pi)\sum_i\gamma_{i1}&=\pi\sum_i\gamma_{i2}\\ \sum_i\gamma_{i1}&=\pi\sum_i(\gamma_{i1}+\gamma_{i2})=n\pi\\ \hat\pi&=\frac1n\sum_i\gamma_{i1} \end{aligned}$$

\(\lambda_1\): terms are \(\sum_i\gamma_{i1}(x_i\log\lambda_1-t_i\lambda_1)\):

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial Q}{\partial\lambda_1}&=\sum_i\gamma_{i1}\Big(\frac{x_i}{\lambda_1}-t_i\Big)\\ &=\frac1{\lambda_1}\sum_i\gamma_{i1}x_i-\sum_i\gamma_{i1}t_i=0\\ \hat\lambda_1&=\frac{\sum_i\gamma_{i1}x_i}{\sum_i\gamma_{i1}t_i} \end{aligned}$$

Maximum: \(\partial^2Q/\partial\lambda_1^2=-\sum_i\gamma_{i1}x_i/\lambda_1^2\lt0\).

\(\lambda_2\): the same steps with component 2's terms:

$$\begin{aligned} \hat\lambda_2&=\frac{\sum_i\gamma_{i2}x_i}{\sum_i\gamma_{i2}t_i} \end{aligned}$$

(e) · One iteration [2]

\(\dfrac{1-\pi^0}{\pi^0}=\dfrac23\), \(\dfrac{\lambda_2^0}{\lambda_1^0}=4\), \(\lambda_2^0-\lambda_1^0=1.5\), so

$$\begin{aligned} \gamma_{i1}&=\frac1{1+\tfrac23\,4^{x_i}e^{-1.5t_i}} \end{aligned}$$

Question B (12 marks). Daily temperature anomalies \(X_1,\dots,X_{150}\) (°C) at a coastal station are thought to come from two weather regimes. An analyst fits \(Z_i\sim\text{Categorical}(\pi_1,\pi_2)\), \(X_i\mid Z_i=k\sim N(\mu_k,2)\) (variance known, 2), using the lecturer's mixture.EM, EM.iter, compute.log.lik and compute.prob.x.z from EM-updated.qmd, unchanged (including dnorm(X, mean=mu.curr[k], sd=sqrt(2))*pi.curr[k]). (a) Inside EM.iter, what does P_ik = prob.x.z / rowSums(prob.x.z) compute, and what is sum(P_ik) for these data? [2]
(b) Which run's estimates would you report as the MLE? Explain what happened in each of the other three runs, quoting numbers from the output. [4]
(c) Using the printed prob.x.z, compute \(P(Z=1\mid X=-0.5)\) at EM1's estimates, and classify all three new days. [2]
(d) Using EM1's estimates, find the anomaly \(c\) below which a day is assigned to regime 1. [2]
(e) From the table, give the misclassification rate. Why can't any rule based on \(x\) alone get it to zero? [2]

(a) · What P_ik is [2]

prob.x.z[i, k] is the joint \(\pi_k^0f(x_i;\mu_k^0,2)=P(X_i=x_i,Z_i=k\mid\theta^0)\), and rowSums is \(P(X_i=x_i\mid\theta^0)\). So, by Bayes' rule:

$$\begin{aligned} \texttt{P\_ik}[i,k]&=\frac{P(X_i=x_i,Z_i=k\mid\theta^0)}{P(X_i=x_i\mid\theta^0)}\\ &=P(Z_i=k\mid X_i=x_i,\theta^0) \end{aligned}$$

That is the E-step. Each row sums to 1, so:

sum(P_ik) \(=n=150\)

(b) · Which run [4]

Report EM1 (EM2 is the same fit): its final \(\ell=-340.44\) is the highest, and the MLE maximises \(\ell\). \(\hat\pi=(0.29,\ 0.71)\), \(\hat\mu=(-2.11,\ 2.47)\).

  • EM2: label switching. Same \(\ell=-340.44\), with \(\hat\pi\) and \(\hat\mu\) in swapped order.
  • EM3: stuck. \(\mu_1^0=\mu_2^0=1\) makes every \(\gamma_{ik}=\pi_k^0\), so \(\hat\pi=(0.4,\ 0.6)\) is unchanged and both means equal \(\bar x=1.16\). \(\ell=-429.79\) is a stationary point, not the MLE.
  • EM4: stopped by max.iter. n.ll \(=4=\) max.iter \(+1\) and the trace is still rising (\(-374.05\to-364.67\), a gain of 9.37). It hadn't converged.

(c) · Classify the new days [2]

$$\begin{aligned} P(Z=1\mid X=-0.5)&=\frac{0.0423}{0.0423+0.0223}=0.65\\ P(Z=1\mid X=0.4)&=\frac{0.0167}{0.0167+0.0691}=0.19\\ P(Z=1\mid X=1.2)&=\frac{0.0052}{0.0052+0.1347}=0.04 \end{aligned}$$

Rule: regime 1 if \(P(Z=1\mid X)\gt0.5\).

\(-0.5\to\) regime 1; \(0.4\) and \(1.2\to\) regime 2

(d) · The boundary [2]

Assign to regime 1 when \(\hat\pi_1f(x;\hat\mu_1,2)\gt\hat\pi_2f(x;\hat\mu_2,2)\). Equal variances, so the constants cancel. Take logs:

$$\begin{aligned} \log\hat\pi_1-\frac{(x-\hat\mu_1)^2}4&\gt\log\hat\pi_2-\frac{(x-\hat\mu_2)^2}4\\ (x-\hat\mu_2)^2-(x-\hat\mu_1)^2&\gt4\log\frac{\hat\pi_2}{\hat\pi_1}\\ 2x(\hat\mu_1-\hat\mu_2)+\hat\mu_2^2-\hat\mu_1^2&\gt4\log\frac{\hat\pi_2}{\hat\pi_1} \end{aligned}$$

Divide by \(2(\hat\mu_1-\hat\mu_2)\lt0\), which flips the inequality:

$$\begin{aligned} x&\lt\frac{\hat\mu_1+\hat\mu_2}2+\frac{2\log(\hat\pi_2/\hat\pi_1)}{\hat\mu_1-\hat\mu_2} \end{aligned}$$

Numbers:

$$\begin{aligned} c&=\frac{-2.1092+2.4677}2+\frac{2\log(0.7137/0.2863)}{-2.1092-2.4677}\\ &=0.1793+\frac{2(0.9134)}{-4.5769}\\ &=0.1793-0.3991\\ &=-0.22 \end{aligned}$$

\(c=-0.22\)

(e) · Misclassification [2]

$$\begin{aligned} \text{rate}&=\frac{9+3}{150}\\ &=0.08 \end{aligned}$$

The regimes overlap: \(N(-2.11,2)\) and \(N(2.47,2)\) are only about 3.2 SDs apart, so some days of each regime fall on the wrong side of \(c\). Given only \(x\), the rule already picks the more probable regime, so the remaining errors are tail draws.
