Bulb lifetimes, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

Setup (fresh model, past-paper shape; about 12 marks). The lifetimes \(X_1,\dots,X_n\) (years) of bulbs from two production lines are independent. Line 2's bulbs fail at twice line 1's rate. For \(i=1,\dots,n\):

$$\begin{aligned} Z_i&\sim\text{Categorical}(\pi,1-\pi)\\ X_i\mid Z_i=1&\sim\text{Exponential}(\lambda)\\ X_i\mid Z_i=2&\sim\text{Exponential}(2\lambda) \end{aligned}$$

with \(f(x;\lambda)=\lambda e^{-\lambda x}\), \(x>0\) (formula sheet, rate form). The parameters are \(\theta=(\pi,\lambda)\). Note the twist: one \(\lambda\) appears in both components.

  1. Write the complete-data likelihood \(P(X,Z\mid\theta)\) using indicators. [2]
  2. Derive \(Q(\theta,\theta^0)=E_{Z\mid X,\theta^0}[\log P(X,Z\mid\theta)]\), with the log densities expanded. [3]
  3. Give the E-step: \(P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)\) in terms of \(\theta^0=(\pi^0,\lambda^0)\), simplified. [2]
  4. With \(n=3\), \(x=(0.2,\ 1.5,\ 0.6)\) and \(\theta^0=(0.4,\ 1)\), compute the three \(P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)\) and \(\ell(\theta^0)\), each to 2 d.p. [3]
  5. Without doing the M-step, what can you say about \(\ell(\theta^1)\), where \(\theta^1\) is the next EM iterate? Justify in one or two sentences. [2]

(a) · Complete-data likelihood [2]

For each \(i\) exactly one indicator is 1:

$$\begin{aligned} P(X,Z\mid\theta)&=\prod_{i=1}^n\big[P(Z_i=1)\,P(X_i\mid Z_i=1)\big]^{I(Z_i=1)}\\ &\qquad\times\big[P(Z_i=2)\,P(X_i\mid Z_i=2)\big]^{I(Z_i=2)}\\ &=\prod_{i=1}^n\big[\pi\lambda e^{-\lambda x_i}\big]^{I(Z_i=1)}\big[(1-\pi)2\lambda e^{-2\lambda x_i}\big]^{I(Z_i=2)} \end{aligned}$$

(b) · \(Q(\theta,\theta^0)\) [3]

Take logs:

$$\begin{aligned} \log P(X,Z\mid\theta)&=\sum_{i=1}^n\Big\{I(Z_i=1)\big[\log\pi+\log\lambda-\lambda x_i\big]\\ &\qquad+I(Z_i=2)\big[\log(1-\pi)+\log2+\log\lambda-2\lambda x_i\big]\Big\} \end{aligned}$$

Only the indicators are random given \(X\). The expectation of an indicator is a probability:

$$\begin{aligned} E_{Z\mid X,\theta^0}\big[I(Z_i=k)\big]&=P(Z_i=k\mid X_i,\theta^0) \end{aligned}$$

So by linearity:

$$\begin{aligned} Q(\theta,\theta^0)&=\sum_{i=1}^n\Big\{P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)\big[\log\pi+\log\lambda-\lambda x_i\big]\\ &\qquad+P(Z_i=2\mid X_i,\theta^0)\big[\log(1-\pi)+\log2+\log\lambda-2\lambda x_i\big]\Big\} \end{aligned}$$

(c) · E-step [2]

Bayes' rule at \(\theta^0\):

$$\begin{aligned} P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)&=\frac{\pi^0\lambda^0e^{-\lambda^0x_i}}{\pi^0\lambda^0e^{-\lambda^0x_i}+(1-\pi^0)2\lambda^0e^{-2\lambda^0x_i}} \end{aligned}$$

Divide top and bottom by \(\lambda^0e^{-\lambda^0x_i}\):

$$\begin{aligned} P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)&=\frac{\pi^0}{\pi^0+2(1-\pi^0)e^{-\lambda^0x_i}}\\ P(Z_i=2\mid X_i,\theta^0)&=1-P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0) \end{aligned}$$

(d) · Numbers [3]

With \(\pi^0=0.4\), \(\lambda^0=1\): \(P(Z_i=1\mid X_i,\theta^0)=\dfrac{0.4}{0.4+1.2e^{-x_i}}\).

$$\begin{aligned} x_1=0.2:\quad&\frac{0.4}{0.4+1.2(0.818731)}=\frac{0.4}{1.382477}=0.29\\ x_2=1.5:\quad&\frac{0.4}{0.4+1.2(0.223130)}=\frac{0.4}{0.667756}=0.60\\ x_3=0.6:\quad&\frac{0.4}{0.4+1.2(0.548812)}=\frac{0.4}{1.058574}=0.38 \end{aligned}$$

Incomplete log-likelihood, with the actual densities:

$$\begin{aligned} \ell(\theta^0)&=\sum_{i=1}^3\log\big[0.4e^{-x_i}+0.6(2)e^{-2x_i}\big]\\ &=\sum_{i=1}^3\log\big[0.4e^{-x_i}+1.2e^{-2x_i}\big] \end{aligned}$$

Responsibilities \((0.29,\ 0.60,\ 0.38)\), \(\ell(\theta^0)=-2.32\)

A positive log term is fine: continuous densities can exceed 1.

(e) · \(\ell(\theta^1)\) [2]

\(\ell(\theta^1)\ge\ell(\theta^0)=-2.32\). For all \(\theta\), \(\ell(\theta)\ge Q(\theta,\theta^0)+C\) with equality at \(\theta^0\), and the M-step maximises \(Q\):

$$\begin{aligned} \ell(\theta^1)&\ge Q(\theta^1,\theta^0)+C\\ &\ge Q(\theta^0,\theta^0)+C\\ &=\ell(\theta^0) \end{aligned}$$
