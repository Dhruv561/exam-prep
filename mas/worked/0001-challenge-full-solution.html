Binomial trays, written out as an exam answer

Everything inside the red rule is what goes on your paper. The grey notes explain the reasoning, and you don't write them. Printing this page hides the notes. Final answers are to 2 d.p., as the exam asks.

Seeds are planted in \(k\) trays. Tray \(i\) has \(m_i\) seeds (known), and \(Y_i\sim\text{Bin}(m_i,p)\) germinate, independently across trays.

(a) Write \(\ell(p)\), find \(\hat p\), and find \(\mathcal I(p)\).
(b) With 20 trays of 10 seeds and 140 germinated in total, give a 95% Wald CI for \(p\).
(c) Now write the model in terms of the logit \(\eta = \log\dfrac{p}{1-p}\), so \(p = \dfrac{e^\eta}{1+e^\eta}\). Find \(\hat\eta\) and \(\mathcal I(\eta)\), build a 95% CI for \(\eta\), and transform it into a CI for \(p\).

(a) · Log-likelihood, MLE, information

Each \(Y_i\sim\text{Bin}(m_i,p)\), independent, so

$$\begin{aligned} L(p)&=\prod_{i=1}^k\binom{m_i}{y_i}p^{y_i}(1-p)^{m_i-y_i}\\ \ell(p)&=c+\sum_iy_i\log p+\sum_i(m_i-y_i)\log(1-p) \end{aligned}$$

Differentiate and set to zero:

$$\begin{aligned} \ell'(p)&=\frac{\sum_iy_i}{p}-\frac{\sum_i(m_i-y_i)}{1-p}=0\\ (1-p)\sum_iy_i&=p\sum_i(m_i-y_i)\\ \sum_iy_i&=p\sum_im_i\\ \hat p&=\frac{\sum_iy_i}{\sum_im_i} \end{aligned}$$

Second derivative:

$$\begin{aligned} \ell''(p)&=-\frac{\sum_iy_i}{p^2}-\frac{\sum_i(m_i-y_i)}{(1-p)^2} \end{aligned}$$

\(\ell''\) contains data, so take expectations with \(\mathbb EY_i=m_ip\):

$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal I(p)&=-\mathbb E[\ell''(p)]\\ &=\frac{p\sum_im_i}{p^2}+\frac{(1-p)\sum_im_i}{(1-p)^2}\\ &=\sum_im_i\Big(\frac1p+\frac1{1-p}\Big)\\ &=\frac{\sum_im_i}{p(1-p)} \end{aligned}$$

\(\ell''\lt0\) for all \(p\), so \(\hat p\) is a maximum. \(c\) holds the binomial coefficients: no \(p\) in it.

(b) · Wald CI for \(p\)

$$\begin{aligned} \textstyle\sum_iy_i&=140,\quad\sum_im_i=20\times10=200\\ \hat p&=\frac{140}{200}=0.70\\ \text{se}(\hat p)&=\sqrt{\mathcal I(\hat p)^{-1}}\\ &=\sqrt{\frac{0.7(0.3)}{200}}\\ &=0.0324\\ \text{CI}&=0.70\pm1.96(0.0324)\\ &=0.70\pm0.0635 \end{aligned}$$

95% CI for \(p\): \((0.64,\ 0.76)\)

(c) · Work on the logit scale

Using \(\log p-\log(1-p)=\eta\) and \(\log(1-p)=-\log(1+e^\eta)\):

$$\begin{aligned} \ell(\eta)&=c+\sum_iy_i\big[\log p-\log(1-p)\big]+\sum_im_i\log(1-p)\\ &=c+\eta\sum_iy_i-\sum_im_i\log(1+e^\eta) \end{aligned}$$

Score, using \(\frac{e^\eta}{1+e^\eta}=p\):

$$\begin{aligned} \ell'(\eta)&=\sum_iy_i-\sum_im_i\,\frac{e^\eta}{1+e^\eta}\\ &=\sum_iy_i-p\sum_im_i=0\\ \hat p&=0.70\\ \hat\eta&=\log\frac{0.7}{0.3}=0.85 \end{aligned}$$

Second derivative, using \(\frac{dp}{d\eta}=p(1-p)\):

$$\begin{aligned} \ell''(\eta)&=-\sum_im_i\,p(1-p)\\ \mathcal I(\eta)&=\sum_im_i\,p(1-p)\quad\text{(no data, so no expectation needed)}\\ \mathcal I(\hat\eta)&=200(0.7)(0.3)=42 \end{aligned}$$

CI on the \(\eta\) scale:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{se}(\hat\eta)&=\frac1{\sqrt{42}}=0.1543\\ \text{CI}&=0.8473\pm1.96(0.1543)\\ &=0.8473\pm0.3024\\ &=(0.5449,\ 1.1497) \end{aligned}$$

Transform each end with \(p=e^\eta/(1+e^\eta)\):

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{e^{0.5449}}{1+e^{0.5449}}&=0.63\\ \frac{e^{1.1497}}{1+e^{1.1497}}&=0.76 \end{aligned}$$

95% CI for \(\eta\): \((0.54,\ 1.15)\). 95% CI for \(p\): \((0.63,\ 0.76)\)

Not symmetric about 0.70, and it can't leave \([0,1]\): that is why CIs are built on the \(\eta\) scale. To 3 d.p. the two CIs differ: (0.636, 0.764) vs (0.633, 0.759).
