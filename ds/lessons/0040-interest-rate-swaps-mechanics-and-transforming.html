Interest rate swaps: mechanics, and changing a loan or an investment

The goal: say who pays what in a fixed-for-floating swap, work out the net payment each period, and use a swap to turn a floating loan into a fixed one (and the reverse) by adding up the all-in cost.

1 · What a swap is (intuition first)

Suppose you borrowed at a floating rate (SOFR plus something) but you would sleep better knowing your interest bill. A bank is happy to take the floating-rate risk. You agree: each period, you pay the bank a fixed rate and the bank pays you SOFR. The SOFR you receive cancels the SOFR you owe your lender, and you are left paying fixed. That agreement is an interest rate swap: an agreement to exchange interest payments, one leg fixed and one floating, on a notional principal (slide 20).

  • The notional is never exchanged. It is only the number you multiply the rates by (slide 21).
  • Payments are netted. Only the difference changes hands each period.
  • Two sides: the fixed-rate payer (receives floating) and the floating-rate payer (receives fixed).
  • Why do it: change fixed to floating or the reverse without refinancing, and (Lesson 41) cut borrowing costs.
  • Rate names (slides 3-4): the floating rate on the slides is SOFR (US, secured overnight, replaced LIBOR); BBSW is the Australian bank-bill equivalent. The maths is identical, and on the exam the floating reference rate will be named for you.

2 · The net payment each period

With notional \(L\), fixed rate \(R_K\), floating rate \(R_F\) set for the period, and payments every six months (accrual 0.5):

$$\text{fixed leg} = L \times R_K \times 0.5,\qquad \text{floating leg} = L \times R_F \times 0.5,\qquad \text{net} = L \times (R_F - R_K) \times 0.5.$$

A positive net goes to the fixed-rate payer (floating received exceeds fixed paid). Lecture example (slides 21-22): Apple pays 3% fixed, JPMorgan pays SOFR, notional $100m, payments every 6 months. First exchange: SOFR over the period is 2.2%. Apple pays \(0.5 \times 3\% \times 100 = \$1.5\)m, JPMorgan pays \(0.5 \times 2.2\% \times 100 = \$1.1\)m, so Apple pays JPMorgan $0.4m. Second exchange, SOFR 2.8%: floating \(=\$1.4\)m, net: Apple pays $0.1m.

Also, the SOFR rate for a period is set at the start of the period and paid at its end.

Predict first: who gains when rates rise?

Pay fixed, receive floating. If SOFR rises above the fixed rate from here on, who gains? Decide, then open.

Reveal

The fixed-rate payer gains: they keep paying a fixed amount but receive a bigger floating amount. The floating-rate payer loses. If rates fall, it reverses. So a borrower with a floating loan who fears rising rates becomes the fixed-rate payer.

3 · Changing a loan: the all-in cost ledger

A swap does not touch the original loan; it adds cash flows on top. To find the new rate, write every cash flow as a line and add:

$$\text{all-in cost} = \text{external loan rate} + \text{swap rate paid} - \text{swap rate received}.$$

Lecture example (slide 25): floating to fixed. Apple borrows at SOFR + 0.1%. In the swap with Citigroup it pays 3.0% and receives SOFR.

Cash flowRate paid (-) / received (+)
Pays the lender-(SOFR + 0.1%)
Receives from swap+SOFR
Pays into swap-3.0%
Net-3.1% (fixed)

The SOFR cancels, leaving the swap's fixed rate plus the loan's spread. Slide 26: fixed to floating. Intel borrows at 3.2% fixed, receives 2.97% and pays SOFR in the swap: \(-3.2\% + 2.97\% - \text{SOFR} = -(\text{SOFR} + 0.23\%)\). The fixed rate left over after the swap's fixed receipt becomes the spread.

Fresh examples (liabilities).

  1. You borrow at SOFR + 0.5%. You pay 4.2% fixed and receive SOFR in a swap. Net: \(-(\text{SOFR} + 0.5\%) + \text{SOFR} - 4.2\% = -4.7\%\). Fixed at 4.7%.
  2. You borrow at 5.1% fixed. You receive 4.15% fixed and pay SOFR in a swap. Net: \(-5.1\% + 4.15\% - \text{SOFR} = -(\text{SOFR} + 0.95\%)\). Floating at SOFR + 0.95%.

Check. You borrow floating at SOFR + 0.5% and enter the first swap above (pay 4.2%, receive SOFR). What fixed rate do you now pay, in % to 1 dp?

%
\(4.2\% + 0.5\% = 4.7\%\). The 0.5% loan spread is not removed by the swap.

Check. You borrow fixed at 5.1% and receive 4.15% fixed while paying SOFR. After the swap you pay SOFR plus how many %, to 2 dp?

%
\(5.1\% - 4.15\% = 0.95\%\), so you pay SOFR + 0.95%.
Common slipsAdding the swap's fixed rate to the loan's fixed rate (the swap receives fixed here, so it is subtracted) · dropping the loan's spread · writing the final answer with the wrong sign convention (say "pay"/"earn" in words) · forgetting the swap's floating leg is the same SOFR as the loan's, which is exactly why it cancels.

4 · Changing an investment

Same ledger, but now the external flow is money received. All-in return \(=\) external return \(-\) swap rate paid \(+\) swap rate received. Lecture examples (slide 27): Apple holds 2.7% bonds, pays SOFR into the swap and receives 3.0%: \(2.7\% + \text{SOFR} - 3.0\% = \text{SOFR} - 0.3\%\) (fixed to floating). Intel holds an investment earning SOFR - 0.2%, receives 2.97% and pays SOFR: \((\text{SOFR} - 0.2\%) - \text{SOFR} + 2.97\% = 2.77\%\) (floating to fixed).

Fresh examples (assets).

  1. A fixed investment earning 3.8%. In a swap you receive SOFR and pay 3.5% fixed. Net return: \(3.8\% + \text{SOFR} - 3.5\% = \text{SOFR} + 0.3\%\).
  2. A floating investment earning SOFR - 0.15%. You receive 3.9% fixed and pay SOFR. Net: \((\text{SOFR} - 0.15\%) + 3.9\% - \text{SOFR} = 3.75\%\) fixed.

Check. Your investment earns SOFR - 0.15%. You swap to receive 3.9% fixed and pay SOFR. Your fixed return, in % to 2 dp?

%
\(-0.15\% + 3.9\% = 3.75\%\); the SOFR you earn cancels the SOFR you pay.

Check. Fixed investment at 3.8%; swap: receive SOFR, pay 3.5%. You now earn SOFR plus how many %, to 1 dp?

%
\(3.8\% - 3.5\% = 0.3\%\): SOFR + 0.3%.

5 · What is a swap worth, and what can go wrong?

At inception the value is zero (or near it): the fixed rate is chosen so that the fixed and floating legs are worth the same (slide 36). As rates move, the value drifts away from zero: positive for one side, negative for the other. Lesson 42 values this as a portfolio of FRAs.

Credit risk and market risk (Tutorial 10 Q2). Market risk is losing from moves in rates or exchange rates. Credit risk is the other side defaulting. In a swap you only face credit risk when the swap is worth more than zero to you: if it is a liability to you, your counterparty is the one exposed. Credit risk therefore depends on the market variables.

Short written item (theory marks)

In 2-3 sentences: a firm has a floating-rate loan and fears higher rates. Explain which side of a swap it takes and what it ends up paying. Then open the model answer.

Model answer

It becomes the fixed-rate payer (pays fixed, receives SOFR). The SOFR it receives offsets the SOFR in its loan, so its all-in cost is the swap's fixed rate plus the loan's spread, fixed whatever happens to rates. The notional is not exchanged, only net interest. If rates fall it ends up worse off than leaving the loan floating, but it has removed the uncertainty.
