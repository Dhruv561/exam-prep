Payoffs and profits · Lesson 11

$$\text{Long call } \max(S_T-K,0) \qquad \text{Long put } \max(K-S_T,0) \qquad \text{Short} = -\text{long}$$
PositionBreak-evenMax lossMax gain
Long call\(K+c\)\(c\)unlimited
Short call\(K+c\)unlimited\(c\)
Long put\(K-p\)\(p\)\(K-p\)
Short put\(K-p\)\(K-p\)\(p\)

Profit = payoff − premium paid (long) or + premium received (short). Bullish: long call, short put. Bearish: long put, short call. Intrinsic: \(\max(S-K,0)\) call, \(\max(K-S,0)\) put. Price = intrinsic + time value (peaks ATM, 0 at expiry). Quote is per share: cost = quote × 100 × contracts.

Straddle: break-evens \(K\pm(c+p)\), max loss \(c+p\) at \(S_T=K\). Bull call spread: break-even \(K_1+\text{debit}\), max loss = debit, max gain \((K_2-K_1)-\text{debit}\).

Traps
Payoff vs profit · time value always ≥ 0 only for American · forgetting contract size · wrong break-even sign for puts

Bounds · Lesson 13

$$\max(S_0-D-Ke^{-rT},0)\le c\le S_0 \qquad \max(D+Ke^{-rT}-S_0,0)\le p\le Ke^{-rT}$$ $$C\ge\max(S_0-K,0),\quad \max(K-S_0,0)\le P\le K,\quad C\ge c,\ P\ge p$$

Proofs: call + \(Ke^{-rT}\) cash and put + share both pay \(\max(S_T,K)\). Recipes: call too cheap → buy call, short stock, lend \(Ke^{-rT}\). Put too cheap → buy put and stock, borrow \(Ke^{-rT}\). Call above \(S\) → sell call, buy stock. Euro put above \(Ke^{-rT}\) → sell put, lend. Amer put below \(K-S\) → buy put and stock, exercise. Profit at \(t=0\) = gap to the bound.

Traps
Using \(S-K\) instead of \(S-Ke^{-rT}\) · forgetting the zero floor · undiscounted dividends · counting dividends after \(T\)

Put-call parity · Lesson 14

$$c+Ke^{-rT}=p+S_0 \qquad c+D+Ke^{-rT}=p+S_0 \qquad c+Ke^{-rT}=p+S_0e^{-qT}$$ $$c-p=S_0-D-Ke^{-rT}=(F_0-K)e^{-rT}$$

Fair put \(p=c+D+Ke^{-rT}-S_0\). Synthetic put = long call + short stock + lend \(Ke^{-rT}\); synthetic stock = call − put + bond. \(c-p\) rises with \(S,r,T\), falls with \(K,D,q\), unaffected by \(\sigma\). \(c=p\) at \(K=F_0\). If market \(c-p\) is above fair: sell call, buy put, buy stock, borrow. Below: reverse. Profit at \(T\) = mispricing × \(e^{rT}\).

American (no dividends): \(S_0-K\le C-P\le S_0-Ke^{-rT}\); with dividends the lower end is \(S_0-D-K\). Given \(C\): \(C+Ke^{-rT}-S_0\le P\le C+K-S_0\).

Traps
Wrong dividend sign · undiscounted \(K\) or \(D\) · discounting \(S\) · trading the wrong side

Early exercise · Lesson 15

No dividends: \(C=c\), never exercise an American call early (\(C\ge S-Ke^{-rT}>S-K\); selling beats exercising). With dividends: only just before an ex-date, and not if \(D\le K(1-e^{-r\tau})\). American put: exercise early when deep ITM; more likely with \(r\uparrow\), \(\sigma\downarrow\), deeper ITM; less likely with \(\sigma\uparrow\), \(T\uparrow\). Exercise beats selling only when time value ≈ 0. \(P>p\) is possible because \(p\le Ke^{-rT}\) but \(P\) can reach \(K\).

$$u=e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}},\; d=\tfrac1u,\; p=\frac{e^{r\Delta t}-d}{u-d},\; f=e^{-r\Delta t}[pf_u+(1-p)f_d],\; \Delta=\frac{f_u-f_d}{S_u-S_d}$$
Case\(p\)Node value

Steps: (1) \(\Delta t\) in years, \(u,d,p\) to 4 dp; (2) stock tree; (3) option values at the end; (4) backward, American: max with exercise at every node; (5) \(\Delta\) at each node. Hedge: long put or short call needs long shares; the hedge changes at each node (rebalance). Arbitrage: option dearer than tree value, sell it and hold \(\Delta\) shares; cheaper, buy it and short \(\Delta\) shares. American call, no dividends: never exercised early.

Traps
\(\Delta t\) not in years · real-world probability used for \(p\) · only taking the max at \(t=0\) on an American put · stock \(p\) used on a futures tree · \(2p(1-p)\) shortcut on an American option

Lognormal (L22): \(\ln S_T\sim\varphi(\ln S_0+(\mu-\sigma^2/2)T,\ \sigma^2T)\), second slot a variance. \(E[S_T]=S_0e^{\mu T}\), median \(S_0e^{(\mu-\sigma^2/2)T}\). 95% interval: logs \(\pm1.96\) s.d., then exponentiate (asymmetric). Real-world exercise probability uses \(\mu\); risk-neutral is \(N(d_2)\).

Hedge (L23): call \(\Delta=N(d_1)\), put \(N(d_1)-1\); shares \(=|\Delta|\times\) options. Written call / bought put: long shares. \(\mu\) absent from the formula. Limits: \(S\to\infty\), \(c\to S-Ke^{-rT}\); \(S\to0\), \(p\to Ke^{-rT}-S\).

Computing (L24): \(d_2=d_1-\sigma\sqrt T\) from unrounded \(d_1\), then round both to 2dp; \(N\) to 4dp from the formula-sheet tables (pp.5–6, both \(x\le0\) and \(x\ge0\), no interpolation; obey the paper if it says otherwise); contract size inside, round once at the end; years not months. Check with parity \(c-p=S_0-Ke^{-rT}\) and bounds. At the forward \(c\approx0.4S_0\sigma\sqrt T\).

Tree link (L25): BSM \(=\) tree as \(N\to\infty\) (\(u=e^{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}\)), same risk-neutral logic, European only. Error ~\(1/N\); ATM odd \(N\) high, even low. 2-mark answer: more steps, converges, risk-neutral both, European.

Implied vol (L26): price increasing in \(\sigma\): bracket, interpolate \(\sigma_{lo}+\frac{c_{mkt}-c_{lo}}{c_{hi}-c_{lo}}(\sigma_{hi}-\sigma_{lo})\). Above your forecast: rich (sell). Equity smirk, currency smile. VIX 15 = 15% p.a.; futures \(\times1000\) (buy 18.5, close 19.3, 1 contract: +$800).

Black (L27): \(c-p=e^{-rT}(F_0-K)\); \(F_0=K\Rightarrow c=p\). Higher \(r\) lowers both \(c\) and \(p\) (discounting only). Multiply by contract size last.

Traps
\(\sigma^2T\) read as s.d. · months for \(T\) · \(d_2\) in place of \(d_1\) in the stock term · \(N(-d)\) read off as \(N(d)\) · \(r\) put inside Black's \(d_1\) · spot instead of \(F_0\) · missing contract multiplier · dividend after expiry subtracted · \(\mu\) vs \(r\) in exercise probability
