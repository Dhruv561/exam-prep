Payoffs · Lesson 1

$$\text{Long: } (F_t - F_0)\times\text{size}\times N \qquad \text{Short: } (F_0 - F_t)\times\text{size}\times N$$

Closing out: take the opposite trade in the same delivery month. The profit is fixed at the close-out price. Two offsetting forwards: payoff \(= Q\,(F_{\text{sell}} - F_{\text{buy}})\), independent of the spot price.

Margin account algorithm · Lesson 1

  1. Totals: initial \(\times N\), maintenance \(\times N\)
  2. Daily gain = (settle − previous price) × size × \(N\), with the sign flipped for a short. Day 1 and newly added contracts are measured from their entry price
  3. Balance strictly below total maintenance → call = total initial − balance (balance becomes initial)
  4. Withdraw only the excess over total initial
  5. Check: deposits − withdrawals + cumulative gain = balance

How many contracts can capital \(K\) buy?\(\lfloor K/\text{initial per contract}\rfloor\)

LeverageReturn on capital \(=\) profit ÷ margin posted, which is much larger than the % move in the underlying
Traps
Restoring to maintenance instead of initial · forgetting size or \(N\) · the wrong sign for a short · marking new contracts from yesterday's settle

Convergence · Lesson 1

\(F_T = S_T\) at delivery. If \(F_T > S_T\): short the futures, buy spot, deliver, and profit \(F_T - S_T\). If \(F_T < S_T\) there's no clean arbitrage, because the short chooses delivery timing. Delivery options held by the short make the contract less attractive to longs, so they lower the futures price.

Same total, different timing: \(\sum (F_t - F_{t-1}) = F_T - F_0 = S_T - F_0\) (ignoring margin interest). Hedged net price \(= F_0\). Clearing house net position \(= 0\). Since 2008 many standardised OTC trades are centrally cleared or collateralised, so distrust "only" statements.

Advantage sorting

Delivery · Lesson 2

The short chooses grade, place and timing → these options lower \(F_0\). Vague quality → shorts deliver the worst grade → the contract fails. Cash-settled (e.g. SPI 200): final settlement price = spot.

Hedging and basis risk · Lesson 3

Natural long (will sell / holds) → short futures. Natural short (will buy) → long futures. Name the hedge after the futures leg.

$$\text{Effective price (either side)} = F_1 + b_2 = S_1 + (b_2 - b_1), \qquad b = S - F$$

Only \(b_2\) is unknown: hedging swaps price risk for basis risk. Versus expectation, a short hedger gains if the basis strengthens and a long hedger gains if it weakens. A perfect hedge locks in \(F_1\), not today's spot. (Slides write the same thing as \(F_0 + b_1\).)

Contract: the first delivery month after the hedge ends (avoid the delivery month). \(N = \) exposure ÷ contract size. Rolling: price \(= S_{\text{final}} + \sum (F_{\text{open}} - F_{\text{close}})\); risks are rollover and liquidity (Metallgesellschaft). FX payable → long forward; receivable → short forward.

Traps
“Futures eliminate basis risk” · “the hedger is now speculating” · “need the margin balance to tell” · for hedging = focus on core business; against = shareholders can diversify themselves

Cross hedging and the MVHR · Lesson 4

$$h^* = \rho\,\frac{\sigma_S}{\sigma_F} = \frac{\sigma_{SF}}{\sigma_F^2} \qquad N^* = \frac{h^* Q_A}{Q_F} \qquad \text{effectiveness} = \rho^2$$

Effective price \(= F_0 + (S_1^* - F_1) + (S_1 - S_1^*)\): ordinary basis + asset-mismatch basis. Std dev left \(= \sigma_S\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\). From a stats table use \(\rho(\text{your spot}, \text{futures})\), \(\sigma(\text{your spot})\), \(\sigma(\text{futures})\); the proxy spot, means, betas and alphas are distractors. Flip direction if \(\rho < 0\). “Ignoring tailing” → use \(N^*\) as above, rounded.

Traps
\(\rho = 1\) doesn't give \(h^* = 1\) (needs \(\sigma_S = \sigma_F\)) · \(h^*\) can exceed 1 · effectiveness is \(\rho^2\), not \(\rho\) · effective price can't be known today (basis risk)

Stock index futures · Lesson 5

$$N^* = (\beta - \beta^*)\,\frac{V_A}{V_F}, \qquad V_F = F_0 \times \text{multiplier}$$

\(N^* > 0\) → short (beta down); \(N^* < 0\) → long \(|N^*|\) (beta up). Works for any sign of \(\beta\). If only \(S, r, q\) are given, first compute \(F_0 = S_0e^{(r-q)T}\). P&L \(\approx \beta^* \times\) market move \(\times V_A\).

Expected return over the period: \(r_f + \alpha + \beta^*(E[r_m] - r_f)\), every rate scaled to the period (6 months: \(r_f/2\), \(\alpha/2\)). \(\beta^* = 0\) locks in \(r_f + \alpha\). Residual risks: market risk if \(\beta^* \ne 0\), basis risk (portfolio ≠ index, maturity mismatch), rounding, beta drift.

Traps
Wrong direction · using only \(\beta\) or only \(\beta^*\) · annual \(r_f\) or \(\alpha\) in a 6-month formula · forgetting the multiplier

Cost of carry and arbitrage · Lesson 6

Rates: \(r_c = m\ln(1 + r_m/m)\); \(r_m = m(e^{r_c/m} - 1)\). Convert to continuous before using \(e^{rT}\).

$$F_0 = S_0e^{rT} \quad\text{(investment asset, no income, no storage)}$$
QuoteTrade at \(t=0\)Profit at \(T\)
\(F_0 > S_0e^{rT}\)Borrow \(S_0\), buy spot, short forward (cash-and-carry)\(F_0 - S_0e^{rT}\)
\(F_0 < S_0e^{rT}\)Short or sell spot, invest \(S_0\), long forward (reverse)\(S_0e^{rT} - F_0\)

Net cash flow at \(t=0\) is 0 and \(S_T\) cancels at \(T\). Contracts \(=\) ($ value ÷ index level) ÷ units per contract. Expected \(S_T\) never enters the price (replication, not a forecast).

Traps
Comparing \(F_0\) with \(S_0\) instead of \(S_0e^{rT}\) · \(S_T\) left in the answer means a missing leg · rounding to 4 dp at each step changes big-notional answers · the reverse trade fails for consumption assets

Income and yield · Lesson 7

$$F_0 = (S_0 - I)e^{rT}, \quad I = \sum_{t_i \le T} D_ie^{-r_it_i} \qquad\qquad F_0 = S_0e^{(r-q)T}$$

One idea: strip out the income the forward buyer misses, then grow at \(r\). \(r > q\) → \(F > S\); \(r < q\) → \(F < S\). A bigger or earlier dividend lowers \(F\). Index futures: F too high → borrow, buy stocks/ETF, short futures; too low → short stocks, invest, long futures. With a discrete dividend, split the loan so the dividend repays the short one.

Traps
Discounting a coupon at the \(T\)-rate · including income paid after \(T\) · reading “F below spot” as mispriced when \(q > r\)

Currency forwards · Lesson 8

$$F_0 = S_0e^{(r - r_f)T} \qquad S_0, F_0 = \text{domestic per 1 foreign}$$

First rewrite as “1 [foreign] = \(S_0\) [domestic]”. In “X per AUD”, AUD is foreign. Flipping the quote swaps \(r\) and \(r_f\). The high-rate currency trades at a forward discount; a premium is arithmetic, not a forecast.

F too high: borrow domestic, buy foreign, deposit at \(r_f\), short forward. Too low: borrow foreign, sell spot, deposit at \(r\), long forward. Size from the end: foreign today \(= Ne^{-r_fT}\). Several maturities: fair value of each to 4 dp, trade only the one that's off. Importer → long forward; exporter → short forward. A wider forward premium makes the importer's hedge dearer.

Commodities and convenience yield · Lesson 9

$$F_0 = (S_0+U)e^{rT} \;\text{ or }\; S_0e^{rT} + C_T \;\text{ or }\; S_0e^{(r+u)T} \qquad F_0e^{yT} = (S_0+U)e^{rT}$$

Investment commodity (gold, silver): equality, \(y = 0\). Consumption commodity (oil, copper): \(F_0 \le\) full carry only, since holders won't sell inventory they need. F below full carry: gold → reverse arbitrage (and you save storage); oil → no arbitrage, \(y > 0\). \(y = \frac1T\ln(F^*/F_0)\); \(c = r - q + u\).

ContangoBackwardation
Industry (vs spot)\(F_0 > S_0\)\(F_0 < S_0\)
Normal (vs expected spot)\(F_0 > E[S_T]\)\(F_0 < E[S_T]\)

Supply shock: prompt rises more than deferred → steeper backwardation, higher \(y\). Glut: deeper contango toward full carry, \(y \to 0\).

Traps
Compounding storage that's paid at \(T\) · “silver has a convenience yield” · “storage allows any premium” · not dividing the log by \(T\)

Valuing forwards and futures · Lesson 10

$$f_{\text{long}} = (F_0 - K)e^{-rT} \qquad f_{\text{short}} = (K - F_0)e^{-rT}$$

Equivalent long forms: \(S_0 - Ke^{-rT}\) · \(S_0 - I - Ke^{-rT}\) · \(S_0e^{-qT} - Ke^{-rT}\) · \(S_0e^{-r_fT} - Ke^{-rT}\). Re-date: \(T\) = time remaining; \(I\) = dividends still to come. Futures are worth 0 after each settlement.

Forward = futures price if \(r\) is constant; corr(rates, price) \(> 0\) → futures \(>\) forward. Calendar bound \(F_2 \le F_1e^{r(t_2-t_1)}\).

Traps
Value is not \(S - K\) · value at inception is 0 (price ≠ value) · sign flip for the short · forgetting to discount
