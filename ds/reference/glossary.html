Glossary

Every lesson uses these terms in exactly this sense. New terms get added with each lesson.

TermMeaningLesson
DerivativeA contract whose value is derived from the price of an underlying asset1
UnderlyingThe asset (commodity, index, currency, …) the derivative is written on1
Spot price \(S_t\)The price for immediate delivery at time \(t\)1
Futures price \(F_t\)The price agreed at \(t\) for delivery at \(T\). Today's is \(F_0\)1
ForwardAn OTC agreement to trade at \(T\) at a fixed price, settled once1
Futures contractAn exchange-traded, standardised forward, settled daily1
Long / shortAgreed to buy (gains if the price rises) / agreed to sell (gains if the price falls)1
Contract size / multiplierThe amount of underlying per contract, or $ per index point (SPI 200: $25)1
Close outEnter the opposite trade in the same contract to cancel the position1
Delivery / cash settlementSettling by handing over the asset / by paying the difference against spot1
Clearing houseThe exchange's intermediary that is counterparty to both sides and guarantees performance1
Marking to market (daily settlement)Moving each day's gain or loss into or out of the margin account1
Initial marginThe deposit required to open a position. A call restores the balance to this level1
Maintenance marginThe floor. A balance strictly below it triggers a margin call1
Margin callThe demand to top the account back up to initial margin1
Settlement priceThe exchange's end-of-day price used for marking to market1
BasisSpot − futures (\(S_t - F_t\)). It goes to 0 at delivery (convergence)1
ArbitrageA riskless profit with no net investment1
Hedger / speculator / arbitrageurReduces an existing risk / bets on a price view / exploits mispricing1
OTC"Over the counter": traded privately between dealers, not on an exchange1
NovationReplacing a trade between buyer and seller with two contracts, each with the clearing house2
Counterparty (credit) riskThe risk that the other side of a contract fails to pay. Forwards carry it; futures largely don't2
Liquidity risk (futures)The risk of having to find cash for margin calls before a position's gains arrive2
Delivery optionsThe short's choice of grade, place and timing of delivery. They lower the futures price2
Final settlement priceFor a cash-settled contract, the last mark-to-market price, set equal to spot2
Open interestContracts outstanding = total long positions = total short positions2
Trading volumeContracts traded in a period, each trade counted once2
Long hedger / short hedgerWill buy the asset later, so goes long / will sell it later, so goes short2
Natural position (naturally long / short)The firm's existing exposure: long if it holds or will sell the asset, short if it must buy it later3
Short hedge / long hedgeA hedge using a short / long futures position, taken by a naturally long / naturally short firm3
Perfect hedgeRemoves all risk. Needs the same asset, close-out at the futures' maturity and an exact size3
Basis strengthening / weakeningThe basis \(S - F\) rises / falls. Short hedgers gain when it strengthens, long hedgers when it weakens3
Closing basis \(b_2\)\(S_2 - F_2\) when the hedge is closed out3
Basis riskUncertainty in the closing basis: the risk left after hedging3
Effective priceThe hedger's net price after the futures gain or loss: \(F_1 + b_2\) (slides: \(F_0 + b_1\))3
Rolling the hedge / stack and rollClosing a near contract and reopening in a later one, repeatedly, to hedge a long-dated exposure3
Rollover riskThe risk from the spread between contracts at each roll3
Cross hedgeHedging with futures on a different but correlated asset4
Asset-mismatch basis\(S_1 - S_1^*\): the spread between your asset and the futures' underlying at close-out4
Hedge ratio \(h\)Size of the futures position ÷ size of the exposure4
Naive hedge\(h = 1\): one unit of futures per unit of exposure4
Minimum-variance hedge ratio \(h^*\)\(\rho\sigma_S/\sigma_F = \sigma_{SF}/\sigma_F^2\), the slope of \(\Delta S\) regressed on \(\Delta F\)4
Hedge effectivenessFraction of variance removed by \(h^*\): \(\rho^2\)4
Tailing the hedgeAdjustment for daily settlement (\(N = h^*S_0Q_A/(F_0Q_F)\)). Finals say “ignore tailing”4
Stock index futuresCash-settled futures on an index level, worth a fixed multiplier per point (SPI 200: A$25)5
Beta \(\beta\)Expected % move of a portfolio per 1% market move; regression slope \(\rho\sigma_P/\sigma_M\)5
CAPM\(E[r] = r_f + \alpha + \beta(E[r_m] - r_f)\) over a period5
Market risk premium\(E[r_m] - r_f\): extra expected return for holding the market5
Alpha \(\alpha\)Return from stock picking beyond what beta explains5
Target beta \(\beta^*\)The beta the portfolio plus futures is set to5
Market-neutral\(\beta^* = 0\): market moves cancel out5
Locking in alphaFully beta-hedging so the expected return is \(r_f + \alpha\)5
Investment assetHeld by many purely as an investment (shares, bonds, indices, gold, silver). Arbitrage pins \(F_0\) exactly6
Consumption assetHeld mainly to be used up (oil, copper, wheat). Arbitrage gives only an upper bound on \(F_0\)6
Continuous compounding\(A\) grows to \(Ae^{rT}\); discount with \(e^{-rT}\)6
Effective annual rateWhat $1 actually earns in a year: \(e^{r_c} - 1\)6
Cost of carry \(c\)Financing + storage − income from holding the asset to \(T\): \(c = r - q + u\)6
Short sellingBorrow the asset, sell it now, buy it back later to return it; you owe any income it pays meanwhile6
Cash-and-carry arbitrageWhen \(F_0\) is above fair: borrow, buy spot, short the forward6
Reverse cash-and-carryWhen \(F_0\) is below fair: short (or sell) spot, invest, long the forward6
Known income \(I\)PV of cash income paid before \(T\), each payment discounted at its own maturity's rate7
Known yield \(q\)Income paid continuously as a % of the asset's value, p.a. continuously compounded7
Dividend yield (index)\(q\) for a stock index: its dividends treated as a continuous yield7
Index arbitrageTrading the index stocks (or an ETF) against index futures when \(F_0 \ne S_0e^{(r-q)T}\)7
Program tradingComputer-executed simultaneous orders across a whole basket of stocks7
ETFExchange-traded fund tracking an index; trades the basket in one order7
Domestic / foreign currency (in a quote)In “X per Y”, Y is foreign (the thing priced, yield \(r_f\)) and X is domestic (rate \(r\)). Set by the quote, not where you live8
Covered interest parity (CIP)\(F_0 = S_0e^{(r-r_f)T}\), enforced by covered interest arbitrage8
Covered interest arbitrageBorrow one currency, convert at spot, invest at the other's rate, lock the conversion back with a forward8
Forward premium / discountThe foreign currency's forward is above / below spot (\(F_0 > S_0\) when \(r > r_f\))8
Hedge ratio (FX)The share of a foreign-currency exposure that is hedged8
Storage cost \(U\) / \(u\)PV of dollar storage costs (net of income) / storage as a continuous % of the price p.a.9
Full carry \(F^*\)The cost-of-carry price \((S_0+U)e^{rT}\), or \(S_0e^{rT} +\) storage paid at \(T\)9
Convenience yield \(y\)Benefit of holding the physical commodity, as a rate: \(F_0e^{yT} = (S_0+U)e^{rT}\). Zero for investment assets9
Industry contango / backwardation\(F_0 > S_0\) (upward curve) / \(F_0 < S_0\)9
Normal contango / backwardation\(F_0 > E[S_T]\) / \(F_0 < E[S_T]\)9
Futures curve; prompt / deferredFutures prices plotted against maturity; nearest / later maturities9
Delivery price \(K\)The price written into a forward at entry (that day's forward price). It never changes10
Value of a forward \(f\)0 at inception; long \((F_0-K)e^{-rT}\), short \((K-F_0)e^{-rT}\)10
Offsetting forwardA new opposite forward entered today at \(F_0\), locking in \(F_0 - K\) at \(T\)10
Value of a futures positionCumulative \(F - K\), already paid via daily settlement; worth 0 after each settlement10
Calendar (spread) bound\(F_2 \le F_1e^{r(t_2-t_1)}\) for two maturities on the same asset, no storage10
Call / put optionRight (not obligation) to buy / sell the underlying at the strike \(K\) by expiry \(T\)11
PremiumThe price paid up front for an option (\(c\) call, \(p\) put; \(C\), \(P\) American)11
Holder / writerLong the option (has the right) / short the option (has the obligation)11
European / American optionExercisable only at expiry / at any time up to expiry11
Payoff vs profitPayoff ignores the premium, profit includes it. Long call profit \(=\max(S_T-K,0)-c\)11
Break-evenCall: \(K+c\). Put: \(K-p\)11
Moneyness (ITM / ATM / OTM)Call ITM if \(S>K\), put ITM if \(S<K\), ATM if \(S=K\)11
Intrinsic value\(\max(S-K,0)\) for a call, \(\max(K-S,0)\) for a put11
Time valuePremium − intrinsic value. Highest near the money, 0 at expiry. Always ≥ 0 for American options, can be negative for a deep-ITM European put11
Straddle / strangleLong call + long put at the same strike / at different strikes. A bet on large moves11
Protective put / covered callStock + long put (floor) / stock + short call (capped upside)11
Bull / bear spreadBull call spread: buy low-\(K\) call, sell high-\(K\) call. Bear is the reverse11
Volatility \(\sigma\)Uncertainty of the stock's return. Raises both calls and puts (limited-liability asymmetry)12
Limited-liability asymmetryThe option holder keeps upside but loses at most the premium, so more spread helps12
Ex-dividend dateDate after which a buyer no longer gets the dividend. The stock drops by about the dividend12
No-arbitrage boundA floor or ceiling on an option price. Violating it gives a riskless profit13
PV of strike \(Ke^{-rT}\)The strike discounted at the risk-free rate, used in all European bounds and parity13
PV of dividends \(D\)Dividends before \(T\), each discounted from its own date. Subtract for calls, add for puts13
Short saleSelling a borrowed share now, to buy it back later13
Put-call parity\(c+Ke^{-rT}=p+S_0\) (European, no dividends). With dividends: \(c+D+Ke^{-rT}=p+S_0\)14
Fiduciary callCall + cash \(Ke^{-rT}\). Matches a protective put at \(T\) (pays \(\max(S_T,K)\))14
Synthetic positionReplicating an instrument with others, e.g. synthetic put = long call + short stock + lend \(Ke^{-rT}\)14
American put-call range\(S_0-K\le C-P\le S_0-Ke^{-rT}\); with dividends the lower end is \(S_0-D-K\)14
Early exerciseExercising an American option before expiry. Beats selling only when time value is about 015
Insurance valueWhat a call's holder keeps by not committing to buy: protection if the stock falls15
One-period tree\(S_0\to S_u,S_d\); \(u=S_u/S_0\), \(d=S_d/S_0\); option \(f_u,f_d\)16
