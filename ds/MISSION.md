# Mission: Derivative Securities (FNCE30007)

## Why
Do well in the FNCE30007 Derivative Securities final exam on **5 November 2026**, starting from scratch on 12 October 2026 (about 3.5 weeks, shared with MAST20030, which is sat the same day).

## Success looks like
- Can do exam-level calculations without looking anything up: margin accounts, hedge ratios, forward/futures pricing, binomial trees, BSM, dividends, Greeks and delta hedging
- Can tell from a scenario which position (long or short, futures or options, how many contracts) a hedger, speculator or arbitrageur should take, and can lay out an arbitrage step by step with cash flows at t=0 and t=T
- Can answer the short written conceptual questions (about 15% of marks) and case-study questions (cases are examinable: Hershey, oil futures, GameStop, binomial on futures options, BSM) in a few sentences, and do short derivations from a given portfolio setup

## Constraints
- About 24 days, shared with DE (same exam day) and MAS (10 Nov)
- Course materials: `lectures/` (slides; the "notes" PDFs are handwritten scans), `tutes/` (questions + solutions), `Derivative Securities Notes.pdf` (2019 student summary), textbook Hull, *Options, Futures and Other Derivatives*
- Past exams in `exams/` and `MST/` are reserved for the user to sit timed. Lessons copy their patterns, tricks and difficulty but never their questions
- Format seen in past finals: 3 h, about 10 multi-part calculation questions; the MST had a Casio FX-82 calculator and a formula sheet
- **Official 2026 final info (lecturer's "Final exam information" PDF, exams/Final exam information.pdf; details in NOTES.md "Official 2026 final exam info")**: Topics 1–10, 30% Topics 1–4 / 70% Topics 5–10; 8 short-answer questions, NO MCQ; 65 marks, 65% of grade; ~85% calculation, ~15% theory; derivations and case studies examinable; Casio fx-82 only; dp specified per question; guest lecture and the traditional binomial valuation approach excluded; binomial ≤ 3 steps; BSM d1, d2 rounded to 2 dp, no interpolation; swaps use one same floating rate for all floating payments

## Out of scope
- Derivations beyond what the lectures show (e.g. stochastic calculus proofs of BSM)
